## The 4 questions every HDB upgrader must answer first

Before you fall in love with a unit, you need clear answers to four questions. Get these right and the rest of the journey is mostly logistics. Get them wrong and you risk holding two homes, or none.

### 1. How much will my HDB actually net me?

The number that matters is not the sale price. It is what lands in your pocket after you refund the CPF you used, plus the accrued interest that has been quietly compounding at 2.5% a year since the day you bought. Many owners are surprised by how much of their "profit" goes straight back into their CPF Ordinary Account rather than into cash.

### 2. How much CPF and cash do I really have to deploy?

Your buying power is the cash proceeds from the sale, plus the CPF that flows back in, plus any savings you are willing to commit. Knowing the split between cash and CPF matters, because some costs (such as the option fee and part of the stamp duty) often need cash up front.

### 3. What can I comfortably afford?

Your loan is capped by two rules. TDSR limits total monthly debt to 55% of gross income. For an EC bought from a developer, MSR also caps the housing loan portion at 30% of gross income. Banks stress test your eligibility at a higher notional rate, even though current bank rates sit around 1.5%. The word that matters here is "comfortably", not "maximum".

### 4. What is my full exit and entry timing plan?

This is the question that protects you. When do you sell, when do you buy, and how do the two line up so you are never caught paying two mortgages, or scrambling for somewhere to live? For HDB owners buying private property, ABSD timing and the sale of your flat are tightly linked. A clear plan removes the panic.

## The 3 most expensive mistakes HDB upgraders make

### Mistake 1: Timing the sale wrong

Sell too early and you may need a rental and a second move while you wait for your new home. Sell too late and you risk paying two mortgages at once, or facing ABSD pressure on the private purchase. The fix is a written timing plan that sequences your sale and your purchase before you commit to either.

### Mistake 2: Underestimating the CPF refund hit

The accrued interest on the CPF you used keeps growing for as long as you own the flat. Owners who only look at sale price minus loan often overestimate their cash proceeds by a wide margin. The fix is to calculate the full CPF refund, principal plus accrued interest, before you assume what you can spend.

### Mistake 3: Shopping for units before knowing their number

It is the most common one, and the most painful. You view a beautiful unit, fall for it, then discover the financing does not work, or it works only by stretching dangerously thin. The fix is the whole point of this roadmap: know your number first, then shop only inside it.

## What good looks like

The figures below are illustrative ranges only. They are not a quote, a forecast, or a real client result. Every household is different. Use them to see the shape of a well planned upgrade, not as a promise.

**An illustrative example (figures are illustrative only)**

A couple past their MOP sells their flat. After refunding CPF principal and accrued interest, a meaningful share of the proceeds returns to their CPF rather than arriving as cash. They combine the refunded CPF, their cash proceeds, and a modest amount of savings to form their deposit. They size the new loan well within TDSR and, where MSR applies, within that limit too, choosing a monthly commitment they can hold comfortably even if rates move. They sequence the sale and the purchase so there is never a month of two mortgages.

The point is not the numbers. The point is the order: numbers first, timing planned, then shopping. That is what calm, confident upgrading looks like.
