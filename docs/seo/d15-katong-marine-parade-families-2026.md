# Living in District 15: Katong, Marine Parade, Joo Chiat for Families in 2026

District 15 has a particular pull on Singapore families that is hard to pin down until you walk it. It is not the cheapest district. It is not the most convenient for the CBD commute. But families who move here tend to stay, and that stubbornness in the market is a data point worth understanding before you decide whether it fits your situation.

This guide covers the neighbourhood character, school options, MRT connectivity now that the Thomson-East Coast Line is fully operational, realistic price bands for 2025 and 2026, and one honest watch-out that I see buyers routinely underweight.

---

## What D15 Feels Like to Live In

Katong, Marine Parade and Joo Chiat sit along Singapore's eastern coastline in a ribbon of old shophouses, low-rise private estates and mature HDB precincts. The architecture is notably different from the glass-and-steel belt of Districts 9 and 10 — Peranakan terrace houses in mint, coral and yellow, tiled five-foot ways, roti prata places that have been trading since the 1970s.

For families, the texture matters. East Coast Park is accessible on foot or by bike from most addresses in the district, giving children a usable green corridor. The food options along Joo Chiat Road and East Coast Road are a genuine quality-of-life asset — not a brochure talking point. Weekend mornings in this neighbourhood feel lived-in in a way that newer developments cannot manufacture.

The resident base skews toward established professionals, many long-term owners who bought in the 2000s and early 2010s. That stability is why demand holds here through softer markets.

## Schools Within 1–2km: Where D15 Earns Its Reputation

School access is the reason a significant portion of D15 buyers are families with children aged four to eleven. The primary school landscape is unusually strong for a single district.

**CHIJ (Katong) Primary** sits in the Marine Parade zone and draws families seeking the mission school culture. The 1km registration buffer is competitive — verify your exact address against MOE's portal before committing.

**Haig Girls' School** in Eunos serves the Katong and Geylang Serai fringe. For families with daughters, a girls-only primary in a district with strong community ties is a meaningful draw.

**Tao Nan School** on Neptune Court is one of Singapore's oldest Chinese-medium-heritage primaries, known for academic rigour. The affiliated secondary pathway is a factor for families planning ahead.

**Ngee Ann Primary** in Potong Pasir is relevant for families on the Siglap or Chai Chee fringe of the district.

The honest read: map your preferred school's 1km circle first, then search for property within it. D15 is not uniformly served — the school buffers are patchy by sub-zone.

## MRT Connectivity: The TEL Changes the Calculation

For years, D15 was underserved by MRT. The East-West Line stops at Paya Lebar and Eunos, which left the core Katong and Marine Parade belt reliant on buses or a drive to the nearest station.

The Thomson-East Coast Line has restructured this. The relevant stations for families living in D15 are:

- **Marine Parade** — serves the central Marine Parade Road belt, with direct TEL access to Orchard (roughly 15 minutes) and Marina Bay
- **Marine Terrace** — covers the Joo Chiat and Tembeling Road corridor
- **Siglap** — serves the upper Siglap and Frankel Estate area, which is popular with families seeking larger freehold landed homes and older condo estates
- **Bayshore** — opens up the East Coast Park fringe closer to Bedok South

This connectivity materially changes commute feasibility. A family where one parent works in Orchard or the central business district and the other needs school-run flexibility now has a viable public transport option that was not realistic five years ago. Property closer to Marine Parade and Siglap stations will reflect this in pricing.

## What You Will Pay: Condo and HDB Price Bands in 2025–2026

**Private Condominiums.** Resale condos in D15 have been trading broadly between $1,800 and $2,400 PSF in the current market cycle, with older leasehold developments anchoring the lower end and newer freehold projects commanding the upper range. For a 1,100 sq ft three-bedder — workable for a family with one or two young children — that translates to roughly $2.0 million to $2.6 million for a mid-tier resale unit.

New launches in the Joo Chiat corridor, including projects positioned around the Straits at Joo Chiat site, are targeting the upper band of that range and in some cases above it. Buyers considering new launches need to model stamp duty, ABSD (if applicable), and the construction wait period against resale options where you can assess the actual unit before committing.

**HDB Resale.** Marine Parade HDB flats are among the most expensive HDB resale stock in Singapore. A 4-room flat here trades in the $700,000 to $950,000 range depending on floor, facing and lease remaining. For families with Singapore Citizen status and household income within HDB grant thresholds, the Enhanced CPF Housing Grant can reduce effective cost — but Marine Parade pricing means grant impact is proportionally smaller than in suburban estates. Buyers with $1 million or more in household savings and a long planning horizon should model the private entry point carefully before defaulting to HDB.

## The Watch-Out: Ageing Leasehold Condos and ECP Traffic

The one factor I see families underweight when they fall in love with D15 is the leasehold profile of the older condo stock.

A significant portion of D15's private condominiums were built in the 1980s and 1990s on 99-year leases, leaving some projects with 50 to 60 years remaining. CPF usage limits tighten at the 60-year mark and bank valuations follow. A unit with 58 years today has 48 years when you sell in a decade — that narrows your buyer pool in ways that are easy to ignore when you are falling in love with the unit.

The second watch-out is ECP noise and traffic for properties fronting or close to the expressway. Marine Parade and East Coast Road have pockets where road noise is a genuine liveability issue. Visit on a Saturday morning and a Sunday evening. The traffic pattern changes materially between days.

## Should Your Family Move to D15?

District 15 rewards families who prioritise lifestyle, walkability and school access over pure commute convenience or land size per dollar. It is not the best district if your priority is maximising square footage for your budget — D18 or D19 will outperform on that metric.

Where D15 wins: children can walk to school and East Coast Park in the same morning. The neighbourhood has genuine character that tends to age well. TEL access now makes the commute viable. And the school catchment — particularly the CHIJ and Tao Nan corridors — is a structural anchor for demand that tends to hold values through softer markets.

If you are an HDB upgrader weighing whether to stretch into D15 private, or a family considering a rental-first approach to test the neighbourhood before buying, the financial modelling matters more than gut feel here. The price bands are not forgiving of a poorly structured entry.
