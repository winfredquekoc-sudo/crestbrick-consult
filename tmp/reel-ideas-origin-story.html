Reel 01 of 10
Why I Became a Realtor — Not the Polished Answer. The Real One.
Hook · First 3 Seconds
"I didn't become a realtor because I loved property. I became one because I was embarrassed."
Script
I didn't become a realtor because I loved property. I became one because I was embarrassed. My parents worked their whole lives and still couldn't figure out how to upgrade from their HDB. Every agent they met either confused them or rushed them. Nobody sat down and actually explained how it worked. And I remember thinking — I want to be the person who actually explains it. Not the guy chasing commission. Not the one who disappears after signing. The one who makes families feel like they actually understand what they're doing with the biggest asset of their lives. Nine years later, I've owned five properties before 30, advised over twenty families through upgrades, and I still go back to that same reason every single time a deal gets hard. It's not about property. It was never about property. It was about not letting another family feel like they were on their own.
CTA
If you're trying to figure out your next move — DM me. Let's talk before you commit to anything.
Visual Note
Start close-up, slightly off-camera gaze. Cut to slow B-roll of HDB void decks / estate shots mid-script. Return to face for final lines. No fancy transitions — raw and still.
Reel 02 of 10
My First Listing: What I Got Completely Wrong
Hook · First 3 Seconds
"My first listing, I prepared the perfect pitch. The client rejected me in under four minutes."
Script
My first listing, I prepared the perfect pitch. The client rejected me in under four minutes. I had the market data. I had the comparable transactions. I had a whole plan for how to price and market their flat. I walked in confident. And then she said: "You're talking about the flat. I need someone who understands my situation." She wasn't selling because she wanted to. She was selling because her family needed to restructure — MOP had just passed, finances were stretched, and she needed to know the whole picture would work out. I didn't have an answer for that. I had a sales deck. I went back the next week with a proper financial timeline — CPF projections, stamp duty, the next purchase plan. She listed with me. That was the day I stopped showing up with a pitch and started showing up with a plan. Hardest lesson I ever got in under four minutes.
CTA

Reel 03 of 10
I Bought 5 Properties Before 30 — Here's What I Actually Learned
Hook · First 3 Seconds
"Five properties before 30. Sounds impressive. The truth is messier than that."
Script
Five properties before 30. Sounds impressive. The truth is messier than that. The first one I nearly didn't buy because I overthought it for six months. The second one taught me that timing matters less than structure. The third one — I made a mistake on ABSD because I didn't plan the sequence properly. Cost me real money. What I actually learned across all five: it's never just about the property. It's about the order of transactions, the CPF position, the loan structure, and what you want your life to look like five years after you sign. Most people focus on which unit. I was always obsessing over the sequence — what do I sell first, what do I buy next, how do I protect liquidity without paying unnecessary tax. That obsession is what I now bring to every client I work with. Five properties didn't make me smart. The mistakes in between did.
CTA
Thinking about your property sequence? Drop a comment or DM — I'll tell you what I'd look at first.
Visual Note

The Client Call That Changed How I Work Forever
Hook · First 3 Seconds
"A client called me at 11pm. Not to ask about property — to tell me their marriage was in trouble."
Script
A client called me at 11pm. Not to ask about property — to tell me their marriage was in trouble. They'd been planning to buy a condo together. Then things changed. And suddenly the whole financial plan we'd built — the CPF usage, the joint loan, the upgrade timeline — it all had to be rethought. I didn't know what to say. I wasn't trained for this. But I stayed on the call. And by the end of it, we had a new path. Not the dream plan — a realistic one. One that protected both of them individually. One that didn't create a financial trap if things didn't work out. After that call, I changed how I do the first consult. Before I talk about any property, I ask: "What's your situation at home? Who are the people this decision affects?" Property is never just property. It sits inside someone's life. And if I don't understand the life, I can't actually help with the property.
CTA
If your situation is more complicated than "just looking to buy" — that's exactly the kind of conversation I'm built for. DM me.
Visual Note
Low light, calm setting — home office or quiet corner. Hold a long pause after "I wasn't trained for this." Let the silence sit. This reel should feel intimate, not produced.

The Deal I Almost Lost Because I Gave Honest Advice
Hook · First 3 Seconds
"I told a client not to buy. Three weeks later they almost signed with someone else — and I was fine with that."
Script
I told a client not to buy. Three weeks later they almost signed with someone else — and I was fine with that. They'd found a condo they loved. Everything looked right on the surface. But when I ran the numbers — the ABSD they'd pay, the rental yield they were assuming, the loan-to-value on their second property — it didn't stack up. Not at that price. Not at that timing. I told them to wait six months. They got frustrated. They called another agent who told them what they wanted to hear. They came back to me two days before OTP. And I told them again: this doesn't work for your situation. The other agent is not wrong about the property. He's just not looking at your whole picture. They didn't sign. Six months later they found a better entry point and saved close to $80,000. The honest advice almost cost me the deal. It's also why they've referred three families to me since.

Reel 07 of 10
What 20+ Families Upgrading from HDB Taught Me
Hook · First 3 Seconds
"After helping over twenty families upgrade from HDB, I've noticed the same mistake — and almost nobody talks about it."
Script
After helping over twenty families upgrade from HDB, I've noticed the same mistake — and almost nobody talks about it. They start with the condo. They go to show flats, they fall in love, they set a budget. And then they work backwards to figure out whether the HDB sale makes sense. That's the wrong order. The right order starts with the HDB. What's the market value now? What's the CPF refund on sale? What does the actual cash and loan position look like after MOP? And only then — what can you actually afford, and does this upgrade genuinely improve your position or just add stress? I've seen families upgrade into a condo and feel poorer than they did in their HDB because they didn't plan the sequence. The property is almost never the hard part. It's the sequencing, the timing, and understanding what "upgrade" actually means for your specific household — not someone else's. Start with your position. Then choose the property.
CTA
HDB and thinking about the next step? Comment "UPGRADE" or DM me and I'll share what I'd look at first.
Visual Note
Outdoor, HDB estate context works well — void deck or carpark. Energetic but grounded. Use a text overlay for key phrases: "wrong order" / "right order." Pacing should be slightly faster here to match the practical, instructive tone.
Reel 08 of 10
Why Most Realtors Show You Listings — I Show You Strategies
Hook · First 3 Seconds
"A listing is just a door. What's behind the door only matters if the door is the right one for you."
Script
A listing is just a door. What's behind the door only matters if the door is the right one for you. Most agents start with inventory. Here are five units in your budget — which one do you like? I start with structure. What does your household look like in five years? Do you have kids entering school? Are your parents going to need to move closer? Do you have ABSD exposure from a second property? Are you planning to rent one out? Once I understand the structure, the listings become obvious. Sometimes the right move isn't buying at all — it's restructuring what you already own. Sometimes it's decoupling a joint property so one party can buy without ABSD. Listings are the output of a strategy. They're not the starting point. The reason I work this way is because I've seen what happens when people lead with the property and backfill the rationale. They end up in a unit they love that doesn't actually work for their life. I'd rather you feel boring confidence at the end than excited regret six months in.
CTA
Want to start with strategy, not listings? 30-min session, link in bio.
Visual Note
Whiteboard or notebook visible in background adds strategic credibility. Direct to camera, measured pace. Slight pause before "Listings are the output of a strategy" — that line deserves a breath.
Reel 09 of 10
The Question I Ask Every New Client Before I Show Them a Single Property
Hook · First 3 Seconds
"Before I show any client a single listing, I ask them one question. Most of them have never been asked it before."
Script
Before I show any client a single listing, I ask them one question. Most of them have never been asked it before. The question is: "If this works out exactly as planned — what does your life look like in five years?" Not what does the property look like. Not what's the return. What does your life look like. Because the answer tells me everything I need to know about what they actually need. Some people say "I want stability, my kids in the same school zone, I'm done moving." That tells me to prioritise permanence over upside. Some people say "I want options. I want liquidity. I might move for work." That tells me we probably need a different structure entirely. The property is a vehicle. If I don't know where you're trying to go, I have no business recommending a vehicle. That one question has saved more deals from going wrong than any amount of market research I could show you. Ask it of yourself before you talk to any agent — including me.
CTA
What's your answer? Drop it in the comments — or DM me and let's build from there.
Visual Note
Slow, deliberate pace for the question itself — let it land. Talking head throughout. Consider a subtle zoom-in at "What does your life look like." End with a genuine, direct look to camera.
Reel 10 of 10
What "Trust Your Realtor" Actually Means — and How to Test It
Hook · First 3 Seconds
"'Trust your realtor' is terrible advice unless you know exactly how to test whether yours deserves it."
Script
"Trust your realtor" is terrible advice unless you know exactly how to test whether yours deserves it. Here's the test. Ask them one question: "Is now actually a good time for me to buy?" A realtor who says yes without knowing your CPF balance, your loan eligibility, your household plans, and your ABSD position — doesn't deserve your trust. They deserve a thank-you and a goodbye. A realtor who says "I need more information before I can answer that" — that's someone worth talking to. The second test: ask them to show you a deal they talked a client out of. If they can't give you one, every single recommendation they make is pointed in one direction — toward a transaction. Trust is earned through inconvenience. The agent who tells you to wait, to hold, or to restructure instead of buy — that's not someone losing your business. That's someone doing their job. I've built my entire practice on this. And the families who trust me the most are the ones I told "not yet" before I ever sold them anything.
CTA
Test me. Ask me if now is a good time for you. DM me your situation and I'll give you a straight answer.
Visual Note
Most confident reel of the series — deliver this one with full eye contact. This is the capstone. Steady shot, no movement. End with a slight pause after the final line before the CTA — let it settle.
