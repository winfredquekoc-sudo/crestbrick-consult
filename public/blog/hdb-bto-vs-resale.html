Should I Buy BTO or Resale HDB in Singapore?

The honest trade-off guide covering wait time, price premium, and the 3 scenarios where each option wins.

By Winfred Quek · CEA R073319H · Crestbrick · Updated May 2026 · ~900 words

Every year around BTO exercise time, I get the same question from clients who are mid-career, mid-savings, and mid-anxiety: should we apply for BTO or just buy resale now?

There is no universal right answer. But there is a framework for arriving at the right answer for your specific situation. This article lays it out, with numbers.

The Core Trade-Off

BTO and resale HDB are fundamentally different financial propositions. Understanding the core trade-off first prevents most of the confusion.

BTO offers a subsidised entry price. A 4-room BTO flat in a non-mature estate might launch at $350,000 to $450,000. The same flat in the resale market, after five years, might trade for $550,000 to $650,000. That is a real difference of $100,000 to $200,000 in entry cost -- and that difference compounds into equity over time.

Resale offers occupancy now. You apply for BTO, ballot, get a queue number, wait 4 to 5 years for construction, then wait another 5 years before you can sell. A resale flat you can move into in 8 to 12 weeks. For a couple with a baby or elderly parents or a lease that is expiring, that timeline difference is not abstract -- it is real life.

The key question is not which is cheaper. It is what the opportunity cost of waiting actually is. For a couple in their late 20s with low fixed costs, waiting 5 years for BTO may be the highest-return decision they make. For a couple in their mid-30s with kids, the same wait may cost them far more in alternative accommodation and delayed portfolio-building than the subsidy is worth.

Worked Financial Comparison

Let us put real numbers to a common scenario: a dual-income couple deciding between a 4-room BTO in Tengah (non-mature) versus a 4-room resale in Tampines (mature estate).

FactorBTO (Tengah)Resale (Tampines)
Purchase priceS$400,000S$580,000
CPF grant availableUp to S$80,000 (EHG)Up to S$80,000 (EHG)
Cash/CPF outlay~S$320,000 net~S$500,000 net
Monthly mortgage (30yr, 2.6%)~S$1,150~S$1,670
Wait before occupancy4-5 years8-12 weeks
MOP end (sell eligible)10 years from now5 years from now (if MOP remaining)
Upgrade runway10+ years5-8 years depending on MOP remaining

Over a 10-year horizon, if both flats appreciate at roughly the same rate (let us assume 3% per annum), the BTO flat at $400k grows to $537k and the resale at $580k grows to $779k. The resale buyer ends up with more absolute equity -- but the BTO buyer got in for $180k less, meaning their return on capital is structurally better.

The catch: the BTO buyer spent 4-5 years waiting, likely paying rent or staying with family, and missed the chance to start building CPF OA accrued interest on a purchased property. When you factor in rent during the waiting period ($2,000 to $2,500/month in a similar area), the cost gap narrows significantly.

Three Scenarios Where Resale Wins

  • Your MOP is already running or almost complete. If you bought an HDB years ago, your 5-year MOP may end in 12 to 24 months. Buying resale now means you can sell your current flat quickly after MOP and use the proceeds for upgrading. BTO's 10-year timeline breaks this sequencing entirely.
  • School proximity matters for your children. Resale lets you choose an existing address in a specific school's Phase 2B catchment zone. BTO locations are determined by the government's release schedule, not your school plan. If your child starts P1 in three years and the right address is a specific mature estate, resale is the only path.
  • You need to move now. A lease expiring, a family situation changing, a work location shift -- resale gives you the occupancy timeline that BTO structurally cannot. Forcing a BTO timeline onto a life situation that requires immediate housing creates hidden costs that rarely appear in the financial comparison.

Three Scenarios Where BTO Wins

  • You are in your late 20s with a long runway and low urgency. If you have stable housing now (family home, subsidised accommodation) and do not need to move for 5 years, the BTO subsidy is essentially free money. You get a lower entry point, better grant eligibility, and a fresh 99-year lease. Use the waiting period to build savings for the upgrade.
  • You want a specific new estate with strong long-term fundamentals. Estates like Tengah, Bayshore, and Springleaf are being built from scratch with infrastructure investment baked in. Buying BTO in these areas at launch pricing captures the appreciation of a brand-new estate -- appreciation that resale buyers in adjacent areas have already seen.
  • You are prioritising portfolio entry cost over lifestyle now. The difference between a $400k BTO and a $580k resale is $180k. At a 3% yield on an investment property later, that $180k is $5,400 per year in passive income permanently. If your goal is portfolio-building over lifestyle optimisation, BTO's lower entry cost compresses the time needed to scale.
