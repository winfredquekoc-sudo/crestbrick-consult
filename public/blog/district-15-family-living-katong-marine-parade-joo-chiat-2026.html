Living in District 15: Katong, Marine Parade, Joo Chiat for Families in 2026

By Winfred Quek  |  CEA R073319H, PropNex  |  May 2026  |  20+ families advised in D15

If you have school-age children and are seriously considering a move to the East Coast, you have probably heard the same things repeated across property forums: good schools, East Coast Park, Peranakan charm, rising prices. All true. What you are less likely to find is a clear-eyed breakdown of what District 15 family living actually costs, which pockets make sense depending on your school priorities, and how the Thomson-East Coast Line changes the calculation in 2026. That is what this article covers. I have worked with more than 20 families in D15 across the range from HDB resale buyers to private property owners deciding between freehold Mountbatten and leasehold new launches near Marine Parade MRT.

Why D15 Remains One of Singapore's Most Sought-After Family Districts

District 15 is classified as Rest of Central Region (RCR), the city-fringe tier that sits between the prime core (CCR) and the suburbs (OCR). For families, this positioning matters more than the label. You are within 15 to 20 minutes of the CBD and Orchard by MRT, yet the neighbourhood fabric feels suburban: landed streets in Joo Chiat, low-rise shophouse rows, walking access to East Coast Park. That combination is genuinely rare in Singapore.

The demand base here is multigenerational. Singaporean families who grew up in the East Coast tend to stay. Expat families with children at international schools or in the Phase 2B/2C school registration priority system actively seek out D15 addresses. And investors follow the owner-occupiers, which has historically kept vacancy rates lower and resale liquidity stronger than comparable OCR developments.

The honest caveat: D15 is not cheap, and 2024 and 2025 saw significant new supply enter the market. Grand Dunman (1,008 units), Tembusu Grand (638 units), The Continuum (816 units), and Emerald of Katong (846 units, essentially sold out by 2025) all completed or reached near-completion in this window. Absorption is still working through the system in 2026. For families buying to owner-occupy over a 10-year-plus horizon, this is noise. For anyone expecting short-term capital gains, it is a real constraint.

Schools Catchment in Katong and Marine Parade

The school question is what drives most family decisions in D15, and it deserves more precision than the usual "good schools nearby" framing.

The primary schools most relevant to a D15 address are Tao Nan School (Marine Parade Road), CHIJ Katong Primary (Mountbatten Road), Kong Hwa School (Guillemard Road), Tanjong Katong Primary, and Ngee Ann Primary (Clementi Avenue, though less relevant for D15 addresses). For families with girls, CHIJ Katong Primary is the anchor school in the district. Tao Nan School carries significant demand from families along Marine Parade Road and Mountbatten, and its 1km and 2km Phase 2B/2C registration zones command a measurable property premium.

At the secondary level, St Patrick's School on Siglap Road serves families with sons in the Catholic school system. Victoria School, one of Singapore's older and more academically regarded secondary schools, sits at King's Road at the western fringe of D15. Dunman High, a independent school with a strong academic profile, is within the district along with Chung Cheng High School on Goodman Road.

The practical implication for property selection: the 1km ring around Tao Nan and CHIJ Katong Primary is where school-driven demand concentrates, and it is where you should verify your address distance before paying a premium for proximity. The Singapore Ministry of Education's online address-to-school distance checker is the ground truth. I have seen buyers assume they were within 1km who were not. Lifestyle and Amenities: East Coast Park, Katong Mall, i12 Katong

For families, lifestyle infrastructure matters as much as schools. D15 delivers this in a way that few Singapore districts can match at this price tier.

East Coast Park is the headline amenity: 15 kilometres of coastline with cycling paths, beach access, camping grounds, and the entire leisure corridor running from Marina Barrage to Changi. For families with young children, having a park of this scale accessible by bicycle from home changes weekend patterns entirely. Residents in Marine Parade and Siglap can reach the beach in under 10 minutes by bike.

For everyday retail and food, i12 Katong at East Coast Road serves as the neighbourhood mall for mid-D15, anchored by Cold Storage, a cinema, and a strong food-and-beverage mix. Parkway Parade at Marine Parade Central is the larger regional mall serving families in the eastern part of the district, with a supermarket, enrichment centres, and a food court. The Katong food belt along East Coast Road and Joo Chiat Road needs no introduction: laksa, poh piah, chicken rice, chilli crab, and some of Singapore's best traditional bakeries are all within walking distance of most D15 addresses.

Joo Chiat itself has evolved. The shophouse conservation area now hosts a mix of independent cafes, restaurants, and boutique businesses alongside the traditional provisions shops and heritage eateries. For families, this means a high-quality daily environment without the sterility of newer planned towns.

Transport Connectivity in 2026

Transport was historically D15's weak point. Before the Thomson-East Coast Line (TEL), residents depended almost entirely on buses or private transport to reach the MRT network. The nearest stations were Paya Lebar MRT (Circle Line and East West Line interchange) and Kembangan MRT (East West Line), neither of which was within comfortable walking distance of most D15 addresses.

The TEL changes this significantly. The stations now serving D15 directly are Katong Park (TEL), Tanjong Katong (TEL), Marine Parade (TEL), Marine Terrace (TEL), and Siglap (TEL). These stations bring the central MRT network to walking distance for the majority of D15 residents for the first time. From Marine Parade MRT, the TEL connects through Stevens interchange to the North-South Line, making Orchard accessible in approximately 20 minutes and the CBD at Shenton Way in around 15 minutes.

Paya Lebar MRT remains a critical interchange for D15, handling Circle Line and East West Line journeys. It is accessible by bus from most parts of D15 and is the gateway to Tampines, Jurong, and Changi connections. Kembangan MRT on the East West Line serves the Siglap and Upper East Coast pockets of D15.

The TEL connectivity was already priced into new launch projects that launched between 2022 and 2024. For resale buyers in 2026, the infrastructure is delivered and functioning rather than speculative. This actually makes the calculus more straightforward: you are buying established connectivity, not a promise.

Property Landscape: What Families Are Buying in 2026

D15 offers three distinct product categories for families, each with different risk-return and lifestyle trade-offs.

Freehold condominiums in the Mountbatten and Amber Road pockets represent the premium tier. These tend to be older developments with larger unit sizes and freeholded land, which for families intending to hold for 15 to 20 years (and potentially pass on) is a meaningful consideration given Singapore's lease decay dynamics. Projects like Amber Park, Coastline Residences, and One Meyer define this segment. PSF for freehold resale in these pockets runs approximately $1,900 to $2,400, depending on unit size, floor, and condition.

Leasehold new launches and recently completed developments along the TEL corridor, particularly the Marine Parade and Tanjong Katong pockets, offer modern facilities and rail connectivity at slightly lower PSF (roughly $2,100 to $2,600 for remaining units in completed projects, $2,300 to $2,900 for new launches). The Continuum at Thiam Siew Avenue and Emerald of Katong at Jalan Tembusu are the reference points for this segment in 2026.

HDB resale in D15 remains an entry point for families who are not yet ready to commit to private property. The Geylang and Marine Parade HDB towns within and adjacent to D15 offer 4-room and 5-room flats in the $700,000 to $950,000 range, with some larger executive flat types approaching and occasionally exceeding $1 million depending on floor and remaining lease. For HDB upgrader families, this stock forms the realistic stepping-stone before a transition to private. [Use the upgrade ROI tool to model your specific transition]

Landed terrace houses in Joo Chiat and the surrounding conservation area represent a third category that attracts a specific type of family buyer: those who want the garden, the space, and the Peranakan heritage character. Terrace houses in Joo Chiat start around $3 million and move upward quickly with renovation quality and plot size. These are owner-occupier purchases almost exclusively and are outside the scope of most HDB upgrader budgets.

What Does a Family Budget Look Like in D15?

Translating the property landscape into real numbers requires a few assumptions. With a bank fixed rate of approximately 1.5% in 2026, Total Debt Servicing Ratio (TDSR) limits of 55%, and Loan-to-Value (LTV) of 75% for a first private property purchase, the practical entry points are as follows.

A family with a combined monthly income of $14,000 and $200,000 in CPF Ordinary Account savings can service a mortgage of approximately $1.1 million to $1.2 million. Adding a 25% down payment funded by CPF and cash, the realistic purchase ceiling is approximately $1.4 million to $1.6 million. At this budget, D15 private condo options are limited but exist: smaller 2-bedroom resale units in older developments, and occasionally a 3-bedroom unit in a less central part of the district. This is also where the larger D15 HDB resale flats sit comfortably.

A family with combined income of $20,000 and $350,000 in CPF OA can stretch to approximately $2 million to $2.4 million in purchase price. This unlocks the mid-range of freehold resale condos, including 3-bedroom units at 1,000 to 1,200 square feet, which is the practical minimum for a family of four. These are the buyers who form the core demand for D15 private condo resale in 2026.

At $3 million and above, the full range of D15 opens up: larger freehold units, semi-detached positioning, and the boutique developments on Amber and Meyer Road. [Run your numbers through the affordability tool for a precise picture]

Is D15 Right for Your Family?

D15 is a good fit for families who are buying to live and hold. The school catchment is genuine, the lifestyle infrastructure is rare, and the TEL has solved the district's historical transport gap. Freehold tenure is available in meaningful quantity, which matters if your planning horizon extends past the children's school years.

It is a harder fit if you are stretching to the upper bound of your budget on the assumption of near-term capital appreciation. The supply overhang from 2024 and 2025 completions is real, and while it does not threaten D15's long-term positioning, it does dampen the short-term outlook for price growth. Buying at peak budget with thin reserves is a risk in any district, and D15 prices do not leave much margin for error.

The families I have worked with who ended up happiest in D15 had one thing in common: they bought for the life they wanted to live there, and the investment case was secondary. That is the right orientation for this district. If you need the investment case to be the primary driver, there are OCR markets with stronger yield and lower entry that deserve a look first.

For families already committed to D15, the decision is really about which pocket and which product type. That is where the specifics of your school timeline, budget, tenure preference, and cashflow tolerance determine the answer. It is not a one-size decision.
