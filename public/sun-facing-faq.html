Sun Facing Checker FAQ

The Sun Facing Checker is a free tool that shows which way any Singapore home faces the sun, when the afternoon west sun hits it, and which nearby buildings cast a shadow over it, for any month and time of day. It runs on a live 3D map of Singapore, so you can search an address or tap a spot directly.

Open the Sun Facing Checker

Which direction is my house facing?

A home's facing is the direction its main window or balcony points towards, measured from true north. In Singapore, homes are commonly described as north facing, south facing, east facing, west facing, or a mix like north east. This tool shows the exact compass direction and the sun's position for any address you search, at any time of day and month.

What is the best facing direction for a house in Singapore?

Singapore sits close to the equator at about 1.3 degrees north, so the sun tracks almost overhead through the year rather than staying low in the south the way it does in temperate countries. Homes facing north or south tend to avoid the harshest low angle morning and afternoon sun that east and west facing homes receive. This reflects general sun geometry for the region, not a valuation judgement on any specific unit.

Why is west sun bad in Singapore?

West facing units in Singapore receive direct sun in the late afternoon, roughly 2pm to 6pm, when the sun sits low enough in the sky to shine straight into windows. This afternoon sun lands during the hottest part of the day, so west facing rooms can feel noticeably warmer through the late afternoon and evening. Nearby buildings, awnings, or landscaping to the west can reduce how much of this sun actually reaches a specific unit.

Is north south facing always the best in Singapore?

North south facing units generally avoid the low, direct morning and afternoon sun that east and west facing units receive, because at this latitude the sun stays mostly to the north or south of overhead through the year. This is a general tendency based on sun geometry, and how much sun actually reaches a unit still depends on its floor level, surrounding buildings, and the time of year. It does not apply in exactly the same way to every unit in every project.

Does my unit get morning sun or afternoon sun?

That depends on which direction the unit faces: east facing homes get direct morning sun, west facing homes get direct afternoon sun, and north or south facing homes get comparatively even light through the day. This tool calculates the sun's exact direction at any time you choose for any Singapore address, so you can check a specific unit.

How hot does a west facing unit get in Singapore?

There is no official Singapore data measuring temperature by unit facing, so no single verified figure applies to every west facing unit. What can be measured is the sun's position, which shows west facing units receiving direct sun roughly 2pm to 6pm, adding heat load during the hottest hours of the day. Curtains, films, or shading can reduce this effect, and this tool itself measures sun exposure, not indoor temperature.

Will a new building block my sunlight?

A taller building built nearby can cast a shadow over a lower unit for part of the day, depending on its height, distance, and position relative to the sun. This tool models shadows from buildings already recorded in OpenStreetMap around any address, so you can see current shadow patterns, though it cannot predict buildings that have not been built yet. The URA Master Plan for a site shows what could legally be built nearby in future.

How do I check sun facing before buying or renting a home?

Search an address on this tool to see the compass direction the home faces, when direct morning or afternoon sun reaches it, and which nearby buildings currently cast a shadow over it, for any month and time of day. Checking a few different times of day and months gives a fuller picture than a single site visit, since sun angles change through the year. For a closer look at a specific unit, including a valuation, message Winfred Quek directly.

Shadows use OpenStreetMap building heights. Where a height is not recorded a typical block is assumed, so treat shadows as a strong guide, not a survey.
