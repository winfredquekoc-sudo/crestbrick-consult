Tenancy renewal and the renewal window

By Winfred Quek · CEA R073319H · Singapore property glossary

What is tenancy renewal and the renewal window? Tenancy renewal is extending a lease, or agreeing a fresh one, with the same tenant before the current lease ends. The renewal window, often the two or three months before expiry, is when the landlord and tenant negotiate new terms or decide to part ways.

Rates and thresholds change. The current figures are kept in one place: the Singapore property rules reference.

Many tenancy agreements include an option to renew clause, giving the sitting tenant first opportunity to negotiate a new term before the landlord markets the unit to others. This is a right to negotiate, not an automatic entitlement to keep the same rent or terms, and either side can decide not to proceed.

As the lease approaches expiry, the landlord typically reviews current market rent for comparable units in the area and puts forward new terms. If both sides agree, a fresh tenancy agreement, or sometimes a renewal letter or addendum to the existing one, is signed, and this new document needs its own stamp duty filed with IRAS, separate from the original.

Timing is the practical crux of renewal. Leaving the conversation too late, close to the actual expiry date, gives neither party enough runway if terms cannot be agreed, meaning the tenant scrambles to find a new place or the landlord scrambles to find a new tenant with little notice.

What commonly goes wrong is exactly that: renewal discussions started too close to the deadline, unclear expectations on whether rent will track current market levels or simply roll over unchanged, and forgetting that a renewed or varied agreement is a fresh stampable document, not a continuation of the old one for stamp duty purposes.

Agree, ideally in the original tenancy agreement, how much notice either party should give about intent to renew or not. As the window opens, compare the proposed rent against current market data before agreeing a figure, and confirm the renewed agreement gets stamped.

A useful habit is to treat the renewal window as a mini negotiation rather than a formality. Rent benchmarks change over a lease term, sometimes meaningfully over two or three years, and either side coming to the table with recent comparable transactions makes the conversation more productive than an opening figure with no context behind it. Where the parties cannot agree, enough notice still lets both sides plan properly rather than scrambling at the last moment.

What to check

  • The notice period either party should give about renewal intentions, ideally set in the original agreement
  • Current market rent for comparable units before agreeing a renewed figure
  • Whether any terms beyond rent, such as included furniture or minor repair responsibilities, should be revisited
  • That a renewed or varied agreement needs its own stamp duty filed with IRAS
  • A clear fallback plan for both sides if renewal terms cannot be agreed in time
