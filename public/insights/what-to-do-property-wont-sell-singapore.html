What to do if your property won't sell

By Winfred Quek · 10-minute read · Last reviewed May 2026

Quick answer: A property that will not sell has one of four causes, and the viewing data tells you which. No viewings means a marketing or price problem, buyers are not even finding the listing or are filtered out by price. Viewings but no offers means a presentation or price problem, buyers are coming but judging the unit as poor value. Genuine market weakness is real but is rarely the whole story for a stuck listing. Diagnose the cause, fix it, and if a sale still does not make sense on your timeline, renting the property out can be the better move while you wait.

Facts verified: May 2026 · Sources linked below

Key Takeaways

  • • A stuck listing has four possible causes: price, presentation, marketing, or market; the viewing data points to which.
  • • No viewings points to a marketing or pricing problem; viewings without offers points to presentation or price.
  • • Price is the most common true cause and the easiest to verify against URA caveat data or HDB resale data.
  • • A stale listing carries a perception cost; relisting fresh after fixing the real problem can help, but only after the fix.
  • • If selling does not work on your timeline, renting the property out is a legitimate path; mind any Seller's Stamp Duty window and the holding economics.

A property sitting unsold is stressful, and stress pushes owners to either blame the market or slash the price in a panic. Neither is a method. There is a method: a stuck listing always has a diagnosable cause, and the data your listing has already generated tells you which one. This piece walks the diagnosis, then the fixes, then the rent-instead alternative.

Why is my property not selling?

There are exactly four reasons a property does not sell. The first diagnostic question is simple: are you getting viewings?

SymptomLikely causeWhat it means
No viewings at allMarketing or priceBuyers are not finding the listing, or your price puts you outside their search band, so they never see it
Viewings, but no offersPresentation or priceBuyers are coming but judging the unit as poor value, or it presents badly in person
Offers, but all far below askingPriceThe market is telling you, through multiple buyers, where the realistic value sits
Genuinely weak across all similar unitsMarketA real downturn in your segment, though this is rarely the sole reason a single listing is stuck

A diagnostic framework. Most stuck listings resolve to price or presentation, not the market.

How do I fix each cause?

If the problem is marketing

No viewings despite a reasonable price usually means buyers cannot find the listing. Fix the reach: ensure the property is listed across all the major portals, replace weak photos with strong, well-lit images, and check the listing actually appears for the searches a buyer would run. A property invisible to buyers gets no viewings no matter how good it is.

If the problem is presentation

Viewings without offers, where the price looks fair, points to the property under-performing in person. Declutter, deep clean, fix small visible defects, and light every room. A buyer who walks into a tidy, bright space can picture living there; one who walks into clutter and a dripping tap offers low or walks. See the full method in how to stage and photograph your property.

If the problem is price

This is the most common true cause, and the hardest for owners to accept. The tests: viewings without offers, or several offers all clustered well below your asking price. Either is the market telling you the price is wrong. Verify it yourself, according to URA, private residential transaction caveats are published online, and according to HDB, resale flat transaction prices are published on the HDB Flat Portal. If your asking price sits above what genuinely comparable units transacted at, a price reduction to the real market level, not more marketing, is the fix. See pricing your property to sell.

If the problem is the market

If genuinely every comparable unit in your segment is struggling, the market is soft. Even so, a correctly priced, well-presented, well-marketed property still transacts in a soft market, it just may take longer and require a realistic price. A soft market is a reason to manage expectations, not a reason to leave a misdiagnosed listing sitting.

Beware the stale-listing trap: A listing that has sat unsold for a long time develops a perception problem, buyers who have seen it repeatedly assume something is wrong. Relisting fresh can help reset that perception, but only after you have fixed the real cause. Relisting the same over-priced, poorly presented property simply restarts the same stall.

When should I rent it out instead of selling?

Sometimes the right answer is not to sell now at all. Renting the property out is a legitimate alternative if:

  • You have no hard deadline forcing a sale, and you can afford to hold.
  • The only way to sell quickly would be a price you are not willing to accept.
  • The rental income reasonably covers, or substantially offsets, the holding costs, the mortgage, property tax, and maintenance.
  • You believe waiting for a stronger market is worth the holding period.

Two cautions. First, check your Seller's Stamp Duty position. According to IRAS, SSD on residential property bought on or after 4 July 2025 is 16% / 12% / 8% / 4% / 0% across a 4-year holding period, so if you are still inside that window, holding longer also moves you toward 0% SSD, which can favour renting and waiting. Second, holding is not free: weigh the rental income honestly against the mortgage interest, property tax, and maintenance before treating renting as a costless pause.

Winfred's Take

When an owner tells me their property will not sell, I do not look at the market first, I look at their viewing log. If nobody is viewing, we have a reach or price problem. If people view and never offer, we have a presentation or price problem. If everyone who offers comes in low, the market has already told us the price. Notice that three of the four diagnoses involve price, because price is usually the real issue, and it is the one owners least want to hear. My honest counsel: diagnose properly, fix the actual cause, and if the maths of a forced sale does not work, renting it out and waiting is a respectable decision, not a defeat. What does not work is leaving a misdiagnosed listing to rot and blaming the market.

Frequently asked questions

My property has had no viewings at all, what is wrong?

No viewings points to a marketing or price problem. Either buyers cannot find the listing, weak photos, missing from major portals, or the price puts you outside the search band buyers are using. Fix the reach and check the price against real comparables.

I get viewings but no offers, what does that mean?

That pattern points to presentation or price. Buyers are coming but judging the unit as poor value, or it shows badly in person. Improve presentation, declutter, clean, fix defects, light it, and honestly re-check the price.

Should I cut my price if the property is not selling?

Only after diagnosing the cause. If you get viewings without offers, or offers clustered well below asking, and your price is above comparable transactions, then yes, a reduction to the real market level is the fix. If the issue is marketing or presentation, a price cut alone may not solve it.

Can I take my property off the market and relist it later?

Yes, and relisting fresh can reset a stale-listing perception. But only relist after you have fixed the real cause, price, presentation, or marketing. Relisting the same flawed listing simply repeats the stall.

Is renting out my property a good alternative if it will not sell?

It can be, if you have no deadline, the rental income reasonably offsets the holding costs, and you are willing to wait. Check your SSD position, holding longer can move you toward 0% SSD, and weigh the rent honestly against mortgage, tax, and maintenance.
