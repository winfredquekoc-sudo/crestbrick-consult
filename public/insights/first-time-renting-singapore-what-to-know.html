Renting in Singapore for the first time: the complete roadmap

By Winfred Quek · CEA R073319H · Published 26 August 2026

Quick answer: Renting in Singapore for the first time follows a fixed sequence: set your budget, shortlist units against your commute and must haves, view them in person, agree rent and lease term with the landlord, sign and stamp the tenancy agreement with IRAS, then handle the handover. By market convention the landlord pays the agent, commonly 0.5 to 1 month of rent for a lease of one year or more, so a tenant on a standard lease usually pays no commission at all. Knowing the sequence before you start saves weeks of confusion later.

Facts verified: August 2026 · Sources linked below

Key Takeaways

  • • Search to signed lease usually takes a matter of weeks once your budget and must haves are set, not months, provided you move quickly at viewings.
  • • By convention the landlord pays their agent, commonly 0.5 to 1 month of rent for a lease of one year or more. A tenant on a standard lease usually pays no commission.
  • • The tenancy agreement is a chargeable document. It must be stamped with IRAS at 0.4% of the total rent for a lease of 4 years or less.
  • • HDB flats and private units sit under different rental rules. HDB rentals need HDB approval and a 6 month minimum stay; private units have a 3 month minimum.
  • • Everything after the deposit and the stamp duty is negotiation and documentation, so read the tenancy agreement before you sign it, not after.

Most people rent long before they ever buy, and most of them do it once with almost no idea what a normal process looks like. That gap is where bad decisions happen: paying a deposit before viewing a unit, signing a lease without reading the repair clause, or finding out after moving in that the flat was never approved for rental at all. This is the first part of an eight part series written for first time tenants in Singapore, locals and foreigners alike. It sets out the whole journey end to end, so every part that follows, cost, search, viewing, negotiation, legality, handover, and life during the lease, sits on a clear map instead of guesswork.

Who does what in a Singapore rental

A rental usually involves up to four parties: the landlord, the landlord's agent, your own agent if you choose to engage one, and you. In practice, most residential rentals in Singapore are handled by a single agent representing the landlord, who lists the unit, conducts viewings, and prepares the paperwork. A tenant can deal with that same agent directly, or bring in their own agent to act on their side, commonly through a co broke arrangement where the two agents split the landlord paid commission. Either way, any agent collecting a commission must be registered with the Council for Estate Agencies (CEA), and you can verify this at the CEA Public Register before you rely on anyone's advice.

What an agent cannot do is give you legal or financial advice beyond the transaction itself, and what they should always do is put the agreed terms in writing before you commit to anything, deposit included.

The realistic timeline from search to keys

There is no official countdown clock on a rental, but a workable sequence looks like this.

Step 1: Set your budget and your must haves. Decide your monthly rent ceiling, area, room count, and the two or three things you will not compromise on. This is the subject of tomorrow's part in this series.
Step 2: Shortlist and view. Browse listings against your budget and commute, then view the realistic candidates in person rather than relying on photos alone.
Step 3: Agree terms. Once you find a unit, negotiate rent, lease length, and move in date with the landlord or their agent, usually backed by a good faith deposit that converts into the security deposit once both sides commit.
Step 4: Sign the tenancy agreement. Review every clause, then sign and pay the security deposit and any advance rent due at signing.
Step 5: Stamp the agreement with IRAS. The lease must be stamped within 14 days of signing, at 0.4% of the total rent for a lease of 4 years or less.
Step 6: Handover. Collect keys, record meter readings, and complete a signed inventory with dated photos before you move your things in.

Once a unit is found, most tenants move from an agreed offer to a signed, stamped tenancy agreement within a week or two. The bigger variable is usually the current occupant's move out date, which sets when you can actually collect keys.

The tenancy agreement is the document that decides everything

Once terms are agreed, the tenancy agreement is what actually protects you, or does not. It should set out the rent and term, the security deposit and what it can be deducted against, who handles minor repairs and up to what cost, and the conditions the unit must be returned in. I cover this clause by clause in a separate guide on tenancy agreement clauses, and the stamping mechanics, who pays, how much, and the deadline, are covered in the guide to tenancy agreement stamp duty. Both are worth reading before you sign anything, not after.

HDB or private, the rules differ. An HDB flat can only be rented out under HDB's own subletting rules, including a minimum rental period of six months and an approval requirement. Private residential property has a minimum rental period of three months. Neither type can legally be let for a short stay below those minimums, Airbnb style. Part 6 of this series covers the full legality layer.

Winfred's Take

Most first time tenants I meet treat the search as the hard part and the paperwork as an afterthought. It is the other way round. Finding a unit you like is easy, Singapore has plenty of supply in most budget bands. What actually determines whether the next twelve months go smoothly is whether the deposit clause, the repair cap, and the reinstatement condition were read properly before signing. Spend your attention there, not on squeezing an extra fifty dollars off the rent.

Frequently asked questions

How long does it take to rent a place in Singapore for the first time?

There is no fixed number, but once your budget and must haves are settled, most tenants move from shortlisting to a signed tenancy agreement within a couple of weeks. The variable is usually how fast you decide after a viewing you like, good units in a popular area do not stay on the market long.

Who pays the agent when you rent in Singapore?

By market convention the landlord pays the agent, commonly 0.5 to 1 month of rent for a lease of one year or more. A tenant on a standard residential lease of a year or more usually pays no commission at all. Short leases and some room only rentals are the exception, so confirm this with the agent before you engage them.

Do I need my own agent to rent in Singapore?

No, it is not required. Many tenants deal directly with the landlord's agent, who represents the landlord's interests, or with the landlord directly. Engaging your own agent can help if you want someone reviewing terms on your side, and since the landlord conventionally pays the commission, doing so often costs you nothing extra.

What is the very first thing I should do before viewing units?

Set a hard monthly budget and write down your must haves versus nice to haves before you look at a single listing. Without that, viewings turn into an emotional exercise and you end up comparing units that were never really comparable on price or location.

Do I need a lawyer to rent a home in Singapore?

Not for a standard residential lease. Unlike buying property, renting does not require a conveyancing lawyer. The tenancy agreement is typically prepared by the agent or landlord, signed by both parties, and then stamped directly with IRAS.
