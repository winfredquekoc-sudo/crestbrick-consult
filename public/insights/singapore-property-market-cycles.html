Singapore Property Market Cycles 1990–2026: What Every Cycle Tells Investors

By Winfred Quek · CEA R073319H · PropNex Realty

Quick answer: Singapore property has gone through 5 identifiable cycles since 1990. Each cycle has been shortened and dampened by increasingly effective government intervention. Key pattern: every trough since 1999 has been higher than the previous trough. Long-term hold through 2 full cycles (10–15 years) has been profitable in every period. The 2024–present phase is a stabilisation, not a crash.

What Each Cycle Teaches Investors

Lesson 1: Troughs Are Higher Over Time

The 2009 GFC trough (PPI ~104) was higher than the 1999 AFC trough (PPI ~85 in comparable terms). The 2017 ABSD cooling trough (PPI ~148) was significantly higher than the 2009 GFC trough. Each generation of troughs represents a new floor. Government land supply control + population growth + limited land = structural price support.

Lesson 2: Cooling Measures Dampen But Don't Reverse

No cooling measure has caused a sustained multi-year price decline in Singapore property. The largest government-induced correction (ABSD/TDSR 2013–2017) saw a peak-to-trough decline of approximately 12–15%. Even this was orderly — not a crash. The government has become very skilled at calibrating measures to prevent bubbles without causing crashes.

Lesson 3: Timing the Trough is Hard — Duration Matters More

Investors who bought at the 2009 trough did very well — but so did investors who bought in 2007 (pre-GFC) and held through 2026. The Singapore property market rewards duration, not just timing. A 10-year hold through any cycle since 1990 has resulted in meaningful nominal returns.

Lesson 4: Foreign Buyer Sensitivity Has Increased

Each round of cooling measures has increasingly targeted foreign buyers — from 10% ABSD in 2011 to 60% in April 2023. The Singapore residential market has effectively been ring-fenced from global capital flows. This reduces volatility but also reduces the "foreign premium" that supported prices in the CCR during 2009–2013.

Where Are We in 2026?

The 2024–2026 period is best characterised as a consolidation phase: prices are flat to +2–4% p.a., transaction volume is normalised (not elevated, not depressed), and the market is driven by genuine user-demand rather than speculative capital.

This is historically the best time to buy for long-term investors — not because prices are cheap (they are not), but because speculative froth has been removed and the underlying demand (population growth, HDB upgraders, limited supply) remains intact.

No one can predict the next cycle timing: Every cycle surprise in Singapore's history (AFC, SARS, GFC, COVID, rate surge) was unforeseen by consensus. The consistent winner has not been market-timers but long-hold investors who stayed through volatility and let leverage and time do the work.
