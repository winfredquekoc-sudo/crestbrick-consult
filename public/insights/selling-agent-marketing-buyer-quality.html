Marketing reach and buyer quality: what an agent's network actually adds

By Winfred Quek · CEA R073319H · Published 4 September 2026

By Winfred Quek · CEA R073319H · Published 4 September 2026

Quick answer: A solo seller's audience is limited by more than effort, it is limited by access. PropertyGuru's own listing policy restricts direct listings to registered property agents, so a private seller cannot post there at all. 99.co offers a free Owner Listings service, but it connects owners to agents, not directly to buyers, by its own description. An agent's listing, by contrast, appears across both major portals and is actively surfaced through the co broke network, the pool of other agents whose own buyer clients might match your property, a channel that runs entirely through agent to agent contact and that a private listing has no way to plug into. Reach and buyer screening are two separate benefits, and this article covers reach; screening is covered in the next part of this series.

Key Takeaways

  • PropertyGuru's own stated policy limits direct property listings to registered agents, so a private seller cannot list on Singapore's largest portal without one.
  • 99.co's free Owner Listings service connects sellers to agents with matching buyer clients, by its own description, rather than putting the listing in front of buyers directly.
  • Co broking, two agents on either side of a deal sharing commission by private arrangement, is how a listing reaches buyers already working with their own agent, a pool a private seller cannot access directly.
  • All property agents must be CEA registered, which gives a seller a regulated, accountable channel rather than an anonymous listing.
  • Reach and buyer quality are separate benefits. Wider exposure raises the odds a matched buyer sees the listing; screening decides which of those buyers earn a viewing slot.

The first article in this series covered pricing. This one covers what happens after the price is set, getting the listing in front of buyers who can actually transact. Sellers often assume marketing reach is mostly a matter of effort, take good photos, post on a few platforms, tell friends. In practice, a meaningful part of an agent's marketing reach is structural, built into how Singapore's property portals and the industry's co broking system are set up, not just how hard any one person works at promoting a listing.

The portal access gap most sellers do not know about

PropertyGuru is widely regarded as Singapore's largest property portal, and its own listing policy, stated on its help and community pages, restricts direct property listings to registered property agents. A private seller cannot create their own listing there. This is not a matter of a form being harder to fill in, the option simply is not offered to individual owners on that platform.

99.co takes a different approach with its Owner Listings service, which is free and open to property owners directly. But by 99.co's own description of the service, it works by connecting your property to real estate agents who have clients with matching requirements, rather than placing your listing in front of buyers browsing the main portal the way an agent's listing appears. In practice, even this owner friendly route still runs through an agent somewhere in the chain, just without one managing the process, negotiation or paperwork on your side of it.

What co broking actually does for a listing

Co broking is the practice of two property agents, one representing the seller and one representing a buyer, working together on the same transaction, with commission shared between them by private arrangement between the agents. This structure means a well marketed listing does not only reach buyers who happen to search the portals themselves. It is actively surfaced to every buyer's agent in the market whose own client's stated requirements, budget, size, location, could match the property, through the ordinary, everyday agent to agent contact that runs across the industry. A solo seller, without a network of buyer's agents to reach, has no way to plug directly into that channel.

Every property agent operating in this system must be registered with the Council for Estate Agencies and is bound by its code of practice, which gives a seller a regulated, accountable channel of buyer interest rather than an unverifiable one. It is worth noting the Council for Estate Agencies itself publishes guidance for owners who choose to sell without an agent, so going solo is entirely legal, it is simply a different reach profile from the one a registered agent's network provides.

A narrower channel list is not just a smaller number of eyeballs. It changes who is even able to find the listing in the first place. A buyer already working with their own agent typically searches through that agent, who is checking the co broke network and the major portals, not scrolling a personal social media feed or word of mouth channel a solo seller relies on. A property can be excellent and still be effectively invisible to a large share of the active buyer pool if it never reaches the channels where the more prepared, financially qualified buyers are actually looking.

Why buyer quality tends to track the channel a buyer came from

Reach and buyer quality are related but distinct. A buyer who reaches a listing through their own agent has usually already had a conversation about budget, financing readiness and requirements with that agent, since a buyer's agent has their own reason to avoid wasting time on a mismatch. This does not mean every enquiry from a portal or a personal contact is a poor lead, plenty of serious buyers browse directly, it means the co broke channel adds a layer of prior qualification that a cold enquiry has not been through. Turning wider reach into actual screening, checking financing, motivation and timeline before a viewing is booked, is a separate discipline covered in the next article in this series.

Winfred's Take

Sellers sometimes tell me they will just post the listing themselves and save the commission on marketing. The honest response is that the biggest portal in the country will not let them, by its own rules, and the free owner route on the other portal still funnels through agents rather than around them. This is not a sales pitch dressed up as a fact, it is simply how the two main platforms are built. The real choice a seller is making is not agent versus no agent in the abstract, it is which channels they can actually appear on, and whether the buyer pool they can reach without one is wide enough for their specific property.

Frequently asked questions

Can I list my own property on PropertyGuru without an agent?

No. PropertyGuru's own listing policy restricts direct property listings to registered property agents, stated on PropertyGuru's own help and community pages. A private seller cannot post their own listing on Singapore's largest property portal, which is one of the more concrete gaps between a solo sale and an agent marketed one.

What is co broking and why does it matter for how many buyers see my listing?

Co broking is when two property agents, one representing you as the seller and one representing a buyer, work together on the same transaction, with commission shared between them by private arrangement. This means your listing is not just seen by buyers who search the portals themselves, it is actively surfaced to every buyer's agent in the market whose client's requirements match your property, a channel a private listing has no way to plug into.

Does 99.co let me list and reach buyers directly?

99.co offers a free Owner Listings service, but by its own description it connects your property to real estate agents who have matching clients, rather than putting your listing in front of buyers directly the way an agent's listing on the main portal is. You are still, in effect, relying on an agent to bring you a buyer, just without one managing the process on your side.

Is a buyer who comes through a co broke agent less serious than one who contacts me directly?

Generally the opposite. A buyer working with their own agent has usually already discussed budget, financing and requirements with that agent before viewing your property, which is a layer of screening a walk in enquiry from a portal or a personal contact has not been through. Buyer quality tends to track how much qualification happened before the viewing, not how the buyer first heard about the listing.

Is an exclusive or an open listing better for reaching the most buyers?

It depends on the agreement's terms, not just the label. An exclusive listing that explicitly permits co broking usually gets both focused marketing effort from one agent and broad exposure to the whole buyer's agent network, while an open listing spread across several agents can attract thinner effort from each because no one agent's commission is secure. This is covered in full in my separate guide comparing exclusive and open listings.

Does more exposure just bring more time wasters, or does it actually improve buyer quality?

Reach and quality are not the same lever, which is why marketing and screening are both part of an agent's job, not just one or the other. Wider exposure across portals and the co broke network increases the odds a genuinely matched, financially qualified buyer sees the property, while a separate screening step, covered in the next article in this series, filters that wider pool down to buyers actually worth a viewing slot.
