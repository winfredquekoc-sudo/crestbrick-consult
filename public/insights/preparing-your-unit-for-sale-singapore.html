Getting your property ready to sell for the first time

By Winfred Quek · CEA R073319H · Published 29 August 2026

Key Takeaways

  • Presentation does not change what your property is worth, it changes how many genuine buyers ask to see it, which is the input the rest of your sale depends on.
  • Decluttering and depersonalising are the highest return work available to a seller, and they cost time, not money.
  • A full renovation rarely returns its full cost at sale. Small visible fixes, a chipped wall, a loose handle, a leaking tap, usually matter more per dollar spent.
  • Smell is one of the fastest ways a buyer forms a negative impression, and sellers rarely notice it in their own home.
  • Photo day preparation should happen after decluttering is complete, since the listing photos are what earn you the viewing, not the walk through itself.

Part 3 of this series covered getting your price right. This part covers getting the unit itself right, since even a correctly priced listing under performs if the photos and the in person experience do not match what the price promises.

Why presentation moves viewings, not just the vibe

Presentation is often treated as a soft, subjective concern, something nice to have if there is time. In practice it has a direct, measurable effect on the number of people who request a viewing, since most buyers today form their first impression from listing photos, long before they ever step inside. A property that photographs cluttered or dim gets fewer viewing requests than an equivalent property that photographs clean and bright, regardless of how the two actually compare in person. Fewer viewings means fewer chances at an offer, so presentation is really a lead generation problem, not a decorating one.

Declutter and depersonalise, the highest return work

Clear surfaces, pack away excess furniture, and remove personal photographs and highly individual decor. The goal is for a buyer to picture their own life in the space, which is harder to do when every surface and wall is filled with someone else's. This work costs time and effort, not money, and it is consistently the highest return preparation a seller can do. Do it before photo day, not after, since the photos taken before decluttering are the ones a buyer sees first, and a second, better set of photos after the fact rarely reaches the same audience as the original listing.

Small fixes worth doing vs renovations that rarely pay back

A first time seller often assumes a big, visible upgrade is the way to a better sale. In most cases the opposite is true. A full renovation is expensive, takes time you may not have, and rarely returns its full cost at the point of sale, since a buyer is paying primarily for space, location and lease or tenure, not for finishes they may prefer to choose themselves. Small, inexpensive fixes tend to matter more per dollar spent, because a buyer reads a visible small defect as a signal about the condition of everything they cannot see.

Worth doing

Touching up chipped paint, tightening or replacing loose cabinet handles and door hinges, fixing a dripping tap, replacing a blown light bulb or a broken switch plate, addressing any visible mould or mildew, and giving the whole unit a thorough, professional level clean.

Rarely worth it before a sale

A full kitchen replacement, new flooring throughout, or a bathroom overhaul, undertaken purely to sell. These are large, personal decisions that the next owner is just as likely to redo to their own taste, so the cost seldom returns in full at the closing table. If your existing finishes are dated but functional and clean, a thorough clean and small repairs usually serve you better than a renovation undertaken specifically to sell.

Lighting and smell, the two senses buyers do not admit matter

Open every curtain and blind, switch on every light in the unit, even during the day, and check that all bulbs work before any viewing or photo session. A bright unit reads as larger and better maintained than an identical unit left dim. Smell matters just as much and is easy to miss, since sellers grow used to their own home's smell over time. Cooking odours, pet smells and simply stale air from a unit that has not been ventilated are among the fastest ways a buyer forms a negative impression, often within seconds of walking in. Ventilate thoroughly before every viewing, and deal with the source of any persistent smell rather than masking it.

Preparing for photo day

Our dedicated guide to staging and photographing your property covers this in more depth, including how to shoot each room. The quick version for a first time seller is below.

RoomQuick checklist
Living roomClear surfaces, arrange seating to show the space, open curtains fully
BedroomsMade beds, cleared nightstands, closet doors closed, personal items packed away
KitchenCleared counters, no dishes in the sink, appliances wiped down
BathroomCleared counters, fresh towels, no visible mould, good ventilation before shooting
Step 1: Complete decluttering and depersonalising across the whole unit, not just the rooms you expect to be photographed most.
Step 2: Complete the small fixes list, chipped paint, loose handles, dripping taps, broken fittings.
Step 3: Deep clean, including areas easy to overlook such as window tracks, extractor fans and grout.
Step 4: On the day itself, ventilate thoroughly, open all curtains, switch on every light, and shoot in good natural daylight where possible.
Do not schedule your first viewings the same week as your final preparation. Buyers who view a unit still mid clean up, or a listing shot before the preparation was finished, form a first impression that is very hard to reverse with a later, better set of photos or a tidier viewing. Finish the preparation, then shoot, then list.

Winfred's Take

The first time sellers who ask me about renovating before a sale are almost always better served by a weekend of decluttering and a proper clean. I have seen a modestly presented but honestly priced unit outperform a more expensive, half finished renovation, simply because buyers could walk in and immediately picture themselves living there. Spend your energy on the things a buyer notices in the first ten seconds, space, light and cleanliness, before you spend a single dollar on anything structural.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need to renovate before I sell my property?

Usually not. A full renovation rarely returns its full cost at the point of sale, since buyers pay for space, location and condition, not for finishes they may want to change themselves. The higher return work is almost always decluttering, cleaning, small visible repairs and better lighting, not a new kitchen or new flooring.

What small fixes actually matter to buyers?

Anything a buyer can see and mentally add up as neglect: chipped paint, loose cabinet handles, a leaking tap, a broken light fitting, mould in the bathroom. These are inexpensive to fix and disproportionately affect how a buyer judges the overall condition of the property, since visible small defects are read as a signal about invisible bigger ones.

Should I declutter before or after I list the property?

Before. Your listing photos are taken after decluttering and depersonalising, not before, since those photos are what most buyers use to decide whether a viewing is worth their time. Doing this work after you already have viewings booked means your best chance at strong first impressions in the photos has already passed.

Does smell really affect whether a property sells?

Yes, more than most first time sellers expect. Cooking odours, pet smells and simply stale, unventilated air are among the fastest ways a buyer forms a negative impression, often within seconds of entering, and it is a factor few sellers notice in their own home because they are used to it. Ventilate thoroughly before every viewing and address the source, not just the smell.

What should I actually do to prepare for photo day?

Complete decluttering and cleaning first, then open every curtain and blind, switch on every light in the unit, put away personal items and excess furniture, and choose a day with good natural light. A property that photographs clean, bright and spacious generates more viewing requests than one that is simply larger or better located but poorly presented in its photos.

Want a second opinion on your unit before photo day?

A quick walk through, in person or over a video call, flags the fixes that actually move the needle and the ones you can safely skip, so you spend your time and money where it counts.
