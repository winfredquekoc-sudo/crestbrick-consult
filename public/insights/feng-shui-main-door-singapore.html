Main Door Feng Shui: What Singapore Buyers Check (2026)

By Winfred Quek · CEA R073319H · Crestbrick

Quick answer: The main door is the mouth of qi in feng shui, the point where energy is believed to enter a home. Singapore buyers check what it faces: not a lift or a neighbour's door head to head, not a downward staircase, not the stove, and not a road or corridor aiming straight at it. The space in front should be bright and open, the bright hall. A door facing a lift is structural and narrows your buyer pool. A cluttered entrance is cosmetic and easy to fix.

Why is the main door the most important thing in feng shui?

Feng shui calls the main door the mouth of qi, the point where energy is believed to enter a home. Grand Master Tan Khoon Yong of Way Fengshui Group, the first practitioner in Singapore awarded Grandmaster status by the International Feng Shui Association, teaches that the main door carries more weight than any other feature in the home. Everything else, the kitchen, the bedroom, the floor plan, is read only after the door.

There is a plain reading too. The main door is the first thing every visitor, and every future buyer, sees. A door that opens onto something jarring, a blank wall, a cluttered corridor, a lift lobby, registers instantly. A door that opens onto light and space feels good the moment you step in, every day you live there. That is why buyers, feng shui conscious or not, tend to react to the same doors.

What is a bright hall and why does it matter?

Outside a good main door, feng shui asks for a bright hall, mingtang in Chinese. This is the open space the door faces, whether that is a private foyer, a corridor or a lift lobby. It should be bright, spacious and free of clutter, so energy can gather before it enters the home.

The plain version of this rule is just good sense. A dim, narrow, cluttered corridor outside your door feels worse every day than an open, well lit one. Inside the home, the same idea applies to the entryway itself: shoes piled at the door, a cabinet blocking the path, or a blank wall facing you the moment you step in all work against a bright hall. None of this needs a renovation. It is usually a 5 minute fix, which is why it sits on the fixable side of the ledger, unlike the door's facing.

How do you check the main door in an HDB versus a condo?

In an HDB flat, the main door usually opens onto a shared corridor, so the checks are about what sits directly ahead and to the sides. Look at the lift lobby: is it around a corner, or directly opposite your door? Check the rubbish chute; a main door beside or facing the chute point is avoided for smell as much as for feng shui. Walk the corridor itself: a straight, long corridor aiming at your door is the T junction problem in HDB form. Some buyers also prefer to avoid unit numbers ending in 4, a cultural preference rather than a classical feng shui rule.

In a condominium, the main door usually opens onto a private lift lobby shared by a handful of units, so the same head to head check applies to your neighbour's door. Step back and look at the block itself. A sharp corner or edge of another block pointing at your unit is read the same way as a corridor T junction, a poison arrow. Check what the development faces from the main door and the balcony alike: a temple, a hospital or a cemetery nearby is a common objection for feng shui conscious buyers, even though it says nothing about the physical quality of the unit.

Which main door problems are fixable and which are structural?

The difference matters because only one type follows the home when you sell.

Structural, weigh these before you buy:

  • A door facing a lift or another door head to head
  • A door facing a downward staircase or escalator
  • A door at the end of a corridor, or facing a road
  • A door facing a sharp building edge opposite
  • A door and stove in a straight line, since the kitchen wall rarely moves

Fixable, do not weigh these too heavily:

  • A dim or cluttered bright hall
  • Furniture or clutter just inside the door
  • A drain running parallel to the door
  • A door that opens onto a toilet

A screen, a plant or a bagua mirror can soften a structural problem, but it does not change the facing itself, and a feng shui conscious buyer at your next sale will still see the same door. Fixable problems live in what you place near the door, not in the door's direction, so the next owner can fix them just as easily as you can.

What does this look like for a real buyer?

Daryl is a Singapore Citizen buying his second property. He already owns a 4 room HDB flat and is viewing a 2 bedroom unit at Riverfront Residences in Hougang, an area he already knows well. At the viewing, he runs the main door checks before he runs anything else.

  • Main door. It opens to a private lift lobby shared with only one other unit, and it does not face the lift doors directly. The lobby has a window at the far end and feels open. Pass.
  • Corridor and road. No long corridor or road lines up with the door from outside. Pass.
  • Building opposite. The block across the courtyard has a curved facade, not a sharp corner aimed at his stack. Pass.
  • Kitchen. The door opens into a small foyer that turns before reaching the kitchen, so there is no straight line between the door and the stove. Pass.
  • One flag. The previous owner left a tall shoe cabinet just inside the door, narrowing the bright hall and partly blocking the view in. Minor and fixable. He plans to swap it for a low console before moving in.

On the investor lens, the door itself carries no structural taboo that would narrow Daryl's future buyer pool. The shoe cabinet is furniture, not layout, so it is entirely his to change. He goes ahead with the purchase.

Does a bad main door facing hurt resale value?

There is no fixed percentage discount for a bad main door, and no verified data set that prices it. What is real is demand. A meaningful share of Singapore buyers screen the main door first, because tradition treats it as the single most important feature in the home. A unit with an obvious door taboo, facing the lift head to head or sitting at the end of a corridor that points a road at it, speaks to fewer of those buyers. Fewer interested buyers can mean a longer time on the market, or more room to negotiate on price. That is a marketability effect, not a mystical one.

The one line to remember: a cluttered bright hall or a shoe cabinet in the way does not follow the unit to the next owner, so it does not weigh on resale. The facing of the door does, because you cannot move it.

Frequently asked questions

What is the single most important feng shui check for a main door?

Whether it faces another door head to head, most often a lift or a neighbour's door. Tradition reads this as two mouths of qi meeting in conflict, and in practice it also means more noise and less privacy. It is usually the first thing feng shui practitioners in Singapore check.

Why is a main door facing a staircase considered bad?

A staircase or escalator going down in front of the door is read as wealth rolling away, because qi cannot gather and settle before it enters. It is treated as a structural taboo, since the staircase position is part of the building and cannot be moved.

Can a main door facing a lift be fixed?

Not fully. A screen, a plant or better lighting can soften how the entrance feels, but none of it changes the facing itself. Because the direction is structural, this is a feature to weigh carefully before you buy rather than something to fix afterwards.

What is a bright hall in feng shui?

The bright hall, or mingtang, is the open space the main door faces, whether a private foyer, a corridor or a lift lobby. It should be bright, spacious and free of clutter, since a dim or blocked entrance is read as trapping good energy outside.

Do HDB flats and condos check the main door differently?

The core checks are the same, but the details differ. HDB buyers pay attention to the lift, the rubbish chute and the corridor outside their door, while condo buyers look at the facing of the block opposite, sharp edges pointing at their stack, and whether the development faces a temple or a hospital.

Does a main door facing a toilet matter?

Yes, a main door that opens straight onto a toilet is treated as impurity meeting the entrance, and there is a practical reading too, since odour and hygiene are real concerns. This layout is uncommon in Singapore flats and condos, and a screen or a closed door helps on the rare occasion it happens.

Does a bad main door facing actually lower resale value?

There is no fixed discount, but a unit with an obvious door taboo appeals to fewer feng shui conscious buyers, which can mean a longer time on the market. Layout, light and price still move value more reliably than any single door rule, but a structural taboo is worth weighing before you buy, since you cannot change it later.

Should I consult a feng shui master before buying based on the main door?

The checks in this guide are general layout checks and do not need your personal details. If you want a reading based on your birth date or kua number, that is a separate and more advanced layer best handled by a qualified feng shui master.
