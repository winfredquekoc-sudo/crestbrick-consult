Novena and the Health City cluster: property outlook

By Winfred Quek, Associate Marketing Consultant · CEA R073319H · Crestbrick Pte Ltd (L31010886H) · Published 13 July 2026

Quick answer: Novena has developed into Singapore's medical hub, anchored by Tan Tock Seng Hospital, Mount Elizabeth Novena Hospital, and a wider Health City Novena masterplan that concentrates healthcare, research and specialist medical facilities in one precinct. That concentration creates a genuinely distinct tenant pool for landlords: doctors, nurses, allied health professionals, medical students and patients, including medical tourists staying for treatment, alongside the district's own strong central location fundamentals. The honest caveat is that Novena's central address already carries a location premium independent of the healthcare story, so the cluster is a real, additive demand driver rather than a reason to overpay for any unit regardless of price. This guide covers what the cluster actually delivers and how to weigh it properly.

Most Singapore districts build their investment case around offices, schools or transport. Novena builds it around hospitals, and that is a more interesting story than it first sounds. Healthcare demand behaves differently from office demand: it is less tied to economic cycles, it draws both local professionals and patients from across the region, and it creates a tenant pool most other central districts simply do not have access to. As an investor minded advisor, I think this deserves a proper look rather than a passing mention.

What Health City Novena actually is

Health City Novena is the masterplan concentrating medical facilities around the Novena area. Its anchor is Tan Tock Seng Hospital, one of Singapore's major public hospitals, alongside private facilities such as Mount Elizabeth Novena Hospital and a cluster of specialist medical centres, clinics and supporting healthcare services. The intent is to bring related healthcare, research and education facilities together within walking distance of each other, similar in spirit to how business parks cluster related industries, but built around medicine rather than technology or finance.

This concentration has been building for years, and it continues to attract specialist practices and supporting healthcare businesses that want proximity to the anchor hospitals. For a buyer or investor, the practical upshot is a district with an unusually deep, specific institutional employer base sitting right in the middle of it.

The tenant pool a hospital cluster actually creates

A concentration of hospitals draws a specific, recurring group of tenants that most office focused districts cannot replicate: doctors, nurses and allied health professionals working shifts at the surrounding institutions, medical students attached to teaching hospitals and research facilities, and patients or their accompanying family members staying for treatment, particularly medical tourists visiting Singapore for specialist care from elsewhere in the region.

This tenant profile has two features worth noting. First, healthcare employment tends to be less cyclical than general office employment, since demand for medical care does not track the broader economy the way corporate hiring does. Second, the patient and medical tourism segment creates demand for shorter and medium term stays, which is a different rental pattern to the typical one or two year lease that dominates most residential rental markets, and it particularly favours condominiums and serviced apartments positioned close to the hospitals over further flung units that only carry the district name.

Where the rental case is strongest, and where it thins out

The healthcare cluster's rental relevance concentrates most directly around private condominiums and serviced apartments genuinely close to the hospitals, the properties best positioned to serve medical staff on shift work schedules and patients or families needing convenient, short notice accommodation near treatment. The closer a unit sits to the actual medical facilities, the more of this specific demand pool it can realistically capture.

Novena's surrounding HDB stock benefits more indirectly, through the area's general amenity, connectivity and central location, rather than through the same specialist tenant pool the hospitals generate directly. That does not make HDB flats in the area a poor choice, but it does mean the healthcare cluster premium argument applies with more force to private property positioned deliberately close to the medical precinct than to public housing further from it.

Novena's fundamentals beyond healthcare

It is worth being clear eyed that Novena would be a solid central location even without the hospital cluster. It sits on the North South Line with strong connectivity toward the city centre and Orchard Road, and it has long been considered a desirable, centrally located residential address in its own right. This matters for how you read pricing: Novena already carries a location premium tied to its central address, quite apart from anything to do with medicine. The healthcare cluster is an additive demand driver layered on top of an already strong base, not the entire reason the district commands its price.

For the broader logic of how central location and connectivity translate into sustained property value across Singapore districts, my what makes Singapore property appreciate guide covers the framework this district fits into.

A hospital cluster is a demand driver, not a blank cheque. The most common mistake in this district is treating the Health City Novena story as justification for paying any price. It is a genuine, differentiated rental demand source, best captured by units positioned close to the medical precinct, but it still has to clear ordinary yield and entry price discipline like any other purchase.

Comparing Novena with other specialist demand districts

Novena is not the only Singapore district where a specific institutional cluster shapes the tenant pool. One north draws technology, media and biomedical research employees. The CBD draws finance and professional services staff. What sets Novena apart is that its anchor tenants are not tied to a single employer or industry cycle in the way a bank or a technology firm is. Hospitals operate around the clock, staff shifts continuously, and patient demand for specialist care does not disappear during an economic downturn the way discretionary corporate hiring can. That structural difference is worth weighing seriously if you are choosing between comparable entry prices in different specialist demand districts.

It is also worth being realistic about scale. Novena's healthcare cluster, while genuine, still competes for tenant attention with the CBD's much larger office workforce and one north's growing research and technology base. The healthcare tenant pool is a meaningful, differentiated slice of overall demand in the district, not the only source of it, and a sensible underwriting approach blends the healthcare thesis with Novena's ordinary residential fundamentals rather than relying on either alone.

A practical note on serviced and short stay demand

Landlords considering units near the medical precinct specifically for the patient and medical tourism segment should understand that this demand behaves differently from a standard one or two year residential lease. Stays can be considerably shorter, sometimes just weeks, tied to a specific treatment course, and the tenant profile skews toward families or companions accompanying a patient rather than a single working professional. This can support a higher effective rent per stay for units configured and marketed for that purpose, but it also typically requires more active management, more frequent turnover, and often coordination with hospitals or medical concierge services that refer patients to nearby accommodation. It is a genuine niche within the broader Novena rental case, but it suits landlords willing to run a more hands on operation rather than those seeking the lowest maintenance option.

How to decide if Novena fits your plan

  1. Prioritise proximity to the medical precinct. If you are investing specifically for the healthcare tenant pool, the closer your unit sits to the hospitals, the more of that demand it can realistically capture.
  2. Weigh private over HDB for the healthcare angle. The strongest version of this thesis applies to private condominiums and serviced apartments, not the district's HDB stock.
  3. Credit the central location on its own merits. Novena's connectivity and central address are real value drivers independent of Health City, and should factor into your price discipline regardless of the healthcare story.
  4. Run the yield numbers properly. A differentiated tenant pool is an advantage, not a guarantee. Underwrite any specific unit on its own entry price and rental math.
