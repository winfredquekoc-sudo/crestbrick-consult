Preparing your house for sale: the pre listing checklist

By Winfred Quek · 8 minute read · Published 17 August 2026

By Winfred Quek · CEA R073319H · Published 17 August 2026

Quick answer: The 30 days before your listing goes live decide how the next 30 go. Before you list: request your loan redemption figure and CPF usage statement, check where you sit on the Seller's Stamp Duty clock, review your tenancy agreement if the unit is tenanted, and decide in outline whether you are selling first or buying first. Then declutter, complete minor repairs, clean properly and get the lighting right before photo day, keeping any renovation as a separate decision rather than a default. Pricing is its own exercise, built on recent comparable transactions and a bank valuation, not a wish. Get the paperwork and the presentation right before the first viewing, and the transaction itself runs the way our other guides describe.

Most guides to selling property in Singapore start at the listing: comparable transactions, viewings, the Option to Purchase, negotiation. Ours do too, and we would rather point you to them than repeat that ground here. This guide covers the 30 days before any of that starts, the paperwork, the presentation and the pricing homework that decide whether your first two weeks of viewings build momentum or just burn through them. Handle this stretch properly and the transaction itself runs the way our other guides already describe.

Paperwork readiness before you list

Buyers move fast once they are interested, and the sellers who lose that momentum are usually the ones still hunting for a number their bank or their CPF account should have handed them weeks earlier. Five things to have in hand before your first viewing.

Your loan redemption figure

Call or log in to your bank to request your outstanding loan redemption figure, the exact amount needed to fully discharge your mortgage. This is the anchor for your entire net proceeds calculation, and figures can change, so request a current one close to when you expect to complete rather than relying on an old statement. Our seller net proceeds guide walks through how the redemption figure combines with your CPF refund, agent commission and legal fees to produce the number that actually lands in your account.

Your CPF usage statement

If you used CPF for the purchase, request your CPF property statement so you know the principal and accrued interest that must be refunded to your CPF account on completion. That refund is not lost, it returns to your own CPF, but it reduces the cash you walk away with, and sellers who only discover the size of it after accepting an offer are the ones who feel short changed at completion. Work this out before you set expectations with yourself, not after.

Where you sit on the Seller's Stamp Duty clock

If you bought within the last few years, check where you sit on the Seller's Stamp Duty schedule before you commit to a listing date. SSD applies within four years of purchase for property bought on or after 4 July 2025, and within three years for property bought earlier; HDB flats are exempt, since the Minimum Occupation Period already does that job. Our Seller's Stamp Duty guide has the full rate schedule and waiver paths, and the current figures are also kept at the Singapore property rules reference.

Tenancy status, if the unit is tenanted

A tenanted unit is not sold the same way as a vacant one. Before you list, read your tenancy agreement in full, the remaining term, the deposit held, and any break or diplomatic clause, because these determine whether you are marketing vacant possession or a unit where the buyer inherits the lease, and that changes your realistic buyer pool. Our guide on buying property with an existing tenant covers the position from the other side of the table, and is worth reading so you know what your buyer will be weighing up.

Sequencing your next home

Decide, at least in outline, whether you are selling first or buying first before you list. That decision shapes how much room you have to negotiate on this sale, and whether you need to plan for interim housing along the way. Our sell first or buy first guide works through the trade offs, and a Property Portfolio Analysis maps the sequencing against your actual numbers rather than a rule of thumb.

Presentation that actually moves buyers

Once paperwork is in hand, presentation is what decides whether the first two weeks of viewings build momentum or quietly stall. None of the following needs a renovation permit or a contractor.

Five things to do before your first viewing

  • Declutter first, decorate second. Clear personal photographs, excess furniture and anything that makes a room feel smaller than it is. Buyers are trying to picture their own life in the space, and your belongings are usually the main thing in the way.
  • Fix the small things. A dripping tap, a cracked switch plate, a door that sticks, none of these cost much to fix, and all of them read as neglect to a buyer who is looking for a reason to negotiate down.
  • Clean properly, not quickly. Grout, window tracks and the inside of the oven, not just the visible surfaces. A unit that looks and smells properly cared for supports your asking price; one that does not invites a lower offer before a word is said.
  • Get the lighting right. Replace dead bulbs, open curtains for daytime viewings, and check how the unit shows and photographs at the specific times you expect viewings to actually happen.
  • Prepare properly for photo day. Clear counters and surfaces, put away laundry and personal items, and do a final walkthrough immediately before the photographer arrives, not the night before.

If the unit genuinely needs more than cosmetic work, treat that as a separate, larger decision with its own cost and timeline, not a pre listing default. Our guides on renovating an HDB flat and the condo renovation approval process cover the rules if you do go down that path. A closer look at the cost and benefit of renovating before a sale is coming as its own guide, so for now we are keeping this section to presentation you can complete without a contractor.

Pricing homework before you set an asking figure

Presentation gets buyers through the door; pricing is what gets them to make an offer, and it is a separate exercise from anything above. Pull recent comparable transactions for units like yours, and get a sense of where a bank valuation is likely to land, because a bank lends on the lower of price or valuation, and a gap between the two becomes cash your buyer has to find on top of the price. Our pricing your property to sell guide covers the full method, triangulating comparable transactions, bank valuation and buyer search psychology into an asking price that sells without leaving money behind. Do this homework before you list, not after the first round of viewing feedback tells you the number was wrong.

Choosing your representation

Whether you list with an agent or go it alone is its own decision, and it is worth making deliberately rather than by default. An agent brings marketing reach, buyer screening, negotiation and the paperwork discipline that keeps a transaction from stalling on a technicality; going without one saves commission but puts all of that on you instead. Our sellers guide covers the full transaction end to end, including how to weigh that choice, once the preparation in this guide is done.

Frequently asked questions

How long before I list should I start preparing?

30 days gives most sellers enough runway to gather paperwork, complete minor repairs and get photo day right without rushing. A tenanted unit, or one that needs more than cosmetic work, can need longer, factor that in once you have read your tenancy agreement or scoped any repair work.

Do I need my loan redemption figure before I list, or only once I have a buyer?

Before you list. It anchors your net proceeds math and your asking price expectations, and requesting it early means no unpleasant surprise once an offer is on the table. Our seller net proceeds guide covers how it fits with your CPF refund and other costs.

Should I renovate before I sell?

Not as a default. Renovation is a separate, larger decision with its own cost, timeline and approval process, covered in our HDB and condo renovation guides. This checklist focuses on presentation, decluttering, repairs, cleaning, lighting and photo prep, that you can complete without a contractor or a permit.

My unit is tenanted. Can I still list it for sale?

Yes, but read your tenancy agreement first. The remaining term, deposit held and any break clause determine whether you are marketing vacant possession or a tenancy your buyer will inherit, and that changes how you position the listing. Our guide on buying property with an existing tenant covers the position from the buyer's side, which is worth understanding before you list.

Do I need an agent to get through this checklist, or can I do it myself?

The paperwork and presentation items in this guide do not require an agent, you can complete all of them yourself. Whether to use an agent for the listing, marketing and negotiation that follow is a separate decision; our sellers guide covers how to weigh it.
