The Complete Negotiation Playbook: What to Ask For + How to Justify It

Article ID: 716 | Published: 28 August 2026

Negotiation is not aggressive. It is strategic. A successful property negotiation in Singapore can save you $20,000 to $100,000 or more depending on the property type and market conditions. Yet most buyers never ask. They accept the asking price and regret it later.

This guide breaks down exactly what to negotiate, how to justify each demand with data, when to negotiate, and how your agent fits into the strategy. Whether you are buying an HDB flat, a private apartment, or a landed property, these 12 levers work across all segments.

12 Negotiation Levers: What to Ask For

1. Inspection Repairs & Credits
If the home inspection reveals defects (leaking pipes, faulty air conditioning, electrical faults), ask the seller to either repair them or credit the cost to you at closing. In Singapore, this is especially common with HDB flats nearing the end of their lease or private properties with aging systems.
2. Appraisal Gap Coverage
If the property appraises below the purchase price, ask the seller to cover part of the gap. This is common when the market has cooled slightly or the comparable sales data does not support the offer price.
3. Closing Cost Credits
Legal fees, registration fees, and agent commissions are real costs. Ask the seller to cover 1–3% in closing costs. In Singapore, this typically means stamp duty or lawyer fees contributions.
4. Price Adjustment (Outright Reduction)
The most direct negotiation. Use comparable sales data to justify a lower price. If three similar units on the same block sold for $20K less, you have a solid argument.
5. Timing & Move Out Date Flexibility
Ask for a longer settlement period if you need it, or a faster close if you have cash. Flexibility on timing is often easier for sellers than cash concessions.
6. Appliances & Fixtures Included
Ask if air conditioning units, kitchen appliances, wardrobes, and light fittings are included or if the seller will credit you for removal. In Singapore, this can be a $3,000–$10,000 swing.
7. Title Clearance & Outstanding Obligations
For HDB, confirm the seller has no outstanding housing loans or defects in title. For private property, ensure all strata arrears and renovation debts are cleared before completion.
8. Survey & Inspection Contingencies
Negotiate the period allowed for inspections and the right to withdraw if major issues emerge. Typical windows: 10–14 days for HDB, 21 days for private property.
9. Earnest Money Terms
Negotiate the earnest money amount (typically 1–5% in Singapore). A lower deposit protects your capital early on.
10. Strata Fees & Maintenance Arrears
For condos and EC properties, ask the seller to clear all strata arrears and maintenance fees before completion. This is non negotiable on your end; it protects you from liability.
11. Seller Concessions (Parking, Storage)
If the property comes with parking lots or storage units, confirm they transfer. If not included in the price, ask for a credit or inclusion.
12. Final Walk Through & Condition
Negotiate the right to a final walk through 24–48 hours before completion to confirm all negotiated repairs are done and the property is in promised condition.

How to Justify Each Point With Data

Use Comparable Sales Data

The strongest negotiation tool is comparable property sales. Request from your agent:

  • Three to five sales of similar units in the same block or neighbourhood sold in the last 3 months
  • Price per square metre for each comparable
  • Condition, fixtures, and lease remaining (for HDB)
  • Days on market for each property

If the property you want is listed at $650,000 but three identical units sold for $630,000 two months ago, that $20,000 gap is your negotiation cushion.

Days on Market Analysis

Properties that have been on the market 60+ days indicate a slow market or overpricing. This is your leverage point. You can confidently ask for 3–5% off if the property has sat unsold for two months or longer.

Inspection Reports as Hard Evidence

A professional home inspection report is your most concrete tool. Quotes from contractors to fix identified issues give you precise numbers to negotiate with. Instead of vague complaints, say: "The electrical rewiring quote is $5,000. Please credit that to me at closing."

Market Trend Data

If the market is softening (prices down 5–10% quarter over quarter), cite PropertyGuru, EdgeProp, or 99.co transaction reports. Sellers know the market has shifted. Use this to your advantage during negotiation.

Timeline: When to Negotiate

Pre offer

Instruct your agent to research comps and prepare a competitive offer. Do not offer the asking price upfront. Start 2–5% below and leave room to negotiate up.

Inspection Period (Days 1–14)

This is your main negotiation window. Once the inspection is complete, use any defects found as leverage to ask for repairs or credits. Most negotiations happen here.

Appraisal Period (Day 7–21)

If the appraisal comes in low, negotiate the appraisal gap immediately. Do not wait until closing. Sellers are most flexible after receiving disappointing appraisal news.

Final Walk Through (24–48 Hours Before Completion)

Confirm all negotiated repairs are complete. If the seller failed to make promised fixes, you have grounds to halt completion or demand additional credit.

Your Agent's Role in Negotiation

A strong real estate agent is your negotiation arm. They are the intermediary and the strategist. Here is what they should do:

  • Positioning: Frame your offer as serious and buyer credible. Sellers respond differently to cash buyers than to financed buyers. Your agent must highlight your strengths (pre approved loan, fast close, no contingencies if applicable).
  • Comps Research: They pull comparable sales and prepare a comp report to justify your offer price. This removes emotion and keeps negotiation data driven.
  • Market Intelligence: They know the seller's motivation (urgent need to move, inherited property, divorce settlement). This shapes negotiation strategy.
  • Tactical Phasing: A good agent negotiates in phases. They may ask for price first, then repairs, then closing credits. This avoids overwhelming the seller.
  • Counteroffers Management: They present counteroffers without emotion, explain your reasoning clearly, and keep lines open for further negotiation.
Pro Tip: If your agent pushes you to accept the asking price without negotiation, consider switching agents. Skilled negotiators save you tens of thousands. That is their job.

FAQ: Common Negotiation Questions

Is it rude to negotiate?
No. Negotiation is a standard part of the property purchase process in Singapore. Every serious transaction includes some back and forth on price or terms. Sellers expect it. Your agent will frame it professionally.
How much can I negotiate off?
Typical range: 2–8% depending on market conditions, days on market, and defects. In a strong seller's market, you may only get 1–2%. In a soft market with many options, 5–8% is more common. Use comps to guide your expectation.
What if the seller rejects my offer?
Negotiate again. Ask your agent why it was rejected and try again with a stronger offer or different terms (e.g., faster close, fewer contingencies). Most first offers do get rejected. This is normal.
Should I negotiate price or terms?
Both, but strategically. If the seller is emotionally attached to the price, focus on terms (faster close, flexibility on move date). If the seller is purely motivated by cash, focus on price reduction. Your agent reads this and adjusts strategy.
What if I need to close fast?
Fast close is leverage in the buyer's favour. Use it. Ask for a lower price in exchange for a tight 30 day settlement. Sellers often prefer certainty and speed over squeezing extra dollars.
Can I negotiate after inspection?
Absolutely. This is the standard time to negotiate. Inspection defects are ammunition. Get repair quotes and come back with data. Sellers cannot ignore a $15,000 electrical rewiring bill.
What about HDB lease concerns?
For HDB, always negotiate based on lease remaining. A flat with 75 years left is worth less than one with 95 years. Banks also price in lease length. Use this in your offer. Many buyers miss this leverage point.
Should I disclose my maximum offer upfront?
No. Never reveal your ceiling. Let your agent manage the back and forth. Sellers do not need to know you would pay 10% more. Let them think they have squeezed the best deal out of you when they negotiate you up.
