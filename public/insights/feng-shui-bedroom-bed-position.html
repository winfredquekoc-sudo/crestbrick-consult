Bedroom Feng Shui: Bed Position, Beams and Mirrors

By Winfred Quek · CEA R073319H · Crestbrick

Quick answer: The commanding position puts the bed where you can see the bedroom door without lying in a straight line with it, ideally against the wall diagonal to the entrance, with a solid wall behind the headboard. Avoid a beam directly overhead, a mirror facing the bed, and a headboard on a wall shared with a toilet. Your feet should not point straight out the door. Almost every part of this moves with your furniture, so it barely touches resale, but it changes how well you sleep.

What is the commanding position for a bed?

The core bedroom principle is the commanding position. Place the bed so you can see the bedroom door from where you lie, without your body running in a straight line with it. The most commanding spot is usually the wall diagonal to the entrance, farthest from the door but still in view of it. The headboard should rest against a solid wall, not a window or an open shelf.

Traditional feng shui reads this as control. You see who enters before they see you, and a solid wall at your back represents support. Strip away the tradition and the same layout still works well in practice. Most people sleep better facing a room they can see fully, with a solid surface behind the head rather than glass or a gap. It is a security instinct as much as a feng shui one, and because you choose your own furniture layout, it is not something that follows the unit onto the market.

Why should you avoid a beam over the bed?

A beam is the one bedroom item that is not simple furniture. It is part of the ceiling structure, so you cannot pick it up and move it the way you would a mirror or a wardrobe.

Traditional feng shui treats an exposed beam directly over the bed, especially across the chest or head, as a source of pressure on the sleeper. Practitioners describe it as qi pressing down on you while you rest. There is a plain reading too. A beam close over a pillow is visually heavy. Many people feel it every time they lie down, whether or not they know the feng shui term for it, and it can make an otherwise good room feel lower and tighter than it is.

You do not need to avoid a unit just because it has a beam. Shift the bed so the beam runs along the room instead of across the sleeper, if the layout allows. Where it does not, a false ceiling can box the beam in and hide it entirely. A canopy bed or a four poster frame with a soft canopy achieves a similar visual cover without construction, and a tall wardrobe placed to interrupt the beam's edge works too. None of these require moving the beam itself, only softening how it reads in the room.

Where should mirrors go in a bedroom?

Do not place a mirror where it faces the bed directly. This includes a mirrored wardrobe door, a dressing table mirror, or a full length mirror on the opposite wall.

Traditional feng shui holds that a mirror facing a sleeping person doubles their energy overnight and can disturb rest. Older guidance also warns that catching your own reflection when you wake in the dark is unsettling. The liveability version is simpler. A mirror opposite the bed catches every movement, every light from outside, every silhouette. Some people wake up startled by their own reflection more than once before they work out why. A mirror on a side wall, or inside a wardrobe you keep closed, avoids the problem entirely and still gives you a full length mirror to dress in front of.

This is one of the easiest fixes in the whole bedroom. Reposition the mirror, or add a cover, and the issue is gone. It has no bearing on resale, because the fix costs nothing and takes an afternoon.

Can the bed share a wall with a toilet?

Avoid placing the headboard against a wall that backs onto a toilet, whether that is your own ensuite or bathroom, or a neighbour's. The same goes for a bed positioned in line with the toilet's plumbing, and a bed that faces a toilet door left open.

Tradition treats a toilet as the least clean corner of a home, and sleeping with your head against that wall is read as resting close to the wrong kind of energy. There is an ordinary version of the same worry. Pipes run inside that wall. Flushing, water pressure and general bathroom noise carry through more than most people expect, especially in an HDB flat or a compact condo bedroom.

Where the room has more than one usable wall, this is a simple furniture decision. Move the headboard to another wall and the issue disappears. It becomes a real constraint only in a small bedroom where one wall is the only place a queen or king bed physically fits, and that wall happens to back onto a bathroom. Check this at the viewing, not after you move in.

Should the bed face the bedroom door?

There is a difference between seeing the door and facing it directly. The commanding position asks you to see the door from an angle. It does not ask you to line the foot of the bed up with the doorway.

A bed placed so your feet point straight out the door is sometimes called the coffin position, because it echoes how a body is carried out of a home. Traditional feng shui avoids it for that reason alone. The practical version is about privacy and draught. A bed in direct line with the door is the first thing anyone sees on opening it, and it sits in the direct path of air moving through the flat.

The fix is the same furniture shift as the commanding position: angle the bed toward the diagonal wall instead of the wall directly opposite the door. Most bedrooms in an HDB flat or condo have enough width to make this adjustment without losing storage or walking space.

What does this look like for a real buyer?

Joel is a Singapore citizen buying his second property. He shortlists a three bedroom unit at The Gazania in Toa Payoh and brings a bedroom feng shui checklist to the viewing, alongside the usual checks on layout and facing.

  • Commanding position. The master bedroom door sits in one corner, and the wall diagonal to it is long enough for a queen bed with a solid wall behind the headboard. Pass.
  • Beam. A structural beam runs across the ceiling near the air conditioning ledge, directly above where the headboard would sit. Flag, not a fail. A false ceiling section or a canopy bed frame would cover it.
  • Mirror. The built in wardrobe has a mirrored sliding door on the wall facing the bed. Flag, fixable. Swap the door panel for a non mirrored one during renovation, or angle the wardrobe to the side wall instead.
  • Toilet wall. The ensuite bathroom sits on the wall next to the bedroom entrance, not behind where the headboard would go. Pass.
  • Door line. The bed's natural position, on the diagonal wall, keeps his feet well away from a direct line to the door. Pass.

Read on the investor lens, nothing here is structural. The beam is the only item that needs a small renovation budget, and beams like this are common across many stacks at this development. Joel proceeds, and notes the wardrobe swap and the beam cover as line items for his renovation quote, not reasons to walk away.

Which bedroom problems are fixable?

Bedroom feng shui behaves differently from the main door or the floor plan. Almost everything on this list is furniture, not structure, which means almost everything is fixable, by you now or by the next owner later. Four items move with your furniture. The beam is fixed into the ceiling, so it is the one item worth budgeting for instead of just rearranging.

The one line to remember: a mirror, a headboard against the wrong wall, or feet pointing at the door all move with your furniture. A beam does not. If a unit has a beam over where the bed naturally sits, budget for a false ceiling or a canopy bed, and treat everything else as a five minute fix.

Frequently asked questions

What is the commanding position for a bed in feng shui?

The commanding position places the bed where you can see the bedroom door without lying directly in line with it, usually against the wall diagonal to the entrance. The headboard should rest against a solid wall rather than a window. It is meant to give the sleeper a sense of control over the room.

Why is a beam over the bed considered bad feng shui?

Traditional feng shui reads an exposed beam directly above the bed as pressure bearing down on the sleeper, especially across the chest or head. Many people also find a low beam over the pillow area visually heavy in daily life. It can be softened with a false ceiling, a canopy bed, or by shifting the bed out of its path.

Does a mirror facing the bed really cause bad luck?

Traditional belief holds that a mirror facing a sleeping person disturbs their energy overnight. There is a practical reading too, since a mirror opposite the bed can startle you awake with movement or reflected light. Either way, the fix is the same: move the mirror to a side wall or keep it inside a closed wardrobe.

Can a bed share a wall with a toilet?

Feng shui advises against placing the headboard on a wall that backs onto a toilet, your own or a neighbour's. Pipe noise and dampness through that wall are the practical version of the same concern. In most bedrooms you can simply move the headboard to another wall.

Should my feet point towards the bedroom door when I sleep?

No. A bed with the feet pointing straight out the door is sometimes called the coffin position and is avoided in traditional feng shui. It also means anyone opening the door has a direct line of sight to you sleeping. Angling the bed toward a diagonal wall solves both concerns.

Does bedroom feng shui affect the resale value of a home?

Rarely on its own. Almost every bedroom item, the bed position, a mirror, a headboard wall, is furniture that the next owner can rearrange, so it does not usually change price. A structural item like a low ceiling beam across the whole room is the exception worth weighing before you buy.

What if the master bedroom cannot fit a commanding bed layout?

Some small bedrooms, especially in compact HDB flats or shoebox condo units, only have one wall long enough for the bed. Where that wall does not allow a perfect commanding position, prioritise a solid wall behind the headboard and a clear view of the door over a strict diagonal placement. A small compromise here matters far less than a structural flaw elsewhere in the unit.

Do I need a feng shui master to plan my bedroom?

Not for the layout checks in this guide, which apply to any bedroom and need nothing about you personally. Personal direction methods based on your birth data and kua number are a separate, more advanced layer best handled by a qualified feng shui master.
