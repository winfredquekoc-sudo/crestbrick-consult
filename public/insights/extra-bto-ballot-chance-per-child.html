The Extra BTO Ballot Chance Per Child: How Much It Really Helps

Published: 7 September 2026 · By Winfred Quek, Crestbrick Pte Ltd

From February 2027, first timer families with a child under 18 will receive one extra ballot chance per child in HDB's Build To Order exercises. If you have two young kids, that is potentially three chances in the draw instead of one. The natural question is how much that actually moves your odds, and the honest answer needs a bit of mechanics before any number means anything.

Timing: How BTO Balloting Actually Works Today

Every eligible application in a BTO exercise is entered into a ballot for the flat type and town it applies for. Each application is assigned a queue number once the ballot is drawn, and applications are then invited to select a unit in queue number order until the flats run out. More applications chasing the same number of units means a lower chance that any single application's number gets drawn early enough to matter. This is separate from the Parenthood Priority Scheme, which sets aside a portion of units for a distinct priority queue and is not what the extra ballot chance changes.

Extra chances are not a priority queue. They add more entries for your household into the same random draw. Parenthood Priority Scheme allocation is a separate mechanism and is unaffected by this change.

Timing: What One Extra Chance Per Child Does

Giving a household extra ballot entries raises its relative share of the total entries in the draw, and a bigger relative share means a better chance that at least one of that household's numbers lands early enough to matter. It does not guarantee a good queue number, it does not move a family ahead of a childless applicant with a better number, and it does not change how many units are available. HDB has not published the exact projected effect on odds for any specific project, and we are not going to invent a percentage that nobody has confirmed. What follows is a worked illustration only, built to show the mechanism, not a forecast for any real launch.

Illustrative example only, not an official figure

Say a hypothetical project draws 2,000 total ballot entries for 200 units, a 1 in 10 chance per entry in a purely random draw. A childless first timer household enters with 1 chance, roughly a 1 in 10 shot. A household with two children under 18 enters with 3 chances under the new rule instead of 1, so its share of the 2,000 total entries roughly triples relative to the childless household, though the exact odds also depend on how many other families bring extra child chances into the same draw. This is a simplified illustration to show the direction of the effect, not a real project's published odds.

Money: Should This Change When You Apply

If you are already planning to apply once your child arrives or turns a year older before the child's eighteenth birthday, the extra chance is a genuine, if modest, tailwind and there is no cost to using it. It should not be the deciding factor in whether to wait for February 2027 versus applying sooner in the November 2026 exercise, since a real project's site, price, and completion date usually matter more to your household budget than a modest odds improvement. See the current BTO application guide for the November exercise details while you weigh the two dates against each other.

Safety: What Stays The Same

Eligibility rules are unchanged: you still need to meet the household income ceiling, still need first timer or eligible second timer status, and the flat allocation process after a good queue number still runs on the normal selection process; see our HDB grants guide for the current eligibility and grant tables. Check HDB's own site for the exact commencement date and eligible child age cutoff before assuming this applies to your family, since the precise implementation details for a scheme this new can shift before it actually launches.

Timing: How This Fits Alongside The Parenthood Priority Scheme

Families with young children already have a separate route worth understanding on its own terms: the Parenthood Priority Scheme sets aside a share of units in each BTO project for eligible families with a child, and those units are allocated to that priority group before the remaining units go to the general pool. The extra ballot chance per child announced for February 2027 is a different lever entirely. It does not touch the size of the priority allocation, and it does not move a family from the general queue into the priority queue. A family can, in principle, benefit from both mechanisms at once: priority allocation gives access to a reserved slice of units, while extra ballot chances improve that family's odds within whichever pool, priority or general, its application actually sits in.

Confusing the two is the most common misunderstanding we hear from clients. If a family qualifies for the Parenthood Priority Scheme, that allocation mechanism does the heavier lifting for their odds. The extra ballot chance is a smaller, additive improvement on top, not a replacement for it, and not a route into a queue the family was not already eligible for.

Frequently Asked Questions

When does the extra ballot chance per child actually start?
HDB has said this applies from the February 2027 BTO sales exercise onward, not the November 2026 exercise. Confirm the exact starting exercise on HDB's own announcement before planning around it.
Does the extra chance apply per child or is there a cap?
The announced mechanic is one extra chance per child under 18. HDB's own page is the source to check for any cap on the number of children counted, since that detail can be refined before the scheme takes effect.
Does having more ballot chances mean I get a better queue number?
No. Each ballot entry still receives a queue number at random. Extra chances raise the odds that at least one of your entries lands a workable number, they do not guarantee a specific or better number.
