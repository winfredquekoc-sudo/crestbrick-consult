Pet rules in Singapore condos: what's actually allowed

By Winfred Quek, Associate Marketing Consultant · CEA R073319H · Crestbrick Pte Ltd (L31010886H) · Published 13 July 2026

Quick answer: There is no single national rule governing pets in Singapore condos. Each Management Corporation Strata Title writes its own house rules, usually covering which pet types are permitted, size or weight limits, breed restrictions, the number of pets allowed per unit, and where within the estate pets may be walked or must be carried. This means the same pet that is welcome in one development can be restricted or effectively barred in another. Separately, national rules on dog licensing and leashing apply everywhere regardless of what the MCST decides. If you own or plan to own a pet, checking the specific development's by laws before you buy or rent is essential, not optional.

Facts verified: 13 July 2026 · Pet policies differ by development and can change · Sources attributed below

I get this question more often than almost any other lifestyle related one from clients with pets: can I bring my dog to this condo? The honest answer is always the same, it depends entirely on the specific development, and there is no shortcut around checking. Unlike car parking allocation or common facility hours, which tend to follow broadly similar patterns across Singapore condos, pet policy is one of the areas where by laws genuinely diverge, sometimes sharply, between one development and its near identical neighbour a few streets away.

Why pet rules are set at the development level, not nationally

Under the Building Maintenance and Strata Management Act, each MCST has the authority to adopt house rules governing how residents use their units and common property, and pet ownership falls squarely within that authority because pets affect noise, common area cleanliness, and other residents' comfort in ways the MCST is entitled to manage. Unlike HDB, where a more standardised set of pet ownership conditions applies across the public housing stock, private condo developments each write their own rules through their management councils, subject to the Act's general framework.

This is why two condos across the road from each other can have meaningfully different pet policies, one permitting dogs up to a certain weight with no breed restriction, the other limiting pets to cats and small breeds only, or requiring specific approval for any dog above a defined size. Neither development is breaking any law by setting a stricter or looser policy than its neighbour, because there is no single national standard for private condo pet ownership to conform to.

The common categories of restriction

While the specific numbers vary, most Singapore condo pet policies address a similar set of questions, even if they answer them differently. Permitted species is the first layer, some developments restrict pets to conventional companion animals, cats, dogs, small caged animals, and exclude anything considered exotic or potentially disruptive. Size or weight limits are common for dogs specifically, since a larger dog is more likely to be perceived as a safety or noise concern by other residents in shared lifts and corridors.

Number of pets per unit is another frequent restriction, capping how many animals a single household may keep, partly to manage noise and partly to prevent a unit becoming an informal shelter or breeding operation. Some developments also specify where pets must be leashed or carried within common areas, often requiring dogs to be carried rather than walked through lobbies and lifts, and may designate or restrict specific green spaces within the estate for pet exercise or relief.

Can an MCST ban pets outright?

Some developments come very close to an outright ban. A handful of Singapore condos have adopted by laws restrictive enough that certain pet categories, particularly larger dog breeds, are effectively excluded, even where the rules are technically framed as size or breed restrictions rather than a blanket prohibition. If pet ownership matters to you, do not assume a development is pet friendly just because it has landscaped grounds or is marketed as family oriented.

Whether a specific development's rules amount to a practical ban depends entirely on how that MCST has worded its by laws, and this genuinely needs to be checked case by case rather than assumed from general impressions of the development or its marketing. A show flat brochure showing a resident walking a dog through a garden is not evidence of the actual pet policy, and I have seen buyers assume otherwise more than once.

What applies regardless of the MCST's own rules

Separate from whatever an individual MCST decides, national requirements around dog ownership apply across Singapore regardless of building type, including licensing obligations and leashing requirements in public areas. These sit on top of, not instead of, whatever the MCST's own house rules specify, so a compliant dog owner needs to satisfy both layers, the national requirements and the development's specific by laws, at the same time.

What to check before you buy or rent with a pet

  1. Request the current house rules in writing. Do not rely on what a seller's agent or a friendly neighbour tells you informally, since by laws can be amended at an AGM and verbal summaries are sometimes out of date.
  2. Ask specifically about your pet's category and size. A general answer of "pets are allowed" is not sufficient if your dog is close to a weight threshold or your breed is on a restricted list somewhere in the by laws.
  3. Understand the common area rules, not just the ownership rule. Where you can walk your pet, whether it must be carried in lifts, and which green spaces are pet accessible affects daily life as much as whether ownership itself is permitted.
  4. Check for recent amendments. Pet policy is one of the more commonly revisited items at AGMs, sometimes tightened after a specific incident, so a policy that was permissive a few years ago may not still be.

What happens if a resident breaches the pet policy

Enforcement generally mirrors how MCSTs handle other by law breaches: an initial notice requesting compliance, followed by escalating fines for continued non compliance, and in persistent cases referral to the Strata Titles Boards for a binding order. Complaints from neighbours, noise, unleashed animals in common areas, unauthorised pet types, are the usual trigger for enforcement action, so a considerate pet owner who keeps to the rules rarely encounters friction even in a stricter development.

Frequently asked questions

Is there a national law on pets in Singapore condos?

No single national rule governs pet ownership across all Singapore condos. Instead, each MCST adopts its own house rules or by laws under the Building Maintenance and Strata Management Act, which can cover permitted pet types, size or weight limits, breed restrictions, the number of pets per unit, and where pets may be walked or exercised within the estate. Separately, all dog owners across Singapore must comply with national licensing and leashing requirements regardless of the MCST's own rules.

Can an MCST ban pets entirely?

Some developments do have stricter by laws that heavily restrict or effectively prohibit certain categories of pets, particularly larger dogs, while permitting smaller animals such as cats or small dogs under weight limits. Whether a specific development bans pets outright or simply restricts them depends entirely on that MCST's adopted by laws, so this must be checked development by development rather than assumed.

What common pet restrictions do Singapore condos have?

The most frequent restrictions cover permitted species, weight or size limits for dogs, the number of pets allowed per unit, and rules on how pets move through common areas such as being carried in lifts or restricted to designated green spaces. The specific thresholds differ significantly between developments, so a policy that seems permissive in one condo may be considerably stricter in another nearby.

What should I check before buying a condo unit if I have a pet?

Request the current house rules in writing from the managing agent or seller's agent, ask specifically whether your pet's type, breed and size are permitted, and check whether the rules were recently amended, since pet policy is commonly revisited at AGMs. Do not rely on general impressions from marketing materials or informal conversations with residents.

What happens if I breach my condo's pet policy?

Enforcement typically follows the same pattern as other by law breaches: an initial notice requesting compliance, escalating fines for continued non compliance, and in persistent cases referral to the Strata Titles Boards for a binding order. Complaints from neighbours are the most common trigger for enforcement action.
