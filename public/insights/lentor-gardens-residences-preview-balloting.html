How balloting works on 18 July

Balloting confuses first time buyers because the word sounds like a lottery you can lose. It is not. Balloting is simply the developer's method for setting a fair order of selection when demand for a launch may exceed the units released at once. Rather than a physical queue where the person who camps overnight gets first pick, registered buyers are each assigned a number by a ballot draw, and units are selected in that drawn order.

The sequence on the day usually runs like this.

  1. Registered buyers gather at the sales gallery on balloting day, having already registered their interest and details beforehand.
  2. A ballot is drawn, assigning each buyer or group a selection order number.
  3. When your number is called, you step up to the unit selection chart and choose from the units still available at that moment.
  4. You pay the booking fee, typically by cheque or the developer's accepted method, to secure the chosen unit and trigger the Option to Purchase process.
  5. You proceed to the standard private purchase timeline: exercising the option, paying stamp duty, and drawing down your loan progressively as construction proceeds.

The practical lesson is that your queue number decides the order you choose in, not whether you get a unit at all. If you are drawn early, you have the full range to pick from. If you are drawn later, popular stacks may already be gone, which is exactly why a ranked shortlist of three or four acceptable units beats fixating on one. The progressive loan drawdown that follows a booking is covered in the progressive payment guide.

What to prepare before the preview

Readiness was the whole game here. The buyers who did well on 18 July did their work before 4 July. Here is the preparation that matters, roughly in order, and it applies just as well to the next launch.

Sort your financing and get an In Principle Approval

Confirm your real budget, then get an In Principle Approval from a bank. This is a preliminary written indication of how much the bank will lend you, based on your income, existing debts and your Total Debt Servicing Ratio. It is not the same as a firm loan, but it tells you whether the unit you want is actually within reach, and it lets you book with confidence rather than committing a booking fee and discovering later that financing falls short. Having it in hand also means you can act fast on balloting day. The full financing picture, including the 75% loan cap and TDSR, is in the loan and financing guide.

Set aside the cash and CPF you will need on the day

You commit money at booking, so the funds must be ready before booking day, not arranged afterward. That means the booking fee, the balance of your downpayment within the option timeline, and Buyer's Stamp Duty. If you are a second property buyer, you also need to plan for ABSD, which materially changes the cash required. Map your CPF Ordinary Account use and your cash position against the actual price once it is published. The illustrative cash and income needs are set out in the affordability guide.

Decide your unit priorities in advance

Once the floor plans and price list are out, rank your choices: preferred bedroom type, then acceptable stacks, facings and floor bands, with a clear sense of the price you will pay for each. Go in with a first choice and at least two or three backups. Because balloting fixes the order of selection, a shortlist protects you if your top pick is taken before your number is called. Knowing your priorities also stops you overpaying for a premium stack in the heat of the day.

Register your interest ahead of a viewing

To take part in balloting, you need to be registered with a salesperson and on the list before the day. Registering early also means you are kept informed of any schedule confirmation or change, get priority showflat appointment slots, and receive the official price list and floor plans as soon as they are released. It costs nothing and commits you to nothing, but it puts you in the room rather than outside it.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Treating the preview as optional. The price list and floor plans only existed from 4 July. Skipping the preview and turning up cold on balloting day meant choosing a unit without having properly studied it.
  • Arranging financing after booking. The booking fee commits you. Sort the In Principle Approval first so you are not exposed if the loan comes back short.
  • Fixating on one unit. Balloting decides your selection order, not your unit. Without backups, an early sell out of your one choice leaves you with nothing to fall back on.
  • Forgetting ABSD on a second property. For a second property buyer, ABSD is a large, separate cash cost that must be planned before the day, not discovered on it.
