Mount Pleasant: the Old Police Academy site's masterplan future

By Winfred Quek · 8 minute read · Published 13 July 2026

By Winfred Quek, Associate Marketing Consultant · CEA R073319H · Crestbrick Pte Ltd (L31010886H) · Published 13 July 2026

Quick answer: The former Old Police Academy site at Mount Pleasant, in the Thomson planning area, has been identified in URA's longer term planning as a site for future rejuvenation, with a conservation led direction that would retain and repurpose some of its heritage buildings rather than clear the site entirely. It sits near the Thomson East Coast Line, close to a planned Mount Pleasant station, and near Marymount MRT, a Circle Line interchange, giving the area genuine dual line access. This is a real long term positive for the surrounding Thomson and Marymount property market, but there is no confirmed development timeline, unit count, or launch date publicly available, so treat it as a signal of direction rather than a catalyst you can time a purchase around.

Facts verified: 13 July 2026 · Development dates and future launch details are official targets or estimates and subject to change · Sources attributed below

Clients occasionally ask me about the Old Police Academy site after seeing it mentioned in a masterplan discussion or a property forum thread, usually with a version of the same question: is this the next Bidadari? It is a fair question, because URA does sometimes take an underused institutional site and turn it into a genuinely desirable estate. But it is also a question worth answering honestly rather than with hype, because this site is at an earlier and less certain stage than people assume.

What the Old Police Academy site actually is

The Old Police Academy sits within the Mount Pleasant area, part of the broader Thomson planning region on the city fringe, close to MacRitchie Reservoir and the green belt that runs through the centre of the island. It served as a police training facility, and like a number of former institutional sites across Singapore, its long term future has been the subject of URA planning attention as the surrounding area continues to develop. Unlike a bare plot of land, a site like this carries physical heritage, including older buildings with architectural character, which shapes how planners think about its eventual use.

The masterplan direction: conservation, not clearance

What distinguishes this site from a typical greenfield or cleared redevelopment plot is the signalled direction toward heritage conservation. Rather than a wholesale demolition and rebuild, the planning intent that has been indicated is one that retains and repurposes elements of the site's existing character, blending old and new rather than replacing one with the other entirely. This is consistent with how URA has approached a number of heritage sensitive precincts elsewhere in Singapore, preserving built heritage as an asset that gives an estate identity rather than treating it as an obstacle to maximum density.

For a buyer, this distinction matters because a conservation led precinct tends to develop differently from a purely new build estate. It usually means lower density than a cleared site might otherwise support, a stronger emphasis on character and green space, and a longer, more deliberate planning and execution process, since conservation work is inherently slower than clearing a site and building from scratch.

The transport context that makes this interesting

Location is what elevates this from a purely heritage story to a property relevant one. The area sits near the Thomson East Coast Line, with Mount Pleasant identified as a station on that line, and Marymount MRT, an interchange with the Circle Line, is also within the vicinity. That combination, direct Thomson East Coast Line access plus proximity to a Circle Line interchange, gives the area a genuinely strong transport case relative to many other city fringe pockets, connecting toward both the northern and eastern parts of the rail network as well as into the city via the Circle Line's loop.

This is the same dynamic that has driven value in other well connected estates across Singapore, station access multiplying the practical reach of a location, which I unpack in more general terms in my MRT distance and property value guide. Mount Pleasant, on paper, sits on the favourable side of that relationship.

What this could mean for Thomson and Marymount property nearby

The honest, qualitative case is this: a heritage led rejuvenation of a well located institutional site, once it eventually proceeds, tends to lift the profile of its immediate surroundings, in the same way that other transformed estates elsewhere on the island have anchored renewed interest in previously overlooked pockets. Existing private developments and HDB estates around Upper Thomson, Marymount and the broader Thomson corridor stand to benefit from any uplift in area profile, additional amenities, and improved public realm that a well executed rejuvenation would bring, on top of the connectivity the area already has through Marymount MRT today.

For a deeper look at how a well connected, well planned Thomson fringe pocket is already performing on its own merits, independent of this specific site, see my Upper Thomson and Bishan property guide.

The honest caveats

There is no confirmed timeline. The Old Police Academy site appears in longer range planning discussions as a site earmarked for eventual rejuvenation. That is a genuine signal of intent, but it is a considerable distance from a confirmed launch date, a named developer, an approved masterplan for the specific site, or any unit count. Development dates and any future launch details discussed in relation to sites like this are, at best, official targets or estimates, and they are subject to change, sometimes by years. Do not treat any specific date you hear secondhand as confirmed.

A second honest caveat is that conservation led sites, precisely because they are more complex to plan and execute than a cleared plot, can take considerably longer to move from signalled intent to actual construction than a standard greenfield site would. That is not a criticism of the approach, which usually produces a more distinctive and durable estate, but it does mean patience is required from anyone hoping to see this site's eventual character shape the surrounding market.

How to actually think about buying nearby now

  1. Do not pay a premium purely for a masterplan story with no confirmed date. If a listing nearby is priced meaningfully above comparable Thomson or Marymount stock on the strength of this site alone, that is speculation, not underwriting.
  2. Value what already exists. Marymount MRT and the Circle Line connection are real today. The Thomson East Coast Line corridor is real today. Underwrite your purchase on those fundamentals first.
  3. Treat the Old Police Academy site as a long dated call option, not a certainty. If it proceeds broadly as signalled, it is a genuine future positive for the area. If it does not, or proceeds on a very long timeline, you should still be happy with the purchase on its existing merits.
  4. Benchmark against comparable city fringe pockets. My best districts to invest in Singapore guide puts the wider Thomson corridor into context against its peers.

Frequently asked questions

What is the Old Police Academy site at Mount Pleasant?

The Old Police Academy is a former police training site in the Mount Pleasant area, within the broader Thomson planning area, that has been identified in URA's longer term planning as a site for rejuvenation. Unlike a typical redevelopment site cleared for new towers, the direction signalled is conservation led, retaining and repurposing some of the site's heritage buildings and character rather than a wholesale demolition and rebuild.

Which MRT stations serve the Mount Pleasant area?

The Mount Pleasant area sits near the Thomson East Coast Line, with Mount Pleasant as a planned station on that line and Marymount MRT, an interchange with the Circle Line, also within the vicinity. This dual line access, direct connectivity along the Thomson East Coast Line corridor plus an interchange nearby, is the core transport case for the area.

When will the Old Police Academy site actually be redeveloped?

There is no fixed, confirmed timeline publicly available for when the site will be developed or what will ultimately be built there. It appears in URA's longer range planning as a site earmarked for future rejuvenation, which is a real signal of intent but a considerable distance from a launch date, a developer, or a confirmed unit count. Treat any specific timeline you hear as speculation until URA or a developer confirms it officially.

Should I buy near Mount Pleasant now hoping the masterplan drives up prices?

I would not buy purely on that thesis. A masterplan signal for a specific site is a genuine long term positive for the surrounding area, but it is not a substitute for underwriting the purchase on its own merits, connectivity, unit quality, price versus comparable Thomson and Marymount stock, and your own holding horizon. If those numbers work without the masterplan story, the masterplan is a bonus. If they only work because of the masterplan story, that is a much riskier bet on an unconfirmed timeline.
