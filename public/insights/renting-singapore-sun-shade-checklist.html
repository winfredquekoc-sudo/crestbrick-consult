Renting in Singapore: what to check about sun and shade before you sign

Published: 8 September 2026 · By Winfred Quek, Crestbrick Pte Ltd

A rental decision usually comes down to one viewing, at one hour, before a year or two year lease. You check the water pressure, the aircon, the mould, the noise. Sun and shade rarely make the list, yet they shape daily life in a rental just as much: where laundry actually dries, how warm a bedroom feels by evening, whether a work desk faces a glaring window all afternoon, and when a child's room gets bright enough to disrupt a nap.

None of this needs a site visit at every hour of the day to check. This article walks through what to look at for laundry, a west facing bedroom, working from home, and kids' rooms, then a short checklist and a two minute way to check any address before you sign.

Laundry drying

Where you can hang laundry, and how much direct sun that spot gets, depends on which direction it faces and what stands nearby. A laundry area that catches a few hours of direct sun most days will dry clothes faster than one that sits mostly in shade from a neighbouring block or an overhang. If drying laundry outdoors matters to your household, ask which direction the service yard or balcony faces, and check how much of the day it is likely to see direct sun for the months you will actually be living there, since this shifts across the year.

This is easy to miss on a single viewing, since most viewings happen at whatever hour the agent could arrange, not necessarily the hour you would normally do laundry. A service yard that looks perfectly sunny during a late morning viewing may sit in a neighbouring block's shadow for most of the afternoon, or the other way round. Checking the address for the specific hours you would actually use that space removes the guesswork.

Afternoon heat load in a west facing bedroom

A home facing west receives direct sun during the late afternoon, roughly from around 2pm through to sunset, because that is when the sun swings low enough in the sky to shine straight into a west facing window. This falls during the hottest part of the day, so a west facing bedroom can feel warmer through the late afternoon and evening than a room facing another direction. There is no official Singapore data measuring temperature by unit facing, so treat this as a description of when direct sun reaches the room, not a fixed number. Curtains, blinds, or a window film can reduce how much of that afternoon sun actually reaches the bed or the rest of the room, and checking whether these are already installed is worth doing at the viewing.

Working from home and glare

If part of your routine involves sitting at a desk near a window for several hours, it is worth knowing which direction that window faces and when the sun sits behind or in front of your screen. A desk facing a west window can mean strong glare on a monitor or during a video call in the mid to late afternoon, while the same desk facing north or south tends to see steadier, less direct light through most of the day. This is a matter of comfort and screen visibility, not a claim about the unit itself, and it is easy enough to check before you commit to a desk position, or before you commit to the unit at all.

Kids' rooms and nap times

A child's room that faces the morning or afternoon sun directly can become noticeably brighter at nap time than a room facing another direction, depending on the hour and the season. Blackout curtains solve most of this, but knowing which direction a room faces before you move in means you can plan for that from day one rather than discovering it during the first difficult nap. The same logic applies to a home office, a study corner, or anywhere else where light at a specific hour actually matters to your routine.

If you are choosing which bedroom in a unit becomes the nursery or the child's room, checking the facing of each bedroom separately before you decide can save a round of furniture moving later. Two bedrooms in the same rental can face different directions even in a fairly ordinary layout, so do not assume they are the same just because they sit side by side.

Where possible, ask to see the unit again around the hour you would normally be working, rather than only at the hour the first viewing happened to fall on. If a second viewing is not practical, a quick online check for the address, at the actual hour and month you care about, is the next best thing before you commit to a desk position or a whole tenancy.

A tenant's checklist before you sign

  • Ask the landlord or agent which direction the unit faces. Get the direction of the main windows and any balcony or service yard.
  • Check it yourself, do not only rely on the listing. A stated facing is not always precise, especially for an irregularly shaped block.
  • View at more than one hour if you can. Our rental viewing checklist covers what else to check room by room, sun facing included.
  • Search the exact address online before you sign. Two minutes is usually enough to see the sun direction, the floor level effect, and any nearby shadow for the months ahead.
  • Match the check to your own routine. A household that dries laundry outdoors, works from home, or has young children benefits most from checking specific hours and rooms, not just a general facing.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I find out which way a rental unit faces before signing?

Ask the landlord or agent directly, and check the exact address yourself as well, since a stated facing on a listing is not always precise. Looking at the unit's actual windows and balcony during a site visit, alongside an online check, gives a more reliable picture.

Does a west facing rental unit affect laundry drying or aircon use?

Sun exposure affects how much direct light and afternoon heat reach a room, which can influence how a household chooses to dry laundry, use blinds, or run the aircon. There is no official measurement tying this to a fixed cost, so check the specific unit's exposure and decide what suits your household.

Should I bother checking sun facing for a rental I am only staying in for a year?

It still takes only a couple of minutes and can matter for daily comfort during that year, such as where a home desk faces, a child's nap schedule, or where laundry gets hung, so it is worth the small effort even for a shorter lease.
