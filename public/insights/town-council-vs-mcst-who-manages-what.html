Town Council vs MCST: Who Manages What

Published: 7 September 2026 · By Winfred Quek, Crestbrick Pte Ltd

A Town Council runs an HDB estate's shared spaces and collects Service and Conservancy Charges under its own law. An MCST runs a condo's shared property and collects maintenance fees and a sinking fund under the Building Maintenance and Strata Management Act. Your first condo means dealing with an MCST, a different body, for the first time.

Timing: two different systems for two different property types

If you have only ever lived in an HDB flat, your experience of estate management has been shaped entirely by your Town Council, an elected body of grassroots representatives responsible for the common areas, lifts, corridors and grounds of your block and estate. A condo works on an entirely separate system. Each condo development has its own MCST, formed automatically once the development is completed and units are sold, made up of all the unit owners collectively, who then elect a Management Council to run day to day matters.

There is no overlap between the two. Your Town Council has no authority over your future condo, and an MCST has no authority over an HDB estate. When you upgrade, you are not dealing with a bigger or fancier version of the Town Council. You are dealing with a completely different structure, with different rules, different meetings and a different by laws framework.

The core distinction: A Town Council is a public, elected body covering a whole estate under HDB and Ministry of National Development oversight. An MCST is a private body made up only of the owners in that one development, governed by the Building Maintenance and Strata Management Act.

Money: S&CC versus maintenance fees and the sinking fund

Both systems collect money from residents for upkeep, but the structure is different. Your Town Council sets Service and Conservancy Charges, commonly called S&CC, which fund the maintenance of lifts, common lighting, cleaning, and other shared upkeep across the estate. S&CC rates are set by the Town Council covering your block and can vary between estates and flat types.

An MCST collects two separate pools of money from owners. Monthly maintenance fees cover the day to day running of the development, such as security, cleaning, landscaping and utilities for common areas. A separate sinking fund, contributed to on top of maintenance fees, is built up over years specifically for large, infrequent works such as repainting the facade, replacing lifts, or major structural repairs. HDB estates do not have an equivalent sinking fund structure sitting alongside S&CC in the same way.

Safety: AGMs, by laws and what each body can actually do about a dispute

An MCST's Annual General Meeting is where owners approve the budget, elect the Management Council, and vote on by law changes that affect everyone in the development, from pet policies to renovation rules. Attending your first AGM as a new condo owner is worth doing, since decisions made there directly affect your monthly costs and what you are and are not allowed to do in your own unit. There is no direct HDB equivalent to this owner voting structure, since Town Councils are run by elected public representatives rather than a meeting of all flat owners.

When something goes wrong, the two systems also differ in what they can do. A Town Council can act on estate wide maintenance issues and enforce estate rules, but disputes between individual flat owners, such as noise or renovation disagreements, generally go through mediation or other channels rather than the Town Council itself. An MCST has somewhat broader powers over its own development, including the ability to enforce by laws against a specific owner, but for disputes between owners that the MCST cannot resolve, the Strata Titles Boards are the specialist forum set up specifically to handle strata disputes.

Know where to escalate: A Town Council dispute usually starts with the Town Council office or an MP's Meet the People session. An MCST dispute usually starts with the Management Council, then the Strata Titles Boards if it cannot be resolved internally. They are not interchangeable.

Safety: what neither body can do

Neither a Town Council nor an MCST can resolve a private tenancy dispute between a landlord and tenant, and neither sets property prices or approves individual sale transactions. Both are focused on shared property and common charges, not on the private legal relationships between residents or owners. If your dispute is actually about a lease, a sale contract, or money owed between two private parties, you will need a different avenue, such as the Small Claims Tribunal or a lawyer, rather than expecting the Town Council or MCST to step in.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does my Town Council have any authority over my new condo?

No. A Town Council only manages HDB estates. Once you move into a condo, your shared property is managed entirely by that development's MCST under the Building Maintenance and Strata Management Act, a completely separate system.

Why does my condo have both a maintenance fee and a sinking fund, when my old flat only had S&CC?

The maintenance fee covers day to day running costs, while the sinking fund is built up over years for large, infrequent works like lift replacement or facade repainting. HDB's S&CC funds ongoing estate upkeep but does not run a parallel sinking fund structure the way an MCST does.

Where do I go if I have a dispute my condo's MCST will not resolve?

The Strata Titles Boards are the specialist forum for strata related disputes an MCST cannot resolve internally, such as disagreements over by laws or common property. This is different from an HDB estate, where estate level issues are generally handled through the Town Council or an MP's Meet the People session instead.
