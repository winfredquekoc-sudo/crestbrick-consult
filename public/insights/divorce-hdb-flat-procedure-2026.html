Divorcing Couple and the HDB Flat: Exact Procedure, CPF Refund, and Timeline

By Winfred Quek · CEA R073319H · PropNex Realty

Quick answer: Divorcing Singapore couples handle their HDB flat in one of 3 ways: sell on open market and split proceeds, one spouse buys out the other, or defer the sale until children finish school. All require a court order. CPF used must be refunded to each party's CPF OA from proceeds. Court-ordered transfers are exempt from BSD, SSD, and ABSD.

The 3 Options for HDB on Divorce

OptionHow It WorksWho It SuitsKey Requirement
1. Sell on open marketBoth parties agree to sell; proceeds split per court orderNeither party can/wants to retain; clean breakHDB MOP must be met (5 years from key collection); court order
2. Buyout (transfer)One spouse pays the other's share and takes sole ownershipOne spouse wants to stay in flat with childrenRetaining spouse must be HDB-eligible; financing required
3. Deferred saleCourt orders sale deferred — typically until youngest child turns 18 or completes tertiary educationChildren need stability; school proximity mattersCourt discretion; strong child welfare case required

The Step-by-Step Process

  1. File for divorce at the Family Justice Court — can be uncontested (simplified) or contested
  2. Parties or court determine how the HDB flat is to be dealt with (at ancillary matters hearing)
  3. Court issues the Divorce Judgment with ancillary orders including HDB flat division
  4. Once the Interim Judgment is made Final (3 months after Interim), present court order to HDB
  5. HDB reviews and approves the transaction (4–8 weeks)
  6. If selling: appoint agent, list, complete sale. If transferring: legal completion with lawyers
  7. CPF refunds processed at completion; each party's CPF OA topped up automatically

CPF Refund: The Numbers That Surprise People

Every dollar withdrawn from CPF for the HDB — downpayment, monthly instalments — must be returned to the respective CPF OA, plus accrued interest at 2.5% p.a. from the date of each withdrawal.

ItemExample (Wife)
CPF principal withdrawn over 6 years$150,000
Accrued interest (2.5% p.a., ~6 yrs)~$23,500
Total CPF refund due to Wife's OA$173,500
Wife's share of sale proceeds (50% of $600K)$300,000
Less: CPF refund to her own OA−$173,500
Wife's cash in hand$126,500

The $173,500 is not lost — it returns to Wife's CPF OA and can be used for a future property purchase or retirement. But it is not cash in hand.

Stamp Duty Exemption

Property transfers executed pursuant to a court order under Women's Charter s.112 are fully exempt from:

  • Buyer's Stamp Duty (BSD)
  • Seller's Stamp Duty (SSD)
  • Additional Buyer's Stamp Duty (ABSD)

This exemption applies to the transfer between the spouses. When the flat is sold to a third-party buyer, normal BSD applies to that buyer.

HDB Eligibility After Divorce: Solo Owner Rules

To retain the HDB flat as sole owner after divorce:

  • SC aged 35+ can buy or retain under the Single Singapore Citizen Scheme (resale flat only)
  • SC with child(ren) in their custody can retain under the Public Scheme (no age restriction)
  • PR spouse who gets sole custody cannot retain HDB alone — must have an SC family member on the flat or sell
