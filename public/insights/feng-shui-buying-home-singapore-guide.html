Feng Shui When Buying a Home in Singapore (2026): A Practical Guide

By Winfred Quek · CEA R073319H · Crestbrick

Quick answer: Feng shui for a Singapore home comes down to three areas: the main door, the kitchen and the bedroom. The main door should open to a bright, uncluttered space and not face a lift, a downward staircase or the stove. The kitchen should keep fire and water apart. The bedroom should let the bed sit in a commanding position, away from a beam. Before you buy, separate structural taboos, which you cannot change, from cosmetic ones, which you can. Most of these checks also improve daily living and resale.

What does feng shui actually change when you buy a home?

Feng shui is a system for arranging a home so that energy, called qi, flows well. In Singapore it guides many buyers when they choose between two units or two stacks. You do not have to believe in qi for it to matter. A home that reads well by feng shui usually reads well to the next buyer too, and a home with an obvious taboo speaks to a smaller pool of buyers when you sell.

Classical feng shui puts most of its weight on three things: the main door, the kitchen and the bedroom. Practitioners such as Grand Master Tan Khoon Yong of Way Fengshui Group teach that the main door is the single most important feature, because it is where qi enters. This guide walks through those three areas, then the shape of the floor plan, and reads each one on what actually affects living in the home and selling it later.

How do you read the main door?

Stand at the door and look out. Tradition calls the space in front the bright hall. It should be open and well lit, so good energy can gather before it enters. Feng shui masters flag a short list of things the door should not face: a lift or a neighbour's door directly opposite, which is read as two mouths meeting head to head; a staircase or escalator going down, read as wealth rolling away; and the stove or kitchen in a straight line, where fire clashes with incoming qi. A road or long corridor aiming straight at the door, known as a T junction, is treated as a poison arrow.

Most of this also has a plain reading. A door opposite a busy lift means foot traffic, noise and less privacy. A door at the end of a long corridor catches wind and stares. The full checklist, and which items you can screen with a divider or a plant, is in the main door feng shui guide.

What makes a floor plan good or bad in feng shui?

Square and rectangular layouts are the ideal. Feng shui reads an irregular shape as having a missing corner, where a part of the home, and the life area it governs, sits outside the walls. Two placements draw the most caution: a toilet in the centre of the home, treated as pollution at the heart, and a bedroom sitting directly above the kitchen. A bedroom door facing a bathroom door across a corridor is a minor, fixable concern.

The shape of a home is structural. You cannot renovate away a missing corner or move a central toilet in an HDB flat. That is why the plan is worth studying before you commit. The full list of layouts to favour and avoid, including how to read the view outside your windows, is in the floor plan feng shui guide.

Where should the kitchen and stove sit?

The kitchen is about the balance of fire and water. The stove is fire. The sink and fridge are water. Feng shui asks you to keep them apart, so avoid a stove placed directly beside or facing the sink or the fridge. Side by side or at a right angle is fine. The stove should also not face the main door, a toilet or a bedroom door, and it should have a solid wall behind it. Master Cecil Lee's well known stove guidelines set out the same idea in seven points.

Kitchen items are usually fixable in a renovation, so they weigh less at the point of purchase than the door or the plan. The full set, with the wood element fix for a stove and sink that face each other, is in the kitchen and stove feng shui guide.

How should the bedroom be laid out?

The core idea is the commanding position. Place the bed so you can see the door without lying in a straight line with it, ideally on the wall diagonal to the entrance, with a solid wall behind the headboard. Avoid a bed under a beam, a mirror that faces the bed, and a headboard on a wall shared with a toilet. Your feet should not point straight out of the door.

Almost all of this is within your control, since you place your own furniture, so it rarely affects resale. It does affect sleep. Seeing the door and having a solid wall behind you are calming, which is why the rule survives. The full bedroom layout, including how to handle a beam you cannot remove, is in the bedroom feng shui guide.

What does this look like for a real buyer?

Wei Ling and Marcus are Singapore citizens buying their first home. They shortlist a 4 room resale flat in Anchorvale, Sengkang, on the eighth floor. At the viewing they run the three checks.

  • Main door. It opens to a short corridor, with the lift lobby around the corner rather than straight ahead. No downward staircase in front. Pass.
  • Kitchen. The stove sits on the wall beside the sink, at a right angle, with about a metre between them. Fire and water are not facing off. Pass.
  • Bedroom. The master bedroom fits a bed on the wall diagonal to the door, with a solid wall behind the headboard and no beam above. Pass.
  • One flag. The common bathroom door faces the second bedroom door across the corridor. That is minor and fixable with a curtain or by keeping both doors shut, not a structural fault.

Read on the investor lens, the unit carries no hard taboo that would shrink its future buyer pool, and the layout is genuinely liveable. The single flag is cosmetic. They proceed with confidence rather than superstition.

Does feng shui affect resale value in Singapore?

There is no fixed feng shui discount or premium. What is real is demand. A meaningful share of Singapore buyers care about feng shui, so a unit with an obvious taboo, a main door facing the lift or a missing corner, speaks to fewer of them. Fewer interested buyers can mean a longer time on the market or a softer final price. That is a marketability effect, not a mystical one.

This is why the fixable versus structural split matters so much. A stove you can move or a mirror you can shift does not follow the home to the next buyer, so it does not weigh on resale. A door facing or a floor plan shape does. When you are choosing between units, spend your feng shui attention on the things that are built in.

The one line to remember: weigh structural taboos hard, treat cosmetic ones lightly. A door facing a lift or a missing corner stays with the home. A bed or a stove position leaves with the furniture.

Frequently asked questions

What are the three most important feng shui areas when buying a home in Singapore?

Traditional feng shui focuses on the main door, the kitchen and the bedroom. The main door is where energy enters, the kitchen is linked to health and wealth, and the bedroom governs rest. Almost every viewing check comes back to these three areas.

Does a home with bad feng shui sell for less in Singapore?

There is no fixed discount. What is real is the buyer pool: a unit with an obvious taboo, such as a main door facing the lift, appeals to fewer feng shui conscious buyers, which can slow a resale. Layout, light and noise move value far more reliably than any single feng shui rule.

Can you fix bad feng shui, or is it set by the layout?

Some items are cosmetic and fixable, like a mirror facing the bed or clutter at the entrance. Others are structural, like a missing corner or a main door that faces a staircase. The structural items are the ones to weigh before you buy, because you cannot move them later.

Is feng shui the same for an HDB flat and a condo?

The core ideas are the same: the main door, the kitchen and the bedroom. The specifics differ. HDB buyers watch the lift, the rubbish chute and the corridor, while condo buyers watch the facing of the block, sharp building edges opposite the unit, and water features.

Do I need my birth date or kua number to choose a home?

Personal direction methods based on your birth data are a separate and more advanced layer. They are best handled by a qualified feng shui master. The layout checks in this guide do not need your birth date and apply to any buyer.

Does the floor number matter in feng shui?

Traditional feng shui does not rank floors by luck, though some buyers avoid the number 4 for cultural reasons. In practice a higher floor changes light, wind and noise, which matter for daily living and resale. Choose the floor for comfort first.

Should I walk away from a home that fails one feng shui check?

Not always. Weigh whether the item is fixable or structural, and how much it affects daily living. One minor, fixable flag is rarely a reason to drop an otherwise strong home, while a hard structural taboo deserves more caution.
