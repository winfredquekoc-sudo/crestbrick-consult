Rightsizing and Retirement

Preparing to rightsize from private property to an HDB flat: the process checklist

By Winfred Quek · 8 minute read · Published 25 August 2026

By Winfred Quek · CEA R073319H · Published 25 August 2026

Quick answer: Since 28 July 2026, the 15 month wait out period no longer stands between a private property owner and an HDB resale purchase, so that clock is not something you need to plan around anymore. What still applies is a separate 6 month rule, if you buy your HDB flat before selling your private property, you must dispose of the private property within 6 months of completion. Before either move, confirm your eligibility, including the Ethnic Integration Policy quota, apply for your HFE letter, and work out whether an HDB loan is even open to you given your ownership history. This guide is the process checklist, our wait out removal series and sequencing guide cover the rules and the economics in full detail.

Moving from private property to an HDB flat used to mean waiting out a fixed period before you were even eligible to buy. That rule is gone, but rightsizing still runs through a genuine process with its own eligibility checks, its own paperwork and its own deadlines, just not the one most people still assume applies. Our guides on the wait out period removal and on sequencing your sale and purchase cover the rules and the trade offs in depth. This guide is the practical checklist that sits alongside them, what to check, apply for and line up, in roughly the order you will need it.

Confirm the wait out period no longer applies to you

On 28 July 2026, the 15 month wait out period that used to apply to private property owners buying an HDB resale flat was removed with immediate effect. If you have been planning around an old 15 month figure, or heard about the rule secondhand, start by reading our guide to the removal so you know exactly who it covers and what has genuinely changed, rather than relying on outdated advice.

Check your eligibility to buy an HDB resale flat

Citizenship and household composition rules still apply to any HDB resale purchase, regardless of your ownership history. One rule that catches out buyers moving from private property specifically is the Ethnic Integration Policy, which sets ethnic quotas at the block and neighbourhood level. When a quota is close to full for your race, the pool of blocks open to you narrows, so it is worth checking the quota position on any specific block you are considering before you get attached to it. Our Ethnic Integration Policy guide explains how the quota works.

Apply for your HFE letter

The HDB Flat Eligibility letter is the mandatory first step before buying any HDB flat, new or resale, and it combines three checks into one: your eligibility to buy, your eligibility for CPF housing grants, and an HDB loan eligibility assessment. Apply for it early, since it is a prerequisite for viewing and applying for a resale flat, not a formality you complete afterward. Our HFE letter guide covers what the application needs and how long it takes.

Decide your sequencing: sell first or buy first

Whether you sell your private property first or buy your HDB flat first changes your stamp duty position, your financing and your risk of a gap between homes. Our guide on sequencing a private to HDB move works through both paths, including how Additional Buyer's Stamp Duty is treated on a buy first path if the sale completes in time. Read it before you commit to either sequence, since reversing course partway through is far more costly than deciding properly at the start.

If buying before selling: the 6 month disposal clock

If you choose to buy your HDB flat before your private property is sold, a rule separate from the old wait out period comes into play. You, your co applicants, your spouse and any listed occupier must dispose of all private property, anywhere in the world, within 6 months of the HDB purchase's completion. This rule is unaffected by the wait out removal and exists specifically to make a buy first sequence workable at all. Our 6 month disposal rule guide covers exactly what starts the clock, who counts, and what your options are if a sale falls through, and our guide on stamp duty on a buy first sequence covers how ABSD remission interacts with the same deadline.

Financing: HDB loan or bank loan

Do not assume an HDB loan is automatically open to you. Eligibility tightens considerably if you have recently owned private property, and for many rightsizers a bank loan ends up being the only realistic option. Check this early, since it affects your budget and your down payment structure well before you start viewing flats. Our HDB loan versus bank loan guide sets out the eligibility criteria in full.

What moving to HDB does to your equity

Selling a leveraged private property and buying an HDB flat outright, or with a much smaller loan, changes your monthly obligations and shifts a portion of your net worth out of a single leveraged asset. That is a genuine strategic question with its own worked numbers, and our equity to debt free HDB guide covers it properly. This checklist simply flags it as something to work through before you fix a budget for the HDB purchase, not after.

Paperwork and professional help

Engage a conveyancing lawyer for both sides of the move, the private property sale and the HDB purchase run on different processes with different documentation, and a lawyer who is coordinating both will catch timing issues a single transaction view would miss. Pull your CPF statement early too, since any CPF refund from the private property sale, and any CPF you intend to use on the HDB side, both need to be worked out well before completion rather than in the final weeks.

Frequently asked questions

Do I still need to wait out a period before buying an HDB resale flat after selling private property?

No. The 15 month wait out period was removed on 28 July 2026, so eligible private property owners can now buy a non subsidised HDB resale flat immediately, provided they are not taking an HDB housing loan for the purchase. Our guide on the wait out removal covers exactly who qualifies.

Can I buy an HDB resale flat before I sell my private property?

Yes, buying before you sell is allowed, but a separate rule requires you to dispose of all private property within 6 months of completing the HDB purchase. This 6 month disposal rule is unrelated to the wait out period and applies regardless of it. Our guide on the 6 month disposal rule covers what counts and what happens if you miss it.

Can I take an HDB loan if I currently own or used to own private property?

Not straightforwardly. HDB loan eligibility tightens considerably if you have recently owned private property, and a bank loan may be your only financing option. Our HDB loan versus bank loan guide covers the eligibility criteria in full.

Does the Ethnic Integration Policy quota apply if I am buying HDB resale as a former private owner?

Yes, the Ethnic Integration Policy quota applies to every HDB resale purchase regardless of the buyer's ownership history, and it can narrow which blocks are open to you depending on your race. Check the quota position on a specific block before you commit to it.

What happens if I cannot sell my private property within 6 months?

You would breach the 6 month disposal rule, which carries real consequences, so treat that deadline as fixed once you complete the HDB purchase rather than as a target to aim for. Our guide on the 6 month disposal rule covers what a realistic sale timeline looks like and what your options are if a sale falls through.
