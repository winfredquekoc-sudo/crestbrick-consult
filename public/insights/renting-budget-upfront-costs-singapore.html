The true upfront cost of renting in Singapore for the first time

By Winfred Quek · CEA R073319H · Published 27 August 2026

Quick answer: Before you get the keys, budget for four things: the security deposit (by convention one month's rent per year of lease), advance rental (commonly one month at signing), stamp duty on the tenancy agreement (0.4% of total rent for a lease of 4 years or less, tenant pays by convention), and, on a standard year long lease, usually no agent fee since the landlord pays that by convention. After you move in, budget separately for utilities account setup and ongoing aircon servicing, both of which catch first time tenants off guard.

Facts verified: August 2026 · Sources linked below

Key Takeaways

  • • The security deposit is conventionally one month's rent per year of lease, held separately from advance rent.
  • • Stamp duty on the tenancy agreement is 0.4% of the total rent for a lease of 4 years or less, and by convention the tenant pays it.
  • • On a standard lease of a year or more, tenants usually pay no agent commission at all, the landlord pays that by convention.
  • • Utilities account setup and ongoing aircon servicing are recurring costs first time tenants routinely underbudget.
  • • A minor repair clause in the lease can expose you to small, real costs early in the tenancy if something breaks.

Part 1 of this series mapped the whole rental journey. This part slows down on the one step that decides whether you can actually afford to start it: the cash you need before you are handed a key, and the recurring costs that follow once you are living there. Most of these figures are not guesswork, they follow either government rules or well established market conventions. Where a number genuinely varies, this guide says so plainly rather than inventing a precise figure that will not match your situation.

The four things you pay before you get keys

Security deposit

By market convention, the security deposit is one month's rent for a one year lease and two months' rent for a two year lease, held by the landlord separately from any advance rent. The tenancy agreement should state the exact amount, what it can be deducted against, and the timeline for its return after handover. I go through this clause in detail in the guide to tenancy agreement clauses.

Advance rental

Most leases also require the first month's rent paid in advance at signing, on top of the security deposit. This is a contractual convention rather than a statutory rule, so always confirm the exact figure with the landlord or agent before you commit funds.

Stamp duty on the tenancy agreement

According to IRAS, a lease of four years or less is charged stamp duty at 0.4% of the total rent over the whole lease term, not a single month. By convention the tenant pays this, though the agreement can allocate it differently, and it must be stamped within 14 days of signing through the IRAS e Stamping Portal. The full mechanics, including how the portal works, are in the guides to tenancy agreement stamp duty and e stamping a document with IRAS.

Agent fee, if any

On a standard residential lease of a year or more, the landlord conventionally pays the agent, commonly 0.5 to 1 month of rent, and the tenant pays nothing. Short leases and certain room only arrangements are the common exception, so ask directly before assuming either way. The full breakdown of who pays an agent and when is in the guide to property agent commission.

The ongoing costs people forget

The costs above get most of the attention because they are due upfront. These are the ones that show up after you move in.

  • Utilities account setup. You will need to open an account with SP Group for electricity and water. Some tenants, particularly those without a local credit history, may be asked for a deposit as part of that setup, so check the current requirement directly with SP Group before move in day.
  • Aircon servicing. Many tenancy agreements make the tenant responsible for regular servicing of the aircon units, commonly on a quarterly basis, at the tenant's own cost, with receipts sometimes required as proof before the deposit is returned. Confirm the exact obligation in your lease rather than assuming.
  • Minor repair clause exposure. Most leases set a cost cap per repair item that the tenant absorbs, with the landlord covering anything above it and any structural repair. The cap itself is negotiated into each lease and varies, so read it rather than guess at it.
Do not confuse an assumption with a quote. Every dollar figure in the worked table above starts from an assumed $3,000 rent chosen purely to demonstrate the arithmetic. Your actual security deposit, advance rental, and stamp duty will scale with your actual rent and lease term. Recalculate with your own numbers before you commit any cash.

Winfred's Take

First time tenants almost always budget correctly for the deposit and forget everything after it. The deposit and advance rental are obvious because someone tells you the number before you sign. Nobody hands you a bill for aircon servicing or a utilities deposit in advance, so it feels like a surprise even though it was always coming. Before you sign anything, ask the landlord or agent directly what your recurring obligations are under the lease, and get the answer in writing, not as a verbal reassurance.

Frequently asked questions

How much deposit do I need to rent in Singapore?

By market convention the security deposit is one month's rent for each year of the lease, so one month for a one year lease and two months for a two year lease, held separately from any advance rent. The tenancy agreement should state the exact amount and the conditions for its return.

Do I pay stamp duty when I rent in Singapore?

Yes. According to IRAS, a lease of four years or less is charged stamp duty at 0.4% of the total rent over the lease term. By convention the tenant pays it, and the agreement must be stamped with IRAS within 14 days of signing.

Do first time tenants pay agent commission in Singapore?

Usually not, on a standard lease of a year or more the landlord pays their agent, commonly 0.5 to 1 month of rent. Short leases and some room only arrangements can shift this, so confirm who pays before you engage anyone.

What upfront costs do first time tenants usually forget?

Opening a utilities account, aircon servicing once you move in, and exposure under the minor repair clause if something needs fixing early in the lease. None of these are large individually, but they add up if you have not budgeted for them.

Is the rent figure used in your cost table a real market rate?

No. The $3,000 monthly rent used in the worked example is an assumption chosen purely to demonstrate how the calculation works. It is not a market estimate for any specific area or unit type. Always check current listings and comparable transactions for the actual rent level in your target area.
