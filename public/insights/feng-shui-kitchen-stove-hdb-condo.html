Kitchen and Stove Feng Shui in an HDB or Condo (2026)

By Winfred Quek · CEA R073319H · Crestbrick

Quick answer: In feng shui, the stove is fire and the sink and fridge are water. Keep them apart: do not place the stove directly beside or facing the sink or fridge, and keep a solid wall behind the stove for backing. Side by side or at 90 degrees works well. If the stove must face the sink, a small plant bridges fire and water. Most kitchen items are fixable in a renovation, so they matter less at purchase than the main door or the floor plan.

Why does the kitchen matter in feng shui?

The kitchen is where a home feeds itself, so traditional feng shui ties it to health and to wealth built up over time. The main door is about what comes in. The kitchen is about what a household keeps. That is why it sits alongside the main door and the bedroom as one of the big three checks buyers run, a framework covered in full in the feng shui home buying guide.

Two elements dominate a kitchen: fire, from the stove, and water, from the sink and the fridge. Traditional feng shui does not ask you to remove either. It asks you to keep them from clashing, using distance, angle or a simple fix, and to give the stove a clear, supported position. Almost everything below is about placement, not the brand of hob or the countertop.

What is the fire and water clash?

Fire and water are opposing elements. The stove carries fire. The sink and the fridge carry water, since both hold or move water and cold. When the stove sits directly beside the sink or the fridge, or faces either one head on, the two elements are read as clashing rather than balancing each other.

This also happens to be sound kitchen design. A stove too close to a sink means water can splash onto a hot hob, which is a safety issue as much as a feng shui one. Keeping some distance also gives you a cleaner workflow between prepping at the sink and cooking at the stove.

Where should the stove not face?

Beyond the sink and the fridge, three more sightlines matter. The stove should not face the main door directly, since fire meeting incoming qi head on is read as a clash. It should not face a toilet door, which pairs fire with what feng shui treats as an unclean function. And it should not face a bedroom door, since the bedroom is a place of rest, not heat.

There is also a vertical check buyers often miss. A stove should not sit directly under a toilet on the floor above. In an HDB block or a condo with a repeating floor plan, you can usually work this out from the unit above's layout, or by asking at the viewing. It is a question about the stack, not something a renovation can fix, so weigh it the way you would weigh any item in the floor plan feng shui guide.

Does the stove need a solid wall behind it?

Yes. Traditional feng shui calls this backing: a stove should have a solid wall behind it, not a window, an open doorway or thin air. The wall is read as support for the fire element, the same way a solid wall behind a bed supports the sleeper in the bedroom.

The same logic extends to the person cooking. The cook should be able to see the kitchen entrance while at the stove, rather than stand with their back to it. This is the commanding position again, the same idea used for the main door and the bed, applied to the kitchen. In most Singapore layouts this is already how the stove sits, since kitchens are narrow and the hob usually runs along an outer wall.

How do you fix a stove and sink that face each other?

If the stove and sink already face each other and you are not renovating the kitchen, traditional feng shui offers a simple fix: place a small plant between them. Fire feeds on wood, and wood is fed by water, so a plant is read as a bridge that carries the water element toward the fire rather than letting the two clash directly.

If you are renovating anyway, the cleaner fix is to move one appliance so the layout reads side by side or at 90 degrees instead. Because this is a kitchen fitting, not a wall you would need to hack, it is one of the lowest cost items on any feng shui checklist. Weigh it accordingly against a structural item like a door facing, which a plant cannot fix.

The one line to remember: kitchen items are fixable, so weigh them lightly at the viewing stage. Spend your attention on the main door and the floor plan shape instead, and treat the stove position as a renovation line item.

What are Master Cecil Lee's stove placement rules?

Master Cecil Lee, a feng shui writer published on Geomancy.net, sets out seven stove placement principles that Singapore renovators still reference today. The seven points repeat and sharpen the same ideas covered above: keep the stove out of the main door's sightline, give it a solid wall behind for support, separate it from the sink and the fridge, and treat the cook's own position, facing the room rather than a blank wall, as part of the layout.

You do not need all seven to make a decision at a viewing. The short version buyers use in practice: keep fire and water apart, give the stove a wall behind it, and keep it out of a straight line with the main door, a toilet or a bedroom door. The rest of Master Cecil Lee's list mostly elaborates on these same three ideas.

What does this look like for a real buyer?

Ryan Lim is a Singapore citizen buying his second property. He is looking at a 3 bedroom unit at The Minton in Hougang, a short walk from Hougang MRT, to add to a small portfolio he started building once his HDB flat passed its Minimum Occupation Period. At the viewing he runs the kitchen checks alongside the agent.

  • Stove and sink. The hob sits along the wall by the window. The sink is on the adjacent wall, at 90 degrees, about a metre and a half away. Fire and water are not facing off. Pass.
  • Stove facing. The stove does not point at the kitchen entrance, the main door, or either bedroom door. Pass.
  • Backing. The stove is set against a solid external wall, not a window or an open shelf. Pass.
  • Fridge. The fridge stands just outside in the dry kitchen area, out of the stove's direct line. Pass.
  • One flag. The wet kitchen door lines up with the yard door rather than the main door or any bedroom door, so it does not touch the fire and water rules at all. It is a minor layout quirk, not a clash.

On the four lens read, the kitchen adds nothing to worry about. Tradition is satisfied, the layout is easy to cook in, no future buyer would flag it, and even if it had failed, a stove or sink move is a renovation line item, not a reason to walk away. Ryan moves on to checking the main door and the floor plan before he makes an offer.

None of this replaces a personal reading. If Ryan wanted the kitchen, or the whole unit, checked against his own birth data, that is work for a qualified feng shui master, not a property agent. My role is different: weighing a quirk like this alongside the price, the loan and the rest of the plan, so a kitchen flag does not derail a good unit.

Frequently asked questions

Why should the stove and sink not face each other?

In feng shui the stove represents fire and the sink represents water. When they face each other directly, the two elements are read as clashing rather than balancing. Placing them side by side or at 90 degrees avoids the clash without any renovation cost.

Can I still buy a unit if the stove faces the sink?

Yes. A stove facing a sink is a kitchen layout, not a fixed feature of the unit, so it can almost always be corrected in a renovation. Weigh it far more lightly than a structural item like the main door facing or the shape of the floor plan.

What is the fix if I cannot move the stove or the sink?

Traditional feng shui suggests placing a small plant between them. Wood sits between water and fire in the productive cycle, so a plant is read as bridging the two elements rather than letting them clash directly. It is a low cost fix that also looks good in a kitchen.

Should the stove face the main door?

No. Fire in a direct line with the main door is read as clashing with the qi entering the home. Most Singapore kitchens sit off to one side of the entrance already, so this is rarely an issue, but it is worth a quick check at the viewing.

Does the stove need a solid wall behind it?

Yes. Traditional feng shui asks for a solid wall behind the stove for support, rather than a window or open space. The same idea applies to the cook, who should be able to see the kitchen entrance rather than stand with their back to it.

Who is Master Cecil Lee?

Master Cecil Lee is a feng shui writer published on Geomancy.net, known for a widely cited set of seven principles for stove placement. Singapore renovators and feng shui guides still reference his rules today, covering facing, backing, and the stove's position relative to the sink and the main door.

Does kitchen feng shui affect resale value?

Rarely on its own. Kitchen layouts are usually changed in a renovation, so a stove position does not follow the home to the next buyer the way a main door facing does. Structural items like the floor plan shape carry far more weight when you decide whether to buy.

Is HDB kitchen feng shui different from a condo kitchen?

The same fire and water principle applies in both. HDB kitchens are more often enclosed with a fixed layout, while many condos add an open dry kitchen next to an enclosed wet kitchen, which gives more choice in where the stove and sink sit. Check both kitchens if your unit has both.
