EV charging and parking lot rules in Singapore condos

By Winfred Quek, Associate Marketing Consultant · CEA R073319H · Crestbrick Pte Ltd (L31010886H) · Published 13 July 2026

Quick answer: Singapore's national push toward electric vehicles requires new developments to be built EV ready and pushes existing condos to progressively roll out charging infrastructure in their car parks. Because car park lots and the electrical systems serving them are common property, the Management Corporation Strata Title, the MCST, controls where charging points go, and installation typically needs a feasibility study followed by approval at a general meeting. Cost allocation between the MCST's sinking fund and individual users varies by development, and owners generally cannot install a private charger without MCST approval.

Facts verified: 13 July 2026 · EV infrastructure requirements continue to evolve; verify current rules with the relevant authorities and your MCST · Sources attributed below

EV charging is quickly becoming one of those condo amenities buyers ask about the way they used to ask about a gym or a function room. It is also one of the least understood, because unlike a swimming pool, it did not exist when most older condos were designed, and its arrival raises genuine governance questions: who decides where the chargers go, who pays, and what happens if you want your own bay. Here is how it actually works.

The national push behind condo EV charging

Singapore's broader move toward electric vehicles is coordinated at the national level, with policy encouraging both public and private charging infrastructure to expand well ahead of the vehicle population that will eventually need it. For new developments, this generally translates into a requirement to build in the electrical cabling and capacity needed to support charging from day one, even if not every lot has a charger installed immediately. For existing developments, including condominiums, the direction of travel is a progressive rollout rather than an overnight mandate, with MCSTs expected to plan for growing demand rather than wait until residents are asking for chargers that cannot be installed.

Because these requirements continue to evolve and differ by development type and age, this is one area where I would not rely on a fixed rule of thumb. If EV charging availability matters to your decision, ask the MCST or managing agent directly what has already been assessed and planned for that specific development.

Why the MCST controls this, not individual owners

In a strata development, car park lots and the shared electrical infrastructure serving them are common property, governed collectively rather than owned outright by any individual unit owner, even if a particular lot is allocated to your unit for use. That means adding charging infrastructure is not simply a matter of an owner hiring an electrician. It is a change to common property, and changes to common property in Singapore condos are governed by the Building Maintenance and Strata Management Act, which generally requires a resolution passed at a general meeting before significant additions or alterations can proceed.

In practice, this usually starts with a feasibility study commissioned by the MCST or its managing agent, assessing the building's electrical loading capacity, fire safety implications, and how many lots could realistically be served without overloading the system. That study then informs a proposal brought to an AGM or EGM for owners to vote on, covering both the physical rollout and how costs will be shared.

How charging bays typically get allocated

There is no single standard model across Singapore condos, but a few common patterns show up repeatedly. Some MCSTs designate a specific number of shared charging bays on a first come, first served or booking basis, similar to how visitor lots are sometimes managed. Others allow owners to apply for a dedicated charger at their own allocated lot, subject to approval and available electrical capacity. A growing number work with a third party charging operator who installs and maintains the hardware, billing users directly through an app rather than routing electricity costs through the MCST's own accounts.

Who pays: installation, electricity and maintenance

Cost allocation is one of the more contentious parts of the conversation at many AGMs, and it varies by development. In some models, the owner requesting a dedicated charger bears the installation cost, with ongoing electricity billed on a pay per use basis through the charging operator's app, keeping the cost squarely with the user. In others, shared infrastructure is funded collectively through the sinking fund, on the reasoning that EV readiness benefits the development's future resale appeal generally, with usage costs still recovered individually from whoever actually charges.

Whichever model a development adopts, it needs to be resolved through the MCST's formal decision making process rather than informally agreed between a resident and the managing agent, both to keep the arrangement fair to non EV owners and to ensure it survives scrutiny if challenged later.

What this means if you are buying or already own

  1. If EV charging matters to you, ask before you buy. Ask the managing agent what feasibility work, if any, has been done, and whether any charging bays already exist or are approved.
  2. If you already own and want a charger, go through the MCST properly. A DIY installation on common property risks being ordered removed, and can create liability issues.
  3. Read the cost model carefully before voting at a general meeting. Understand whether you are voting to fund shared infrastructure through the sinking fund or simply to approve a framework for individually funded requests.
  4. Expect this to keep evolving. As EV adoption grows, expect more AGM agenda items on this topic, and expect earlier movers among MCSTs to set informal benchmarks that later ones follow.

Frequently asked questions

Are Singapore condos required to install EV charging points?

Singapore's national push toward electric vehicles means new developments are generally required to be built EV ready, with the necessary cabling infrastructure in place, while existing developments including condominiums are expected to progressively roll out charging capability over time. Exactly how much and how fast this applies to any specific existing condo depends on current regulations, which continue to evolve, so owners should check the latest requirements rather than assume a fixed rule.

Who decides where EV charging points go in a condo car park?

The Management Corporation Strata Title, or MCST, is responsible for planning and approving EV charging infrastructure on common property, typically after a feasibility study covering electrical loading, safety and available lots. Because charging points usually involve altering common property, the installation generally requires approval through a resolution at a general meeting under the Building Maintenance and Strata Management Act.

Who pays for EV charging installation and electricity in a condo?

Cost allocation varies by development and by model. Some condos have the requesting owner bear the installation cost for a dedicated bay, with electricity billed on a pay per use basis through the charging operator. Others fund shared infrastructure through the sinking fund, with usage costs recovered from users. The MCST typically decides the model during the approval process.

Can I install my own EV charger at my condo parking lot?

Generally not without MCST approval, since car park lots and the electrical infrastructure serving them are common property in most strata developments. Owners wanting a dedicated charger typically need to apply through the MCST, which will assess electrical capacity, safety compliance and fairness to other owners before approving any installation.

Does adding EV charging affect a condo's property value or appeal?

It can be a genuine amenity for a growing share of buyers as EV adoption increases, similar to how other shared facilities factor into a development's appeal. It is one of several practical considerations, alongside maintenance fees and MCST management quality, that buyers increasingly weigh when comparing otherwise similar condos.
