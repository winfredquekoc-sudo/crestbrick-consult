Singapore Property Succession Guide 2026

Will, CPF Nomination, and Trust the three documents every Singapore property owner needs, and what happens when any one of them is missing.

By Winfred Quek · CEA R073319H · Updated May 2026
Quick answer Your property passes by Will (or intestacy if none). CPF savings follow the CPF Nomination only a Will cannot redirect them. Property held as joint tenants passes to the surviving owner regardless of any Will. To control the full picture, you need: a valid Will, an updated CPF Nomination, the right ownership structure (joint tenancy vs tenancy in common), and a Lasting Power of Attorney.

Rates and thresholds change. The current figures are kept in one place: the Singapore property rules reference.

The Three Document Framework

Most Singapore property owners assume a Will solves everything. It covers far less than people think. Property succession in Singapore spans three separate legal frameworks with different rules and limitations.

DocumentWhat It CoversWhat It Cannot Cover
WillPrivate property (tenancy in common), HDB (tenancy in common), investments, cash, personal assetsCPF savings, joint tenancy property, nominated insurance proceeds
CPF NominationOA, SA, MA, RA balances including CPF refunded on property saleThe property itself; only CPF account monies
TrustPrivate property; controlled distribution over time; multi generation planningHDB flats (cannot be in trust); rarely worthwhile under $3M portfolio

How Joint Tenancy Overrides Your Will

Most married couples in Singapore hold their HDB flat or first condo as joint tenants. The right of survivorship means the surviving co-owner automatically receives the deceased's share regardless of what the Will says. Your children, siblings, or parents named in the Will get nothing from that property.

This suits many couples who want the surviving spouse to keep the home. It becomes a problem when you have children from a previous relationship, when you want to leave a share to elderly parents, or when property is co-owned with a business partner or sibling.

Marriage Revokes Your Will and CPF Nomination
In Singapore, marriage automatically revokes any existing Will. Your CPF Nomination made before marriage is also revoked upon marriage. If you marry and fail to update both documents, you die intestate and your CPF monies go to the Public Trustee under intestacy rules. Update both within weeks of your wedding and after every major life event.

Intestate Succession What the Law Decides Without Your Input

Dying without a valid Will (or with a Will that does not cover a property) triggers the Intestate Succession Act. For non-Muslims, the distribution formula is fixed:

Surviving FamilyWho Inherits
Spouse and childrenSpouse 50%, children split remaining 50% equally
Spouse only (no children, no parents)Spouse receives all
Spouse and parents (no children)Spouse 50%, parents 50%
Children only (no spouse)Children split equally
Parents onlyParents split equally
No surviving familyEstate passes to the Government (bona vacantia)

For Muslims, inheritance is governed by Faraid (Islamic inheritance law) administered through the Syariah Court, with proportions fixed by religious law and not overrideable by Will for assets subject to Faraid.

The CPF Disconnect

When you die and your property is sold, the CPF used for purchase is refunded to your CPF account first. Those CPF monies then flow to your CPF Nominees not the beneficiaries named in your Will. If your Will says "everything to my brother" but your CPF Nomination says "wife," your wife receives the refunded CPF and your brother receives the cash proceeds. Both documents must be aligned to achieve the result you intend.

Trusts for Multi Property Families

A trust can hold private property in Singapore and deliver outcomes impossible with a Will: income to a surviving spouse, capital to children at age 30, asset protection from creditors, and bypassing probate entirely. The main costs: $5,000 to $20,000+ to set up, plus ongoing trustee and administrative fees. Most cost effective for portfolios valued above $3M to $5M or where distribution complexity justifies the expense.

HDB flats cannot be held in trust.

Succession Planning Checklist

1. Review ownership structure. Check each property: joint tenancy or tenancy in common? If joint tenancy does not match your succession intention, sever to tenancy in common via SLA (private) or HDB.
2. Make or update your Will. Name executors and specific beneficiaries for each property. Update after marriage, divorce, births, deaths of beneficiaries.
3. Update your CPF Nomination. Ensure nominees align with Will beneficiaries where relevant. Marriage revokes the nomination renominate immediately after the wedding.
4. Make a Lasting Power of Attorney. Name a property donee. Without an LPA, loss of mental capacity requires a Deputyship Order from court expensive and slow.
5. Consider a trust if you hold three or more properties or have complex multi beneficiary requirements across generations.
6. Review every 3 to 5 years or after any major life event: marriage, divorce, new child, property purchase or sale, death of a beneficiary.

ABSD and Inherited Property

There is no estate duty in Singapore (abolished 2008). However, once an heir is registered as legal owner, the inherited property counts toward their ABSD tally. A child who already owns one property and inherits another will pay 20% ABSD (SC) on any further purchase. Strategic planning such as timing the transfer before the heir buys their first home, or using a testamentary trust to delay registration can reduce this exposure.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does my Will cover my CPF savings?

No. CPF savings are governed by your CPF Nomination exclusively. A Will cannot override a CPF Nomination. Without a nomination, CPF monies go to the Public Trustee under intestacy rules.

What happens if I die without a Will in Singapore?

The Intestate Succession Act applies. For a married person with children: spouse gets 50%, children split the other 50% equally. The estate requires Letters of Administration from court typically 6 to 18 months.

Does marriage revoke my existing Will?

Yes, automatically. You must make a new Will after getting married. Your CPF Nomination is also revoked on marriage renominate immediately after the wedding.

Can a trust hold my HDB flat?

No. HDB flats must be held directly by eligible individuals they cannot be placed in a trust structure.

Is there estate duty or inheritance tax in Singapore?

No. Singapore abolished estate duty in 2008. However, an inherited property counts toward the heir's ABSD tally for any future property purchase.

How do I convert from joint tenancy to tenancy in common?

Lodge a Severance of Joint Tenancy with SLA for private property, or apply to HDB for HDB flats. Private property conversion takes 1 to 2 weeks and costs a few hundred dollars in stamp duty and legal fees.

What is the difference between a Will and a Lasting Power of Attorney?

A Will takes effect after death and governs asset distribution. An LPA operates during your lifetime if you lose mental capacity, allowing a trusted person to manage your property and finances on your behalf.

More questions

Does a Will cover my CPF savings?

No. CPF savings are governed exclusively by the CPF Nomination, not a Will. If you have no CPF Nomination, your CPF monies are distributed by the Public Trustee under intestacy rules.

Does a Will cover my HDB flat?

Yes, if the flat is held as tenancy in common. If held as joint tenants, the surviving owner takes the whole flat automatically regardless of any Will.

What happens to my property if I die without a Will?

The Intestate Succession Act applies. Property goes to spouse and children by formula. For Muslims, Syariah law governs. The process requires Letters of Administration from court, 6 to 18 months and significantly more costly than probate with a Will.

Does marriage revoke my existing Will in Singapore?

Yes. Marriage automatically revokes any existing Will in Singapore. Your CPF Nomination made before marriage is also revoked. Make a new Will and CPF Nomination immediately after getting married.

Can I set up a trust to hold my Singapore property?

Yes, for private property. Trusts allow controlled distribution, asset protection, and avoidance of probate delays. HDB flats cannot be held in trust.

Can I leave my HDB flat to my children via a Will?

Yes, if held as tenancy in common. Children must meet HDB eligibility (Singapore citizen or PR under approved schemes). If ineligible, HDB may require a sale.

What is the difference between a Will and an LPA?

A Will takes effect after death. An LPA (Lasting Power of Attorney) takes effect if you lose mental capacity while alive, allowing a trusted person to manage your property and finances.

Where should I start with property succession planning?

Start by reviewing your ownership structure (joint tenancy vs tenancy in common), then engage a lawyer for a Will and LPA, then update your CPF Nomination. A property advisor can help with the ABSD and structural implications.

Can I gift property to my child before death to save on succession complications?

Yes, but adding a child to the title or transferring outright triggers BSD, ABSD (if child already owns property), and potentially SSD. It is often more tax efficient to transfer via Will than during lifetime.

What happens to CPF monies after the property is sold on death?

CPF used for the property is refunded to the CPF account first. Those CPF monies are then distributed according to the CPF Nomination, not the Will. Both documents need to align with your wishes.

How long does probate take in Singapore?

With a valid Will: 3 to 6 months for straightforward estates. Without a Will (Letters of Administration): 6 to 18 months. Contested estates can take years.
