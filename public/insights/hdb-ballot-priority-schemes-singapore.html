HDB ballot priority schemes: how they actually improve your odds

By Winfred Quek, Associate Marketing Consultant · CEA R073319H · Crestbrick Pte Ltd (L31010886H) · Published 13 July 2026

Quick answer: HDB runs several priority schemes that give an applicant a better chance in the BTO ballot without changing the flat type or price they are eligible for. The Married Child Priority Scheme rewards couples applying near a parent's home. The Multi Generation Priority Scheme rewards a married child and parents applying together for the same project. The Tenant Priority Scheme helps public rental households move toward home ownership. None of these guarantee a flat, they improve your relative odds within the pool you are competing in, and each has its own qualifying conditions that need to be met precisely. This guide walks through how each scheme actually works and how to pick the right one for your household.

Facts verified: 13 July 2026 · Scheme conditions and priority quotas are HDB policy and can change · Sources attributed below

I get a version of this question every BTO launch: is there any way to improve my ballot chances beyond just applying and hoping. The honest answer is yes, but only if your household's actual living situation genuinely fits one of HDB's priority schemes. These are not tricks or workarounds, they are deliberate policy tools designed to reward specific family arrangements, mainly living near or with parents, and they only work if your application is honestly built around the qualifying relationship rather than bolted on to try to game the system.

Why priority schemes exist

Singapore's housing policy has consistently tried to make it easier for extended families to live close together, both for practical caregiving reasons and because it reflects how many Singapore families actually want to live. Rather than simply hoping applicants sort themselves that way, HDB built ballot priority directly into the BTO and Sale of Balance Flats application process, so that a couple choosing to live near their parents, or apply alongside them, gets a real, structural advantage over an applicant with no such connection applying for the same flat.

This sits alongside, and is distinct from, the flat types built specifically for multi generation living, like the 3Gen flat covered in my 3Gen flat eligibility guide. A priority scheme improves your ballot odds for standard flat types; a 3Gen flat is an entirely different, larger flat type designed for three generations to share one unit. You can, depending on your situation, use a priority scheme without ever considering a 3Gen flat, or the two can work together if a family is applying for a 3Gen unit near other relatives.

The Married Child Priority Scheme

The Married Child Priority Scheme gives a married couple priority in the ballot when applying for a flat in the same town as, or within a defined distance of, one set of parents. The logic is straightforward: you do not need to live in the same block or even the same development as your parents, you simply need to be applying to live meaningfully near them, and the scheme rewards that choice with better ballot odds than an applicant with no geographic connection to the project.

This is the scheme most young couples end up using, since it does not require parents to be applying for a flat themselves, only to already live in or near the town the couple is applying to. It is a good fit if your priority is proximity to family for support with children or ageing parents, without wanting to formally combine households.

The Multi Generation Priority Scheme

The Multi Generation Priority Scheme goes a step further: it applies when a married child and their own parents both apply for flats in the same BTO project, intending to live near or with each other, and both applications receive improved priority as a result. This scheme requires coordination, since both generations need to be applying to the same launch at the same time, which is a bigger commitment than the Married Child Priority Scheme but delivers priority for two households, not just one.

Families weighing this scheme are often also weighing whether to go further and apply for a 3Gen flat instead of two separate units. The Multi Generation Priority Scheme keeps each generation in their own flat while living close by; a 3Gen flat combines everyone into one larger unit. Which is right depends entirely on how your family actually wants to live day to day, not on which option carries better ballot odds, since both carry a real advantage over a standard application.

The Tenant Priority Scheme

The Tenant Priority Scheme is aimed at a different group entirely: households currently living in an HDB public rental flat who are applying to buy their own BTO flat. It gives these households priority in recognition of their move from rental housing toward home ownership, and eligibility runs off the specific conditions of the public rental scheme the household is currently under, rather than the general income ceiling and family nucleus rules that apply to typical applicants.

If this is your situation, it is worth checking your eligibility for this scheme specifically before applying under a general category, since the priority it confers can meaningfully change your odds compared to competing in the open pool. For the wider mechanics of what disqualifies an HDB loan application, which sometimes intersects with a household's rental or prior ownership history, see my why HDB loan applications get rejected guide.

Choosing the right scheme for your household

  • Applying as a couple, want to live near one set of parents: Married Child Priority Scheme.
  • Applying alongside your own parents for the same project: Multi Generation Priority Scheme.
  • Currently in a public rental flat, moving toward ownership: Tenant Priority Scheme.
  • Want three generations under one roof in one unit: not a priority scheme at all, this is the 3Gen flat type itself.
A priority scheme is not a guarantee. It improves your relative standing within the applicant pool for that flat type and project, it does not reserve a unit for you. Popular projects in mature estates can still be genuinely competitive even within a priority category, so apply with a realistic view of the odds, and have a fallback plan, such as the Sale of Balance Flats exercise, if the ballot does not go your way.

How to apply correctly under a priority scheme

  1. Confirm the qualifying relationship precisely. Distance, town boundaries and what counts as living near a parent are defined specifically by HDB, so check the current conditions rather than assuming based on what you have heard.
  2. Coordinate timing with your parents if applying under the Multi Generation scheme. Both applications need to align to the same launch for the priority to apply.
  3. Do not misrepresent your situation. Applications are checked against actual residency and family records, and an inaccurate application risks disqualification rather than just a lower priority.
  4. Treat the scheme as one part of a wider application strategy. Flat type, town choice and timing across BTO launches still matter alongside whichever priority scheme you qualify for. My BTO application guide covers the full process end to end.

Frequently asked questions

What is the Married Child Priority Scheme?

The Married Child Priority Scheme gives priority in the BTO ballot to a married couple applying for a flat in the same town, or within a set distance, as one set of the couple's parents. It rewards families who choose to live near each other, and it applies whether the couple is applying for a new flat while the parents stay put, or in coordination with a parent's own application.

What is the Multi Generation Priority Scheme?

The Multi Generation Priority Scheme gives priority when a married child and their parents apply together for flats in the same BTO project, intending to live near or with each other. It is aimed squarely at extended families who want the convenience of living in the same development, and both applications benefit from the improved chances when submitted together under the scheme.

What is the Tenant Priority Scheme?

The Tenant Priority Scheme gives priority to households currently living in a public rental flat under HDB's rental scheme who are applying to buy their own BTO flat. It is designed to help rental tenants progress toward home ownership, and eligibility follows the specific conditions of the public rental scheme rather than the general income ceiling rules that apply to other applicants.

Can I combine more than one priority scheme?

Generally you apply under whichever single scheme best fits your household's actual situation, since the schemes are built around different qualifying relationships and living arrangements. Rather than trying to combine schemes, the more effective move is to make sure your household correctly meets the specific conditions of the one scheme that applies to you, since a technically ineligible application under the wrong scheme does not improve your odds at all.

Do priority schemes guarantee a flat?

No. A priority scheme improves your relative chances against the general pool of applicants for the same flat type in the same project, it does not guarantee an outcome. Popular projects and mature estates can still be competitive even within the priority category, so treat the scheme as an advantage to use, not a certainty to rely on.
