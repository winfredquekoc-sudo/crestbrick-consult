What happens on completion day when buying property

By Winfred Quek, Associate Marketing Consultant · CEA R073319H · Crestbrick Pte Ltd (L31010886H) · Published 13 July 2026

Quick answer: Completion day is when your lawyer disburses your funds, your CPF withdrawal, bank loan drawdown and any remaining cash balance, to the seller's lawyer, in exchange for the executed transfer documents and the keys. Neither buyer nor seller usually needs to be present; the exchange happens between the two law firms. Before releasing any payment, your lawyer runs a final set of checks: confirming the title remains clear, that loan and CPF funds have actually landed in the client account, and that all conditions in the contract have been met. Once funds are released and documents exchanged, you are the legal owner, and keys typically follow shortly after, often collected directly at the property.

Facts verified: 13 July 2026 · Process described reflects standard Singapore conveyancing practice · Sources attributed below

By the time completion day arrives, most of the emotional weight of a property purchase has already passed. You have viewed, negotiated, exercised the option, waited through title searches and requisitions, and arranged your financing. Completion day itself can feel anticlimactic precisely because it is designed to be: a well run completion is a quiet administrative handover between two law firms, not a dramatic event you need to personally navigate. But understanding what actually happens on that day makes the handful of days before it, when your lawyer is chasing loan letters and CPF confirmations, make a lot more sense.

What completion day actually is

Completion is the final step of a property transaction: the point at which ownership legally transfers from seller to buyer, in exchange for full payment of the purchase price. It follows the Sale and Purchase Agreement or, for HDB resale, the second appointment process, and represents the moment all the preparatory legal and financial work converges into a single coordinated exchange. Your lawyer and the seller's lawyer agree a completion date in advance, then work backward from it to ensure every piece, financing, CPF, documentation, is ready in time.

It helps to think of completion less as an event and more as the visible endpoint of weeks of quiet coordination. From the moment you exercise the Option to Purchase, your lawyer is already working toward this date: title search and requisitions go out early, your bank loan application is progressed in parallel, and your CPF withdrawal request is timed so the funds land with enough buffer before completion, not on the day itself. By the time completion actually arrives, the bulk of the risk in the transaction has typically already been managed. What remains is largely a matter of confirming that everything lined up as planned and executing the final exchange cleanly.

Funds disbursement mechanics

On completion day, your lawyer's client account receives the full purchase price from three possible sources: your bank loan proceeds, your CPF withdrawal if applicable, and any cash balance you are contributing directly. Once all funds are confirmed as received, your lawyer disburses this total sum to the seller's lawyer, who in turn is responsible for settling any of the seller's own obligations, such as discharging their existing mortgage, before passing the net proceeds to the seller. This entire exchange happens lawyer to lawyer, using the property's designated completion account, without either buyer or seller needing to physically transfer money themselves.

CPF and bank loan drawdown

If you are using CPF Ordinary Account savings, your lawyer submits a withdrawal request to CPF Board in the lead up to completion, and CPF Board disburses the approved amount directly into your lawyer's client account, not to you personally. Your bank works similarly: once your loan is approved and all conditions are met, the bank disburses the loan amount to your lawyer's client account ahead of or on completion day itself. Both of these need to be timed precisely, since completion cannot proceed until all expected funds have actually landed, which is why your lawyer typically confirms receipt a day or more ahead of the scheduled completion date rather than leaving it to the same morning.

Key handover and final checks

Physical key handover is usually a separate, simpler step from the funds and documents exchange. Once your lawyer confirms completion has occurred, keys are typically released, often directly at the property, sometimes through the agent, or in some cases collected from the seller's lawyer's office. The keys are the final, tangible marker of the transaction closing, but the legal transfer itself has already happened by the time you are holding them.

Do not schedule movers for completion day itself. Even a well prepared completion can shift by a day due to a banking cutoff, a delayed CPF confirmation, or a last minute document correction. Build a buffer of at least a few days between your planned completion date and any moving, renovation or lease notice commitments.

What your lawyer verifies before releasing payment

  1. Title and requisition status remain clear. Your lawyer reconfirms nothing has changed since the earlier title search and requisition responses, such as a newly lodged caveat.
  2. All funds have actually been received. Bank loan proceeds and CPF funds are confirmed as landed in the client account, not merely approved or in transit.
  3. Contract conditions have been satisfied. Any conditions written into the Sale and Purchase Agreement, such as vacant possession or specified fixtures remaining, are checked off.
  4. The seller's transfer documents are in order. The instrument of transfer and any related documents are correctly executed and ready for lodgement with the Singapore Land Authority.
  5. Any outstanding items from requisitions have been resolved. If an earlier check flagged something needing the seller's action, your lawyer confirms it has actually been cleared, not merely promised.

What can delay or derail completion day

The most common causes of a slipped completion date are financing related: a bank loan disbursement that has not been processed in time, or a CPF withdrawal request submitted too close to the deadline. Beyond financing, an unresolved issue from a title search or requisition, discussed in more detail in my guide to title searches and requisitions, can also hold things up if it surfaces late. On the seller's side, delays can arise if they are not fully ready to vacate or if their own onward purchase has fallen behind schedule. Most of these are administrative rather than structural problems and resolve within a matter of days, but they are exactly why experienced buyers avoid making irreversible commitments, like a moving date or a lease termination notice, tied precisely to the original completion date.

Frequently asked questions

What actually happens on completion day for a property purchase?

On completion day, your lawyer coordinates the disbursement of funds, comprising your CPF withdrawal, bank loan drawdown and any cash balance, to the seller's lawyer, in exchange for the signed transfer documents and the keys to the property. Your lawyer runs final checks before releasing any payment, and once everything is confirmed in order, the transaction is complete and you become the legal owner.

Do I need to be present on completion day?

Not usually. Completion is typically handled entirely between the buyer's and seller's lawyers, without either party needing to attend in person. Keys are usually collected separately, often at the property itself or through the agent, once your lawyer confirms completion has taken place.

How does CPF get used on completion day?

If you are using CPF Ordinary Account savings toward your purchase, your lawyer submits the withdrawal request to CPF Board ahead of completion, and CPF Board disburses the approved amount directly to your lawyer's client account for onward payment to the seller as part of the completion funds, rather than paying you directly.

What final checks does my lawyer run before releasing payment?

Your lawyer confirms the title search and requisition responses remain clear, verifies the loan facility and CPF funds have actually been received into the client account, checks that any conditions in the Sale and Purchase Agreement have been met, and confirms the seller's side has prepared the transfer documents correctly, before authorising the funds to be released.

What can delay or derail completion day?

Common causes include a bank loan disbursement that has not come through in time, a CPF withdrawal request that was submitted late, an unresolved issue from a requisition or title search, or the seller not being ready to vacate or hand over the property as agreed. Most delays are administrative and resolve within days, but they can push back your move in date.
