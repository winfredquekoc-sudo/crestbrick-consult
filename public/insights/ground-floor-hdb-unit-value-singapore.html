Ground floor HDB units: resale value and buyer considerations

By Winfred Quek, Associate Marketing Consultant · CEA R073319H · Crestbrick Pte Ltd (L31010886H) · Published 13 July 2026

Quick answer: Ground floor HDB units are typically priced below equivalent higher floor units in the same stack, but not by a fixed amount, and not always for the same reason. The genuine trade offs are no lift dependency and easier mobility against reduced privacy, more exposure to security concerns, and noise from corridor or void deck activity, sometimes including proximity to funeral wakes traditionally held there. Some newer ground and first storey units also carry a Private Enclosed Space, a patio owners can enclose into extra living space, which works against the discount rather than reinforcing it. This guide covers what actually drives ground floor pricing and who is genuinely well suited to buying one.

Facts verified: 13 July 2026 · Pricing patterns vary by estate and by unit; treat as general guidance, not a valuation · Sources attributed below

Ground floor is the flat type buyers either actively want or actively avoid, and I rarely meet someone in between. That makes it a genuinely interesting segment of the resale market: demand is real on both sides, which means pricing is set by who happens to be looking at a given block at a given time, more than by a fixed rule about what ground floor is worth. Understanding the actual drivers, rather than the generic assumption that ground floor equals cheaper and worse, helps both buyers and sellers price it correctly.

What consistently pulls the price down

The reasons ground floor units tend to transact at a discount are specific and worth naming individually, because they do not all apply equally to every unit. Reduced privacy from people walking past windows and along the corridor is the most universal one. Security is next: a ground floor unit is more accessible from outside than an upper floor unit, and even where the actual risk difference is modest, the perception genuinely shapes buyer demand and therefore price. Noise from common area activity, whether that is children playing, a nearby coffee shop, or general foot traffic, also weighs on units closest to high activity zones. And in blocks where the unit sits directly beside the void deck, some buyers specifically avoid it because Singapore void decks traditionally also host funeral wakes, on top of the general noise and lack of privacy that proximity brings.

None of these factors are unique to ground floor in the abstract, a mid floor unit directly above a busy void deck entrance has some of the same issues, but ground floor concentrates several of them at once, which is why the discount shows up consistently across estates even though its size varies a great deal.

What consistently pulls the price up, or removes the discount altogether

The most underappreciated feature here is the Private Enclosed Space, offered on selected ground and first storey units in some HDB projects. A PES is a patio or yard area attached to the unit that the owner can enclose into additional usable living space, something no upper floor unit has any equivalent of. Where a ground floor unit carries a genuine PES, it is not a discount unit at all, it is a unit with a feature upper floors cannot offer, and it should be priced and shopped for on that basis rather than assumed to be cheaper by default.

Beyond PES, no lift dependency is a real and growing value driver as Singapore's population ages and more households include an elderly member or young children in a pram. A ground floor unit that is quiet, set back from the void deck, and does not face a busy path can command pricing much closer to a mid floor unit than the generic stereotype suggests.

How to actually evaluate a specific ground floor unit

  • Check if it has a PES. This alone can change the pricing conversation entirely; ask specifically, do not assume.
  • Walk the unit at different times of day. Foot traffic, noise and privacy exposure vary significantly by time and day of week.
  • Check what is directly outside the windows. A unit facing a quiet garden reads very differently from one facing the main pedestrian route into the block.
  • Ask about the void deck's typical use. Some void decks are quiet community spaces; others are consistently active, which matters more to some buyers than others.
  • Compare against genuinely similar transacted units. Price against other ground floor units in comparable positions, not against the block average or a generic percentage discount rule.
Do not price a ground floor unit off a rule of thumb. I regularly see sellers underprice a genuinely good ground floor unit, one with a PES or a quiet outlook, because they assume ground floor automatically means a fixed discount. I also see buyers overpay for a ground floor unit directly beside a busy void deck because they anchored on a headline low price per square foot without weighing the specific position. Price against comparable recent transactions for similarly positioned units, not the flat type label alone. My HDB resale pricing guide covers the broader framework for getting this right.

Who genuinely suits a ground floor purchase

Ground floor is a legitimate first choice, not a fallback, for buyers who weight practical mobility and, where available, extra outdoor space above privacy and floor height. Elderly buyers or families supporting an elderly parent benefit meaningfully from no lift dependency, particularly during a lift breakdown or maintenance window. Families with young children can find a PES genuinely useful as a safe, enclosed outdoor area. And buyers who are simply not bothered by street level living, and who prioritise a lower entry price, can capture real value in blocks where the discount is larger than the actual downside justifies.

Conversely, buyers who value privacy highly, who are sensitive to noise, or who are specifically uncomfortable with proximity to void deck activity should treat ground floor as a genuine trade off to weigh carefully rather than a way to save money with no real cost. There is no universally right answer here, only a fit between the unit's actual characteristics and what you personally value.

Selling a ground floor unit: how to position it correctly

  1. Lead with the genuine advantages. No lift wait, easier access, and a PES if you have one, are real selling points to a specific buyer profile, not weaknesses to apologise for.
  2. Be upfront about the outlook. Buyers who are going to be bothered by proximity to the void deck or a busy path will discover it on viewing regardless, so address it directly rather than letting it become a negotiation surprise.
  3. Benchmark against genuinely similar units, not the block average. A ground floor unit with a quiet outlook and a PES deserves pricing closer to a mid floor comparable, not a blanket discount.
  4. Target the right buyer. Elderly buyers, multi generation households and young families are typically the most motivated ground floor buyers; make sure your marketing reaches them.

Frequently asked questions

Are ground floor HDB flats always cheaper?

Not always, and not by a fixed amount. A ground floor unit facing a busy void deck or a road tends to transact below a comparable higher floor unit in the same stack, but a ground floor unit that is quiet, well positioned and especially one with a Private Enclosed Space can command pricing much closer to, or in some cases above, a similar mid floor unit. The discount depends heavily on the specific unit's outlook and features, not on being ground floor alone.

What is a Private Enclosed Space and does it add value?

A Private Enclosed Space, or PES, is a patio or yard area attached to selected ground and first storey units in some HDB projects, which the owner can enclose to extend the flat's usable living space. Where offered, it is a genuine value adding feature specific to ground floor living, since upper floor units have no equivalent, and it should be factored into pricing rather than assumed away as a simple discount unit.

Is security a real concern for ground floor flats?

It is a real consideration for many buyers, whether or not it reflects the actual crime rate in a given estate. Ground floor units are more accessible from outside than upper floor units, and window grilles, gate security and general visibility from the corridor or void deck genuinely affect how safe an owner feels, which in turn affects buyer demand and, over time, resale pricing.

Why do some buyers avoid ground floor units near the void deck?

Void decks in Singapore host a wide range of community activities, and traditionally also funeral wakes, which some buyers prefer to avoid living directly beside. Combined with general foot traffic noise and reduced privacy from passersby, a unit directly adjacent to an active void deck tends to see softer demand than one set further back, even within the same block.

Who should actually consider buying ground floor?

Ground floor suits buyers who value no lift dependency, easier mobility for elderly residents or young children, and potentially a lower entry price or a PES, more than they value privacy, higher floor views or distance from common area noise. It is a legitimate and often underrated choice for the right buyer, not simply a compromise unit.
