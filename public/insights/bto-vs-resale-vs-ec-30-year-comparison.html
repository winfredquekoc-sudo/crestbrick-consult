BTO vs Resale vs EC: How the Long Term Choice Compares

Published: 8 September 2026 · By Winfred Quek, Crestbrick Pte Ltd

BTO, resale and Executive Condominium are not interchangeable choices. In short: BTO offers the largest subsidy but a multi year wait, resale gives immediate occupancy at a market price with no fresh subsidy, and an EC sits between HDB and private condo pricing but must be financed with a bank loan, not an HDB loan. The right choice depends on your timeline, income ceiling and financing, not on which one appreciates fastest.

Safety: Eligibility and Income Ceiling Differ by Type

From 24 August 2026, the household income ceiling for most subsidised BTO and resale grant schemes is 16,000 dollars a month for families and 8,000 dollars for singles, while the Executive Condominium ceiling is 18,000 dollars a month. A household above the HDB ceiling but below the EC ceiling may find an EC is the only public housing route still open to them. Always check the exact ceiling for the specific scheme or grant you intend to use, since some grants apply a lower ceiling than the base purchase eligibility.

Money: Subsidy and Grant Structure

A BTO flat is priced by HDB below the equivalent resale market value, so the subsidy is built into the launch price itself. A resale flat has no developer subsidy, but eligible first timer buyers can still apply for CPF Housing Grants on top of the resale price, such as the Enhanced Housing Grant and Family Grant, which reduce the effective cost. An EC is developed and sold by a private developer at a price between HDB and full private condo levels, and eligible buyers can apply for the CPF Housing Grant for ECs, subject to the EC income ceiling, but not the HDB specific grants used for BTO or resale.

Money: Loan Type and the MSR and TDSR Gates

BTO and resale flats can be financed with either an HDB concessionary loan, subject to the household income ceiling, or a bank loan. An EC cannot use an HDB loan at all, only a bank loan, because it is developed privately. The Mortgage Servicing Ratio, capping loan repayment at 30 percent of gross income, applies to both HDB flats and ECs, since both are treated as public housing types for this rule. The Total Debt Servicing Ratio, capping all debt repayment at 55 percent of gross income, applies across all three options. A private resale condo, by contrast, is subject to TDSR only, with no MSR ceiling.

Practical effect: an EC buyer clears the same MSR test as an HDB buyer, but must qualify for a bank loan rather than the HDB concessionary rate, which can mean a different interest rate and loan to value limit.

Timing: Waiting Period and Minimum Occupation Period

A BTO flat typically takes three to five years to complete from application to key collection, reflecting construction time. A resale flat is available for immediate occupation, generally within two to three months of completing the purchase. An EC under construction from a fresh land sale also involves a multi year wait similar to a new private condo launch, while a completed EC resale unit offers immediate occupancy like an HDB resale flat.

All three carry a Minimum Occupation Period before you can sell or rent out the whole unit. BTO and resale HDB flats carry a five year MOP. Executive Condominiums currently carry a five year MOP as well, but HDB has announced this will rise to ten years for newer EC land sale sites, phasing in as those projects reach completion around 2029 to 2030. Check the specific land sale batch and launch date of any EC you are considering before assuming the MOP length.

Safety: Resale Eligibility Rules Also Apply

Buying an HDB resale flat is also subject to the Ethnic Integration Policy, which sets an ethnic quota by block and neighbourhood to maintain a balanced mix of communities. This can restrict which resale units you are eligible to buy in a given block, separate from any income or citizenship rule, and it is a government housing policy, not a preference set by an individual seller.

Timing: What the Market Data Actually Shows

HDB resale prices fell for two consecutive quarters in the first half of 2026, the first back to back quarterly decline in roughly seven years, even as million dollar flat resales hit a record 201 transactions in August 2026. Private residential prices, which set the ceiling ECs eventually compete against once they privatise, rose 0.5 percent quarter on quarter in the second quarter of 2026, following a 0.9 percent rise in the first quarter. These are historical movements only, not a forecast, and past price direction is not a guarantee of future appreciation for any of the three options.

Money: Maintenance and Ongoing Costs Differ Too

A BTO or resale HDB flat carries a modest monthly service and conservancy charge set by the town council, with no separate sinking fund contribution required from individual owners beyond that charge. An Executive Condominium, like any condominium, requires a monthly maintenance fee covering shared facilities such as lifts, security and any pool or gym, and periodically a special levy for major works such as repainting or structural repairs once the building ages. Over a long holding period, this ongoing difference in monthly cost is real and should be budgeted alongside the purchase price, even though the exact amount varies by development and cannot be quoted as a single figure without knowing the specific project.

Safety: Grants Come With Conditions

CPF Housing Grants are not automatic once you clear the income ceiling. Most grants require you to be a first timer applicant, meaning you and your spouse have not previously enjoyed a housing subsidy, and some grants require you to live near parents or adult children to qualify for a proximity component. Check the specific conditions for each grant on the HDB website for your exact household situation, since combining several grants is possible but each has its own separate eligibility test.

Decision Checklist

  • Can you wait three to five years, or do you need a home within months
  • Is your household income above or below the HDB and EC ceilings for your target scheme
  • Do you want to inspect the actual unit before committing, which only resale and completed EC units allow
  • Are you comfortable financing with a bank loan only, which is required for an EC
  • Have you checked the specific MOP that applies to the EC batch you are considering

There is no single winner across BTO, resale and EC. The honest exercise is matching each option's price, subsidy, financing and timeline against your own household ceiling, savings and patience, rather than assuming one path is always superior.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which is cheaper, BTO or resale?

BTO is priced with a government subsidy below the equivalent resale market value, so it is generally the cheaper upfront option, in exchange for a three to five year wait. Resale has no fresh subsidy but eligible first timer buyers can still apply for CPF Housing Grants to reduce the effective cost.

Can I get an HDB loan for an Executive Condominium?

No. An Executive Condominium is developed by a private developer, so it must be financed with a bank loan, not an HDB concessionary loan. The Mortgage Servicing Ratio of 30 percent still applies to the bank loan, alongside the Total Debt Servicing Ratio of 55 percent.

How long is the minimum occupation period for BTO, resale and EC?

BTO and resale HDB flats carry a five year Minimum Occupation Period. Executive Condominiums currently also carry a five year MOP, but HDB has announced this will rise to ten years for newer EC land sale sites, phasing in as those projects complete around 2029 to 2030.
