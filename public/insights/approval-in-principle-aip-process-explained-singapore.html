Approval in Principle: how it works and why to get one first

By Winfred Quek, Associate Marketing Consultant · CEA R073319H · Crestbrick Pte Ltd (L31010886H) · Published 13 July 2026

Quick answer: An Approval in Principle, or AIP, is a bank's indicative assessment of how much you can borrow, based on your income, debts and a preliminary credit check, before you have found a specific property. It confirms a likely loan quantum, not a guarantee, and typically remains valid only for a limited window, often around 30 days depending on the bank. Getting one before you start viewing protects you from falling in love with a unit you cannot actually finance, and from losing an option deposit because the numbers did not work out as assumed. For HDB purchases, the equivalent mandatory step is the HFE letter rather than a bank AIP.

The single most common financing mistake I see is a buyer who starts house hunting with a number in their head that no bank has actually confirmed. They saw a friend's purchase, did rough mental maths, and went shopping. An Approval in Principle exists precisely to close that gap before you commit emotionally, or financially, to a specific unit. It costs you a short application and some documents. It saves you from the much more expensive mistake of exercising an Option to Purchase on a flat you cannot actually finance at the price you agreed.

What an AIP actually confirms

An AIP is a bank's preliminary assessment of your borrowing capacity, based on your declared income, existing debt obligations, and a credit bureau check, run against the Total Debt Servicing Ratio framework that Singapore banks are required to apply. It typically results in a letter stating an indicative loan quantum the bank is prepared to lend, subject to a specific property being identified and formally valued. That word, indicative, is doing a lot of work. It is the bank's best estimate given what you have told them, not a locked in commitment.

What it does not confirm is the final approved amount once you have identified an actual unit. The bank still needs to value that specific property, and if the valuation comes in lower than the purchase price, or if your financial position has changed since the AIP was issued, the final loan quantum can differ from what the AIP indicated. Treat it as a strong planning number, not a locked in ceiling.

How to apply for an AIP

Most banks in Singapore let you apply for an AIP directly, often online, by submitting recent payslips or income documents, your latest CPF contribution history, existing loan and credit card statements, and identification documents. The bank runs its TDSR calculation, currently applying a 55 percent TDSR ceiling with a stress test floor around 4 percent on the interest rate used for the assessment, and returns an indicative loan quantum, sometimes within a few business days.

If you are self employed, have variable income, or have foreign currency income, the documentation requirements are usually heavier, since banks apply additional scrutiny and sometimes a haircut to less predictable income streams before running the same TDSR calculation. It is worth applying for an AIP earlier rather than later if your income profile falls into any of these categories, simply because the assessment itself can take longer.

How long an AIP stays valid

AIP validity periods are set by individual banks rather than being standardised across the industry, and they commonly run for a limited window, often in the order of 30 days, though this varies by lender. If your search extends beyond that window, most banks will let you refresh the assessment, sometimes with updated documents or a fresh credit check if enough time has passed. The practical implication is timing your AIP application to roughly coincide with when you expect to seriously start viewing and offering, rather than obtaining one many months before you are ready to transact.

Why house hunting without one is risky

An unverified budget is not a budget. Without an AIP, your sense of what you can afford is built on assumptions rather than a bank's actual assessment of your income, debts and credit profile. The most expensive version of this mistake is exercising an Option to Purchase, paying a non refundable deposit, and then discovering during the loan application that your actual borrowing capacity is meaningfully lower than you assumed.

Beyond the deposit risk, house hunting without a confirmed number also wastes time. Viewing units above your real capacity sets an anchor that makes everything within your actual budget feel like a downgrade, when in fact it was never really within reach. An AIP resets that anchor to something real before you start comparing units emotionally rather than financially.

AIP versus final approval: what can still change

Even with a positive AIP in hand, several things can still move between that assessment and your final loan approval. The bank's valuation of the specific property you commit to can come in below the agreed purchase price, which affects how much the bank will lend against it regardless of your AIP quantum. Your own financial position can change too, a new car loan, a change of job, or an unexpected dip in income can all alter the TDSR calculation when the bank runs the full application. Treat the period between your AIP and your actual purchase as a window in which you should avoid taking on new debt or making major financial changes if you can help it.

AIP versus HDB's HFE letter

For HDB purchases, the process is structured differently. Rather than an optional bank AIP, HDB requires an HFE letter, or HDB Flat Eligibility letter, as a mandatory prerequisite before you can apply for a BTO flat, buy an HDB resale flat, or apply for an HDB concessionary loan. The HFE letter combines an eligibility check with an indicative loan assessment into a single mandatory step that HDB itself administers. If you are financing an HDB purchase through a bank loan rather than the HDB loan, you may still want a bank AIP alongside your HFE letter to compare the two financing routes. The full mechanics of that letter are covered in my HFE letter guide.

Getting practical: steps before you view

  1. Gather your documents early. Payslips, CPF statements, existing loan records and identification, so the AIP application itself is not the bottleneck.
  2. Apply to more than one bank if you are comparing packages. This also gives you a sense of how different banks assess your specific income profile, though be mindful of how many credit enquiries you generate in a short window.
  3. Time it to your search. Apply close to when you plan to seriously start viewing, given the limited validity window most AIPs carry.
  4. Treat the AIP number as a ceiling, not a target. Building in a buffer below your AIP quantum protects you against a lower than expected valuation or a change in your financial position before completion.

Frequently asked questions

What is an Approval in Principle in Singapore?

An Approval in Principle, or AIP, is an indicative assessment from a bank of how much home loan you are likely to qualify for, based on your income, existing debts and a preliminary credit check, before you have identified a specific property. It confirms the bank's initial willingness to lend up to a certain quantum, but it is not a final loan approval.

How long is an AIP valid for?

AIP validity periods are set by each bank and commonly run for a limited window, often around 30 days, though this can vary by lender and should always be confirmed directly with the bank issuing it. If your house hunt runs longer than the validity period, you will typically need to refresh the assessment.

Is an AIP a guarantee I will get the loan?

No. An AIP is an indicative assessment, not a binding commitment. Final loan approval depends on the specific property's bank valuation, your finalised documentation at the point of application, and your financial position remaining materially unchanged between the AIP and the actual loan application.

What is the difference between an AIP and an HFE letter?

An AIP is issued by a bank for a bank loan and is optional but recommended before house hunting. An HFE letter, or HDB Flat Eligibility letter, is issued by HDB and is a mandatory prerequisite before applying for a BTO flat, buying an HDB resale flat, or applying for an HDB loan. The HFE letter combines eligibility and an indicative loan assessment into a single mandatory step.

Does getting an AIP affect my credit score?

Applying for an AIP typically involves the bank performing a credit check as part of its preliminary assessment. Multiple AIP applications across several banks within a short period could contribute to multiple credit enquiries, so it is reasonable to be selective about how many banks you approach. Confirm the specific credit reporting impact with each bank if this is a concern.
