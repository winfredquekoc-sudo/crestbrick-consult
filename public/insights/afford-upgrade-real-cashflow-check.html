Can I Afford the Upgrade? The Real Cashflow Check for HDB Upgraders

Article ID: 723 · Published: 28 August 2026 · By Winfred Quek, Crestbrick Pte Ltd

You just received your HDB resale completion letter. Your flat sold for S$650K. Your banker pre approved you for S$2.5M. So naturally, you can upgrade to a S$2M condo, right?

Wrong. Affordability and qualification are not the same thing.

Your bank says you qualify based on TDSR (Total Debt Service Ratio). But TDSR is a lender's safety margin, not your lifestyle margin. This article walks through the real numbers: the ones your mortgage broker doesn't put in bold, the hidden costs that blindside upgraders, and the portfolio trade offs that determine whether you're truly ready to move up.

Real talk: Over 40% of upgraders report post purchase regret within 2 years. Not because they can't afford the payment. Because they didn't account for the full cost of living in the new property, and their lifestyle margins evaporated.

Section 1: The Financial Reality Check

How Banks Calculate Your Borrowing Capacity

Lenders use two main metrics: TDSR and MSR (Mortgage Service Ratio). Both determine how much you can borrow.

TDSR = (Total Monthly Debt Obligations) / (Gross Monthly Income) Legal ceiling: 55% (for most borrowers) For a S$1.8M property with 25% down (S$450K down, S$1.35M loan): Monthly mortgage @ 4% over 30 years = S$6,450 Other debts (car, cards, etc.) = S$500 Total obligations = S$6,950 Your household income must be ≥ S$12,636 to qualify But wait, this assumes 4% interest. Banks stress test at 4.5% SORA.

Condo Maintenance Fund

HDB residents pay S$0. Condo residents pay into a sinking fund for common area upkeep: lifts, lobbies, roof repairs, landscaping, security.

Typical range: S$400 to 800 per month depending on building age, size, and amenities. Older condos (15+ years) trend toward the higher end. New buildings start lower but rise as major systems age.

Property Tax

Unlike HDB (no annual property tax), private properties are subject to annual tax. The rate depends on annual value (AV), typically 4 to 6% of gross rent that could be collected.

Example: S$1.8M condo in central location might have AV of S$60K, yielding annual tax of S$2,400 to 3,600. A S$2.5M property could hit S$4,000+.

Strata Special Levies

Every 5 to 8 years, older condos face major repairs: roof replacement, structural work, structural glazing. The management committee issues a special levy.

Real scenario: A 20 year old 300 unit condo needed structural glazing works. Total: S$9M. Per unit share: S$30K. Most residents weren't budgeted for this surprise.

Critical: Before upgrading, request the building's reserve study and maintenance records. A healthy reserve (ideally 8 to 12 months of operating costs) suggests lower future levy risk.

CPF Accrued Interest Repayment

When you sell your HDB, the government recoups any CPF withdrawn for purchase, plus accrued interest (currently 2.5% p.a.). This liability can surprise upgraders.

Example: You purchased your HDB 30 years ago for S$180K using S$150K CPF. That S$150K has accrued S$120K in interest. Your sales proceeds: S$650K. CPF repayment due: S$270K. Net down payment: S$380K (vs. S$650K assumed).

ABSD 20% (Additional Buyer's Stamp Duty)

First time private property buyers from HDB are exempt from ABSD. But if you already own another private property, ABSD applies: 20% of purchase price or market value, whichever is higher.

This is included in your down payment calculation. Example: S$1.8M property, ABSD = S$360K. If you budgeted only S$400K down, you have just S$40K cushion. No room for surprises.

Section 3: Gap Analysis, HDB Proceeds to Private Property

Here's where many upgraders hit reality: their HDB sale doesn't give them as much down payment as assumed.

The Typical Equation

HDB Resale → Private Property Down Payment

Assume HDB resale price: S$650K (typical for 30 year old 4 room flat)

Subtract:

  • Agent commission (2.5%): S$16,250
  • Legal fees: S$800
  • Survey fee: S$400
  • Appraisal: S$300
  • CPF accrued interest: S$120,000
  • ABSD 20% on private property (if applicable): S$360,000

Total deductions: S$498,000

Net available for down payment: S$152,000

(This assumes a S$1.8M private purchase; ABSD applies at 20%.)

Problem: Most lenders require 25% down on a S$1.8M property = S$450,000. You're short by S$298,000.

Solution paths:

  • Borrow more: Use S$152K down, borrow S$1.648M (91.6% LTV). Most lenders cap LTV at 85% for private property. Not feasible.
  • Downsize property: Buy S$1.2M instead. 25% down = S$300K. You have S$152K. Still short by S$148K. Rethink.
  • Use joint funds: Draw S$300K from investment portfolio or savings. Reduces future investment capacity.
  • Delay and save: Wait 2 years, accumulate S$200K more. Then upgrade.

The CPF Trap

After selling your HDB, most of your CPF goes back into the government's pocket (accrued interest repayment). What remains is locked in Medisave (can't touch until 55).

Here's the cruel twist: if you buy a leasehold property with <30 years remaining, you can't use CPF to pay down the loan in retirement. Your private property becomes illiquid when you need it most.

Avoid this trap: When upgrading, target properties with ≥35 years lease remaining. Properties built pre 1990 may have <30 years left. Check the lease carefully.

Section 4: The Lifestyle Reality, Upgrader's Remorse

Why Upgraders Actually Move

  • Family expansion: Second child means kids need separate rooms. HDB flat space doesn't allow.
  • Investment narrative: "Upgrading is always the right move." (It's not. Depends on portfolio plan.)
  • Status/prestige: Condo living implies success. Private property ownership feels like an achievement.
  • Space and freedom: HDB resale restrictions mean you're tied to that unit. Private property feels more permanent.

The Lease Decay Problem

HDB flats are 99 year leasehold. You buy into a property with 60+ years of lease remaining. Private condos are typically 99 year too. But not always. Some older condos have 70 to 80 years left.

Here's the trap: As the lease decays, property value plummets. A S$1.8M condo with 35 years left might be worth S$800K with 20 years left. CPF regulations prevent you from using CPF to pay down the mortgage in retirement (if <30 years remain).

You're forced to sell before lease decay hits, or hold a depreciating asset. This isn't an investment. It's a liability with an expiry date.

Section 5: Portfolio Strategy, Property Sequencing and Refinancing

Now we shift perspective: your first condo upgrade isn't just about lifestyle. It's the first move in a multi property portfolio play. This changes the affordability calculation entirely.

The Two Property Portfolio Plan

Many upgraders think of their move as one off: "We upgrade to a condo, live there for 20 years, then downsize in retirement." Wrong.

Smart upgraders think sequentially:

  1. Property 1 (Years 0 to 5): Owner occupy condo. Build equity through appreciation + mortgage paydown.
  2. Refinance decision (Year 3 to 5): If condo appreciated 15 to 20%, refinance to unlock S$200K to 300K equity.
  3. Property 2 (Year 5+): Use refinanced equity as down payment for investment property (condo in CBD or rental focused location).
  4. Timeline to multi property ownership: 7 to 10 years.

Section 6: Decision Framework, Can You Really Afford It?

The 4 point Checklist

  1. Stress test TDSR at 4.5% interest rate. If TDSR > 45%, you're over leveraged. Skip this property size.
  2. Calculate full monthly cost (mortgage + maintenance + tax + insurance). Ensure surplus is ≥S$25K/month after all obligations.
  3. Account for CPF accrued interest + ABSD. Verify your down payment covers both without draining savings.
  4. Ask: Can I buy Property 2 in 5 years? If the answer is no, your down payment on Property 1 is too aggressive.

Section 7: FAQ, 10 Common Upgrade Questions

1. My bank pre approved me for S$2.5M. Can I afford it?
Probably not. Bank pre approval is based on TDSR alone, which assumes you can spend 55% of gross income on debt. That leaves 45% for everything else: taxes, living expenses, kids' education, emergencies. Most households need 30 to 35% margin for comfort. If you can get pre approved for S$2.5M but your genuine surplus (after full costs) is tight at S$1.8M, go with S$1.6M instead.
2. Should I use all my CPF for the down payment?
No. Keep a CPF buffer (S$50K to 100K) for surprise costs: strata levies, home repairs, refinancing. If you drain CPF completely and hit a S$20K special levy, you're forced to use credit. Use CPF strategically to reach 25 to 30% down, then pause.
3. What if my income drops 10 to 20% after upgrading?
This is why stress testing at 4.5% interest matters. If you can't afford the mortgage at 4.5%, you won't survive a 10% income drop. Avoid buying at the absolute top of your TDSR range. Aim for properties where you can handle both rate risk and income loss simultaneously.
4. Is upgrading a good investment?
It depends. If you hold for 10+ years in a location with strong rental demand and you refinance to build a portfolio, yes. If you buy at peak prices in a stagnant area and hold for 5 years, you might break even or lose money after transaction costs. Treat it as an investment only if you have a clear refinance plan and portfolio roadmap.
5. Should I extend my mortgage to 35 years to lower payments?
Lower payments feel good initially, but you'll pay S$300K+ more in interest over 35 vs. 30 years. At age 65, you'll still have S$600K owed. Stick to 30 years if possible. If 30 years doesn't fit your budget, the property is too expensive.
6. Can I buy a property with no down payment (100% financing)?
Most banks cap LTV at 85% for owner occupied condos (90% for first time buyers in certain schemes, but that's HDB only). For S$1.8M, you need at least 15% down (S$270K). If you don't have that, wait and save. Stretching to 100% LTV means zero equity cushion and you're underwater if market dips.
7. What's the best location for my first upgrade property?
From a portfolio perspective: Central (CBD, Orchard, Marina Bay) if you plan to rent it later. Suburban (Punggol, Sengkang, Woodlands) if you plan to owner occupy long term. East coast (East Coast, Katong) offers balance. Avoid ultra-remote areas or properties with short lease remaining (<70 years). Resale and refinance ability depend on location.
8. Should I buy a resale or new launch condo?
Resale = immediate occupancy, known condition, existing community, established appreciation. New launch = price discount (sometimes), modern finishes, warranty, but not occupied (3 to 5 years wait), and uncertainty on appreciation. From a portfolio view, resale is safer for your first upgrade because you get immediate rental income potential (for refinance strategy) vs. waiting 5 years.
9. How much should I have in emergency savings before upgrading?
Aim for 6 to 12 months of total obligations (mortgage + condo fees + property tax + insurance + other debts). For our S$1.8M example with S$8,670 monthly obligations, that's S$52K to 104K. Many upgraders upgrade with zero savings cushion. That's gambling. Even a 2 month income disruption could force a rushed refinance or sale.
10. Can I rent out my first upgrade property if I need to relocate?
Depends on your mortgage terms. Most banks allow rental after owner occupancy (1 to 5 years varies by lender). Rental income helps with refinancing, but doesn't all count as your income (typically 70 to 80% counts toward debt servicing ratio). Plan for this: check your loan agreement before upgrading.

The Final Word

Upgrading from HDB to private property is a legitimate milestone. But it's not the automatic next step. It's a financial decision that requires you to:

  • Stress test your mortgage at 4.5%+ interest rates, not 3% current rates.
  • Account for condo maintenance, property tax, and special levies as part of your real monthly cost.
  • Verify your down payment covers CPF accrued interest and ABSD without killing your savings.
  • Think of the property as part of a multi property portfolio plan, not a one off purchase.
  • Build in a 20 to 30% margin for unexpected costs, income loss, or rate shocks.

If the numbers work under all these conditions, you can afford it. If they don't, either wait, save more, or buy a smaller property first. Your future self will thank you.
