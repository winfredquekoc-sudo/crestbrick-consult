ABSD Singapore 2026: complete FAQ for foreigners and SPRs

By Winfred Quek · CEA R073319H · 10 minute read · Last reviewed July 2026

Quick answer: ABSD Singapore 2026 rates for non-citizens: foreigners pay 60% on every residential purchase; SPRs pay 5% on their first property and 25% on the second. These rates are unchanged since April 2023. Nationals of the US, Switzerland, Iceland, Liechtenstein, and Norway are exempt from the foreign rate under Free Trade Agreements and pay Singapore Citizen equivalent rates instead.

Facts verified: July 2026 · Source: IRAS ABSD page

If you are a foreigner or Singapore Permanent Resident researching what ABSD Singapore 2026 will cost you on a property purchase, this page answers the questions I hear most often. The rules for non-citizens are specific, the numbers are large, and the mistakes are expensive. Read this before you sign anything.

The key facts: Singapore's Additional Buyer's Stamp Duty applies to almost every residential purchase by a non-citizen, the 60% foreigner rate that came into effect in April 2023 is still in force as of July 2026, and there are a small number of legitimate paths to reduce or recover the duty depending on your nationality, marital status, and purchase structure.

What is ABSD and who has to pay it in Singapore?

Additional Buyer's Stamp Duty (ABSD) is a tax on top of the standard Buyer's Stamp Duty (BSD) that Singapore applies to residential property purchases. It was introduced in 2011 and has been raised multiple times since, most significantly in April 2023 when the foreign buyer rate jumped from 30% to 60%.

ABSD is paid by the buyer, not the seller. It is calculated on the purchase price or the market value of the property, whichever is higher. It is due within 14 days of exercising the Option to Purchase (OTP) or signing the Sale and Purchase Agreement. ABSD cannot be rolled into your mortgage; it must be paid in cash.

Who must pay it:

  • Singapore Citizens buying a second or subsequent residential property
  • Singapore Permanent Residents buying any residential property
  • Foreigners (non-Singapore Citizens, non-PRs) buying any residential property, with narrow FTA exceptions
  • Companies, trusts, and other entities buying any residential property

ABSD applies to private condominiums, landed houses, apartments, HDB resale flats (where foreigners are not eligible to buy at all), and executive condominiums after the 10 year privatisation period. It does not apply to commercial or industrial property.

What are the ABSD Singapore 2026 rates for foreigners, SPRs, and Singapore Citizens?

The rates below are the current ABSD Singapore 2026 rates. They have been unchanged since the April 2023 revision.

ABSD is applied to the full purchase price, not a portion of it. A foreigner buying a S$2M condo pays 60% of S$2M, which is S$1.2M in ABSD alone, before BSD. Use the ABSD calculator to model your specific numbers.

Note on the SPR second property rate: Many sources still cite 25% for SPR second property, which is the current rate. Some older guides cite 30% (the pre 2023 rate for SPR third property, or the post 2023 PR second property rate depending on version). Confirm with IRAS or your conveyancing lawyer before committing to any figure on a live transaction.

Did the 60% foreigner ABSD rate change in 2026? Is it still in effect?

Yes, the 60% foreigner rate is still in effect as of July 2026. It was introduced on 27 April 2023 by the Ministry of Finance and IRAS as part of a coordinated set of cooling measures. No changes to this rate have been announced since.

The 2023 hike was the single largest ABSD increase in the policy's history, doubling the foreign rate from 30% to 60% in one move. The stated intent was to moderate demand from foreign buyers in a market where Singaporeans and PRs were already facing affordability pressure. The data since 2023 shows foreign buyer volumes dropped significantly, which suggests the measure has achieved its stated objective. That makes a near term reversal unlikely from a policy perspective.

If you are planning a Singapore property purchase as a foreigner and are waiting for the rate to fall, there is no current signal from the government that a reduction is coming. The more productive question is whether you qualify for an FTA exemption, or whether your purchase structure can be optimised within the existing rules.

Which nationalities are exempt from the 60% foreigner ABSD rate under Free Trade Agreements?

Under Singapore's Free Trade Agreements, nationals of certain countries receive Singapore Citizen equivalent ABSD treatment. As of 2026, this applies to citizens of:

  • United States of America
  • Switzerland
  • Iceland
  • Liechtenstein
  • Norway

A qualifying national from one of these countries pays 0% ABSD on their first Singapore residential property, 20% on the second, and 30% on the third and subsequent. That is a saving of 60 percentage points on a first purchase compared to the standard foreign rate.

The exemption comes from Singapore's bilateral FTAs and multilateral agreements (the US exemption flows from the US Singapore FTA; the European countries are covered under the EFTA Singapore FTA). These are treaty obligations, not temporary concessions.

Three conditions you must meet for FTA treatment:
  • You must be a citizen (passport holder) of the qualifying country. Green card, permanent residency, or other visa status in that country does not qualify.
  • You must purchase the property in your personal name. Using a company, trust, or any other entity forfeits the FTA benefit. The entity rate of 65% applies regardless of your nationality as the ultimate beneficial owner.
  • You must not also hold Singapore Permanent Residency or Citizenship. If you later become SPR or SC, your ABSD status changes to that category.

If you hold dual citizenship, you should confirm the applicable treatment with IRAS directly or via a conveyancing lawyer before signing. The interaction of dual citizenship with FTA ABSD treatment is fact specific.

How is ABSD calculated and when is it due?

ABSD is calculated as a flat percentage of the higher of the purchase price or the market value assessed by IRAS. If you negotiate a price below market value, IRAS will apply ABSD to the market value, not the discounted transaction price. This matters for distressed sales and intra-family transfers.

The payment timeline:

  • If you exercise an Option to Purchase (OTP): ABSD is due within 14 days of exercising the OTP.
  • If you sign a Sale and Purchase Agreement directly (common for new launches): ABSD is due within the timeframe specified in the S&P Agreement, typically 14 days from the date of the agreement.
  • For sub-sales and other transfer instruments: within 14 days of the instrument date.

ABSD is a cash cost. It cannot be financed through your bank mortgage, and it cannot be paid using CPF funds (CPF housing withdrawal is for BSD and downpayment only). For a foreign buyer purchasing a S$3M condominium, the ABSD alone is S$1.8M, due in cash within 14 days. This is the single most important planning consideration for foreign buyers: the cash requirement at signing is substantially larger than the downpayment alone.

Run your total upfront cash requirement through the ABSD calculator before committing to a purchase. The number that matters is BSD plus ABSD plus the minimum cash downpayment, not just the deposit.

What is developer ABSD remission for new launches and how does it work?

Developers of new residential projects must pay ABSD on their land acquisitions, but they can claim a full remission of that ABSD if they complete construction and sell all units within 5 years of acquiring the land. This is the developer ABSD remission scheme.

This has no direct bearing on what a buyer pays. As an individual buyer of a new launch unit, your ABSD is based on your own buyer profile (foreigner, SPR, or SC), not the developer's land acquisition costs. The developer remission is a supply side measure designed to keep residential development commercially viable; it does not transfer to purchasers.

The indirect effect is that developer pricing can sometimes be calibrated to the additional cost burden they carry if they fail to sell within 5 years, which creates genuine pressure on developers to price competitively in slower markets. But this is a developer economics consideration, not a buyer ABSD reduction mechanism.

Should an SPR buy a property alone or jointly with a Singapore Citizen spouse?

This question comes up constantly for SC/SPR couples and the answer depends on your specific situation, your existing property holdings, and your longer term portfolio plans. I cannot give you a direct recommendation here, but I can lay out the structural logic so you understand what the key variables are.

The key difference between sole SPR purchase and joint SC/SPR purchase:

What is the ABSD spousal remission scheme for SC/SPR couples buying a matrimonial home?

When a married couple buys a new matrimonial home while already owning an existing one, the purchase technically triggers ABSD at the second property rate. The spousal remission scheme allows couples where at least one spouse is a Singapore Citizen to claim a full refund of that ABSD, provided they sell the existing property within 6 months of the new property's completion or TOP date.

The conditions are strict:

  1. At least one spouse must be a Singapore Citizen at the time of purchase.
  2. Both spouses must be named on the title of the new matrimonial home. A sole name purchase does not qualify.
  3. All existing residential properties must be sold within 6 months of the trigger date (legal completion for resale; TOP for new launch).
  4. Neither spouse can hold any interest in any other residential property after disposal.

The ABSD is paid upfront at stamping and refunded by IRAS after successful disposal and submission. There is no escrow arrangement. On a S$1.5M purchase with 20% ABSD, that is S$300,000 tied up in cash until the existing property is sold and the IRAS remission claim is processed.

What is the step by step process to claim ABSD remission after selling the existing property?

The remission claim process runs through the IRAS myStampDuty portal. Here is the sequence:

Step 1: Pay ABSD in full at stamping. No deferral is available. The 14 day payment deadline applies regardless of your remission eligibility.
Step 2: Note your trigger date. For resale properties, this is the legal completion date of the new purchase. For new launches, this is the Temporary Occupation Permit (TOP) date. Set a reminder for the 4 month mark to give yourself buffer for the sale process.
Step 3: Sell the existing property. The sale must reach legal completion (not just OTP) within 6 months of the trigger date. An exercised but uncompleted sale does not satisfy the condition.
Step 4: Prepare your documents. You will need the stamp certificate from the new purchase, the completion account from your conveyancing lawyer on the sold property, NRICs of both spouses, and your marriage certificate. Have these ready before the 6 month deadline passes.
Step 5: Submit the remission claim via IRAS myStampDuty within 6 months of the trigger date. Submission after the deadline is not accepted, even if the sale completed within the window. File immediately after your existing property's completion, do not wait.
Step 6: IRAS processes the refund, typically within 4 to 8 weeks of a complete submission. The refund is credited to the bank account you specify. No interest is paid on the ABSD amount during the holding period.

The most common reason remission claims are rejected is that the 6 month deadline was missed. The second most common is that the new property was held in one spouse's sole name. Both are avoidable with proper planning before signing.

For a more detailed walkthrough, see the ABSD remission claim process guide.

What are the most common ABSD mistakes made by foreigners and SPRs in Singapore?

These are the errors I see most frequently when buyers come to me after the fact:

  • Buying before obtaining PR. If you are on an Employment Pass planning to convert to PR, your ABSD rate as a foreigner is 60%. If you buy first and obtain PR after, you pay the 60% foreigner rate permanently on that transaction. The rate does not get retrospectively revised when your status changes. If PR is on the horizon, the sequencing decision between obtaining status and buying property is worth careful thought.
  • Joint purchase with SC spouse without understanding the ABSD implications. Some SPRs assume that buying jointly with an SC spouse automatically gives them the SC rate. It does for a first property joint purchase (SC 0% rate applies if it is the first property for both). But if the SC spouse already owns a property, the joint purchase attracts SC second property ABSD. Knowing which scenario applies before the OTP is signed is essential.
  • Assuming FTA treatment applies automatically. If you are a qualifying FTA national, you must still be aware that the benefit requires a personal name purchase. Buyers who default to using a company structure because their accountant or home country advisor recommended it for other reasons inadvertently pay 65% entity ABSD instead of 0%.
  • Not having the cash ready. The 14 day ABSD payment deadline from OTP exercise is absolute. Foreign buyers who plan to remit funds from overseas need to account for international transfer timelines, bank processing delays, and currency conversion. A week is not always enough lead time. Plan for ABSD payment to arrive before OTP exercise, not after.
  • Buying the new property before finalising the remission conditions. Couples who structure a transaction for matrimonial remission but put the new property in one spouse's name, or who retain a small share in an older property that was not properly discharged, forfeit the remission entirely. Confirm title structure and property count with your conveyancer before signing, not after.
  • Treating ABSD as a reason not to buy when the asset's long term returns absorb it. At 60%, foreign ABSD is punishing. But for some buyers in the right situation, a Singapore property's appreciation, rental yield, and portfolio role still justifies the all-in cost. The math has to be done honestly, not emotionally. If the 10 year numbers work at post ABSD cost, ABSD is a price of entry, not a deal breaker.

Are there any announced or expected ABSD changes in 2026?

As of July 2026, no changes to ABSD rates have been announced by the Ministry of Finance, IRAS, or the Monetary Authority of Singapore (MAS). The rates in the table above are current.

Singapore reviews property cooling measures periodically in response to market conditions. The 2023 hike was driven by elevated transaction volumes and rising prices in a low rate environment. Since then, transaction volumes have moderated and the SG property market has seen steadier, slower price growth rather than the rapid appreciation of 2021 to 2022. Whether this prompts any relaxation of cooling measures is speculative.

The government has historically been reluctant to signal rate changes in advance. Policy shifts, when they come, tend to be announced with immediate effect. Buyers should plan around current rates rather than anticipated future changes. If you are weighing a purchase now versus later on the basis of potential ABSD reduction, be aware that this is a bet on policy, not a calculation you can make with any confidence.

Frequently asked questions

What is the ABSD rate for foreigners in Singapore in 2026?

60% on all residential property purchases, regardless of whether it is their first or subsequent property. This rate has been unchanged since April 2023. Nationals of the US, Switzerland, Iceland, Liechtenstein, and Norway are exempt under Free Trade Agreements and pay SC equivalent rates instead.

What is the ABSD rate for Singapore PRs in 2026?

5% on the first residential property and 25% on the second. From the third property onward, PRs pay 30%. These rates are unchanged since the April 2023 revision.

Can a foreigner avoid ABSD in Singapore?

Generally no, unless you are a national of the US, Switzerland, Iceland, Liechtenstein, or Norway, in which case you pay SC equivalent ABSD rates under Free Trade Agreements. The purchase must be in your personal name; a company vehicle does not qualify for FTA treatment.

Can an SPR married to a Singapore Citizen claim ABSD remission?

Yes. A couple where at least one spouse is a Singapore Citizen can claim full ABSD remission on a new matrimonial home, provided the existing property is sold within 6 months of the new property's completion date (resale) or TOP date (new launch), and both spouses are named on the new title.

When is ABSD due in Singapore?

ABSD must be paid within 14 days of exercising the Option to Purchase (OTP) or signing the Sale and Purchase Agreement. It is calculated on the purchase price or market value, whichever is higher. It is not a loan financeable cost and must be paid in cash.

Did the foreigner ABSD rate change in 2026?

No. The 60% foreigner rate introduced in April 2023 remains unchanged in 2026. No new cooling measures affecting ABSD rates were announced through July 2026.
