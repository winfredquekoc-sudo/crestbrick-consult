Commission Transparency

How rental agent commissions work in Singapore, for landlords and tenants

By Winfred Quek · CEA R073319H · Published 9 September 2026

Quick answer: In most Singapore rentals, the landlord pays the agent commission, commonly ranging from around half a month's rent to one month's rent depending on the lease term, and tenants typically pay nothing. This is common practice, not a fixed rate. Under Singapore's property agent conduct standards, both landlords and tenants can ask about and negotiate commission directly and openly with their agent.

Rates and thresholds change. The current figures are kept in one place: the Singapore property rules reference.

Facts verified: September 2026 · Sources linked below

Key takeaways

  • CEA does not fix or prescribe rental commission rates. Every figure is a private, negotiated arrangement recorded in the Estate Agency Agreement for the lease.
  • The landlord typically pays the agent. Common practice ranges from around half a month's rent to one month's rent, often varying with lease length.
  • Tenants usually pay nothing. Where a tenant's own agent is involved, that agent is commonly co broked, sharing part of the commission the landlord already agreed to pay.
  • Tenants may face a direct fee in specific situations, such as engaging their own agent for a hard to find unit, a very short lease, or a direct landlord listing with no agent involved.
  • The CEA prescribed Estate Agency Agreement framework covers lease transactions the same way it covers sale and purchase, so the same negotiation and disclosure standards apply.

Rental commission in Singapore causes more quiet confusion than almost any other property cost. Landlords are not always sure what is standard. Tenants are sometimes surprised to be asked for a fee at all. This guide sets out who typically pays, what common practice looks like across different lease terms, and why both landlords and tenants are entitled to raise and negotiate the number directly with their agent, rather than treat it as fixed or unspoken.

Who pays the agent in a Singapore rental?

In the great majority of Singapore rentals, the landlord pays the agent commission. This holds whether the landlord's own agent sources the tenant directly, or a separate agent representing the tenant is involved. When two agents are part of a deal, the arrangement is commonly a co broke split, where the landlord's agent shares part of the agreed commission with the tenant's agent. The tenant, in that scenario, still pays nothing directly.

Tenants encounter a direct fee only in specific situations, covered in detail further down this guide. As a general rule, if you are a tenant being asked to pay commission, it is reasonable and expected for you to ask exactly why, and for your agent to explain the arrangement clearly before you commit to anything.

The transparency rule applies to rentals too

Singapore's property agent conduct standards do not treat rentals differently from sales when it comes to commission transparency. CEA's own consumer guidance states plainly that commissions are not fixed, and you are free to negotiate the amount or rate, and this applies to a lease exactly as it applies to a sale or purchase. The CEA prescribed Estate Agency Agreement framework covers exclusive and non exclusive arrangements for lease transactions using the same structure as sale and purchase agreements, first mandated in January 2011.

The Code of Ethics and Professional Client Care (CEPCC), prescribed under the Estate Agents (Estate Agency Work) Regulations 2010 made under the Estate Agents Act, sets the same conduct standard for a rental listing as for any other transaction, including the duty to avoid conflicts of interest and to be clear about the terms of any agreement. There is no carve out that makes rental commission a quieter or less negotiable conversation.

Before you sign anything: whether you are a landlord appointing an agent to find a tenant, or a tenant considering paying your own agent, confirm the commission figure, whether GST applies, and the length of any exclusive appointment, capped at three months, in a signed Estate Agency Agreement. Verify the agent's registration at cea.gov.sg/aceas/public-register before proceeding.

When do tenants encounter agent fees, and how to negotiate them?

Tenants are not immune from commission entirely. There are a few scenarios where paying an agent directly is the normal and reasonable arrangement:

  • Engaging your own agent for a specific or hard to find unit. If you want an agent actively searching on your behalf, particularly for a tight budget, a specific building, or an unusual requirement, that dedicated search effort is sometimes structured as a fee you pay directly, agreed upfront.
  • Very short leases. Landlords are sometimes less willing to fund the full commission on a short term lease, so the arrangement may shift toward the tenant contributing, or the fee being split differently between both sides.
  • Direct landlord listings with no agent involved. If a landlord is renting out without an agent, and you still want your own representation to review the tenancy agreement and handle the process, that agent's fee is a matter between you and them alone.
  • Relocation or corporate leasing packages. Some arrangements, particularly for expatriate tenants, involve a different fee structure agreed as part of a broader relocation service.

In every one of these cases, the amount is negotiable. There is no schedule anywhere that fixes what a tenant should pay. Ask early, get the figure in writing, and confirm what the fee actually covers before you commit.

Negotiating rental commission, whichever side you are on

Step 1: Ask directly. Whether you are a landlord or a tenant, ask your agent how they are paid on this specific listing before any marketing or search begins.
Step 2: Tie it to the lease term. If a longer lease is on the table, it is reasonable to discuss whether that affects the commission basis.
Step 3: Clarify co broke upfront. As a landlord, ask whether your agent expects to co broke with a tenant's agent, and confirm this does not change your total cost.
Step 4: Confirm GST treatment. Ask whether the figure quoted is inclusive or exclusive of GST if the agency is GST registered.
Step 5: Get it in the Estate Agency Agreement. Whatever is agreed, commission amount, basis, and GST treatment, should appear in the signed EAA before the listing goes live or the search begins.

Landlords sometimes assume the commission conversation is settled before they even sit down, and tenants sometimes assume they should never be asked to pay anything at all. Frequently asked questions

Who pays the agent commission in a Singapore rental, the landlord or the tenant?

In most Singapore rentals, the landlord pays the agent commission. Tenants typically pay nothing unless they specifically engage their own agent and agree separately to pay for that service, which should always be documented before the search begins.

How much is rental agent commission typically?

Common practice for landlords typically ranges from around half a month's rent to one month's rent, often depending on the lease term, though this is negotiated and varies by agent, agency, and property. There is no rate fixed by CEA or any other authority.

Can tenants negotiate or avoid paying agent commission?

Most tenants pay no commission because the landlord funds the agent, including any co broke share to the tenant's own agent. Where a tenant does engage and pay their own agent directly, that fee is fully negotiable and should be agreed in writing before any search work begins.

Does commission change for a longer lease term?

Common practice often ties commission to lease length, with shorter leases sometimes attracting a smaller amount and longer leases a larger one, but this is a matter of negotiation between the landlord and the agent rather than a fixed schedule set by any regulator.

Can I discuss commission directly with my agent before signing?

Yes. Singapore's property agent conduct standards are explicit that commission is not fixed and is open to negotiation between you and your agent. This applies equally to landlords and tenants, and the figure should be confirmed in writing before any marketing or search begins.

What should be in the Estate Agency Agreement for a rental listing?

The CEA prescribed Estate Agency Agreement for a lease should state the agent's name and registration number, the agreed commission amount or basis, whether GST applies, the marketing and viewing arrangements, and the duration of any exclusive appointment, capped at three months per agreement.
