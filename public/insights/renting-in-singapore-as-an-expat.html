Renting in Singapore as an expat: a practical how to guide

By Winfred Quek · 9 minute read · Last reviewed 27 Aug 2026

Quick answer: Most expats in Singapore rent either a HDB flat (SGD 2,500 to 4,000 monthly for a 3 room in central Singapore) or a private condo (SGD 4,000 to 8,000 monthly). Budget three months of rent as deposit, get a lawyer to review your lease, and use PropertyGuru or 99.co to compare. Neighbourhoods matter more than price: MRT distance, shopping, and school catchment are worth extra rent.

Rates and thresholds change. The current figures are kept in one place: the Singapore property rules reference.

Facts verified: 27 Aug 2026 · Sources linked below

Key Takeaways

  • Rental deposit is typically two to three months of rent. Landlords can withhold deposit for unpaid rent, utilities, or damage. Always get a written move out inspection report.
  • HDB vs condo trade off: HDB is cheaper and more spacious; condo is more convenient with community amenities and shorter lease commitment options.
  • MRT distance is the single biggest quality of life variable. Anything within 500m of an MRT station commands a premium but saves 1.5 hours per week in commute time.
  • Property tax on HDB rentals is a landlord cost that you may be required to share; always clarify utility billing (water, electricity, gas) before signing.
  • Expat community neighbourhoods (Tiong Bahru, East Coast, Orchard) have higher rents but faster exit windows if your assignment ends early.

Renting in Singapore as an expat means navigating a tight, fast moving market where good properties disappear within days. Speed matters, but so does doing your homework upfront. This guide walks you through the process, neighbourhood by neighbourhood, cost by cost, and lease clause by clause.

HDB flats vs private condos: which is right?

HDB flats are cheaper and more spacious. A 3 room HDB in Tiong Bahru rents for SGD 2,800 to 3,400 per month and has a separate kitchen, living room, and three genuine bedrooms. The same budget in a private condo gets you a 1 bedroom plus a study with shared amenities and higher management fees.

Private condos are more expat friendly: most landlords expect short term leases (12 to 24 months), furnished units are standard, and the building management handles some tenant coordination. HDB landlords often require 24 month minimum leases and expect tenants to handle their own repairs. But HDB rent is 30% cheaper for equivalent space.

Neighbourhood picker: where to rent

Singapore is small; a 20 minute MRT ride spans from Kranji in the north to Jurong East in the west. But neighbourhoods vary dramatically in expat friendliness, cost, and school quality. Here is a quick framework.

Tiong Bahru & Outram (Central). Expat hub with walkable streets, restaurants, and a strong rental market. Rent is premium (HDB 3 room: SGD 3,500 to 4,200; condo: SGD 6,000 to 8,000). Good for families on company relocation packages.
East Coast & Katong (East, Beach). Family oriented, beach access, expat schools nearby. HDB 3 room: SGD 2,800 to 3,500; condo: SGD 5,000 to 7,000. Popular with Australian and UK expats.
Orchard & River Valley (CBD adjacent). High rise condos, shopping, nightlife. Rent is premium (condo: SGD 6,500 to 9,000). Minimal HDB options. Good for young professionals.
Bukit Timah & Holland Village (Central West). Lower density, expat families, schools. HDB scarce; mostly landed houses and small condos. Rent: SGD 5,000 to 8,000. Long commute to CBD.
Clementi & West Coast (West, Value). Lower cost, young professionals, good MRT. HDB 3 room: SGD 2,400 to 3,000; condo: SGD 4,000 to 5,500. Less expat heavy, more value.

The renting process: step by step

  1. Search PropertyGuru or 99.co for available units in your target neighbourhood. Filter by rent, bedrooms, and MRT distance. Set alerts; the best units are rented within 48 hours of posting.
  2. Contact the agent or landlord. Schedule viewings for 3 to 5 units to understand your options. Ask about lease terms, utilities included, and deposit requirements upfront.
  3. Narrow to two finalists. Visit during evening or weekend to assess the area noise and foot traffic. Check water pressure, AC, and appliance functionality.
  4. Negotiate on rent and lease terms. If you are comfortable signing a 24 month lease, landlords often accept 5 to 10% lower rent. Request first month, last month, and a security deposit (total three months).
  5. Have a lawyer review the lease. Singapore does not require written leases, but unwritten agreements lead to disputes. A lawyer charges SGD 200 to 400 to review. It is worth it. Ensure the lease specifies rent review clauses, deposit return conditions, and early termination penalties.
  6. Pay the deposit and first month's rent. Use a bank transfer, never cash. Get a receipt and a written lease agreement signed by both landlord and tenant.
  7. Move in inspection. Document the flat's condition with photos (rooms, walls, appliances). Email the photos to the landlord as proof of initial condition. This protects your deposit on move out.

Key lease clauses to negotiate

Rent review. Does rent escalate on year two? Ask for a fixed rent for the full lease term, or a maximum 3% annual increase capped at 5% total. This protects you from surprise increases.

Utilities. Are water, electricity, and gas included in the rent? If not, who pays the deposit for each utility? Clarify before move in to avoid confusion when the utility company invoices arrive.

Early termination. Can you exit early if your company reassigns you? Negotiate a two month notice termination clause with a penalty of one month's rent, rather than forfeiting the entire lease. This is common for expat tenancies.

Deposit return timeline. The lease should state that the landlord returns your deposit within 30 days of move out, minus documented damage. Anything beyond 30 days is a red flag.

Common expat renting mistakes

  • Renting on a handshake. Singapore law permits oral leases, but disputes are hell. Always get a written lease, even a simple email exchange stating the terms, and get the landlord to sign it.
  • Not inspecting utilities before moving in. Water, electricity, gas, and internet are utilities expats forget to confirm. Schedule inspections and deposits before you hand over money.
  • Ignoring neighborhood walkability. Renting a cheap unit on a quiet street means a 15 minute bus ride to the MRT. The rent savings evaporate in transport costs and time.
  • Assuming furnished means move in ready. Furnished in Singapore often means a bed, a sofa, and a table only. Bring your own kitchen basics, toilet paper, and cleaning supplies.
  • Overcommitting to long leases early. If you are new to Singapore, rent for 12 months first. After 12 months, you know the city well enough to commit to a 24 month lease.

Winfred's Take

Expats often overpay for rent because they are in a hurry. Companies will only cover a certain rental budget; tenants then search within that budget and miss better value 5 minutes further away. Take time to map three or four neighbourhoods. Get a lawyer to review your lease. Ask the landlord if rent is negotiable if you commit to 24 months. Most landlords prefer a longer lease to frequent tenant turnover. The 10 to 15% you save by being patient and strategic more than pays for a lawyer's review.
