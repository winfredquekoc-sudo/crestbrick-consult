Foreign Buyer ABSD in Singapore, and What It Changes

Published: 8 September 2026 · By Winfred Quek, Crestbrick Pte Ltd

A foreign buyer purchasing residential property in Singapore pays Additional Buyer's Stamp Duty of 60 percent of the purchase price or market value, whichever is higher, since 27 April 2023. In short: on a 2 million dollar property, that is 1.2 million dollars in ABSD alone, on top of the standard Buyer's Stamp Duty, due within 14 days of exercising the Option to Purchase.

Money: The Current ABSD Schedule

ABSD depends on citizenship status and how many residential properties you already own. Singapore Citizens pay 0 percent on a first property, 20 percent on a second, and 30 percent on a third or subsequent property. Singapore Permanent Residents pay 5 percent on a first property, 30 percent on a second, and 35 percent on a third or subsequent property. Foreign buyers pay a flat 60 percent regardless of how many properties they already own. Entities, such as companies, pay 65 percent, which is higher than the personal foreigner rate, not lower, so buying through a company does not reduce ABSD.

Common misconception: using a holding company to buy residential property in Singapore does not give a foreign buyer a lower ABSD rate. The entity rate of 65 percent is higher than the personal foreigner rate of 60 percent.

Safety: The Treaty Nationality Exception

Nationals of the United States, Switzerland, Liechtenstein, Norway and Iceland are treated the same as Singapore Citizens for ABSD purposes, under Singapore's Free Trade Agreement commitments to those countries. That means 0 percent ABSD on a first residential property, 20 percent on a second, and 30 percent on a third or subsequent property, the same schedule as a citizen, with no permanent residency required. Every other nationality pays the full 60 percent foreigner rate from the first purchase.

Money: What 60 Percent Actually Costs

Applying the current rate directly: a foreign buyer purchasing a 2 million dollar property pays 1.2 million dollars in ABSD. Add the standard Buyer's Stamp Duty, which is calculated on a tiered scale up to 6 percent of the price for the portion above 1.5 million dollars, and the buyer's total stamp duty bill runs well past 1.3 million dollars before financing costs are even considered. For most individual investors, this concentrates ABSD driven purchases among buyers with substantial cash reserves, since the duty is due in cash shortly after signing, separate from any mortgage.

Safety: HDB Is Not an Option for Foreigners

Foreigners who are not citizens or Permanent Residents cannot buy an HDB flat under any scheme. A foreign buyer's options in Singapore residential property are limited to private condominiums and, in restricted categories, landed property that requires separate approval under the Residential Property Act. This is a hard eligibility boundary, not a pricing consideration.

Timing: Holding Periods and ABSD Relief

ABSD relief for a foreign buyer is narrow. In practice, a refund generally requires a change in status, such as later becoming a Singapore Permanent Resident or citizen, and specific conditions apply. There is no general time based reduction in the ABSD rate itself simply for holding the property longer. Any investor modelling a purchase should plan on the full 60 percent being a permanent, non recoverable cost unless a status change genuinely applies to their situation, and should verify current relief conditions directly with IRAS rather than assuming eligibility.

Money: Financing and Currency Considerations

Singapore's loan to value limits apply regardless of nationality, generally up to 75 percent for a first residential mortgage from a bank, though individual banks often apply stricter internal criteria to foreign buyers without local income, which can mean a lower loan quantum in practice. A foreign investor holding a Singapore dollar asset while earning income in another currency also carries currency risk: if the Singapore dollar weakens against the investor's home currency, property appreciation in Singapore dollar terms can be partly or fully offset when converted back. This is a real risk to plan for, not a return to promise, and no fixed hedging cost or expected return should be assumed without your own bank or adviser's current pricing.

Timing: When ABSD Is Actually Assessed

The ABSD rate that applies is the one in force, and the buyer's status as it stands, on the date the Sale and Purchase Agreement is signed, not the date an earlier Option to Purchase was granted or the date price negotiations began. A buyer who expects a status change, such as an approaching grant of Permanent Residency, should understand that the rate is locked in at execution, so timing the signing relative to a genuine, confirmed status change is the only lever available, and this should never be based on an assumption that a status change will happen, only on a confirmed outcome.

Money: Ongoing Costs Beyond the Purchase

Beyond ABSD and Buyer's Stamp Duty at purchase, a foreign owner of Singapore residential property pays annual property tax based on the property's annual value, at rates set by IRAS, and if the property is rented out, the rental income is taxable in Singapore regardless of the owner's residency or citizenship. A non resident individual's rental income is generally taxed at a flat rate unless the owner qualifies to be assessed on the progressive resident scale, so check your specific tax residency position with IRAS or a tax adviser rather than assuming the resident rate applies.

Educational Only, Not a Deal Recommendation

None of the above is advice to buy or hold a specific property, and it is not a guarantee of any return, appreciation or rental yield. It is a description of the current rules so a prospective foreign buyer can model the real cash requirement before making an offer, and it should be checked against your own tax and legal advisers before you act.

Checklist for a Foreign Buyer

  • Confirm your citizenship against the treaty nationality list before assuming the 60 percent rate applies
  • Add ABSD and Buyer's Stamp Duty together to get your true cash requirement at signing
  • Check with your bank on the loan to value and documentation it actually offers to foreign buyers
  • Confirm HDB is not an option and plan within private property from the start
  • Speak with a tax adviser in your home country about currency and reporting implications

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the ABSD rate for foreigners buying property in Singapore?

Foreign buyers who are not citizens or Permanent Residents pay a flat 60 percent Additional Buyer's Stamp Duty on any residential property purchase, regardless of whether it is their first purchase. This rate has applied since 27 April 2023.

Are any nationalities exempt from the higher foreigner ABSD rate?

Nationals of the United States, Switzerland, Liechtenstein, Norway and Iceland are treated the same as Singapore Citizens for ABSD purposes under Singapore's Free Trade Agreement commitments, paying 0 percent on a first residential property, 20 percent on a second, and 30 percent on a third or subsequent property.

Does buying through a company reduce ABSD for a foreign buyer?

No. Entities, including companies, pay 65 percent ABSD on residential property, which is higher than the 60 percent personal rate for a foreign individual. A holding company structure increases the ABSD cost rather than reducing it.
