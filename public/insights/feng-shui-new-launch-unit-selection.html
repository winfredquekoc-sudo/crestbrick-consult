Picking a new launch unit by feng shui

By Winfred Quek · 10 minute read · Published 17 August 2026

By Winfred Quek · CEA R073319H · Published 17 August 2026

Quick answer: New launch feng shui screening happens on paper, before the building even exists, using the developer's site plan and floor plans rather than a physical walk through. Check the overall development's orientation and whether it sits at a T junction, then check your specific stack for facing, what it looks out onto, and proximity to the lift lobby, refuse chute or a transformer room. Water features and landscaping in front of a development are commonly discussed as favourable, though schools differ on how much weight to give exact placement. Buying a new launch unit by feng shui is a different exercise from buying resale. You cannot stand in the actual unit, watch the light move through it in the afternoon, or see what the neighbouring plot will eventually look like. Everything has to be read off paper: the site plan, the stack list, and the floor plan of your specific unit type. Done properly, this is not a disadvantage, since it means you can screen an entire development before the showflat even opens.

Why new launches are different from resale

A resale unit's surroundings are fixed and visible; you can walk the block and see exactly what it faces. A new launch's surroundings are partly promised and partly unknown: the developer's site plan shows what is planned, but neighbouring plots may still be undeveloped or under a different masterplan zoning that could change what eventually gets built next door. This means new launch feng shui screening leans more heavily on the site plan and the development's own layout than on the neighbourhood, at least until nearby plots are built out.

Timing also enters the picture in a way it does not for an older resale unit. Under the flying star system, a building's construction year sets a natal chart that stays with the building for its full twenty year period, so a launch completing in the current period carries a different chart from a building completed twenty years earlier. We go into this in detail in our Period 9 feng shui guide.

Reading the site plan

Start with the whole development's orientation relative to the main access road. A development entrance and main block facing directly into a T junction, where a road ends and points straight at the site, is one of the more consistently flagged concerns, the same as it is for an HDB block. A development set back from the road with some landscaping or a drop off court in front reads more favourably under most schools.

Water features are common in new launch landscaping, and this is a genuine point of disagreement between schools worth naming plainly. Compass School practitioners working with the flying star system often look for water in a specific sector relative to the building's chart, since water is associated with wealth qi in that framework. Form School practitioners tend to weight it more simply: is the water feature proportionate to the space, does it sit calmly rather than rushing toward the entrance, and does it look natural rather than ornamental for its own sake. Neither view treats "there is a pool, therefore it is auspicious" as sufficient on its own.

Stack selection factors

Once you are past the whole site, narrow down to your stack. Corner stacks generally get two facing directions and more light, similar to HDB corner units, but check what those two directions actually face: a corner stack looking into a neighbouring development's service yard has not gained much. Stacks positioned directly beside the lift lobby, the refuse chute, or a transformer or pump room are commonly avoided, both for the classical concern about qi and for the very practical concern about noise, smell and vibration.

Also check whether your stack faces a neighbouring block within the same development at close range, which can happen in denser sites with multiple towers, and whether the unit's main door lines up straight through to a balcony door or a full height window on the opposite side, the same through corridor concern covered in our floor plan guide.

Balloting and why a shortlist beats a single favourite

Singapore new launches are typically sold through an e application balloting process rather than a first come queue, which means popular stacks, feng shui favoured units included, often draw more applications than there are units available. If enough buyers apply for the same handful of stacks for similar reasons, whether feng shui, view, or simply corner unit appeal, you can be balloted out of your first choice entirely even with a valid application in early. Our balloting and e application guide covers the mechanics of that process in full.

The practical takeaway is to walk into a launch with a ranked shortlist of two or three acceptable stacks rather than fixating on a single unit, since a rigid single choice leaves you with nothing to fall back on if the ballot does not go your way. Apply the same screening method covered above to each stack on your shortlist, not just your top pick, so that whichever one you are eventually offered has already been checked rather than accepted under time pressure on the day.

Timing and why Period 9 comes up

If you are working with a practitioner who follows the flying star system, they will want to know the development's expected Temporary Occupation Permit year, since that is generally what sets the building's natal chart period. A launch completing within the current Period 9 window, running from 2024 to 2043, carries a different chart to an older Period 8 building completed between 2004 and 2023. What this means in practice, and where practitioners disagree on how much weight to give it, is covered fully in our dedicated Period 9 feng shui guide, since it is detailed enough to deserve its own treatment rather than a short summary here.

Working with showflat visits

A showflat is a marketing tool, not a preview of your actual unit's orientation. Many showflats are built off site as a generic mockup of a popular unit type, and some are deliberately mirrored to fit the display space, which means the door position and layout flow you experience in the showflat may not match your chosen stack at all. Always ask the sales team to pull up your specific stack on the site plan and confirm its actual compass facing, rather than assuming the showflat tells you anything about direction.

Sales staff are not feng shui practitioners. Some new launch sales teams are well briefed on facing and stack positions because buyers ask constantly; others will tell you what sounds reassuring rather than what is accurate. Verify facing against the actual site plan yourself, or bring a practitioner along to a private appointment if it matters enough to you.

New launch feng shui checklist

  • Check the development's overall orientation and whether it sits at a T junction
  • Check whether the site plan places water, if any, calmly rather than rushing at the entrance
  • Confirm your specific stack's compass facing directly from the site plan, not the showflat
  • Check what your stack's windows actually look out onto once other plots are built out
  • Check proximity to the lift lobby, refuse chute, or any transformer or pump room
  • Trace the unit's internal floor plan for a straight line from door to balcony or window
  • Ask the sales team for the expected completion year if construction period matters to you
  • Cross check any claims the sales team makes about facing against the printed site plan
  • Compare your shortlisted stacks against each other, not just against a single ideal
  • Revisit the unit's reading once you have both the site plan and the internal floor plan together

Frequently asked questions

Can I check feng shui before the unit is even built?

Yes, and in some ways it is easier than resale, since the developer's site plan and floor plans are available before launch. You can check the development's overall orientation, where your block sits relative to the main road and any T junction, and your stack's facing and internal layout entirely from paper. What you cannot check yet is the finished view once neighbouring plots are developed, which is a genuine limitation of buying off plan.

Does the showflat layout match the actual unit?

Not necessarily. Showflats are often built as a generic mockup of a unit type, sometimes even mirrored left to right for the display space available, and it may not sit on the actual site with the actual compass facing your chosen stack will have. Always ask the sales team for your specific stack's facing on the site plan rather than relying on the showflat's orientation.

Do water features in front of a development always help?

Not universally, and this is a point where schools genuinely differ. Water positioned correctly in front of a building, meaning in the appropriate sector and not rushing directly toward the entrance, is widely regarded as supportive of wealth qi by Compass School practitioners under the flying star system. Form School practitioners weight it differently, focusing more on whether the water feature is proportionate and calm rather than its exact sector placement. Either way, a pool you can see from your unit is not automatically favourable just because water is present.

What is a bad stack to avoid in a new launch?

The most commonly flagged stacks are ones directly facing the main road at a T junction, ones facing a neighbouring block's blank wall or service yard at close range, and ones sitting directly beside the lift lobby, refuse chute or transformer room, since these carry both the classical concern and a very practical noise or smell downside.

Does Period 9 matter when picking a new launch unit?

Some practitioners weight it heavily, others do not. Under the flying star system, a building's construction period sets its natal chart, so a new launch completing in the current Period 9 window carries a different chart to a resale unit built under Period 8. Form School practitioners generally weight physical facing, layout and surroundings just as highly or higher than the period chart. We cover Period 9 specifically in a dedicated guide.
