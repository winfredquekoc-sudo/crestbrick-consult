Beauty World: Bukit Timah's integrated transport hub redevelopment

By Winfred Quek, Associate Marketing Consultant · CEA R073319H · Crestbrick Pte Ltd (L31010886H) · Published 13 July 2026

Quick answer: Beauty World is being redeveloped around an integrated transport hub that brings the MRT station, a bus interchange, retail space and community facilities into one connected development, replacing the older, more fragmented arrangement of shops, bus stops and surface car parks. This is a Bukit Timah story on its own terms, distinct from the landed housing enclaves further along the district and from the separate Turf City redevelopment along Dunearn Road. For the low rise precinct immediately around the station, the upgrade means better walkability, more retail and a genuine amenity lift. For a buyer, the honest read is that a good part of that value is already anticipated by the market, so entry price and use case matter more than the redevelopment headline itself.

Bukit Timah is a district that means very different things depending on which corner you are standing in. Say the name to one buyer and they picture Good Class Bungalows off Sixth Avenue. Say it to another and they picture the low rise apartments and hawker fare around Beauty World and King Albert Park. Both are correct, and both are part of the same URA planning area, but they are not the same property market. This guide is specifically about the Beauty World corner, and specifically about what its integrated transport hub redevelopment changes for the precinct immediately around it.

What the Beauty World redevelopment actually is

Beauty World MRT station sits on the Downtown Line, serving a precinct that has long combined ageing shopping centres, a busy bus interchange, hawker centres and a mix of low rise apartments and older condominiums. The redevelopment plan brings these pieces together into an integrated hub, the same model Singapore has used successfully at several other stations: rail, buses, retail and community space stitched into a single walkable development rather than left as separate, disconnected structures.

For residents, the practical change is simple to picture. Instead of crossing a busy junction between a bus stop, an ageing mall and the MRT entrance, the hub design consolidates these into one connected precinct, cutting the friction of daily commuting and errands. That kind of streetscape upgrade is a genuine quality of life improvement, independent of anything happening to prices.

Why this is a distinct story from the rest of Bukit Timah

It is worth being precise about geography here, because Bukit Timah gets talked about as if it were one market. The landed housing and Good Class Bungalow areas near Sixth Avenue, Swiss Club Road and the Bukit Timah nature reserve fringe are an entirely different asset class, priced and transacted on their own terms. Separately, the Turf City site further along Dunearn Road is undergoing its own redevelopment story, which I cover in depth in my Turf City transformation guide. Beauty World is neither of those. It is the district's transport and retail spine, a walkable, MRT anchored node serving the surrounding low rise apartments and older condominiums, and its redevelopment is specifically about strengthening that hub function.

The low rise precinct around the hub

The immediate catchment around Beauty World is dominated by older walk up apartments and a number of established condominium developments, many built decades ago when the area's low rise character was set by planning guidelines that have kept building heights modest compared to the newer, denser estates further out. That has preserved a quieter, more established residential feel right next to a genuinely busy transport node, which is an unusual combination in Singapore. As the hub redevelopment progresses, the precinct's core appeal, walkability to rail plus a low density residential character, is likely to be reinforced rather than replaced, since the redevelopment is focused on the hub itself rather than a wholesale rezoning of the surrounding low rise blocks.

The honest risks and misconceptions

Anticipation often runs ahead of completion. Markets tend to price in a known redevelopment well before it finishes, particularly one as visible and long discussed as an MRT hub upgrade. If you are buying specifically because of the hub story, be clear eyed about how much of that value the current price already reflects, and about the construction period disruption, noise, dust, altered pedestrian routes, that typically accompanies a project like this before it delivers the benefit.

A second misconception is treating Beauty World as interchangeable with the rest of Bukit Timah. The landed enclaves nearby do not share the same demand drivers, buyer profile or price behaviour as the apartments and condominiums around the hub, and conflating the two leads to poor comparisons. Benchmark Beauty World against other MRT anchored, low rise residential nodes across the island, not against Bukit Timah's landed market.

How to decide if this fits your plan

  1. Separate the hub story from the district story. You are evaluating a transport and retail upgrade in one corner of Bukit Timah, not the district's landed market as a whole.
  2. Check building age and lease type carefully. Many nearby developments are older; a strong location does not offset a short lease or a building nearing the end of its practical life without renovation.
  3. Discount for anticipation already priced in. Ask what specifically has not yet been reflected in current asking prices before assuming the redevelopment is your edge.
  4. Weigh construction period disruption against your holding horizon. If you plan to hold long term, short term disruption matters less than if you need to sell or rent during the works.

Frequently asked questions

What is the Beauty World integrated transport hub?

It is a redevelopment plan around Beauty World MRT station on the Downtown Line that combines the train station, a bus interchange, retail space and community facilities into a single integrated development, similar in concept to hubs built at other MRT nodes around Singapore. It replaces the older, more fragmented arrangement of bus stops, ageing shopping centres and surface car parks that has characterised the area for decades.

How is Beauty World different from the rest of Bukit Timah?

Bukit Timah as a district spans very different characters, from the landed housing and Good Class Bungalow areas near Sixth Avenue and Swiss Club Road, to the low rise precinct around Beauty World and King Albert Park, to the redevelopment story unfolding separately at Turf City further along Dunearn Road. Beauty World is the district's transport and retail node, a walkable, MRT anchored pocket rather than a low density residential enclave, and its redevelopment is specifically about that hub function.

Will the redevelopment affect nearby home values?

An integrated transport hub typically improves the immediate walking catchment around a station by upgrading retail, connectivity and street level amenity, which tends to support values for homes within that catchment over time. It does not automatically lift the wider Bukit Timah low rise precinct uniformly, since older walk up apartments, landed homes and newer condominiums each respond to different demand drivers. Treat the hub as one input among several, not a guarantee.

Is Beauty World a good place to buy now?

It depends on what you are buying and why. For an owner occupier who values Bukit Timah's schools, greenery and MRT access, the redevelopment is a genuine quality of life upgrade regardless of price movement. For an investor, the honest read is that construction periods around a redevelopment can bring short term disruption, and much of the long term upside from an improved hub is often anticipated by the market well before completion, so entry price discipline matters more than timing the announcement.
