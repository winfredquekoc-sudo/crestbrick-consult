Retrenched with a mortgage: what relief options exist

By Winfred Quek · 9 minute read · Published 13 July 2026

By Winfred Quek, Associate Marketing Consultant · CEA R073319H · Crestbrick Pte Ltd (L31010886H) · Published 13 July 2026

Quick answer: If you are retrenched with an active mortgage, the single most important move is contacting your bank before you miss a payment, not after. Banks generally have real flexibility to restructure a loan, extend tenure, offer a temporary interest only period, or arrange a payment holiday, for customers who engage proactively. Alongside that, check your CPF Ordinary Account balance against your property's Valuation Limit and Withdrawal Limit, since continued CPF usage can bridge part of the gap. There is no standing government scheme purely for job loss outside exceptional circumstances, so your own preparation, cash runway and early conversation with the bank do the real work.

I have sat across from clients in this exact position more than once: a stable income disappears overnight, and a mortgage that felt entirely manageable a month ago suddenly feels like the biggest risk in the household. The instinct many people have is to go quiet, hoping to sort it out before anyone notices. That instinct is almost always the wrong one. What follows is the realistic, non alarmist sequence I walk clients through when a job loss meets an active mortgage.

Why speed matters more than the size of the problem

Banks in Singapore are, in practice, far more willing to work with a borrower who reaches out at the first sign of trouble than one who has already missed one or two payments. A proactive conversation signals that you are managing the situation; a missed payment followed by silence signals risk, and risk triggers a more rigid, less forgiving process on the bank's side. The gap between these two paths is often the difference between a manageable restructuring and a genuinely difficult recovery.

This is true whether your loan is with a bank or with HDB. Reach out as soon as retrenchment is confirmed, not after you have exhausted your savings trying to keep payments going on your own. Waiting until you are truly desperate removes options that were available to you earlier.

What to actually say to your bank

Be direct and factual: you have been retrenched, here is your expected timeline for finding new income, and you want to understand what restructuring options exist. Banks commonly have several tools available, though exact terms vary by institution and by your specific loan: temporarily reducing instalments, switching to an interest only period for a defined window, extending the loan tenure to lower monthly payments, or in some cases a short payment deferment. None of these are guaranteed, and all of them typically require you to demonstrate a credible plan for resuming full payments, but none of them are available to a borrower who has not asked.

Get any agreement in writing before you rely on it, and understand exactly what it costs you longer term, whether that is more interest paid over the life of the loan from an extended tenure, or exit terms if you are still within a mortgage lock in period. For how lock in periods interact with any restructuring decision, see my mortgage lock in guide.

Using your CPF Ordinary Account as a bridge

If you have been using CPF Ordinary Account funds toward your mortgage, that usage does not automatically stop when your income does, provided you have not exhausted the Valuation Limit or Withdrawal Limit tied to the property. CPF housing usage draws down your accumulated OA balance rather than requiring active monthly contributions, so an existing balance can continue to cover instalments for a period even without employment income. This is a genuine, immediate lever, but it is finite: once your OA balance runs low or the limits are reached, the shortfall needs to be covered in cash, which brings you back to needing a plan with your bank or a source of income.

Do not empty your CPF blindly. Running your Ordinary Account balance to zero to keep mortgage payments flowing without a parallel plan for restoring income can leave you with no buffer left when a genuinely urgent need arises. Use CPF as one part of a considered plan, not a way to avoid a difficult conversation with your bank.

Refinancing and repricing as a longer term move

If your retrenchment coincides with, or is followed shortly by, a period where you regain some income but at a lower level, it is worth revisiting whether your current loan package is still the right fit. Refinancing to a different bank, or repricing within your existing bank, can sometimes lower your monthly instalment meaningfully, particularly if your original package was taken out when rates or terms were less favourable. This is not an immediate crisis response, since refinancing takes time and typically requires demonstrable income, but it belongs in your medium term recovery plan. For the mechanics and when each makes sense, see my refinancing guide.

What happens if you do nothing

It is worth being honest about the downside path, because understanding it is what motivates early action. Missed mortgage payments typically escalate through a series of reminder notices from the bank or HDB. Continued non payment without an agreed plan can eventually lead to default proceedings and, in the most serious cases, forced sale of the property to recover the outstanding loan. This is not a fast process, and there are usually multiple points where engaging the bank can still change the outcome, but each missed payment without contact narrows those options. For what a full inability to keep paying can eventually mean, see my guide on what happens if you cannot pay your mortgage.

Building the plan, step by step

  1. Contact your bank or HDB immediately. Do this in the first days after retrenchment is confirmed, not after your first missed payment.
  2. Check your CPF Ordinary Account balance and limits. Understand how many months of instalments it can realistically cover before cash is required.
  3. Build a bare bones household budget. Know exactly what your minimum cash runway looks like, including the mortgage, so any restructuring conversation is grounded in real numbers.
  4. Ask your bank about every restructuring tool available. Reduced instalments, interest only periods, and tenure extension are common; ask specifically rather than assuming only one option exists.
  5. Revisit refinancing once income stabilises. A lower rate or a longer tenure taken deliberately, rather than under duress, can permanently ease your monthly burden.

Frequently asked questions

What should I do first if I am retrenched and have a mortgage?

Contact your bank before you miss a single payment. Banks in Singapore generally have far more flexibility to restructure a loan for a customer who reaches out proactively than for one who has already defaulted. Explain your situation honestly, ask what restructuring options exist, and get any agreement in writing before you rely on it.

Can I use CPF to cover mortgage payments if I lose my job?

If you have CPF Ordinary Account funds and have not exhausted the Valuation Limit or Withdrawal Limit on the property, you can generally continue using OA funds toward monthly instalments even without active employment income, since CPF usage for housing draws on your accumulated balance rather than new contributions. Once those limits are reached, or your OA balance runs low, you will need to cover instalments in cash, which is where early bank engagement becomes critical.

What happens if I simply stop paying my mortgage?

Missing payments without engaging your bank is the worst path. After a period of missed instalments, typically escalating through reminder notices, the bank can begin default and eventually foreclosure proceedings, which for an HDB loan or bank loan can ultimately lead to forced sale of the property. Engaging the bank early, even with an imperfect plan, is materially better than silence.

Are there government relief schemes specifically for mortgage payments during unemployment?

Singapore does not run a standing, dedicated mortgage relief programme purely for job loss outside of exceptional, time limited schemes announced during specific crises. The consistent, always available levers are direct negotiation with your bank on restructuring, your own CPF Ordinary Account balance, and, for HDB owners in genuine hardship, HDB's own assistance channels for HDB loan holders. Check current official guidance, since schemes and eligibility can change.
