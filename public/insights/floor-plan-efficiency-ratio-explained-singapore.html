Floor plan efficiency ratio: why two 800 sqft units can feel different

By Winfred Quek · 8 minute read · Published 13 July 2026

By Winfred Quek, Associate Marketing Consultant · CEA R073319H · Crestbrick Pte Ltd (L31010886H) · Published 13 July 2026

Quick answer: Efficiency ratio is the proportion of a unit's strata floor area, the number quoted in listings and used to calculate PSF, that is actually usable internal living space, after accounting for structural columns, air conditioning ledges, bay windows, planter boxes and similar nonfunctional areas that are included in the strata title but do not translate into furnishable space. Two units of identical strata size can have meaningfully different efficiency ratios depending on layout design, and the less efficient one delivers less real living space for the same price. This matters more than headline size alone, because you live in usable area, not strata area, and a savvy buyer checks the floor plan closely rather than trusting the quoted square footage at face value.

I have walked clients through units that were quoted as the same size on paper and felt nothing alike in person. One felt genuinely spacious, with a layout that used almost every square foot for something functional. The other, identical strata area, felt cramped, with a chunky air conditioning ledge eating into the living room and a bay window along the bedroom that looked good on the floor plan but held nothing but a decorative cushion in real life. That difference has a name, efficiency ratio, and understanding it changes how you should be reading floor plans, not just headline sizes.

What strata area actually includes

Strata area is the total floor area attributed to a unit under its strata title, and it is the number that appears in listings, transaction records, and the PSF calculation everyone anchors on. Critically, strata area is not the same thing as usable, furnishable living space. It typically includes structural elements like columns and walls, and depending on the development's design, can also include air conditioning ledges, bay windows, planter boxes, and in some layouts, a share of void space or other partly functional areas that contribute to the quoted size without contributing meaningfully to how you actually live in the unit.

None of this is improper or hidden; it is simply how strata area has always been defined and quoted. The issue is that most buyers treat the quoted square footage as a proxy for how much living space they are getting, when it is really a proxy for how much you are being charged for, which is a related but distinct question.

Why the gap varies so much between developments

Efficiency ratio is not a fixed feature of Singapore private property; it varies significantly by development and even by unit type within the same development. Older developments and simpler, boxier layouts tend to have less wasted area, since air conditioning ledges and bay windows became more prevalent design features in newer launches over recent decades. A development that maximises marketable strata area through generous bay windows and prominent air conditioning ledges will show a materially lower efficiency ratio than one with a more compact, no frills layout, even at an identical headline PSF and strata size.

This is also why comparing two projects purely on PSF, without checking efficiency ratio, can mislead you about which one actually offers better value. My PSF versus quantum guide covers the broader framework for reading price comparisons correctly, and efficiency ratio is one of the most common hidden factors that explains why a seemingly cheaper PSF unit does not always feel like better value once you are standing inside it.

How to actually read a floor plan for efficiency

Most developer floor plans, if you look at them closely rather than glancing at the headline size, mark out air conditioning ledges, bay windows, and planter boxes as distinct shaded or hatched areas separate from the main living space. Add up the approximate proportion of the total floor plate these areas occupy, and you have a rough, workable estimate of how much of the quoted strata area is genuinely usable. A unit where these areas make up a small fraction of the total is more efficient than one where they eat up a larger share, even if both are quoted at the identical strata size.

Walk the actual unit, or a showflat of the same layout, whenever possible. A floor plan estimate is a useful screening tool, but nothing substitutes for standing in the space and judging whether the living areas, bedrooms and kitchen feel genuinely proportionate to the quoted size. Some layouts that look fine on paper reveal awkward furniture placement, tight circulation, or a bedroom that cannot fit a standard bed frame comfortably, none of which show up clearly in a two dimensional plan.

Where efficiency ratio matters most

The impact of efficiency ratio compounds at smaller unit sizes, where every square foot of waste is a proportionally larger share of the total. A one or two bedroom unit with a bulky air conditioning ledge and a deep bay window can lose a meaningful chunk of its already limited footprint to nonfunctional area, while the same design choices in a much larger unit are proportionally less painful. This is one reason small, efficiently laid out units can feel surprisingly livable while larger, less efficient units of a similar effective usable size do not necessarily feel more spacious. My small unit versus big unit investment guide covers this dynamic from the investment angle, and efficiency ratio is a big part of why the comparison is not as simple as size alone suggests.

Efficiency ratio also matters more for dual key and unusual layout configurations, where the floor plan is doing more structural work to divide the unit, sometimes at the cost of additional wall area and circulation space that further erodes usable proportion. If you are considering a dual key or similarly configured unit, scrutinise the floor plan even more closely than usual, since my dual key condo guide covers how these layouts trade flexibility for some efficiency loss.

A practical checklist before you compare units on size

  1. Pull the actual floor plan, not just the headline strata size, for every unit you are seriously comparing.
  2. Identify and estimate the nonfunctional area, air conditioning ledges, bay windows, planter boxes, as a proportion of the total floor plate.
  3. Compare efficiency ratio alongside PSF, not instead of it, since a lower PSF on a less efficient layout may not actually be cheaper on a usable space basis.
  4. Walk the actual unit or an identical showflat layout before finalising, since a floor plan estimate is a screening tool, not a substitute for standing in the space.
  5. Weigh the impact more heavily on smaller units, where wasted area is a larger proportional loss of your already limited footprint.

Frequently asked questions

What is floor plan efficiency ratio?

Efficiency ratio is the proportion of a unit's strata floor area that is actually usable internal living space, after accounting for walls, structural columns, air conditioning ledges, bay windows and planter boxes. It is usually expressed as usable area divided by strata area. A higher ratio means more of what you pay for is genuinely furnishable space.

Why do two units of the same strata size feel so different?

Because strata area includes items that are not functional living space, structural columns, air conditioning ledges and bay windows among them. A unit with a compact, boxy layout and minimal wasted area will feel noticeably larger to live in than one of the identical strata size with an awkward layout or a bulky air conditioning ledge.

How can I estimate a unit's efficiency ratio from a floor plan?

Identify areas marked separately as air conditioning ledge, bay window, planter box or void on the floor plan, estimate what proportion of the total floor plate they occupy, and subtract that from the whole. Developments with detailed, clearly marked floor plans make this far easier to estimate than simplified layouts.

Does efficiency ratio matter for HDB flats too?

Less so, since HDB flat floor areas are generally quoted as more straightforwardly usable space without the same prevalence of bay windows, planter boxes and expansive air conditioning ledges common in private condominium design. Efficiency ratio is overwhelmingly a private condominium consideration.
