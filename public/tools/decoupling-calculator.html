Frequently asked questions

What is property decoupling and how does it work?

Decoupling means transferring one spouse's share of a jointly held property to the other, so only one name remains on it. The exiting spouse then counts as owning no property and can buy a second home at the lower first property ABSD rate. It is a restructuring strategy, not a loophole, and the numbers must justify the costs.

How much does decoupling cost in Singapore?

The main costs are legal fees and BSD on the value of the share being transferred, typically totalling around S$5,000 to S$8,000. There may also be loan restructuring and CPF refund implications. This calculator subtracts those transfer costs from the ABSD you would save to show your net benefit clearly.

When is decoupling worth doing?

As a rule of thumb in this tool, a net benefit above S$15,000 is clearly worth exploring, between S$1 and S$15,000 is marginal and depends on your wider plans, and below zero means transfer costs exceed the ABSD saved. The verdict turns on your property value and ABSD rate, which you enter to get an instant read.

Does decoupling affect my CPF and home loan?

Yes, and these sit outside the headline ABSD maths. The exiting spouse usually has to refund CPF used, with accrued interest, back into their CPF account, and the remaining owner must qualify for the loan on their own income. These factors can change the decision, so they are worth modelling before you proceed.

Is decoupling legal for HDB flats?

HDB stopped allowing part share transfers between owners except in specific circumstances such as divorce, so decoupling is generally a private property strategy rather than an HDB one. This calculator is built for SC, PR and foreigner profiles on private homes. If you hold an HDB flat, the right path is usually different and worth a conversation.
