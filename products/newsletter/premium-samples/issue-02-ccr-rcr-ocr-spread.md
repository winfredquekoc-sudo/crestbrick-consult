# Premium Issue #02 — The CCR/RCR/OCR PSF spread, and what it tells you about cycle position

**Tier:** Crestbrick Premium ($29/mo)
**Length:** ~1,820 words
**Single topic:** Reading the spread between Core Central, Rest of Central, and Outside Central Region PSF as a leading indicator

---

Most retail buyers in Singapore look at one number when they evaluate a market: median PSF. They check it on PropertyGuru, see it's gone up, and conclude the market is hot. Or they see it's flat and conclude the market is dead.

Median PSF tells you almost nothing on its own. The spread between regions tells you almost everything.

This issue is about one spread in particular: the gap between Core Central Region (CCR), Rest of Central Region (RCR), and Outside Central Region (OCR) median private non-landed PSF. I have been tracking this spread monthly since 2017. It is, in my view, the single most useful sentiment gauge available to a Singapore property investor — and it is freely available from URA's REALIS portal.

Here is what it has told me, and what it is telling me right now.

---

## What the spread is, mechanically

URA publishes quarterly transacted prices for new sale and resale private non-landed homes, segmented into:

- **CCR** — Districts 9, 10, 11, Downtown Core, Sentosa. The "prime" segment.
- **RCR** — Districts 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 7, 8, 12, 13, 14, 15, 20. The "city fringe."
- **OCR** — Everything else. The "heartland private."

Take the median PSF for each. Compute two spreads:

- **CCR − OCR spread** (the wide gauge)
- **CCR − RCR spread** (the narrow gauge)

I render this as a clean line chart with three traces — CCR (top), RCR (middle), OCR (bottom) — overlaid with a fourth trace showing CCR-minus-OCR PSF gap on a secondary axis. Quarterly data, x-axis from 2010 to current quarter. Shaded vertical bands mark cooling-measure dates (2013 TDSR, 2018 ABSD hike, 2021 ABSD hike, 2023 ABSD hike, 2024 LTV change). The picture that emerges is not random. It cycles.

---

## What the spread has done — a 15-year readback

Indicative quarterly figures, drawn from URA quarterly real estate statistics. Numbers are representative of trend, not exact:

| Period | CCR PSF | RCR PSF | OCR PSF | CCR − OCR | CCR − RCR |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2010 Q4 (post-GFC recovery) | $2,350 | $1,400 | $950 | $1,400 | $950 |
| 2013 Q3 (pre-TDSR peak) | $2,510 | $1,580 | $1,140 | $1,370 | $930 |
| 2017 Q2 (en-bloc euphoria starts) | $2,290 | $1,640 | $1,150 | $1,140 | $650 |
| 2018 Q3 (post-ABSD hike) | $2,420 | $1,790 | $1,290 | $1,130 | $630 |
| 2021 Q4 (post-COVID surge) | $2,510 | $2,100 | $1,490 | $1,020 | $410 |
| 2023 Q2 (post-ABSD hike to 60% for foreigners) | $2,610 | $2,300 | $1,720 | $890 | $310 |
| 2025 Q4 (current cycle) | $2,840 | $2,520 | $1,960 | $880 | $320 |

Two patterns jump out.

**Pattern 1 — the CCR-OCR spread compresses across cycles.** In 2010, OCR traded at a 60% discount to CCR. By 2025, it trades at a 31% discount. OCR has structurally caught up.

**Pattern 2 — the CCR-RCR spread compresses faster, and at extremes, signals cycle position.** The spread sat around $900–$950 throughout the 2010–2014 cycle. It collapsed to $400 in 2021 and stayed sub-$350 through 2023–2025. *Compression of this spread historically precedes either a CCR rally or an OCR/RCR cooldown.* It rarely stays compressed for long.

---

## What the spread reveals about market mood

When the CCR-OCR spread is **wide** (>$1,200): the market is in a "flight to quality" or "early-cycle" phase. CCR holds value; OCR lags. Buyers are cautious. Foreigners are present in CCR. Locals are buying in OCR for owner-occupation, not investment. *This is when you should be looking at OCR new launches in the right districts.*

When the spread **compresses to mid-range** ($800–$1,100): the market is in mid-cycle. Money has rotated outward. RCR and OCR are leading on momentum. CCR is consolidating. *This is the phase where most retail "investor" buyers enter — and historically the worst time to buy OCR for capital appreciation, because the gap has already closed.*

When the spread is **tight** (<$800): late cycle. OCR has run. The narrative says "heartland private is the new mass-market gold." Foreigners have largely exited CCR after ABSD hikes. *This is when CCR starts to look interesting again — not because it will surge, but because the asymmetry has flipped. Downside in OCR exceeds downside in CCR for the first time in years.*

We are in the third regime right now. CCR-OCR sits at $880. CCR-RCR sits at $320. The spread has been this tight only twice before in the last 15 years: 2007 (pre-GFC) and 2013 (pre-TDSR). Both times, the OCR-to-CCR rotation reversed within 18 months.

I am not predicting a crash. I am telling you what the spread has done historically when it has reached this position.

---

## The mechanism — why the spread compresses, and why it matters

The CCR-OCR spread compresses for three reasons:

1. **Cooling measures hit foreign demand harder than local demand.** ABSD on foreigners is now 60%. CCR has historically depended on foreign capital. When that capital exits, CCR PSF stalls.
2. **Local upgraders chase OCR/RCR new launches.** HDB upgraders flush with $300k–$600k of CPF and resale gains push OCR PSF upward. There are far more of them than there are foreign CCR buyers.
3. **Developer land bids reflect this.** Recent GLS sites in OCR (Lentor, Tampines, Pasir Ris) have been bid at land rates that imply break-even PSFs in the $2,200–$2,500 range. That is RCR territory from five years ago.

The compression is not irrational. It is the visible price of a structural shift in who is buying Singapore property. But "not irrational" is not the same as "sustainable." Spreads that compress this far have always either widened (CCR catches a bid) or collapsed further with OCR coming down.

---

## Action — when the spread narrows below $800, do this

This is the actionable rule I now use, and which I am putting in front of premium subscribers explicitly:

**When CCR-OCR spread closes below $800 PSF, AND CCR-RCR spread closes below $300 PSF, simultaneously, for two consecutive quarters:**

1. **Stop adding OCR exposure for capital appreciation.** Owner-occupation purchases are still fine. Investment purchases at this point in the cycle have historically underperformed for the next 36 months.
2. **Start screening CCR resale in Districts 9, 10, 15** for stock that has been listed >120 days, with motivated sellers. The arbitrage is in old freehold leasehold-equivalent stock that has been held since 2012–2014 and is now selling roughly flat to entry price. There is real value here, but it needs work to find.
3. **Raise the threshold for new-launch OCR purchases** to require >2.5% projected rental yield AND >$200 PSF below comparable RCR — not one or the other.
4. **Watch the URA Q1 2026 release** (scheduled late April). If CCR PSF prints above $2,900 and OCR prints flat or down, we have entered the rotation phase. That is the trigger to act.

---

## What I would do

If I were sitting on $1.5m of investment capital today, with no existing private exposure, I would not buy OCR new launch. I would wait one quarter for the URA Q1 2026 print, and if the spread holds tight, I would deploy 60% of that capital into a CCR resale freehold purchase in District 9 or 10, sub-$2,500 PSF, and 40% into cash awaiting a second window.

That is not advice for you. Your situation is different — your tax position, your loan headroom, your time horizon, your risk capacity. But the framework is the framework. The spread is the spread. Read it, don't ignore it, and don't let an agent tell you OCR is "still cheap" when the data on URA's own portal says the rotation has already happened.

The market doesn't ring a bell at the top. But it does narrow a spread.
