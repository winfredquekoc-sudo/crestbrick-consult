# Case Study — The $42K Mistake

**Module 1 deliverable · 4-Pillar Investor Course**
**Type:** Anonymised real client scenario
**Reading time:** ~8 minutes
**Purpose:** Show what agent-mind actually costs, on a normal Singaporean household — not an outlier

---

## The household

- "K" and "M", married, both 34 at time of original purchase.
- Two children under 5.
- Combined income at purchase: ~$215K/year.
- Lived in a 4-room HDB in the East, fully paid down to ~$110K outstanding.
- Cash + CPF available for next move: ~$320K.
- Stated long-term goal: own a private property; eventually own a second for rental income before age 50.

A perfectly normal HDB-upgrader profile. The kind I see every week.

---

## The original decision (2021)

K met an agent through a colleague in early 2021. The agent showed him a 2-bedder OCR new launch, $1.45M, 99-year leasehold. The pitch was clean:

- "Strong location, MRT in 2026, schools nearby."
- "OCR is where the upside is — CCR's overpriced."
- "You can rent it out if you don't move in."

K signed. Joint name was the default suggested. Standard 75% loan. ABSD: $0 (first private for both). BSD: ~$36K. Done.

On paper, fine. The unit itself was reasonable. Pillar 1 (Affordability) and Pillar 3 (Cash Flow) were both green. The agent wasn't lying. He just wasn't *complete*.

What nobody mapped: K and M had a stated plan to buy a second property within 5 years, for rental.

---

## The audit, two years later (2023)

K came to me in mid-2023 because the second-property plan was now active. M wanted to use her clean name to buy a $1.05M 1-bedder in the RCR.

Here's what we found, running the 4-Pillar audit retrospectively on the 2021 purchase:

### Pillar 1 — Affordability: 8/10

Fine. Stress-tested through the +200bps move that actually arrived in 2022–2023. Loan was sustainable. No issue here.

### Pillar 2 — Structure: 3/10. The killer.

This is where the $42K vanished.

Because the 2021 OCR purchase was in **joint name**:

- For the planned second property under M's name — *technically her first* — the joint ownership of the first property meant **she was treated as already owning property** for ABSD purposes.
- Result: 20% ABSD on the $1.05M second property = **$210K**.
- Versus the alternative scenario, had the 2021 unit been single-name (K only): M's first private would have been ABSD-free.

The "fix" we discussed:

**Option 1 — decouple the 2021 unit.** Transfer M's half to K. BSD on the transfer half (~$725K portion): **~$23K**. Plus legal fees ~$4K. Plus refinancing costs ~$3K. Plus IRAS scrutiny risk under the post-2023 99-1 guidance — they had to do a clean, full transfer, not a contrived 1% slice.

**Total decoupling cost: ~$30K** to reopen the structure. Then M proceeds with the $1.05M as her first private. ABSD-free.

**Option 2 — sell-then-buy.** Sell the 2021 unit. SSD risk (held <3 years): **12% × $1.45M = ~$174K** if sold in 2023. Killer. Discarded.

**Option 3 — buy second under M anyway and pay full ABSD.** $210K out the door. Discarded.

Option 1 was the only viable path. They took it. Total avoidable cost vs. a counterfactual where the 2021 purchase had been single-name K from day one:

- Decoupling BSD: ~$23K
- Legal fees: ~$4K
- Refinancing penalty: ~$3K
- Compliance/CPF reset friction: ~$12K of opportunity cost on locked CPF
- **Total: ~$42K, plus 4 months of delay on the second purchase.**

### Pillar 3 — Cash Flow: 7/10

Owner-occupied through 2021–2023, became rental in 2024. Yield decent enough. No major issue.

### Pillar 4 — Exit: 4/10

Never written. "We'll see in 7–8 years." Buyer pool not analysed. Exit triggers not defined. Not a *current* failure but a deferred one.

---

## What it would have looked like with the 4-Pillar audit upfront

Same household, same 2021 starting point, same property if they wanted it:

1. **Pillar 2 audit** would have flagged the M-second-property plan within the first 30 minutes.
2. Recommendation: 2021 unit goes in **single name (K only)**. Slightly tighter on Pillar 1 — single-income debt service on the loan — but stress-test still passes given household reserves.
3. M's name stays clean for the planned 2024–2026 second purchase.
4. **Pillar 4 audit** would have produced a written exit memo: "Hold to year 8, sell to fund retirement-stage downsizing or hold as legacy." Trigger: -15% from peak, or rental yield below 2.5% net for 18 consecutive months.
5. Net 2021 cost difference: $0 extra. Net 2026 outcome: $42K saved, second property purchased on schedule.

That's the entire delta. One Pillar, audited upfront, would have saved a year's worth of school fees.

---

## The lesson

K and M did not get bad advice. They got *agent-mind* advice — complete on the unit, blind to the portfolio.

The $42K is not the worst-case version of this story. I see worse. I've seen households pay $90K+ in avoidable ABSD because the first private was structured wrong, and the SSD window made decoupling worse than just paying. I've seen people lock themselves into legacy-hold by mistake when they wanted a cash-out.

But $42K is the *normal* cost. It is what a household of this profile should expect to leak across a 5–10 year horizon when no Pillar 2 audit happens upfront.

The 4-Pillar audit is a 90-minute exercise. The math on $42K saved across 90 minutes of upfront work is — well, you can compound it yourself.
