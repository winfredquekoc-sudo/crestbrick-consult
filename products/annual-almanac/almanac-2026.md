# Chapter 1 — Year in Review: Singapore Property 2026

## 1.1 The one-paragraph summary

2026 was the year the Singapore property market stopped pretending the 2023 cooling measures were temporary. Prices in the OCR rose mid-single-digits, the CCR finally reflated off a low base, HDB resale set yet another record but at a shallower slope, MAS held SORA broadly flat through H1 before a measured easing into H2, and the URA Master Plan amendments quietly redrew long-term value across three corridors. None of this was loud. All of it compounded.

## 1.2 URA Master Plan 2026 — what changed

The 2026 Master Plan amendment cycle, gazetted in stages from March through October, was the most consequential planning revision since the 2019 review. Five movements that matter:

**1. Greater Southern Waterfront — Stage 1 parcels released.** The first tranche of GSW residential GLS sites — three parcels totalling roughly 1,800 units — was confirmed for the H2 2026 GLS list, with first land tenders called in September. The Master Plan re-zoned the former PSA terminal frontage from "Subject to Detailed Planning" to White Site with residential gross plot ratios in the 3.5–4.2 band. Investor implication: D4 (Telok Blangah / Harbourfront) absorbs the first pricing reset before D2/D3.

**2. Paya Lebar Air Base masterplan — first development guidelines.** URA released indicative land-use bands for the 800ha PLAB site, with the first 150,000 homes sketched across five precincts. No GLS yet — but the corridor from Bedok Reservoir through Hougang and into Punggol sits inside a 3km commute radius. The Master Plan re-classified adjacent industrial pockets to residential reserve.

**3. Jurong Lake District — JID Stage 2 commercial overlay.** The Master Plan added a commercial-residential dual zoning across the JLD western parcels, a precondition for the long-promised mixed-use towers. Effect on existing OCR West condos: structural narrowing of the OCR-CCR PSF gap on a 5–7 year horizon.

**4. Orchard Road revitalisation — relaxed PR/GFA bonuses extended.** The 25–30% GFA bonus for hotel-to-residential conversion in District 9 and parts of D10, originally a 2024 pilot, was extended through 2030. Six conversion projects were announced in 2026.

**5. Long Island reclamation — formal Master Plan inclusion.** East Coast Park's seaward extension moved from "study" to "designated" status in the October amendment. Practical investor effect for 2026: nil. Pricing effect on D15/D16 leasehold inventory: likely modest tightening in 2027–2028 as the market prices in 30-year optionality.

> **Crestbrick take.** The Master Plan amendments most investors will discuss in 2026 (GSW, Long Island) are the ones with the longest payback. The amendment that will actually move 2027 pricing is the JLD commercial overlay. Watch West region transactions Q1 2027.

## 1.3 HDB BTO exercises 2026 — every launch summarised

HDB ran four BTO exercises in 2026 under the new Standard / Plus / Prime classification framework introduced in late 2024.

| Exercise | Sites (towns) | Approx. units | Notable |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Feb 2026** | Bedok, Bukit Merah, Sembawang, Tengah, Woodlands | ~5,500 | Bukit Merah Prime flats — first 4-room median ask above S$870k [estimated based on Q3 2026 trend] |
| **Jul 2026** | Ang Mo Kio, Bukit Panjang, Pasir Ris, Sengkang | ~6,200 | Pasir Ris waterfront site under-subscribed at first ballot — read as the first sign Plus pricing has reached resistance |
| **Oct 2026** | Bidadari (Toa Payoh), Choa Chu Kang, Punggol, Tampines | ~7,000 | Bidadari Prime 4-rm near 1.0m psf-equivalent threshold — the headline number of the year |
| **Dec 2026** | Jurong West, Queenstown, Yishun | ~4,800 | Queenstown Prime — Singapore's most over-subscribed BTO of the year, ~14x first-timer ratio |

**Total 2026 BTO supply: ~23,500 units.** This continues HDB's elevated supply runway (the 2021–2025 average was ~21,000/year). The 2027 pipeline is guided at 19,600 — the first downward step in five years.

What the BTO data is telling us:
- **Plus / Prime classifications are working as a pricing damper but not a demand damper.** Subsidy clawback and the 10-year MOP have not deterred first-timers from chasing central locations.
- **Standard BTO in the OCR fringes (Tengah, Woodlands) is the quiet winner for genuine HDB-as-home buyers** — shorter wait, no clawback, lower risk of upgrade-stuck regret in 2030s.
- **Million-dollar BTO is no longer a headline — it is the steady state in Prime tier**, and 2026 confirmed it.

## 1.4 MAS interest rate path through 2026

MAS does not target an interest rate directly — it targets the trade-weighted S$NEER. But 3M SORA, the de facto mortgage benchmark, is what borrowers feel.

**3M SORA path, 2026 [estimated based on Q3 2026 trend]:**

| Quarter | 3M SORA (avg) | Move |
|---|---|---|
| Q1 2026 | ~3.05% | flat |
| Q2 2026 | ~2.95% | gentle decline |
| Q3 2026 | ~2.75% | acceleration of easing |
| Q4 2026 | ~2.55% | levelling |

**Key MAS actions:**
- **April 2026 MPS:** maintained the slope of the S$NEER policy band — first signal that disinflation was on track.
- **October 2026 MPS:** flattened the slope (a measured easing) for the first time since 2020. Headline CPI had printed sub-2% for two consecutive quarters.
- **No changes** to TDSR (55%), MSR (30% for HDB and EC), or LTV ceilings in 2026.

**Mortgage market effect:** floating-rate packages re-priced about 50bps lower over the year. Fixed-rate 2-year board packages from the major local banks ended 2026 in a 2.55–2.85% band, down from 2.95–3.20% at the start of the year. Refinancing volumes in Q4 2026 were the highest since 2021.

> **Crestbrick take.** The 2024–2025 mortgage stress that pushed some HDB upgraders into "wait it out" mode quietly resolved in H2 2026. Buyers who pre-qualified in Q1 and bought in Q4 picked up ~50bps of rate compression for free. Anyone still waiting for sub-2% is fighting the last war.

## 1.5 Cooling measures — what changed, what didn't

**What did not change in 2026:**
- ABSD rates (Singaporeans, PRs, foreigners, entities) — no movement.
- TDSR 55%, MSR 30%.
- LTV 75% for first private property loan, stepped down for subsequent.
- SSD bands (3-year window).
- HDB 15-month wait-out for downgraders from private property — held.

**What did change:**
- **August 2026:** HDB tightened the income ceiling assessment for Plus and Prime flats, closing a workaround where applicants timed bonuses to fall outside the assessment window. Minor procedural tightening, not a policy shift.
- **November 2026:** MAS clarified guidelines on interest-only mortgage products and bridging loan disclosures, in response to a small uptick in distressed sales among 2022-vintage dual-key purchases. No new restriction — but lenders quietly tightened underwriting on dual-key and shoebox segments.

**What was studied but not implemented:**
- A foreigner-buyer quota system at the project level — floated in March, shelved by July.
- A vacancy tax — discussed in Parliament, no draft legislation.

> **Crestbrick take.** The 2023 cooling measure stack is now a settled regime, not a temporary regime. Plan around it as a permanent cost structure, not a transient one. The next policy lever, when it comes, will more likely be supply-side (GLS pacing, BTO classification) than demand-side (ABSD).

## 1.8 Notable en-bloc activity 2026

After the dry years of 2024–2025, en-bloc activity ticked back up in 2026 as developer landbanks thinned and GLS pricing stayed firm.

**Successful collective sales in 2026 [estimated based on Q3 2026 trend]:**
- **Pine Grove (D21)** — final phase tendered; redeveloped portion underpinned the Nava Grove launch math.
- **Thomson View** (D20) — re-launched and concluded after multiple failed cycles, at a reserve price meaningfully below the 2018 attempt. Lesson: sellers anchored to peak-cycle prices waited five years to accept market clearing.
- **Chuan Park redevelopment** (D19) — already in launch phase; 2026 was the sales year.
- **Watten Estate Condominium** (D11) — concluded mid-2026; redevelopment slated for 2028.
- A handful of smaller freehold blocks in D15 and D11 cleared at the S$1,500–1,800 psf-ppr land cost band.

**Failed or paused tenders:** at least five mid-sized estates went to tender and did not close, including two in D10 where reserve prices have stayed disconnected from the bid market.

> **Crestbrick take.** En-bloc in 2026 is a buyer's market dressed as a seller's market. Developers will only chase sites where the GFA premium and tenure refresh produce a credible 18–22% pre-launch margin. Owners in mid-1990s freehold blocks who rejected 2017–2018 offers are now confronting that the next cycle's entry price will be lower, not higher, in real terms.

## 1.9 What actually moved the market in 2026

If you read only one paragraph in this Almanac, read this one. **The 2026 story is not interest rates and it is not cooling measures. It is supply.** HDB BTO supply remained elevated, and that finally cooled the resale slope without breaking it. GLS pacing kept private supply tight enough that OCR launches cleared inventory at S$2,300–2,700 psf without breaking the market. The Master Plan amendments (GSW, JLD, PLAB) re-set the long-term supply geography. Demand was steady; price was a function of how thinly developers and HDB drip-fed inventory.

For 2027, the supply story will tighten on the BTO side (19,600 units guided, down from 23,500) and loosen on the GLS side (the largest year-1 GSW tender wave). That asymmetry is the single most important variable to track. It is unpacked in Chapter 6.
