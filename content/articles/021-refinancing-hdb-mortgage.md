# Refinancing Your HDB Mortgage: When It Makes Sense

Refinancing an HDB mortgage means switching to a new lender or negotiating a lower rate with your current bank. With Singapore's volatile interest rates, refinancing can save $20,000–100,000+ over a 20-year loan if done at the right time.

## Why Refinance?

**Rate drops:** When SIBOR or bank prime lending rates fall, your old mortgage rate becomes expensive. A refinance locks in the new lower rate.

**Switch banks:** Different banks offer different rates. Your current bank's rate might be 0.3–0.8% higher than a competitor's.

**Loan restructure:** Change tenure (e.g., 20 to 25 years) to lower monthly payments, or shorten tenure to pay off faster.

## Example: $300,000 Mortgage, Rate Drop from 2.8% to 2.2%

**Current situation:**
- Loan balance: $250,000 (10 years into a 25-year mortgage)
- Current rate: 2.8%
- Current monthly payment: $1,180
- Remaining tenure: 15 years

**After refinancing to 2.2%:**
- New monthly payment: $1,070 (on same 15-year tenure)
- **Monthly savings: $110**
- **5-year savings: $6,600** (before refinancing costs)

**Refinancing costs:**
- Legal fees: $1,200–1,500
- Bank processing: $300–600
- Valuation (if required): $500–800
- **Total costs: ~$2,200–2,900**

**Break-even point:** 21–26 months. After that, you're in pure savings territory.

## Early Repayment Penalties

Not all HDB loans have penalties, but if yours does, factor it in:

**Penalty scenarios:**
- 0.25% of outstanding loan balance (e.g., $250k loan = $625 penalty)
- Or a flat fee ($1,000–2,000)
- Or a percentage of the interest you would have paid (rare, but check your loan agreement)

**If your penalty is $2,000:**
- Total refinancing cost: ~$4,200–4,900
- New break-even: 38–44 months (still under 4 years for a 0.6% rate drop)

## When NOT to Refinance

**Staying for less than 2 years:** Refinancing costs (~$2,200) don't pay off.

**Tiny rate drop (< 0.2%):** The monthly savings ($15–30) don't justify the costs.

**Late in your loan tenure:** If you have 3–5 years left, refinancing resets the loan clock. The interest you save might be offset by the longer payoff.

**Floating-to-fixed trade-off:** If you're moving from a floating rate to fixed, weigh the stability benefit against potentially higher rates.

## How to Refinance: Step by Step

1. **Check your current rate and terms:** Review your HDB loan agreement for early repayment penalties.

2. **Get competing offers:** Contact 3–5 banks. Most offer free rate quotes. Compare rates AND processing fees.

3. **Calculate your break-even:** (Refinancing costs) ÷ (Monthly savings) = months to break-even.

4. **Lock in the rate:** Once you accept an offer, ask for a rate lock (typically 30–60 days while paperwork processes).

5. **Complete legal and valuation:** The new bank will order a valuation. Your lawyer handles the discharge of the old mortgage and registration of the new one.

6. **Wait for drawdown:** Funds disburse, old loan is repaid, new loan begins. Most take 4–6 weeks.

## Refinancing Frequency

You can refinance multiple times, but only if rates justify it. A rule of thumb: **refinance if you save 0.4% or more and plan to stay in the property for 2+ years.**

**Smart investors** refinance every 2–3 years if rate environments shift, capturing compounding savings. But don't over-refinance—each refinance costs money and time.
