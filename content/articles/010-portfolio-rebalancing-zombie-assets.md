# Portfolio Rebalancing: Audit Framework and Zombie Asset Identification

Investors with multiple properties often end up with underperforming assets—"zombie properties" that generate poor cash flow, face maintenance issues, or are geographically misaligned with market trends. Most investors hold these out of inertia. A systematic audit reveals which assets should be reallocated.

## The Zombie Asset Definition

A zombie asset is a property that:
- Yields <2% gross (below benchmark)
- Faces chronic tenant turnover (>1 per year)
- Requires repeated major repairs ($10k+ per year)
- Is in a declining or flat market (no appreciation for 5+ years)
- Ties up capital better deployed elsewhere

Example: A $400k property yielding 1.8%, with tenant turnover every 10 months and $8k annual repairs. Net cash flow after costs: <$2k/year (~0.5% net yield). This is a zombie.

## Portfolio Audit Framework

**Step 1: Map All Holdings**
Create a table:
- Property address
- Purchase date and price
- Current market value
- Outstanding mortgage
- Annual rent (or vacancy if owner-occupied)
- Annual costs (tax, maintenance, insurance)
- Net annual cash flow
- Gross yield

**Example Portfolio:**
| Property | Purchase | Current | Mortgage | Rent/Year | Costs | Net Cash | Yield |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| HDB Tampines | 2016 | $450k | $0 | $27k | $3k | $24k | 6% |
| Condo Clementi | 2019 | $800k | $400k | $32k | $8k | $24k | 3% |
| Landed Jurong | 2020 | $950k | $600k | $36k | $10k | $26k | 2.8% |
| EC Bishan | 2022 | $550k | $350k | $20k | $6k | $14k | 2.5% |

**Step 2: Identify Underperformers**
- Properties yielding <2%: Zombies. Consider exit.
- Properties with consistent losses: Zombies. Exit immediately.
- Properties with tenure >8 years but <3% yield: Rebalancing candidates (possibly sell to capture appreciation, redeploy capital to higher-yield new purchases).

In the example: EC Bishan (2.5% yield, only 2 years held) and Jurong landed (2.8%) are underperformers.

**Step 3: Calculate Rebalancing Scenarios**

**Scenario A: Hold All**
- Current annual cash flow: $24k + $24k + $26k + $14k = $88k
- Current total debt: $1.35M
- Current portfolio value: $2.75M

**Scenario B: Sell Zombie (EC Bishan)**
- Sell EC at $550k (assume no appreciation; 2-year hold)
- Proceeds after ABSD (already paid), agent (2.5%), costs: ~$520k
- Use $520k to reduce mortgage on Clementi condo ($400k → $200k) OR deploy into higher-yield HDB rental
- New portfolio: 3 properties (HDB Tampines, Clementi condo, Jurong landed, plus upgraded Clementi mortgage)
- New annual cash flow (from freed capital): +$10-15k/year (interest savings on reduced mortgage)

**Scenario C: Sell Underperformer (Jurong Landed) & Redeploy**
- Sell Jurong at $950k
- Proceed after SSD (5+ year hold = 0%), agent (2.5%): ~$927k
- Use $200k to pay off EC Bishan mortgage; remaining $727k to buy higher-yield HDB rental ($400k at 5% yield = $20k/year)
- Free up $350k (EC mortgage no longer needed); use $150k to pay down Clementi mortgage
- New portfolio: 4 properties, lower debt, higher yield on new HDB rental

**The Calculation Detail (Scenario C):**
- Lost cash flow from Jurong: -$26k
- Gained cash flow from HDB rental: +$20k
- Interest saved (less debt): +$8k (approx)
- Net change: -$26k + $20k + $8k = -$2k/year downside initially

BUT:
- New HDB rental will appreciate 2-3%/year; Jurong landed was appreciating <1%/year
- Rebalanced portfolio is lighter on debt, lower risk
- 5-year outlook: HDB appreciation gain $(400k × 2% × 5) = $40k; Jurong land would have gained $(950k × 1% × 5) = $47.5k. Close.

Lesson: Rebalancing isn't always about immediate yield; it's about alignment with market trends and risk management.

## The Emotional Barrier

Most investors hold zombies because:
1. **Sunk cost fallacy:** "I've held this for 10 years; I can't sell now." (Irrelevant; what matters is forward returns)
2. **Appreciation hope:** "It'll appreciate eventually." (Possibly, but at what rate vs. alternatives?)
3. **"It's paid off":** "No mortgage, so cash flow is good." (Cash flow % on capital is what matters, not absolute cash)
4. **Complexity:** "Selling involves ABSD, agent fees, taxes—too complicated." (Yes, but the math says sell anyway)

## Rebalancing Triggers

Audit your portfolio annually and rebalance if:
- Any property yields <2% for 2+ consecutive years
- A property requires $15k+ in unexpected repairs (signals aging)
- A property experiences 2+ tenant defaults in a year (signals tenant mismatch)
- A property's location enters decline (new competing supply, infrastructure removal, economic shift)

## Checklist: Portfolio Audit & Rebalancing

- [ ] List all properties: address, price, mortgage, rent, costs
- [ ] Calculate yield for each (annual cash flow ÷ current value)
- [ ] Identify underperformers (<2% yield or negative cash flow)
- [ ] Model sell scenario for each zombie (tax, ABSD, agent fees, net proceeds)
- [ ] Model redeploy scenario (buy higher-yield replacement or reduce debt)
- [ ] Compare 5-year outlook: hold vs. sell/redeploy (appreciation + yield + risk)
- [ ] Execute rebalancing on underperformers
