# Bridging Loans: Buying Before You Sell Explained

A bridging loan is short-term financing that bridges the gap between buying a new property and selling your old one. It's expensive but solves a timing problem: you find the perfect upgrade, but your current property hasn't sold yet.

## How Bridging Loans Work

**Scenario:** 
- You own an HDB flat worth $400k (not yet sold)
- You want to buy a condo for $550k (found a perfect deal)
- Closing on the condo is in 2 months; your HDB sale will close in 4 months

**Problem:** You need $110k (down payment on condo) before your HDB closes.

**Solution:** Bridging loan for $110k covers the gap. When HDB sells in 4 months, proceeds repay the bridge.

## Bridging Loan Terms

**Loan amount:** Typically 80% of the property's value (or less)

**Interest rate:** 3–6% annually (higher than mortgages because of short term and risk)

**Tenure:** 6–12 months (very short compared to mortgages)

**Monthly payment:** Interest-only (principal due as lump sum at end)

**Security:** First mortgage on the new property being purchased

## Cost Example: $110,000 Bridge at 4.5% for 6 months

**Monthly interest:** $110,000 × 4.5% ÷ 12 = $412/month

**Total interest over 6 months:** $412 × 6 = $2,472

**Loan origination fees:** 1–2% = $1,100–2,200

**Legal fees:** $500–800

**Total cost to bridge:** $4,072–5,472

**Compare to:** If you delayed the condo purchase by 6 months, you'd miss the deal entirely—it might appreciate or be sold to another buyer.

## When Bridging Makes Sense

**Scenario 1: Perfect property appears, old property almost sold**
- Your HDB will sell in 3–4 months (agreement in principle, just waiting for completion)
- Dream condo has only 1–2 other offers coming; won't wait
- Bridge for $50–100k to cover the gap
- **Cost: $1,500–3,000 interest + fees**
- **Benefit: You secure the condo instead of losing it**

**Scenario 2: Rate environment is rising; lock in before rates spike**
- Rates have been low for years (2.3%); Fed is hiking
- Condo you want is at $500k; expect it to appreciate 5%+ when rates rise (lower buying power pushes prices up)
- Bridge to buy now; sell HDB over next 6 months at your leisure
- **Cost: $3,000–5,000 in bridge interest**
- **Benefit: You capture potential $25k+ appreciation + avoid rate spikes**

## When Bridging is a Bad Idea

**Scenario 1: Old property hasn't sold, no concrete offer**
- HDB has been on market 3 months, soft market, no offers
- You ASSUME it will sell in 4 months, but reality: it may take 9–12 months
- You bridge $100k, but after 6 months the HDB still hasn't sold
- **Result:** Bridge rolls over to 12 months; you pay $12,000+ in interest on a risky bet

**Scenario 2: Rates are dropping; no urgency to buy**
- Rates falling; condo prices might drop too
- Bridging costs $4,000; condo might drop 3–5% in value over 6 months
- **Result:** You pay $4,000 to capture a property that's depreciating

**Scenario 3: New property is speculative**
- You want to buy a condo "as an investment" but aren't sure about the area
- HDB is your only income-producing asset (if you're renting it out)
- Bridging ties up capital and creates risk if the investment underperforms
- **Result:** Overleveraged with no margin for error

## Bridging vs. Selling First

**Sell-first approach:**
- Sell HDB first (completes in 2–3 months)
- Use proceeds for condo down payment (no bridge needed)
- **Pro:** No bridge costs
- **Con:** May face timing gap (1–2 months between sales); have to rent/stay with family temporarily

**Bridge approach:**
- Buy condo now (secure the deal)
- Sell HDB over next 6 months (on your timeline, not market's)
- **Pro:** Secure the condo first; don't lose deals
- **Con:** Pay bridge interest; carry two properties briefly

**Which is better?** Depends on property market conditions and your psychology. If deals are competitive, bridging is worth the cost.

## Banks That Offer Bridging

Most Singapore banks offer bridging, but terms vary:

- **DBS, OCBC, UOB:** 3.5–5% rates, up to 80% LTV, 12-month terms
- **Private banks:** 3–4% rates, more flexible terms (some allow 18-month draws)
- **Non-bank lenders:** 4–6% rates, faster approval, less strict criteria
