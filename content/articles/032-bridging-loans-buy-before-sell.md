# Bridging Loans: Buying Before You Sell Explained

A bridging loan is short-term financing that bridges the gap between buying a new property and selling your old one. It's expensive but solves a timing problem: you find the perfect upgrade, but your current property hasn't sold yet.

## How Bridging Loans Work

**Scenario:** 
- You own an HDB flat worth $400k (not yet sold)
- You want to buy a condo for $550k (found a perfect deal)
- Closing on the condo is in 2 months; your HDB sale will close in 4 months

**Problem:** You need $110k (down payment on condo) before your HDB closes.

**Solution:** Bridging loan for $110k covers the gap. When HDB sells in 4 months, proceeds repay the bridge.

## Bridging Loan Terms

**Loan amount:** Typically 80% of the property's value (or less)

**Interest rate:** 3–6% annually (higher than mortgages because of short term and risk)

**Tenure:** 6–12 months (very short compared to mortgages)

**Monthly payment:** Interest-only (principal due as lump sum at end)

**Security:** First mortgage on the new property being purchased

## Cost Example: $110,000 Bridge at 4.5% for 6 months

**Monthly interest:** $110,000 × 4.5% ÷ 12 = $412/month

**Total interest over 6 months:** $412 × 6 = $2,472

**Loan origination fees:** 1–2% = $1,100–2,200

**Legal fees:** $500–800

**Total cost to bridge:** $4,072–5,472

**Compare to:** If you delayed the condo purchase by 6 months, you'd miss the deal entirely—it might appreciate or be sold to another buyer.

## When Bridging Makes Sense

**Scenario 1: Perfect property appears, old property almost sold**
- Your HDB will sell in 3–4 months (agreement in principle, just waiting for completion)
- Dream condo has only 1–2 other offers coming; won't wait
- Bridge for $50–100k to cover the gap
- **Cost: $1,500–3,000 interest + fees**
- **Benefit: You secure the condo instead of losing it**

**Scenario 2: Rate environment is rising; lock in before rates spike**
- Rates have been low for years (2.3%); Fed is hiking
- Condo you want is at $500k; expect it to appreciate 5%+ when rates rise (lower buying power pushes prices up)
- Bridge to buy now; sell HDB over next 6 months at your leisure
- **Cost: $3,000–5,000 in bridge interest**
- **Benefit: You capture potential $25k+ appreciation + avoid rate spikes**

## When Bridging is a Bad Idea

**Scenario 1: Old property hasn't sold, no concrete offer**
- HDB has been on market 3 months, soft market, no offers
- You ASSUME it will sell in 4 months, but reality: it may take 9–12 months
- You bridge $100k, but after 6 months the HDB still hasn't sold
- **Result:** Bridge rolls over to 12 months; you pay $12,000+ in interest on a risky bet

**Scenario 2: Rates are dropping; no urgency to buy**
- Rates falling; condo prices might drop too
- Bridging costs $4,000; condo might drop 3–5% in value over 6 months
- **Result:** You pay $4,000 to capture a property that's depreciating

**Scenario 3: New property is speculative**
- You want to buy a condo "as an investment" but aren't sure about the area
- HDB is your only income-producing asset (if you're renting it out)
- Bridging ties up capital and creates risk if the investment underperforms
- **Result:** Overleveraged with no margin for error

## Bridging vs. Selling First

**Sell-first approach:**
- Sell HDB first (completes in 2–3 months)
- Use proceeds for condo down payment (no bridge needed)
- **Pro:** No bridge costs
- **Con:** May face timing gap (1–2 months between sales); have to rent/stay with family temporarily

**Bridge approach:**
- Buy condo now (secure the deal)
- Sell HDB over next 6 months (on your timeline, not market's)
- **Pro:** Secure the condo first; don't lose deals
- **Con:** Pay bridge interest; carry two properties briefly

**Which is better?** Depends on property market conditions and your psychology. If deals are competitive, bridging is worth the cost.

## Real Case Studies: When Bridging Worked and When It Didn't

### Case Study 1: Tanjong Pagar Upgrade Success (Bridging Worked)

**Profile:** Executive, 38 years old, $600k annual income, found perfect 3BR condo in Tanjong Pagar

**Situation:**
- Current property: Punggol 4BR HDB, valued $450k; has been listed 2 months, strong interest (multiple viewings)
- Agent feedback: "Offer coming in 2 weeks" (soft signal, not guaranteed)
- New property: Tanjong Pagar condo, $850k, closing in 45 days
- Down payment needed: $170k (20% of $850k)
- HDB closing expected: 60 days (after condo closing)

**Bridge Decision:**
- Amount: $170k (just the down payment, not ABSD or fees)
- Rate: 4.2% (OCBC competitive bid)
- Duration: 6 months (conservative; HDB expected to close in 60 days)
- Monthly cost: $170k × 4.2% ÷ 12 = $595/month
- Total 6-month cost: $3,570 in interest + $1,800 in fees = **$5,370 total**

**Outcome:**
- HDB sold within 60 days as expected (market was strong)
- Proceeds: $430k (after agent fee, legal)
- Used to repay bridge + pay condo balance
- Excess cash: $260k (kept as reserves)
- Result: **SUCCESSFUL BRIDGE.** Paid $5,370 to secure dream property; HDB sold on expected timeline

**Lesson:** Bridging worked because (1) old property had concrete buyer interest, (2) timing was only 2 months off, (3) market was stable, (4) income supported both payments temporarily.

### Case Study 2: Clementi Investment Property Disaster (Bridging Failed)

**Profile:** Investor, 45 years old, $400k annual income, trying to flip to better-appreciation area

**Situation:**
- Current property: Clementi resale condo, valued $600k, listed 3 months with NO offers (mature area, slow market)
- New property: Woodlands new launch EC (pre-MRT), $450k, closing in 60 days
- Down payment needed: $90k (20%)
- Timeline assumption: "Market will warm up; someone will offer for Clementi in 4 months"

**Bridge Decision:**
- Amount: $90k
- Rate: 4.8% (higher rate due to longer uncertainty)
- Duration: 12 months (extended because original property hadn't sold)
- Monthly cost: $90k × 4.8% ÷ 12 = $360/month
- Total 12-month cost: $4,320 in interest + $2,000 in fees = **$6,320 total**

**What Went Wrong:**
- Month 2: Clementi still no offers (market slower than expected)
- Month 6: Bridge up for renewal; lender required new appraisal (property depreciated 2%)
- Month 8: Finally got offer for Clementi at $575k (down from $600k listing)
- Month 12: Clementi closed; bridge finally repaid

**Outcome:**
- Bridge cost: $6,320 (12 full months of interest)
- Clementi sold for $575k (down $25k from listing)
- Net loss: $31,320 ($25k depreciation + $6.3k bridge cost)
- EC value (Woodlands): Appreciated 8% by month 12 ($450k → $486k), but gains didn't offset bridge + depreciation losses
- Result: **FAILED BRIDGE. Investor underwater by $31k despite eventual property appreciation.**

**Lesson:** Bridging failed because (1) original property was illiquid (no buyer interest for 8+ months), (2) market assumption was wrong, (3) bridge cost compounded a weak market decision, (4) investor forced to accept below-market sale to exit bridge.

## Bridge Rollover Scenarios: What Happens If Your Sale Slips

**Scenario: Your old property doesn't close on time**

**Month 6 Bridge Maturity (Original Plan: Property sold by now)**
- Lender calls: "Bridge matures in 30 days. Is sale closing on time?"
- You: "Actually, closing is delayed to month 8. Can we extend?"

**Lender Options:**
1. **Extend the bridge:** Month 7–12 extension, new rate negotiation (typically 0.5% higher for extension), new legal fees ($500–800)
2. **Refinance into a longer-term loan:** Convert $90k bridge to 3–5 year personal loan or mortgage-backed facility (different lender, new appraisal, takes 2 weeks to close)
3. **Force repayment:** Lender recalls bridge; you must find alternative financing (personal loan at 6–8%, or mortgage if eligible) or delay new property purchase

**Rollover Cost Example:**
- Original bridge: $90k @ 4.8% for 6 months = $2,160 interest
- Rollover: $90k @ 5.3% (0.5% premium) for 6 more months = $2,385 interest + $600 legal
- **Rollover cost: $2,985 additional**
- **Total bridge cost if 12 months: $6,145** (vs. original $6,320 if planned for 12)

**Prevention Tactics:**
- Get firm sale timeline from agent (not "probably" but "contract signed, closing date X")
- Negotiate bridge approval for 12 months upfront (not 6-month renewable) to lock rates
- Secure a 2-month extension option at time of origination (costs $500 but guarantees extended rate)
- Plan a "worst-case 9-month" timeline when deciding to bridge (if old sale could slip 3 months)
