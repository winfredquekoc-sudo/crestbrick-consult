# Selling Property in Singapore: What to Do 365 Days Before Listing

Selling a property in Singapore is a 12-month marathon, not a sprint. Sellers who begin preparation a full year ahead capture 10-15% better net proceeds through deliberate refinancing, positioning, and market timing. First-time sellers, upgraders, and investors all benefit from this extended prep window.

## Money: Debt Paydown & Reserve Building (Months 1-6)

**Refinance to accelerate equity release.** If you're holding a mortgage at 2.8%, refinancing upward (rates now 3.2-3.5%) seems counterintuitive—but consider: 6 extra months of standard payments vs. 2-3 years paying down naturally. The rate increase is marginal; the acceleration is significant. Refinance only if you have 5+ years of ownership and positive equity.

**Target: Pay down $30-50k principal over 6 months.** This reduces buyer friction around your mortgage payoff. A 5-year-old $600k property with $520k remaining mortgage is tougher to sell than one with $470k remaining (same property, $50k difference signals equity strength).

**Build a $30-50k reserve.** Legal fees ($2-3k), inspections ($1.5-2k), agent commissions (2-2.5%), and holding costs during sale period (property tax, maintenance, insurance) consume $15-30k. A reserve eliminates the panic discount if sale drags.

## Timing: Audit Your Market Position (Months 3-6)

**Research comparable sales in your district.** Pull URA transaction data (public), PropertyGuru sold prices, and agent reports. Which flats/units sold in the last 90 days? At what prices? Are prices trending up or flat? Document 10-15 recent comps; this is your pricing anchor.

**Identify your strength windows.** HDB flats in Ang Mo Kio? Chinese New Year and May-June are peak (upgrader demand). Private condo in mature area? Post-January is softest (New Year spending, school fees paid). Investors offloading? List just before a new MRT opens nearby (buyers anticipate appreciation).

**Watch macro signals.** Interest rate expectations, HDB launch calendars, developer supply. If rates are rising and HDB launches bulk supply next quarter, your 6-month window (180 days out) matters. Buyers anticipate these; early listers capture demand before hesitation sets in.

## Safety: Title Audit & Disclosure Prep (Months 6-9)

**Commission a title insurance report.** Costs $500-800, takes 2-3 weeks. Identifies encumbrances, easements, or caveats that could derail deals late in the process. A $600k sale falling through over a buried title issue costs you $15-30k in lost time and repricing.

**Photograph defects now, document repairs.** Roof leaks, electrical faults, water stains—capture evidence of what you've done (or not done) about them. Buyers will inspect anyway; your proactive disclosure builds trust. Have contractor quotes ready for anything major.

**Prepare an honest disclosure statement.** Even though Singapore doesn't mandate seller disclosure like other jurisdictions, documenting known issues (past water damage, pest treatment, previous structural work) protects you legally and speeds closing.

## Upgraders & Investors: Property Positioning

**Upgraders:** Use this year to downsize debt on your existing property, freeing TDSR capacity for your next purchase. By month 12, you're mortgage-light and mortgage-ready for the upgrade.

**Investors:** Use this year to convert rental tenancies to empty possession timelines. Month 12 tenancy ends → 30-day notice → occupied unit selling into a buyer market with fewer contingencies. Empty possession commands 5-10% premiums over tenanted.

**First-time sellers:** Use this year to build equity narrative. A flat owned 8 years vs. 3 years reads differently to buyers. Market your stability.

## The 365-Day Edge

Sellers who start this timeline are rarely panicked by inspection findings, mortgage payoff delays, or market softness. They've built reserves, reduced debt, and positioned their property for strong buyer demand. Net proceeds typically run 8-15% higher than rushed sellers who list 60 days after deciding to sell.
