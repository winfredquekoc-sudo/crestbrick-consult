# ABSD Singapore 2026: Complete FAQ for Foreigners and SPRs

If you are a foreigner or a Singapore Permanent Resident thinking about buying property here, Additional Buyer's Stamp Duty — ABSD for short — is the single biggest number you need to understand before you commit to anything.

ABSD is a tax levied on top of the standard Buyer's Stamp Duty (BSD). The rate you pay depends on your citizenship status and how many residential properties you already own. For foreigners buying in 2026, the rate is steep. For SPRs it is more manageable, but still a material cost that changes the numbers on any investment case.

This FAQ covers every question Winfred hears from overseas buyers and SPRs. Read through it before you run any figures.

---

## 1. What is ABSD and why does Singapore charge it?

ABSD stands for Additional Buyer's Stamp Duty. The Singapore government introduced it in 2011 as a demand-side measure to cool the residential property market and prioritise homeownership for citizens over speculative or foreign demand.

It is charged as a percentage of the purchase price or market value of the property, whichever is higher. You pay it on top of BSD, which applies to all buyers regardless of nationality.

The key point: ABSD is not a one-time charge you can amortise away over many years. You pay it upfront at completion, usually within 14 days of exercising the Option to Purchase. On a $2 million property, a 60% ABSD is $1.2 million cash out the door before you own a single square foot.

---

## 2. What is the ABSD rate for foreigners in Singapore in 2026?

Foreigners pay **60%** ABSD on every residential property purchase in Singapore. This applies whether it is your first property or your fifth. There is no reduced rate for a first purchase.

This rate was raised from 30% to 60% in April 2023, as part of the government's most aggressive round of cooling measures to date. The rate has not changed since and there is no indication it will be revised downward in 2026.

On a $3 million condominium, a foreigner pays $1.8 million in ABSD alone, before factoring in BSD, legal fees, or any other transaction costs. For most foreigners, the only viable path to owning Singapore residential property at this rate is through significant capital or a very specific long-term strategy.

---

## 3. What is the ABSD rate for Singapore Permanent Residents (SPRs)?

SPRs pay ABSD at these rates in 2026:

- **1st residential property: 5%**
- **2nd residential property and beyond: 30%**

If you are an SPR buying your first home in Singapore, the 5% ABSD is significant but not prohibitive on most price points. On a $1.5 million resale HDB or condominium, that is $75,000 in ABSD.

The 30% rate on a second property is where SPRs need to plan carefully. At that level, the math on a second property only works if the asset appreciation case is strong or if the purchase is part of a broader restructuring strategy.

---

## 4. Are there any exemptions or remissions from ABSD for foreigners?

Yes, under specific Free Trade Agreements (FTAs), nationals of the following countries are treated on par with Singapore Citizens for ABSD on their **first** residential property purchase:

- **United States** (under the US-Singapore FTA)
- **Iceland, Liechtenstein, Norway** (under the EU-Singapore FTA, which covers EEA member and associated states)
- **Switzerland** (under the Swiss-Singapore FTA)

This means a US citizen buying their first residential property in Singapore pays no ABSD, the same as a Singapore Citizen buying their first home.

Important caveats. The FTA remission applies only to the **first** property and only when the buyer is an individual, not a corporate entity. If a US citizen already owns a residential property in Singapore, the second purchase is taxed at the standard citizen rate for a second property (20%), not at zero. The FTA does not give you permanent foreigner-exemption status. It applies property by property.

Citizens of all other nationalities pay the standard 60% foreigner rate.

---

## 5. Can foreigners buy HDB flats in Singapore?

No. Foreigners cannot buy HDB flats, whether new BTO units or resale flats on the open market. HDB flats are restricted to Singapore Citizens and, in certain circumstances, SPRs.

This restriction applies regardless of your wealth, residency duration, or employment status in Singapore. Even Employment Pass holders of many years cannot buy HDB.

If you are a foreigner, your residential property options in Singapore are limited to private properties: condominiums, apartments, and landed houses in certain designated areas (though landed housing for foreigners requires separate approval from the Land Dealings Approval Unit and is rarely granted).

---

## 6. Can SPRs buy HDB flats?

SPRs can buy **resale HDB flats** on the open market, subject to conditions. They cannot apply for new BTO flats from HDB directly.

To buy a resale HDB flat, an SPR must form an eligible family nucleus. The most common scenario is an SPR married to another SPR, or an SPR married to a Singapore Citizen. A single SPR cannot buy a resale HDB flat.

Additionally, the purchasing SPR must have held PR status for at least three years before the purchase. This is a hard rule, not a guideline.

SPRs buying resale HDB are also subject to the 5% ABSD if it is their first property purchase. There is no ABSD exemption for SPRs on HDB resale, though they do benefit from the lower first-property rate.

---

## 7. How is ABSD calculated? Is it on the purchase price or something else?

ABSD is calculated on the **higher of** the purchase price or the market value of the property as assessed by IRAS.

In most standard transactions, the purchase price and market value are close enough that the distinction does not matter. But if you are buying at a price significantly above or below market, IRAS will apply the assessment to the higher figure.

The formula is simple: ABSD payable = ABSD rate x (purchase price or market value, whichever is higher).

BSD is calculated separately using a tiered formula and is not part of the ABSD calculation. See the [Buyer's Stamp Duty guide](/blog/absd-second-property-guide) for the BSD breakdown.

---

## 8. When do you have to pay ABSD?

ABSD must be paid within **14 days** of exercising the Option to Purchase (OTP) for a resale property, or within 14 days of the date of the Sale and Purchase Agreement (S&P) for a new launch or developer sale.

You cannot defer ABSD. It is not rolled into your mortgage. It must be paid from cash or CPF (for eligible buyers). For foreigners, CPF is not available since foreigners do not contribute to CPF, so the full ABSD must come from cash.

Late payment attracts penalties. IRAS takes stamp duty deadlines seriously.

---

## 9. Can ABSD be refunded?

ABSD can be remitted or refunded under specific circumstances, but the conditions are narrow.

The most common remission scenario involves **married couples with at least one Singapore Citizen spouse** buying a property jointly. If they own an existing property and sell it within six months of buying the new one, they can apply for a refund of the ABSD paid. This is the so-called ABSD remission for couples upgrading.

There is no equivalent remission for foreigners buying their first property, even if they subsequently sell it. Once paid, it does not come back.

SPRs who later obtain Singapore Citizenship do not receive a retrospective refund of ABSD paid as an SPR.

If a property transaction is aborted and the S&P rescinded, stamp duty including ABSD may be refunded subject to IRAS approval, but this is a case-by-case process.

---

## 10. What is ABSD on trust purchases?

Since May 2023, properties purchased into a trust attract a flat ABSD rate of **65%**, regardless of the beneficial owner's citizenship or property count.

This measure was introduced to close a loophole where properties were placed in trust for minor children or other beneficiaries to circumvent the standard ABSD tiers. Under the trust rate, even a Singapore Citizen parent purchasing a property into trust for their citizen child pays 65%.

There is a narrow remission pathway where the beneficial owner is a Singapore Citizen and meets certain conditions, but the process is administrative and not guaranteed. In practice, trust purchases are now rarely used as an ABSD planning tool.

---

## 11. How does ABSD work for a married couple with different nationalities?

ABSD for a married couple is determined by the **higher-rate buyer's citizenship status**.

Example: A Singapore Citizen marries a foreigner. They buy a property jointly as their first purchase. The foreigner's 60% ABSD rate applies to the full purchase price, not just the foreigner's share. The joint purchase is treated as though both buyers are foreigners for ABSD purposes.

This is one of the most costly misunderstandings in mixed-nationality couples. Simply adding a citizen spouse to the title does not reduce ABSD if the foreigner is also on the title.

The reverse planning implication: if a citizen buys a first property solely in their own name (no foreign co-owner), no ABSD applies. The foreign spouse can be added to the title post-purchase in some circumstances, though this constitutes a separate transfer that may attract its own stamp duty.

---

## 12. Does owning overseas property affect my ABSD rate in Singapore?

No. ABSD is calculated based only on the number of **Singapore residential properties** you own at the time of purchase. Overseas properties are not counted.

An SPR who owns ten investment properties in the UK and Malaysia, but no property in Singapore, still pays 5% ABSD on their first Singapore residential purchase.

However, if you own Singapore commercial or industrial property, that does not count either. ABSD applies specifically to residential property.

---

## 13. What about the 99-to-1 ownership structure? Is it still viable?

The 99-to-1 ownership structure, where one buyer takes a 99% share and a second buyer takes a 1% share to manage ABSD exposure, was placed under formal IRAS scrutiny from May 2023. IRAS has stated it will treat these arrangements as stamp duty avoidance if the primary purpose is to reduce the ABSD payable.

In practice, IRAS can assess stamp duty on the full market value based on the combined ownership position and apply penalties. The risk is not theoretical. Several buyers have faced additional assessments after IRAS reviewed their 99-to-1 transactions.

This does not mean all split-ownership structures are illegal. Legitimate cases with genuine proportional investment rationale exist. But using a fractional split purely to minimise ABSD is no longer a low-risk strategy. Get proper tax advice before attempting it.

---

## 14. What should a foreigner or SPR do before buying in Singapore?

Before you make any offer, work through these steps.

First, confirm your citizenship status and how many Singapore residential properties you own. This determines your ABSD rate with certainty.

Second, check if you qualify for any FTA remission. If you hold a US, Swiss, Norwegian, Icelandic, or Liechtenstein passport, you may pay zero ABSD on your first property.

Third, run the full cost of acquisition: purchase price plus BSD plus ABSD plus legal fees plus any agent commissions. For foreigners, the ABSD alone can represent 50-60% of the property's value. Make sure your equity and cash position accommodates this.

Fourth, consider the [TDSR and loan eligibility](/blog/hdb-upgrade-timeline) constraints. ABSD is a cash cost, not a loanable amount. Your loan quantum is calculated on the property price without ABSD, but your total cash requirement is substantially higher.

Fifth, think about exit. ABSD is a sunk cost the moment you pay it. Your break-even holding period for capital recovery is longer than for a citizen buyer paying no ABSD. Model the appreciation scenario honestly before committing.

## The bottom line on ABSD in 2026

ABSD is not going away and the rates are not coming down in the near term. The Singapore government has been explicit that the cooling measures reflect its long-term housing policy, not a temporary intervention.

For SPRs buying a first home, 5% is manageable and should not be a dealbreaker on an otherwise sound purchase. For those planning a second property, the 30% rate requires serious financial modelling before committing.

For most foreigners, 60% fundamentally changes the investment calculus. The few scenarios where it still makes sense are long-horizon holds, very specific asset types, or buyers who qualify for FTA remission.
