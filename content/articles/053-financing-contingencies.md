# Financing Contingencies: When Your Mortgage Falls Through

You've made an offer. Seller accepts. You're pre-approved at $700k. But 2 weeks before closing, the bank's appraisal comes in at $680k. You wanted to borrow 75% = $525k. Now bank will only lend 75% of $680k = $510k. You're $15k short in down payment.

This is the financing contingency crisis. It happens in 3-5% of deals in Singapore. Here's how to navigate it.

## The Three Financing Failure Points

**Failure 1: Appraisal comes in low (40% of financing collapses)**

You offer $700k. Bank orders appraisal; comes back $680k (below purchase price).

Bank won't lend above appraised value (risk management). Your LTV calc:
- Appraised value: $680k
- LTV 75%: Can borrow $510k max
- Your offer: $700k
- Shortfall: $190k down payment (not $210k, but $190k) = **$20k more cash needed**

**Your options:**
1. Pay extra $20k cash (if you have it)
2. Renegotiate offer down to $680k (seller has to agree)
3. Walk away (if financing contingency is in contract)

---

**Failure 2: Employment verification fails (35% of financing collapses)**

You were pre-approved 3 months ago. But:
- You changed jobs (lender sees risk; new employer, new income stability)
- You had a gap in employment (even 2 weeks)
- Your income couldn't be verified (contract role, no payslips)
- Your boss left (lender verifies with HR; gets delayed answer)

Bank pulls your mortgage days before closing. **Deal fails.**

---

**Failure 3: Credit event** (25% of financing collapses)

Between pre-approval and closing:
- You took a car loan (debt increased)
- Your credit card max-out (credit ratio worsens)
- Late payment on existing loan (credit score drops 50+ points)
- Identity fraud (improper inquiry on your report)

Your TDSR worsens. Bank re-runs assessment; says **"You're now 65% TDSR; exceeds 60% cap. Mortgage denied."**

---

## How to Protect Yourself: Financing Contingency

**In your offer contract, include:**

"This offer is contingent upon buyer obtaining mortgage approval for [amount] at [rate] by [date]. If financing falls through, buyer may withdraw offer and retrieve deposit."

**This clause protects you if:**
- Appraisal is low (you can walk without losing deposit)
- Employment can't be verified (you can walk)
- TDSR exceeds cap (you can walk)

**Without this clause:**
- Low appraisal: You forfeit deposit or forced to pay extra cash
- Employment fails: You forfeit deposit
- TDSR exceeds: You forfeit deposit or face litigation (seller sues for specific performance)

## The Appraisal Crisis: Case Study

**Scenario: You offer $700k for Parc Riviera unit**

Pre-approval: $525k (75% LTV on $700k assumed)

**Week 2: Appraisal ordered; comes back $680k**

Bank says: "We'll only lend 75% of $680k = $510k"

**Your shortfall: $190k down (not $210k)**

**Difference: $20k** (extra cash out of pocket)

**Your options:**

**Option A: Renegotiate with seller**
- Original offer: $700k
- New offer (based on appraisal): $680k
- Seller response: "I listed at $725k; you offered $700k; now you want $680k after 2 weeks? No."
- Outcome: Seller refuses; you walk or pay $700k anyway

**Option B: Pay extra $20k cash**
- You find $20k from savings/family loan
- Close at $700k with $510k mortgage + $190k down payment
- Outcome: You stretch finances; TDSR ratio is now higher; refinance later becomes harder

**Option C: Renegotiate mortgage**
- Ask bank: "Can you go to 76% LTV?" (instead of 75%)
- Bank says: "No; 75% cap is policy"
- Outcome: Bank won't budge

**Option D: Walk (if financing contingency exists)**
- Contract says: "Contingent on mortgage approval"
- Appraisal failure = mortgage approval failed
- You withdraw; seller refunds your $70k deposit
- Outcome: You lose 2-4 weeks of time; but keep capital

## Why Appraisals Come in Low

**Reason 1: Property is overpriced**
- Market comps suggest $680-690k
- You offered $700k (above market)
- Appraiser doesn't justify premium

**Reason 2: Building has structural issues**
- Building facade defects (HDB structural remediation ongoing)
- Condo aging (>30 years old; deferred maintenance)
- Residents' poor reputation (high defaults, negative press)

**Reason 3: Appraiser is conservative**
- Uses recent sales only (past 3 months)
- Ignores optimistic forecasts (MRT opening in 2 years)
- Market is soft; banks tell appraisers "be conservative"

**Reason 4: Comparison units are weak**
- Recent sales in building were at low prices (distressed sales)
- Appraisal relies on these weak comps

## How to Avoid Appraisal Collapse

**1. Get independent appraisal before making offer**

Cost: $500-800
Timeline: 1 week

You learn true market value *before* offering. You offer $680k (confirmed by independent appraiser), not $700k (guess). When bank appraises, they likely match your pre-offer appraisal.

**2. Offer based on market comps, not listing price**

- Listing: $725k (seller's optimism)
- Market comps: $695-710k (recent sales)
- Your offer: $700k (top of comp range)

Bank appraisal will match comps ($695-710k range). Your $700k offer is at top of range; less likely to fail.

**3. Don't overbid in hot markets**

In competitive markets, buyers often overbid (emotional decision). Appraisals don't follow emotions.

- Offer: $750k (just trying to win)
- Comps: $720k
- Appraisal: $720k
- Shortfall: $30k

Better: Offer $720k (at comps); lose bid to emotional buyer; find next property (save $30k)

## Employment Verification: Red Flags

**Banks are more strict on employment if:**

1. **You changed jobs in past 6 months**
   - Pre-approval based on old employer; current employer may be flight risk
   - Bank may request letter from new employer confirming role, income, permanence
   - Delay: 2-4 weeks while HR letter is produced

2. **You're self-employed or contract**
   - Bank needs 2+ years of tax returns (not payslips)
   - Takes longer to verify; delay common
   - Lender may request accountant letter

3. **You have gaps in employment** (even 2 weeks)
   - Between job A and job B
   - Lender sees risk; may require written explanation

4. **Your income is bonus-dependent**
   - Base salary: $80k
   - Bonus: $40k (not guaranteed)
   - Bank uses base only ($80k) for TDSR calculation
   - You might not qualify if income drops without bonus

**How to avoid:**
- Don't change jobs 6 months before closing
- If must change: Notify bank immediately; provide letter from new employer
- Lock in bonus: Get written confirmation from employer that bonus is payable

## TDSR Collapse: The Hidden Risk

**Your TDSR calculated at pre-approval: 58%**

But between pre-approval and closing:

- You financed a car: +$300/month debt
- New TDSR: (existing debt + car payment + new mortgage) / income = 62%
- **Exceeds 60% cap; mortgage denied**

**How to avoid:**
- Don't take on debt between pre-approval and closing
- No car loans, no credit card charges, no personal loans
- Don't increase credit card usage (even if paid off monthly; lender counts max utilization)

## The Checklist: Financing Contingency Protection

Before making offer:

- [ ] Get independent appraisal ($500-800)
- [ ] Verify your employment letter (show it to lender; confirm no surprises)
- [ ] Check your credit report (PDPA-authorized portal; ensure no errors)
- [ ] Calculate your TDSR conservatively (include car loans, credit card limits, everything)
- [ ] Confirm rate lock (how long is your pre-approval rate guaranteed?)
- [ ] Include financing contingency in offer ("contingent on mortgage approval by [date]")

After offer accepted:

- [ ] Don't change jobs
- [ ] Don't take on new debt
- [ ] Don't max out credit cards
- [ ] Don't make late payments
- [ ] Respond promptly to bank's verification requests
- [ ] Provide employment letter if lender requests

Before closing:

- [ ] Confirm mortgage approval is final (not conditional)
- [ ] Confirm appraisal matches your assumptions
- [ ] Don't pull your credit (even for final verification; minimizes inquiries)

## Real Example: Financing Collapse Story

**Buyer: Pre-approved for $700k condo purchase**

**Week 1:** Offer accepted at $700k. Down payment: $210k. Mortgage: $490k.

**Week 2:** Appraisal comes in $680k. New mortgage cap: 75% of $680k = $510k. **Shortfall: $20k cash needed.**

**Week 3:** Buyer calls new employer (changed jobs 4 weeks ago). HR delays employment letter (new hire, paperwork backlog). **Employment verification stalls.**

**Week 4:** TDSR re-calculated. New car financed (buyer bought car during purchase process, thinking "soon I'll own new place"). New TDSR: 62%. **Exceeds 60% cap.**

**Week 5:** Lender says: "Appraisal is low, employment unverified, TDSR exceeds cap. We're pulling the mortgage."

**Outcome:** 
- Buyer has no financing contingency in contract
- Buyer forfeits $70k deposit (or sued by seller for $15k cost of re-listing)
- Buyer defaults; credit score drops 100+ points

**If buyer had contingency:**
- Financing contingency triggered (appraisal failure)
- Buyer withdraws offer; retrieves $70k deposit
- Buyer keeps capital; avoids credit damage

## What to Do If Financing Falls Through

**If contingency is in your contract:**

1. Notify seller within 2-3 days (don't wait)
2. Provide written evidence (appraisal report, lender denial letter)
3. Request deposit return
4. Seller must refund deposit within 5 days
5. Move on to next property

**If contingency is NOT in your contract:**

1. Contact lender; request written reason for denial
2. Explore: Can you increase down payment? Get co-signer? Switch lenders?
3. If no options: Consult lawyer; may be able to void contract if lender failure is documented
4. Worst case: Forfeit deposit or sue seller (expensive, likely lose)
