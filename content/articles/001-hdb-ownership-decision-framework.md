# HDB Ownership Decision Framework: Singapore First-Time Buyer Checklist

Buying your first HDB flat is the single biggest financial decision most Singaporeans make. The rules are complex: CPF withdrawal limits, income thresholds, eligibility windows, subsidy calculations. Miss one rule and you discover mid-application that you're ineligible or can only afford less than you thought.

This framework walks through every gate a first-time buyer must pass. Use it to validate your readiness before spending time searching for flats.

## Eligibility: The Citizenship & Marital Status Foundation

**Singapore Citizens:** Can buy HDB flats, with full subsidies and CPF withdrawal rights. This is the baseline.

**Singapore Permanent Residents (SPRs):** Can buy HDB flats but lose citizenship subsidy. Price is 10-15% higher than citizen pricing for the same flat. CPF withdrawal is allowed at normal thresholds.

**Foreigners:** Cannot buy HDB flats. Period. If you're not a citizen or SPR, explore private condominiums instead.

**Marital Status Matters:**
- **Singles:** Can buy if age 35+. Below 35, singles cannot buy HDB except in specific schemes (widowed parents, divorcees with dependent children).
- **Married couples:** Can buy at any age (one spouse minimum 21). Both must be Singapore citizens or PRs (different rules apply if one is foreign).
- **Divorced with dependent children:** Can buy as a single if you have custody and are below 35.

Gate 1: Confirm you meet citizenship and marital status requirements before proceeding.

## Income Ceiling: The Hard Cutoff

HDB flat purchases come with an income ceiling. Exceed this, and you're ineligible to buy (you must buy private property instead).

**Current Income Ceiling (2026):** SGD 14,000 per month for a couple, SGD 7,000 for a single.

This is calculated as the **combined monthly gross income** of all household members who will be named on the flat purchase agreement.

**Income sources counted:**
- Salary from employment (gross, before CPF deductions)
- Self-employment income (last 2 years' average)
- Rental income from property
- Dividends and investment returns
- CPF withdrawals (treated as income if reinvested)

**Income sources NOT counted:**
- CPF balance (your savings account; doesn't count as income)
- Bonus or variable pay (only regular guaranteed income counts)
- Gifts from family

Gate 2: Calculate your household's combined monthly gross income. If you exceed the ceiling, you cannot buy HDB.

## Minimum Downpayment: CPF vs. Cash Trade-Off

HDB flats require a minimum downpayment of **5% of the purchase price**. You can pay this downpayment from either CPF savings or cash (or a mix).

**Example:** Flat costs SGD 500,000. Minimum downpayment = SGD 25,000.

**CPF Downpayment:**
- Use your Ordinary Account (OA) balance to pay downpayment
- CPF OA limit: You can withdraw up to **80% of your OA balance** for property, minus the amount needed for your retirement sum (Medisave account is untouchable)
- Typical availability: SGD 30,000-60,000 for first-time buyers

**Cash Downpayment:**
- Pay from savings, bonus, gifts, or work income (after CPF deductions)
- If your CPF is insufficient, you'll need cash to make up the gap

**Strategic choice:** If CPF is tight, you might choose to put down more cash (10-15%) to reduce your mortgage and monthly payments. If cash is tight, use maximum CPF allowed.

Gate 3: Check your CPF OA balance. Calculate how much CPF you can withdraw (80% of OA, minus retirement buffer). Determine how much additional cash you need.

## Mortgage Qualification: MSR & TDSR Thresholds

HDB mortgages are subject to two affordability gates: **MSR** (Monthly Servicing Ratio) and **TDSR** (Total Debt Servicing Ratio).

**MSR (Monthly Servicing Ratio):** Your monthly HDB loan payment cannot exceed **30% of your monthly gross income**.

Example: If you earn SGD 5,000/month, your HDB loan payment cannot exceed SGD 1,500/month. A SGD 500,000 flat with a 35-year mortgage at 2.6% interest costs roughly SGD 1,540/month—you'd be slightly over MSR. To qualify, you'd need a higher downpayment or lower-priced flat.

**TDSR (Total Debt Servicing Ratio):** All your monthly debt payments (HDB loan, car loan, credit card, personal loan, other mortgages) cannot exceed **60% of your monthly gross income**.

Example: If you earn SGD 5,000/month, your total debt payments can't exceed SGD 3,000/month. If your HDB loan is SGD 1,500 and car loan is SGD 1,200, you're at SGD 2,700—you have only SGD 300 left for other debts.

**Practical implication:** Pay off your car loan or credit card before applying for an HDB mortgage. Reduce existing debt to improve your TDSR headroom.

Gate 4: Calculate your monthly gross income. Check if an HDB mortgage at your target price passes MSR (loan payment ≤ 30% of income). Check if you'll pass TDSR (all debts ≤ 60% of income). If either fails, reduce your target price or increase income.

## CPF Contribution History: The MOP & Wait Times

Certain schemes require you to have been **contributing to CPF for a minimum period (MOP)** before you're eligible.

**Standard BTO (Build-To-Order) and Resale:** Typically require 12 months of CPF contributions immediately before application.

**Special schemes (e.g., Second Dipper Scheme):** May require longer history (3+ years) or employment verification.

**Self-employed buyers:** Must have filed income tax returns for at least 2 consecutive years.

Gate 5: Confirm you meet the CPF contribution history requirement for your target flat type (BTO, resale, etc.).

## HDB Loan Terms: How Long & At What Rate

**Maximum Loan Tenure:** 35 years (or until age 65 for those over 30, so max tenure shrinks as you age).

**Interest Rate:** Currently pegged to **HDB's mortgage rate** (fixed for 2-3 years, then adjusts). As of 2026, rates are around 2.6% (variable by timing).

**Loan-to-Value (LTV):** You can borrow up to **80% of the purchase price** (meaning 20% downpayment minimum). But MSR/TDSR constraints often force you to put down more.

Gate 6: Use an HDB mortgage calculator to estimate your monthly payment at your target price, tenure, and income level. Verify it passes MSR.

## Subsidy Quantum: How Much Does HDB Discount?

HDB flats are heavily subsidized. A flat worth SGD 800,000 on the private market might cost SGD 400,000-500,000 as an HDB flat, depending on location and your income.

**Subsidy factors:**
- **Location:** Central locations (CBD, Clementi) have lower subsidies. Outer towns (Jurong, Woodlands) have higher subsidies.
- **Income:** Lower-income brackets receive higher subsidies.
- **Flat size:** 3-room and 4-room flats have the highest subsidies (family-focused). 2-room flats have lower subsidies (targeted at young, low-income buyers).

The actual subsidy is complex and determined by HDB using a formula that weights location, size, and your income. You'll see the subsidized price when you bid on the flat.

Gate 7: Research HDB pricing in your target locations. Use HDB's flat selection tool to see typical prices and understand whether they fit your budget.

## Resale vs. BTO: Timing & Availability Trade-Off

**BTO (Build-to-Order):** You apply for a new flat that will be constructed. Prices are fixed at application (subsidized). Delivery is 3-5 years out. Huge demand; many applicants don't get their choice.

**Resale:** You buy an existing HDB flat from a current owner. Prices fluctuate. Delivery is immediate (within 2-3 months). Lower demand; easier to find a flat.

**First-time buyer choice:** BTO offers lower prices and subsidy certainty. Resale offers immediate occupancy and choice. Most first-time buyers prefer BTO for cost; impatient buyers choose resale.

Gate 8: Decide your timeline. If you need housing urgently, plan for resale. If you can wait 3-5 years, apply for BTO and lock in a subsidized price.

## Joint Purchase Strategy: The Multiple Applicants Path

If you're part of a couple or multi-generational household, you can **jointly apply** with family members to increase your combined income and affordability.

**Joint purchase example:** Single earner (SGD 5,000/month) + spouse (SGD 4,000/month) + parent (SGD 3,000/month) = SGD 12,000 combined gross income. All three are named on the flat title.

**Pros:**
- Higher combined income → higher affordability
- Shared ownership means shared liability

**Cons:**
- All co-owners must be citizens/PRs
- All co-owners are liable for the full mortgage (not proportional)
- If one co-owner has debt, it counts toward household TDSR

Gate 9: If your solo income is tight, explore joint purchase with family members to boost combined income.

## Checklist: Your HDB Readiness

- [ ] Confirm citizenship/PR status and marital status eligibility
- [ ] Calculate household gross income; confirm it's below the ceiling (SGD 14k/month for couples)
- [ ] Check CPF OA balance; calculate CPF available for downpayment
- [ ] Determine cash downpayment needed to reach 5-20% down
- [ ] List all existing debts (car loan, credit card, personal loan)
- [ ] Calculate MSR: is HDB loan ≤ 30% of monthly income?
- [ ] Calculate TDSR: are all debts ≤ 60% of monthly income?
- [ ] Verify CPF contribution history meets MOP requirements
- [ ] Research HDB flat prices in your target locations
- [ ] Decide BTO (3-5 year wait) vs. Resale (immediate)

If all gates pass, you're ready to apply or start flat hunting. If any gate fails, adjust your plan (higher income, lower price, reduce debt, or wait for income growth).
