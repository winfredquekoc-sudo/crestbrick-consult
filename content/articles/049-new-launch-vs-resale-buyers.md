# New Launch vs Resale: What You Actually Get (and Lose)

New launch condo: $850/sqft. Resale condo, same building, 2 years old: $780/sqft. Both are 4-bedrooms. 9% price gap. Why? And which should you buy?

The answer depends on risk tolerance, holding period, and whether you value warranty over instant occupancy.

## The Price Gap Explained

**New launch premium: 8-15% above resale in same area**

A 1,000 sqft new launch at $850k sells as resale (2 years later) for $740-780k. Where does the $70-110k disappear?

**1. Developer marketing costs (3-5%)**
- Showflat, sales agents, advertising, brochures
- You pay this, even if you never visit the showflat

**2. Buyer's choice (2-3%)**
- New units: you pick finishes, layout
- Resale: fixed unit, fixed finishes
- Flexibility commands premium in hot market

**3. Timing premium (2-5%)**
- New launch: completion 2-3 years out
- Buyer bet: "Prices will rise before I move in"
- Loser: if market softens or interest rates spike

**4. Defects liability (2-3%)**
- New: developer warrant defects for 10 years (Singapore law)
- Resale: "as-is"; any defect = your problem
- Peace of mind has a price

**5. Completion uncertainty (1-2%)**
- New: you carry cost if completion delays (rate hikes, supply chain)
- Resale: you move in tomorrow

**Total: 10-18% premium for new launch uncertainty + warranty.**

## New Launch: The Case For

**You buy new if:**

1. **You can afford to wait 2-3 years for completion**
   - No immediate occupancy need
   - Happy to stay in current place or rent

2. **You believe prices will rise**
   - Interest rates falling or MRT opening nearby
   - New supply drying up
   - GLS site shows luxury demand

3. **You value choice and warranty**
   - Pick your finishes (marble, carpentry, appliances)
   - Developer warrants defects 10 years
   - No surprise repairs in year 1

4. **You're locked into location**
   - Work/school nearby, not relocating
   - Willing to camp in current flat for 2-3 more years

**Resale: The Case Against**

- You rent meanwhile (opportunity cost: $3-4k/month × 36 months = $108-144k)
- Prices could fall instead (rate hikes, recession)
- Completion delays 6-12 months (common in Singapore)
- Developer goes insolvent (rare, but has happened)

## Resale: The Case For

**You buy resale if:**

1. **You need to move now**
   - Job relocated, family expanding, divorce/separation
   - Renting is unsustainable or temporary
   - You move in this month, not 2026

2. **You're risk-averse**
   - Hate surprises (delays, price drops, interest rate changes)
   - Resale price is known, certain
   - Move-in condition is visible

3. **You believe prices are peak (or will fall)**
   - Interest rates rising, cooling measures in effect
   - Developer inventory piling up
   - You'd rather own at 15% discount than gamble on new

4. **You want defect-free immediate occupancy**
   - Previous owner found + fixed defects (not you)
   - You know exactly what you're getting
   - No "surprise foundation cracks" on takeover

**New Launch: The Case Against**

- You pay 10-15% premium for uncertainty
- Completion delays common (6-18 months)
- Interest rates could spike before completion (carrying cost jump)
- Defects in year 1-2 (plumbing, electrical, paint)
- You never see the actual unit before closing (showflat ≠ real unit)

## The Math: New Launch Gamble vs Resale Certainty

**Scenario A: New Launch (2027 completion)**

Unit: 1,000 sqft, $850/sqft = $850k

**Financing:**
- Down: $212.5k (25%)
- Mortgage: $637.5k (75%)
- Rate: 3.8%
- Monthly: $2,045/month

**Holding period 1-3:**
- Year 1: Paying mortgage, no occupancy yet; developer delays 6 months
- Year 2: Still waiting; interest rates spike to 4.2% (your new refinance rate; existing 3.8% lock protected)
- Year 3: Complete; move in; discover defects in plumbing, paint, air conditioning

**Costs:**
- Mortgage paid (3 years): $73,620
- Carrying cost (rent elsewhere, 18 months avg): $54,000
- Defect repairs (year 1-2): $8,000
- **Total invested: $935,620**

**Resale value (2027):** $780k
- **Loss: $155,620**

---

**Scenario B: Resale (Same unit, bought in 2024 @ $780k)**

Unit: 1,000 sqft, $780/sqft = $780k (same building, 2 years old)

**Financing:**
- Down: $195k (25%)
- Mortgage: $585k (75%)
- Rate: 3.8%
- Monthly: $1,875/month

**Holding period 1-3:**
- Year 1: Move in immediately; no surprises
- Year 2: Interest rates spike to 4.2% (you refinance, rate goes up, but you refinance old balance at ~4.0% compromise)
- Year 3: Building appreciation (avg 2-3% annually) = unit now worth $830k

**Costs:**
- Mortgage paid (3 years): $67,500
- Moving costs: $3,000
- Maintenance reserves: $4,000
- **Total invested: $849,500**

**Resale value (2027):** $830k
- **Gain: −$19,500** (profit after all costs)

---

**Comparison:**
- New launch: −$155,620 loss (bet wrong on prices)
- Resale: −$19,500 loss (conservative, but broke even)

**Difference: $136,120 saved by buying resale**

## The Defects Reality (Year 1-3)

**New launch (year 1):**
- Paint defects: cracks, uneven finishes (~$2-3k to fix if not developer warranty)
- Plumbing leaks: hidden defects in concealed pipes (~$5-10k)
- HVAC issues: compressor failure, ductwork noise (~$3-8k)
- Structural: door frames misaligned, floor not level (~$2-5k if cosmetic)

**Developer warranty covers these.** But coordination = 3-6 months, inconvenience = high.

**Resale (year 1):**
- None (previous owner dealt with them)
- You inherit solid building history (inspection report shows prior issues)
- You have realistic expectation of age-related wear

**Cost difference: $10-25k** (defect repair + warranty claim hassle) favors resale.

## The Completion Risk: New Launch Horror Stories

**Common delays (Singapore 2020-2026):**
- 6-12 month delays (54% of launches)
- 12-18 month delays (23% of launches)
- 18+ month delays (9% of launches)

**Your cost if delayed:**
- Rate lock expires; new rate is 0.5-1% higher
- Your MSR jumps $250-400/month
- Your TDSR might exceed 60% (mortgage gets denied/reduced)
- You're stuck renting longer (extra $3-4k/month)

**Example:**
- Original: $637.5k mortgage at 3.8% = $2,045/month
- Delayed 12 months; rate re-lock at 4.3% = $2,290/month
- **Extra $245/month × 360 months = $88,200 lifetime cost**

**Resale:** No delay risk. You own the unit in 3 weeks.

## The Buyer's Choice: Decision Matrix

| Factor | New Launch | Resale | Winner |
|--------|-----------|--------|--------|
| Price certainty | ❌ (8-15% premium risk) | ✓ (known price) | Resale |
| Completion risk | ❌ (6-18m delays common) | ✓ (immediate) | Resale |
| Defect risk | ❌ (year 1-2 common) | ✓ (previous owner dealt) | Resale |
| Occupancy timeline | ❌ (2-3 years out) | ✓ (now) | Resale |
| Price appreciation | ✓ (if market rises) | ❌ (already discounted) | New Launch |
| Customization | ✓ (pick finishes) | ❌ (fixed finishes) | New Launch |
| Rate lock risk | ❌ (rates may spike) | ✓ (rate secured) | Resale |
| Mortgage certainty | ❌ (completion contingency) | ✓ (loan approved for known unit) | Resale |

**Verdict:** Resale wins on risk-adjusted basis. New launch wins only if prices rise >15% in 3 years *and* you can absorb delays.

## When New Launch Makes Sense

- **Prices rising >4% annually** (MRT opening, new district development)
- **You have 2-3 year timeline** (not moving immediately)
- **Interest rates falling** (not spiking)
- **You're highly selective** (need exact layout, finishes)
- **Developer has 95%+ completion track record** (check their history)

## When Resale Wins

- **You need occupancy in 3 months** (job relocation, downsizing)
- **Interest rates spiking** (rate lock risk too high)
- **Market is soft** (prices falling, not rising)
- **You're risk-averse** (hate delays, surprises)
- **Prices already peaked** (new launches discounting heavily)

## The Checklist: New Launch Decision

If you're leaning new launch, verify:

- [ ] Developer's track record (80%+ on-time completion?)
- [ ] Completion date (realistic, not 2029 someday?)
- [ ] Your holding period (can you wait 2-3 years? )
- [ ] Rate lock (does pre-approval lock rate through completion date?)
- [ ] Carrying cost (can you absorb $3-4k/month rent if delayed?)
- [ ] Market outlook (prices rising next 3 years?)
- [ ] Interest rate forecast (will rates spike?)
