# Negotiation Tactics for Buyers: When Price Moves

You make an offer $50k below asking price. Seller counters asking for only $20k reduction. You're now $30k apart. Standard negotiation, but one wrong move costs you $30-50k in concessions. This article shows what actually moves sellers in Singapore.

## The Three Leverage Points

**Leverage 1: Inspection findings (40% success rate)**

Buyer inspects; finds: cracked ceiling, leaking pipes, electrical issues. Cost to fix: $15-25k. Seller's reaction:
- Option A: "That's your problem; fix it after you close"
- Option B: "OK, reduce price by $15k"
- Option C: Ignore and hope buyer doesn't get detailed inspector report

**You win if:** You commission a detailed inspection *before* making offer, use findings to anchor your counteroffer.

**You lose if:** Inspection is cosmetic; findings are minor ($2-3k repairs only).

---

**Leverage 2: Time pressure (35% success rate)**

Seller has a job relocation deadline (closing in 30 days) or refinance deadline (need to pay off mortgage by month-end). Seller doesn't want property held up by inspections or contingencies.

**You win if:** You're the clean offer (no contingencies, ready to close fast).

**You lose if:** You're the late offer (10 people ahead, seller doesn't need you).

---

**Leverage 3: Buyer pool weakness (25% success rate)**

No competing offers. Market is soft (inventory > demand). Seller has listed 4+ months with no traction.

**You win if:** You're the only bidder in months of listing.

**You lose if:** It's a hot property (sold in 2 weeks to someone else).

## Inspection Findings: The Negotiation Anchor

**Myth:** "All inspection findings are negotiable."

**Reality:** Only findings totaling $10k+ move the needle. Minor cosmetics ($1-3k) are brushed off.

**Defects that work:**
- Structural cracks (ceiling, walls): $8-15k repair
- Plumbing leaks (concealed pipes): $10-20k
- Electrical (outdated wiring, panel failure): $5-12k
- HVAC (compressor failure, duct issues): $6-10k
- Foundation (water seepage, subsidence): $15-30k

**Defects that don't work:**
- Paint cracks: $1-2k (ignored)
- Door frame misalignment: $0.5k (cosmetic)
- Light fixtures: $0.2k (cheap to replace)
- Appliance age: $1-3k (you expected it)

**Tactic:**
1. Get detailed inspection ($800-1,500; not the $200 online cursory check)
2. Identify findings totaling $12-25k
3. Get contractor quotes for each defect
4. Calculate total repair cost
5. In counteroffer: "Inspection found $18k in defects; reduce price by $15k, or I'll get them fixed after close (but walk if findings are major)"

**Example:**
- Original offer: $650k
- Seller counters: $680k
- You: "Inspection found $16k in plumbing + electrical. I'll do $660k to cover repairs."
- Seller: "Fine, $665k" (you got $15k reduction)

## Time Pressure: Using Seller's Deadline

**Seller scenario: Job relocation to KL in 30 days**

Seller needs sale to close ASAP so they can move. Competing offer is clean; your offer has contingencies (inspection, financing contingency, appraisal contingency).

**You win if:**
- You waive contingencies
- You close in 14 days (instead of standard 28)
- You don't require inspection (or inspection is very quick)
- You have pre-approval letter ready

**You lose if:**
- You add contingencies ("subject to financing," "subject to inspection")
- You ask for 45-day close
- You say "need appraisal" (signals finance uncertainty)

**Tactic:**
1. Get pre-approval letter before making offer
2. In offer, state: "Closing in 14 days, subject to inspection only (no financing contingency)"
3. Seller sees confidence (you're already pre-approved, just need appraisal)
4. Seller values speed over $10k extra; accepts lower price

**Example:**
- Original offer: $650k (14-day close)
- Seller counter: $675k (takes 45 days, needs time to move)
- You: "Stick with $655k at 14 days; I'm ready to sign today"
- Seller: Tempted by speed; accepts $660k at 14 days

## Contingency Negotiation: What Works

**Contingency 1: "Subject to satisfactory inspection"**

Seller hates this (gives you walk-away option if you change mind). But necessary for protection.

**How to negotiate:**
- Get pre-inspection done (show seller the results are good)
- In offer: "subject to inspection, but inspector already cleared structure/plumbing/electrical"
- Seller feels safer (you're unlikely to walk after pre-inspection came back clean)
- Gives you credibility; you're not fishing for deal-breakers

**Contingency 2: "Subject to appraisal"**

Seller also dislikes (if bank appraises lower, you renegotiate or walk).

**How to negotiate:**
- Get bank appraisal ordered before making offer (show seller proof of appraisal request)
- In offer: "Subject to appraisal by [Bank X], estimated $650-670k" (show you're expecting appraisal to support price)
- Pre-appraisal reduces contingency risk for seller

**Contingency 3: "Subject to financing"**

This is negotiable *only if you don't have pre-approval*. With pre-approval, you should NOT use financing contingency (unnecessary).

**How to negotiate:**
- Remove this contingency entirely if pre-approved
- Tells seller: "I'm guaranteed mortgage, no financing risk"
- Increases your negotiating power by 15-20%

## The Counter-Offer Sequence

**Round 1: Your initial offer**

You: $650k, 28-day close, subject to inspection + appraisal
- Shows you're serious but protected
- Leaves room for negotiation

**Round 2: Seller counter**

Seller: $680k, 30-day close (or they might ignore your contingencies and counter with all cash expectations)
- Seller wants $30k more
- But time window same (28 vs 30 days negligible)

**Round 3: Your counter-counter**

You: "$660k, 21-day close, remove inspection contingency (pre-inspection shows clear)"
- You just added $10k to offer
- But you removed a contingency (seller value-add)
- You cut close timeline (seller priority: speed)
- You signaled credibility (pre-inspection = you're not looking for deal-breaker)

**Seller thinks:** "They went from $650 to $660 and removed inspection contingency and accelerated close. That's good. I'll accept $665k."

**Result:** You end at $665k instead of $680k ($15k savings) and close faster (seller happy).

## What Doesn't Work (Expensive Mistakes)

**Mistake 1: "I'll pay $650k *if* you fix the plumbing"**

Seller hears: "You're responsible for repairs." Sellers hate this (adds legal liability). They counter-reject offer entirely.

**Better:** "I'll pay $660k; I'll handle plumbing repair post-close."

---

**Mistake 2: Making first offer at bottom price**

You: $620k (15% below ask on $730k property)

Seller reaction: "This is disrespectful; I'm pulling the listing." Seller walks.

**Better:** $680k (7% below ask). Room to negotiate down, not insult seller.

---

**Mistake 3: Threatening to walk**

"If you don't come down to $660k, I'm pulling my offer."

Seller doesn't care if you have 5 other offers queued behind you. Threats make sellers dig in.

**Better:** "My best and final offer is $665k. Let me know your thoughts by [date]."

---

**Mistake 4: Renegotiating after inspection**

You: Agreed at $665k. Inspection finds $12k defects. You counter: "OK, $655k or I walk."

Seller feels trapped; lawsuit risk ensues. Also: Seller has already mentally closed the deal; re-opening negotiations feels like betrayal.

**Better:** Inspect BEFORE making offer. Bake findings into initial offer.

## The Real Example: Parc Riviera, $725k asking

**You:** Looking at 4-bed, asking $725k. Market comp suggests $695-710k.

**Pre-offer homework:**
- [ ] Get pre-approval (key: removes financing contingency)
- [ ] Get pre-inspection ($1,000; reveals major defects)
- [ ] Get contractor quotes (plumbing crack, ceiling repair)
- [ ] Market research (3 comps sold $695-720k in past 3 months)
- [ ] Seller research (listed 8 weeks; likely frustrated)

**Pre-inspection findings:**
- Plumbing leak in wall: $10k repair estimate
- Ceiling crack: $2k
- Paint: standard age-related, cosmetic
- **Total: $12k**

**Offer strategy:**
- Opening: $680k (6% below ask)
- Reason: "Market comp ($695-710k) + inspection findings ($12k) = justified below-ask offer"
- Pre-approval letter attached (removes financing risk)
- Close timeline: 21 days (seller wants fast exit)
- Contingencies: inspection only (pre-inspection done + clear on structure), no financing (pre-approved)

**Seller counter:** $710k (meets your comp research)

**Your counter:** "Inspection found $12k plumbing issue; combined with market comps, my best offer is $695k, 21-day close, no contingencies."

**Seller thinks:** 
- Market comps DO support $695-710k
- You already inspected (not looking for deal-breaker)
- You're pre-approved (no financing risk)
- You want 21-day close (they value speed for relocation)
- Your offer is clean (no contingencies = easy close)

**Seller accepts:** $700k

**Result:** You saved $25k (from $725k ask to $700k) through:
1. Inspection findings ($10k anchor)
2. Market comp research ($10k)
3. Removed contingencies ($5k perceived value)

## The Checklist: Before Making Your Offer

- [ ] Pre-approval letter (removes financing contingency)
- [ ] Pre-inspection done; contractor quotes obtained
- [ ] Market comps researched (3-5 recent sales in building or neighborhood)
- [ ] Seller situation known (timeline, motivation for selling)
- [ ] Timeline clear (how fast can you actually close?)
- [ ] Contingencies mapped (which ones are necessary, which can you remove?)
