# Tenant Buyout: When to Pay, How Much, and How to Avoid Overpaying

You're selling. Tenant has 2 years left on lease. You have two options: wait out the lease (6-month sale delay, rental discount on sale price), or buy them out now (upfront cost, empty possession premium). Most sellers overpay by 30-50% because they negotiate emotionally instead of strategically.

## The Economics

**Scenario: $600k property, tenant on $2,800/month lease, 24 months remaining**

**Option A: Wait out the lease**
- Property with tenant: sells at $550k (15% rental discount)
- Tenant continues paying rent 24 months: $67,200 gross rent
- Your net: $550k + $67.2k = $617.2k
- Timeline: 24 months

**Option B: Buy out tenant now, sell empty**
- Empty possession property: sells at $600k
- Buyout cost: X (to be determined)
- Your net: $600k - X
- Timeline: 6 months

**Breakeven:** $600k - X = $617.2k → X = -$17.2k (you can't afford to buy out!)

**Reality:** Empty possession is worth $50k, not $75k. Revised math:

**Option B revised:**
- Empty possession property: sells at $625k
- Buyout cost: X
- Your net: $625k - X

**Breakeven:** $625k - X = $617.2k → X = $7.8k

So you should pay maximum $7-8k to buy out. Anything more is overpaying vs. waiting out.

## What Tenants Actually Want (Ranked)

1. **Cash payout** (60% of tenants): Simple, clean exit. Pay $8-15k, they leave.
2. **Rent abatement** (25%): "Reduce my rent to $2k for final 3 months, I'll leave." Costs you $2,400, but it's disguised as a favor.
3. **Extra time to find housing** (10%): Extend lease 3-6 months. Sounds cheap; it's expensive (lost empty-possession window).
4. **Deposit refund + cash bonus** (5%): Complex; avoid.

## The Negotiation Strategy

**Week 1: Information gathering**
- What's tenant's financial situation? (Will $10k cash sway them vs. $20k?)
- Do they have 3 months' housing budget elsewhere? (If yes, $8-12k is reasonable)
- Is lease rock-solid (signed, notarized)? Or informal month-to-month? (Informal = leverage)

**Week 2: Anchor your offer**
- Open at $5k ("standard relocation fee for mutual termination")
- Tenant counters $20k ("I need time to find a place")
- You negotiate to $9-12k (realistic middle ground)

**Week 3: Sweeten with non-cash incentives**
- Offer to cover move-out cleaning ($1-2k value, costs you $300-500)
- Offer 2-week overlap if they need it (delay their exit, no cost to you)
- Offer reference letter for their next landlord ("Excellent tenant, on-time payer")

These feel generous to tenants but cost you nothing.

**Week 4: Close or escalate**
- If tenant won't budge below $18k, walk. Waiting out the lease is cheaper.
- Document everything in writing (email summary, not handshake)

## The Legal Gotcha

**Lease termination must be mutual.** You cannot unilaterally evict a tenant to create empty possession for sale (unless they breached). A forced eviction costs $5-15k and takes 3-6 months—longer than waiting out the lease.

**Solution:** Frame buyout as "mutual early termination" in writing. Both parties sign. Protects you legally and gives tenant psychological closure ("I chose to leave").

## Tenant Psychology: What Moves the Needle

- **Specific deadline:** "Move out by June 30" (vs. vague "soon")
- **Reason clarity:** "Selling property, need empty possession" (vs. "just need you out")
- **Fairness signal:** "Standard relocation fee is $8-12k; I'm offering $10k" (vs. "Take it or leave it $5k")
- **Exit help:** "I'll cover professional movers" (vs. "You're on your own")

Tenants who feel respected negotiate faster and cheaper than tenants who feel bullied.

## The Checklist: Should You Buy Out?

- [ ] Empty possession premium ($50-100k more than tenanted sale): ___
- [ ] Waiting cost (lost rent × months of delay): ___
- [ ] Net benefit of empty possession: (premium - waiting cost) = ___
- [ ] Maximum fair buyout: (net benefit × 80%) = ___
- [ ] Tenant's likely opening demand: ___

**If tenant's opening > your maximum fair buyout: Walk. Wait out the lease.**

## Real Example: When Buyout Made Sense

Property: $700k, tenant on $2.5k/month, 12 months left.

- Empty possession premium: $80k ($700k empty vs. $620k tenanted)
- Waiting cost: $30k (12 months rent loss, plus 6-month sale delay costs $15k holding)
- Net benefit: $50k
- Fair buyout: $40k (80% of net benefit)

Tenant opens at $35k. Deal at $38k. Seller nets $622k cash + $38k savings = $660k effective. vs. $620k waiting out. **Buyout was worth it.**
