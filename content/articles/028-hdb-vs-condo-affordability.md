# HDB vs Condo: Affordability Comparison and Financing Constraints

The choice between HDB (public housing) and Condo (private residential) comes down to affordability. HDB is cheaper upfront and has favorable financing; Condo is pricier but offers amenities and better appreciation potential.

## Price & Down Payment Comparison

**HDB Flat (4-room, non-central):**
- Typical resale price: $350–400,000
- Down payment (15%): $52,500–60,000
- Mortgage available: $297,500–340,000
- Financing rate: 2.3–2.8% (HDB or bank)

**Condo (2-bedroom, suburban):**
- Typical price: $500–600,000
- Down payment (20%): $100,000–120,000
- Mortgage available: $400,000–480,000 (80% LTV cap)
- Financing rate: 2.6–3.2% (bank only, no HDB)

**Cost difference:** Condo requires $40,000–60,000 more cash upfront, plus higher interest rates.

## Financing Constraints: LTV Caps

**HDB Loan (owner-occupied):**
- Max LTV: 80–90% (depends on age of flat and borrower's age)
- Minimum down payment: 10–20%
- Interest rates: Fixed at 2.6% (as of 2026) or based on SIBOR + 0.6%

**Bank Mortgage (first residential property):**
- Max LTV: 75–80% (private property)
- Minimum down payment: 20–25%
- Interest rates: Variable, based on SIBOR + bank margin (2.3–3.2%)

**Bank Mortgage (second residential property):**
- Max LTV: 45% (dramatic drop)
- Minimum down payment: 55%
- Interest rates: Higher (+0.5%), due to higher default risk

**Key insight:** If you're buying a 2nd property as a Condo, the 45% LTV cap makes it nearly impossible to finance without substantial cash.

## Monthly Payment & MSR/TDSR

**HDB Example: $350,000 flat, 20-year mortgage at 2.6%, $70,000 down**
- Loan: $280,000
- Monthly payment: $1,340
- MSR: $1,340 ÷ income (must be ≤ 30%)
- Income needed: $4,467/month minimum

**Condo Example: $500,000 unit, 25-year mortgage at 2.9%, $100,000 down**
- Loan: $400,000
- Monthly payment: $1,680
- MSR: $1,680 ÷ income (must be ≤ 30%)
- Income needed: $5,600/month minimum

**Takeaway:** To afford a Condo (same affordability), you need ~$1,100 more monthly income.

## Total Ownership Cost Over 30 Years

**HDB Scenario:**
- Purchase: $350,000
- Down payment: $70,000
- Mortgage (20 years): $1,340/month × 240 = $321,600
- Maintenance + property tax (30 years): $150/year × 30 = $4,500
- Total invested: $396,100
- Projected value (2.5% annual appreciation): $938,000
- Sale net (after 2.5% agent commission): $914,450
- **Net gain: $518,350**

**Condo Scenario:**
- Purchase: $500,000
- Down payment: $100,000
- Mortgage (25 years): $1,680/month × 300 = $504,000
- Maintenance + sinking fund (30 years): $400/year × 30 = $12,000
- Total invested: $616,000
- Projected value (3.5% annual appreciation): $1,528,000
- Sale net (after 2.5% agent commission): $1,489,300
- **Net gain: $873,300**

**Condo wins on total gain** ($873k vs $518k), but requires $220k more upfront investment ($616k vs $396k).

## Amenities & Quality of Life

**HDB:**
- No clubhouse or gym (some BTO projects add these)
- Parking: Common, flat rates (~$50/month)
- Maintenance by HDB (older flats may have aging issues)

**Condo:**
- Facilities: Gym, pool, function rooms, concierge
- Parking: Included or optional (~$100–300/month)
- Maintenance: Managed by MC (Joint Management Body); strata costs $300–600/month

**HDB advantage:** Cheaper upkeep, predictable costs.

**Condo advantage:** Better amenities, stronger community feel (for some).

## Resale Speed & Market Dynamics

**HDB:**
- High demand, especially for well-located estates (Tampines, Jurong, Bishan)
- Resale time: 2–6 months typical
- Buyer pool: Broad (upgraders, 1st-timers, investors)
- Price power: Limited; HDB prices cluster by estate and flat type

**Condo:**
- Lower buyer pool but higher prices attract investors
- Resale time: 3–12 months (more variable)
- Buyer pool: Narrower (upgraders, expatriates, investors)
- Price power: Higher (unique units, views, developer brand)

**HDB advantage:** Faster sales, less negotiation friction.

**Condo advantage:** Room to negotiate (fewer comparable units).
