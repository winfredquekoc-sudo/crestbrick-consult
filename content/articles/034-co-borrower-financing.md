# Co-Borrower Financing: Joint Mortgages and When They Help

Adding a co-borrower (spouse, parent, partner) to your mortgage doubles your income for qualification purposes. It's a powerful tool if one person's income alone isn't enough.

## How Co-Borrower Qualification Works

**Standard MSR/TDSR (single borrower):**
- Income: $5,000/month
- Max affordable mortgage: $1,500/month (30% MSR limit)
- Loan amount: ~$300,000 @ 2.6%

**With co-borrower:**
- Borrower A income: $5,000
- Borrower B income: $4,000
- **Combined income: $9,000**
- Max affordable mortgage: $2,700/month (30% MSR limit)
- Loan amount: ~$540,000 @ 2.6%
- **Increase in borrowing power: +$240,000**

## Co-Borrower Scenarios

**Scenario 1: Spouse (most common)**
- Both husband and wife take the mortgage
- Both incomes count toward MSR/TDSR
- Both are liable for the debt if one defaults

**Scenario 2: Parent co-signs**
- Adult child wants to buy but income is just under the threshold
- Parent adds their income to help qualify
- Parent is jointly liable; mortgage appears on parent's credit

**Scenario 3: Business partner or friend**
- Less common; banks scrutinize closely to ensure real financial relationship

## Tax Implications

**Spouse joint mortgage:**
- No tax issues; standard arrangement
- Both get CPF housing withdrawal entitlements (if first-time buyer)

**Parent as co-borrower:**
- Parent's income counts for qualification
- But: If parent passes away, co-borrower liability doesn't end immediately; estate must clear the liability
- If parent is co-owner (not just co-borrower), property is part of parent's estate (affects will/inheritance)

## Benefits of Co-Borrower

**Benefit 1: Higher loan amount** (as shown above: +$240k)

**Benefit 2: Lower interest rate** (some banks offer co-borrower discounts)

**Benefit 3: Easier qualification** (dual income stability)

## Risks & Downsides

**Downside 1: Joint liability**
- If you default, bank can pursue both borrowers
- Co-borrower's credit score is affected if payments are missed

**Downside 2: Harder to exit**
- If you want to buy a 2nd property, co-borrower's existing debt counts against their TDSR for the new property
- Makes it hard for the co-borrower to qualify for their own property later

**Downside 3: Marriage/relationship breakdown**
- If spouses divorce, sorting out mortgage liability is complex
- Courts typically award the property to one spouse; the other's name stays on the mortgage (creates ongoing liability)

## Exit Strategy: Removing a Co-Borrower

**Can you remove a co-borrower from an existing mortgage?**

**Generally, yes, but it's complex:**
1. Refinance the mortgage in the remaining borrower's name only
2. Remaining borrower must qualify on their own income (may require a smaller loan)
3. Co-borrower must be formally released by the bank
4. This triggers refinancing costs (~$2,000–3,000)

**Timing:** Typically after 5+ years when loan has been paid down and remaining borrower's income is stable/risen.
