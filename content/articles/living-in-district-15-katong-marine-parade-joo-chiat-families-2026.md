# Living in District 15: Katong, Marine Parade, Joo Chiat for Families in 2026

*By Winfred Quek | Crestbrick Pte Ltd (CEA R073319H | L31010886H)*

---

Most families who ask me about District 15 frame it as a lifestyle question. The peranakan shophouses, the hawker centres, the beach proximity. That is real, and it matters. But if you are spending $1.4 million to $2.5 million on a home here, the lifestyle should not be the main reason you buy. The fundamentals need to hold up too.

This article walks through what families actually need to know before committing to D15: schools by name and distance, transport realities post-Thomson-East Coast Line, property price ranges by type, and whether the investment case is strong enough to justify the premium over, say, D16 or D19.

---

## Why Families Choose District 15

District 15 sits along Singapore's southeastern coast, covering Marine Parade, Katong, Siglap, and Joo Chiat. For families with school-age children, it checks several boxes that matter when you look beyond the Instagram aesthetic.

First, the school network is genuinely strong within a tight radius. You are not relying on crossing into another planning area for a reputable primary school. Second, East Coast Park gives children actual outdoor space, not just mall food courts. Third, the private property stock here is primarily low-rise and mid-rise, meaning you are more likely to get a ground-level unit with a yard or a larger floor plate than in, say, Orchard or Bugis.

The argument against D15 has historically been transport. That has changed materially with the Thomson-East Coast Line (TEL) Phase 4 opening, which brought Marine Parade MRT and Marine Terrace MRT to the district in 2024. Families who previously relied entirely on the ECP or buses now have direct rail access to the CBD, Orchard, and Woodlands.

## Schooling Landscape: What Parents Need to Know

This is where most families start, and rightly so. Phase 2A and Phase 2B proximity for Primary 1 registration makes location a material factor in school access.

**Tao Nan School** (Marine Parade) is one of the most sought-after primary schools in D15. It sits within Marine Parade estate and is within 1 km of many Marine Parade HDB flats and condominiums along Marine Parade Road and Amber Road. Places here are competitive. If Tao Nan is a priority, proximity to its 1-km radius should be a non-negotiable filter in your property search.

**Haig Girls' School** is located along Haig Road in the Katong subzone. It draws families from across D15. Single-sex school culture, strong DSA track record. Worth noting that girls-only schools tend to have slightly less competition at Phase 2B than co-ed options in the same area.

**CHIJ Katong Primary** sits near Still Road and draws from the Joo Chiat and Katong belt. Catholic mission school with an established parent community. Again, the 1-km proximity rule applies, and the streets feeding into this school include Tembeling Road, Joo Chiat Place, and sections of East Coast Road.

**Victoria School** (boys, secondary) is a feeder concern rather than a primary school target, but families with sons should note it as a downstream option. Several primary schools in D15 have established DSA pipelines into Victoria.

**Dunman High School** (along Tanjong Rhu Road, technically on the D15 fringe bordering D14) is a top autonomous school with a dual O-Level and IB track. Families buying in D15 with long-horizon schooling plans often factor this in.

One honest note: school proximity guarantees nothing beyond registration priority. You should verify 1-km and 2-km boundaries directly with MOE before purchasing, as boundaries are reviewed periodically.

## Lifestyle and Amenities

East Coast Park gives children 15 km of usable outdoor space: cycling paths, beach access, barbecue pits, and hawker stalls at East Coast Lagoon Food Village. For families, this kind of everyday infrastructure ages well.

Katong and Joo Chiat are walkable in a way most of Singapore is not. East Coast Road and Joo Chiat Road have wet markets, independent cafes, and enrichment centres sitting side by side. Parkway Parade and i12 Katong cover the practical mall needs without requiring a trip to Orchard.

The Thomson-East Coast Line has rebalanced D15's transport significantly. Marine Parade MRT and Marine Terrace MRT connect the district directly to the TEL spine. From Marine Parade, Marina Bay is roughly 15 minutes and Orchard under 25. That is comparable to many OCR addresses that command lower prices. If you drive, the ECP gives fast CBD access outside peak hours but factor in the actual commute from the specific street you are considering, not just the general district label.

## Property Landscape: HDB, Private, and PSF Ranges

**HDB in D15** is concentrated in Marine Parade estate, one of Singapore's older HDB towns. These flats are mature estate units, many now 40 to 50 years old. Five-room flats along Marine Parade Road have transacted in the $750,000 to $900,000+ range in recent years. Lease considerations matter here given the age of stock. If you are an HDB upgrader looking at this estate specifically, run the lease decay numbers before committing. See the [HDB Upgrader Guide 2026](#) for a full framework on what to check.

**Private condominiums** in D15 span a wide range. Older freehold and 999-year leasehold projects along Amber Road, Meyer Road, and the Tanjong Katong belt typically transact at $1,800 to $2,400 psf depending on condition, size, and remaining lease term. Newer projects post-2015 tend to cluster above $2,200 psf. Verify current transacted prices against URA Realis before anchoring to any figures in this article.

**Landed housing** exists in D15 but the supply is thin and the price quantum high. Joo Chiat semi-detached houses and terraces have traded above $4 million in recent cycles. Not the entry point for most HDB upgraders.

**New launches and notable 2026 projects:** The D15 pipeline remains tight on large-scale new launches. Emerald of Katong (Joo Chiat vicinity) has been a significant launch in the Katong belt, generating strong buyer interest and competitive balloting. If you are considering new launches in D15, the supply constraint itself is a signal, fewer new projects means resale and sub-sale transactions carry more weight in price discovery. Keep an eye on URA's pipeline data for any upcoming GLS or en-bloc sites in the district.

## The Investment Angle: Rental Yield and Capital Growth

District 15 does not offer the highest gross yields in Singapore. Typical gross yields for private condominiums here run between 2.5% and 3.5% for a 3-bedroom unit, depending on project age and lease tenure. Freehold units on Amber Road and Meyer Road tend to sit closer to 2.5% gross given higher purchase prices. Verify current rental transactions on URA Realis before anchoring to any figure.

Where D15 earns its premium is capital preservation through scarcity. The district is largely built out, with limited new supply incoming. Freehold land in an established coastal belt with reputable primary schools tends to hold its price floor through property cycles better than OCR mass-market condos.

For couples considering structuring ownership across two names to manage ABSD exposure on a future purchase, the freehold stock in D15 makes a viable long-term hold. The [Decoupling Singapore Guide](#) covers the mechanics of separating ownership without triggering a sale.

D15 is not a high-yield play. If rental income is your primary objective, other districts will serve you better. If you want a family home that holds value, sits near good schools, and benefits from improving transport infrastructure, the case here is defensible.

## Should You Buy in D15 in 2026?

The decision comes down to what you are optimising for.

If school proximity is the primary driver, map the schools you want against the 1-km radius and search within that boundary. Do not buy at the edge of the radius and assume you will qualify. Confirm current boundaries directly with MOE before committing.

If you are an HDB upgrader moving to your first private property, the entry price at $1.6 million to $2 million for a D15 condo is workable depending on your household income and CPF position. Run the TDSR numbers before you fall in love with a unit. The [HDB Upgrader Guide 2026](#) walks through the full cash outlay and CPF considerations.

If you are buying as a second property and managing ABSD exposure, the freehold nature of much of D15's private stock makes it a more suitable long-term hold than a 99-year leasehold condo in a lower-demand district.

D15 rewards families who think in decades, not years. The price already reflects the schools, the lifestyle, and the improved transport. There is no hidden value here. What you are buying is quality of life with reasonable capital protection over a long hold.
