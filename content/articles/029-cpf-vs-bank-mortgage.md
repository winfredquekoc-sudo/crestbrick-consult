# CPF vs Bank Mortgage: Which Financing Is Cheaper?

First-time HDB buyers face a choice: use CPF OA (Ordinary Account) savings to pay down the mortgage, or minimize CPF use and borrow more from the bank. The decision affects both monthly cost and long-term wealth.

## The Two Financing Approaches

**CPF-Heavy Approach:**
- Use CPF OA savings for maximum down payment
- Borrow remaining from bank/HDB at lower loan amount
- Monthly payment: Lower
- CPF OA tied up: Not earning returns

**Bank-Heavy Approach:**
- Minimize CPF use (only mandatory Central Provident Fund withdrawal)
- Borrow more from bank at higher loan amount
- Monthly payment: Slightly higher
- CPF OA remains invested: Keeps earning 2.5%/year interest

## Interest Rate Comparison

**HDB Loan Rate:** Fixed at 2.6% (as of 2026)

**Bank Mortgage Rate:** Floating, typically SIBOR + 0.6–0.8%, currently ~2.3–3.2%

**Bank vs HDB:** Banks are often 0.3–0.4% cheaper than HDB loans. For a $250,000 loan over 25 years, that's a $20,000–30,000 savings.

## Scenario: $350,000 HDB Purchase, $70,000 Down Payment Available

### Option A: CPF-Heavy ($50k CPF + $20k cash down)

- CPF down payment: $50,000
- Cash down payment: $20,000
- Loan from bank: $280,000 at 2.6% (HDB rate)
- Monthly payment: $1,340
- CPF OA left after purchase: $5,000 (minimal buffer)
- CPF OA growth foregone: 0% (used up)

### Option B: Bank-Heavy ($10k CPF + $60k cash down)

- CPF down payment: $10,000 (minimum mandatory for retirement)
- Cash down payment: $60,000
- Loan from bank: $280,000 at 2.5% (bank rate, 0.1% cheaper)
- Monthly payment: $1,330
- CPF OA left after purchase: $45,000 (earning 2.5%/year)
- CPF OA growth over 25 years: $45,000 × (1.025)^25 = $120,000

**Comparison:**
- Option A: Monthly payment $1,340, CPF depleted
- Option B: Monthly payment $1,330 (saves $10/month), CPF grows to $120k
- **Option B wins:** $10/month savings + $120k CPF growth over 25 years

**Caveat:** Option B requires $60k liquid cash; Option A is easier if you're cash-constrained.

## CPF Withdrawal & Retirement Impact

**Key constraint:** CPF you withdraw for housing doesn't grow for retirement.

**Example:**
- CPF OA balance at age 35: $100,000
- Withdraw $50,000 for HDB down payment
- Remaining: $50,000
- At age 55, that $50,000 grows to: $50k × (1.025)^20 = $81,600
- What if you hadn't withdrawn? $100k × (1.025)^20 = $163,200
- **Retirement impact: $81,600 less at age 55**

This is a significant opportunity cost. Unless you're cash-strapped, minimize CPF withdrawal.

## When to Use More CPF

**Use CPF aggressively if:**
- You have limited liquid cash and high debt elsewhere (credit cards, personal loans)
- Interest rates are rising (pay off expensive debt first)
- You're concerned rates will spike and you won't afford the mortgage later
- You plan to earn substantial salary increases and can replenish CPF later via voluntary contributions

**Use CPF minimally if:**
- You have good liquid cash reserves ($60k+)
- You're young (35–40) and CPF earnings matter for retirement
- Bank rates are significantly cheaper than HDB rates (0.3%+ difference)
- You can invest the freed-up capital at returns > CPF rate (2.5%)

## CPF Re-contribution Strategy

Some buyers use a hybrid approach:

**Year 1–5:** Minimize CPF use; build liquid cash reserves and invest aggressively.

**Year 6–10:** Pay down the bank mortgage faster using increased salary; re-contribute to CPF from bonuses to rebuild retirement savings.

**Benefit:** You get the flexibility of a larger bank mortgage initially, then aggressively pay it down and reinvest in CPF as your income grows.

## Scenario: Rising Interest Rates

**2026: Bank rate at 2.5%; HDB rate at 2.6%**
- Option: Minimize CPF, maximize bank borrowing

**2027: Fed hikes; bank rate rises to 3.5%; HDB rate to 3.2%**
- Your bank mortgage floats up; HDB rate moves slower
- **Regret:** You should have used more CPF when rates were low

**Lesson:** If you're uncertain about rate direction, use more CPF to lock in the current rate environment.
