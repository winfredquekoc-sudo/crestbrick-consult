ABSD Singapore: Frequently Asked Questions for Foreigners, SPRs, and Expats

By Winfred Quek, Crestbrick Pte Ltd, CEA R073319H. Updated June 2026.

If you are not a Singapore Citizen and you are thinking about buying residential property here, Additional Buyer's Stamp Duty is the number that will shape every other decision. The rates are high, the rules have nuances, and the consequences of getting the structure wrong are expensive. This FAQ covers the questions I hear most often from foreigners on employment passes, SPRs planning to upgrade, and expat couples trying to figure out what they actually owe.

Use the ABSD calculator on this site to compute your exact liability once you know your buyer profile and target price.

1. What is the ABSD rate for foreigners buying property in Singapore in 2026?

60%. That is the rate for any residential property purchase by a foreigner who is not a Singapore Citizen, not a Singapore Permanent Resident, and not a national of a country covered by a qualifying Free Trade Agreement.

The rate applies to the full purchase price or the market valuation, whichever is higher. There is no concession for first-time buyers, no tiered structure, and no cap. If you are a foreigner buying a SGD 2 million condominium, you pay SGD 1.2 million in ABSD on top of Buyer's Stamp Duty (BSD). That SGD 1.2 million must be funded in cash and paid to IRAS within 14 days of exercising the Option to Purchase.

This rate has been at 60% since 27 April 2023, when it was doubled from 30% as part of a coordinated government cooling measure. As of June 2026, there has been no change and no public signal from the government of a pending revision.

Which nationalities count as foreigners?

Any individual who is not a Singapore Citizen and not a Singapore Permanent Resident is classified as a foreigner for ABSD purposes. This includes people on Employment Passes, Personalised Employment Passes, Dependent Passes, Long-Term Visit Passes, and any other immigration status short of SPR or citizenship. Nationality on your passport is what determines your ABSD classification, not where you live or how long you have been in Singapore.

2. What is the ABSD rate for Singapore Permanent Residents (SPRs) in 2026?

SPRs pay ABSD on a tiered basis depending on how many residential properties they own at the point of purchase:

  • First residential property: 5%
  • Second residential property: 30%
  • Third and subsequent residential properties: 30%

On a first property, 5% is manageable. On a SGD 1.2 million condominium, that is SGD 60,000. It adds to your upfront cost but it does not necessarily kill the investment case if the property is well-chosen.

The second property is where the numbers shift dramatically. At 30%, a SGD 1.5 million second property carries SGD 450,000 in ABSD. That is before BSD, legal fees, and any renovation costs. The return you need to generate over your holding period to simply break even on stamp duty is significant, which is why most SPRs who want to own two properties spend considerable time looking at ownership structure before they buy the second one.

How does ABSD work for an SPR couple buying together?

When two buyers are involved in a single transaction, ABSD is assessed at the rate applicable to the buyer who attracts the higher rate, applied to the entire purchase price. It is not blended or split.

For two SPRs who both own no property, buying together as first-time buyers, the rate is 5%. If one of those SPRs already owns a property and the other does not, the transaction is treated as a second-property purchase by the SPR who already owns, and the rate steps up accordingly. Both buyers are assessed together, not independently.

3. Do Americans and EU nationals get any ABSD exemption in Singapore?

Yes, under specific Free Trade Agreements. Nationals of the following countries are treated at parity with Singapore Citizens for ABSD purposes on their first residential property purchase:

  • United States of America (under the US-Singapore FTA)
  • All European Union member states (under the EU-Singapore FTA)
  • Iceland, Liechtenstein, Norway, and Switzerland (under the EFTA-Singapore FTA)

Parity with a Singapore Citizen on a first purchase means 0% ABSD. On a second property, the SC second-property rate of 20% applies, not the standard 60% foreigner rate. That is a meaningful saving at any price point.

What you need to know before relying on an FTA exemption

Three things that catch people out. First, the remission applies to individuals, not entities. A US company buying Singapore residential property does not benefit from the FTA provision. Second, it is based on the nationality shown on your passport, not your country of residence. A German national resident in Singapore qualifies. A British national who has spent their career in Germany does not. Third, you must actively apply for the remission with IRAS and provide supporting documentation. It is not applied automatically at the point of signing. If you believe you qualify, get this confirmed with IRAS before you commit to a purchase.

UK nationals are not covered. Neither are Australians, Canadians, Chinese, Indian, Malaysian, Japanese, or South Korean nationals. All of those pay the standard 60% foreigner rate.

4. Can EP, PEP, or DP holders buy private property in Singapore?

Yes. There is no restriction on foreigners purchasing private residential property in Singapore, including condominiums and apartments. Employment Pass holders, Personalised Employment Pass holders, Dependent Pass holders, and LTVP holders can all buy. The restriction that applies to foreigners is on landed residential property, which requires separate approval from the Singapore Land Authority.

For ABSD purposes, pass holders who are not citizens or SPRs are treated as foreigners. A French national on an EP in Singapore pays 0% ABSD on a first property (EU FTA). A British national on an EP pays 60% on any residential purchase. A Filipino national on a DP pays 60%. Your pass type is irrelevant to ABSD. Your passport nationality and residency status are what matter.

Does PR status change my ABSD position?

Significantly, yes. Moving from foreigner status to SPR reduces the ABSD on a first property from 60% to 5%. That is a SGD 660,000 saving on a SGD 1.2 million condominium. If you are on a path to PR and you are planning to buy residential property in Singapore, the timing of your PR application relative to your property purchase is worth thinking through carefully. PR status is also the gateway to purchasing resale HDB flats, which have no ABSD for SPRs buying their first public housing unit.

6. What is the difference between ABSD and BSD, and how is the total calculated?

Buyer's Stamp Duty (BSD) is the baseline transaction tax paid by every buyer of any property in Singapore, regardless of nationality or how many properties they own. It is computed on a progressive scale based on purchase price. ABSD is an additional tax layered on top, applied selectively based on buyer profile.

Both are computed on the higher of the purchase price or the property's market valuation. If you negotiate SGD 1.8 million for a condominium and the bank's valuation comes back at SGD 1.85 million, both BSD and ABSD are calculated on SGD 1.85 million.

A worked example for an SPR buying their first property at a negotiated price of SGD 1.5 million, market value assessed at SGD 1.5 million:

  • BSD: approximately SGD 44,600 on a progressive scale across the purchase price bands
  • ABSD at 5%: SGD 75,000
  • Total stamp duty: approximately SGD 119,600

That SGD 119,600 is due within 14 days of signing the OTP. It cannot be financed through a mortgage. You need to have those funds liquid and available when you exercise the option.

For a foreigner buying the same property at SGD 1.5 million:

  • BSD: approximately SGD 44,600
  • ABSD at 60%: SGD 900,000
  • Total stamp duty: approximately SGD 944,600

This is why cash planning matters before signing anything. See the glossary entry on BSD and ABSD if you want to understand how the progressive BSD bands work in detail.

7. Are there any ABSD remissions available to foreigners or SPRs?

There is no ABSD remission available to foreigners who are not nationals of an FTA-covered country. If you are a British, Australian, or Canadian national buying residential property in Singapore, 60% is the rate and there is no legitimate structure that eliminates it.

For SPRs, two scenarios involve remission provisions worth knowing.

SC-SPR married couples

An SC and SPR who are legally married can apply for ABSD remission under specific conditions set by IRAS. The conditions are detailed and the remission is not guaranteed. You need to apply, provide documentation, and meet the eligibility criteria. If you are in this situation, verify the current conditions directly at iras.gov.sg before you structure any purchase around this remission. Do not assume it applies to your transaction without confirming first.

SC-SC married couples

Two Singapore Citizens buying jointly as their first property together also have a remission path available if one of them individually already owns a property. The structure and eligibility for this remission is also subject to IRAS conditions. Again, verify before relying on it.

Outside of FTA nationalities and these specific couple remissions, ABSD is not negotiable and not waivable.

8. What is decoupling and is it a viable strategy for SPRs in 2026?

Decoupling is an ownership restructuring approach used by couples who jointly own one property and want to purchase a second without paying the second-property ABSD rate on the entire purchase.

The mechanics work like this. A couple jointly owns Property A. One co-owner transfers their share to the other. After the transfer, one person owns Property A outright. The person who transferred their share now has no residential property in their name. When they then buy Property B, they are a first-time buyer for ABSD purposes and pay the first-property rate rather than the second-property rate.

For an SPR couple, the saving is the difference between 5% (first property rate) and 30% (second property rate) applied to the purchase price of Property B. On a SGD 1.5 million purchase, that is SGD 375,000 versus SGD 75,000 in ABSD: a saving of SGD 300,000.

For an SC and SPR couple, the SC buys Property B in their sole name as a second property at the SC rate (20%), while the SPR who transferred their share in Property A now has no property and can buy separately in their own name at the SPR first-property rate (5%).

There are costs and conditions attached. The transfer of a share in an existing property attracts BSD on the transferred value. If there is a mortgage on Property A, the loan structure will need to be reviewed because the bank's security changes when ownership changes. The TDSR (Total Debt Servicing Ratio) rules still apply to the purchasing party independently. And if the transferred property was bought under HDB rules, different restrictions may apply.

Decoupling is not a universal solution. Whether it makes sense depends on the specific numbers, your financing position, and your holding plans for both properties. A proper cash flow model comparing the costs of decoupling versus simply paying the higher ABSD is what drives the decision, not a rule of thumb. Read more in the property glossary entry on decoupling.

9. Why do foreigners still buy Singapore property despite the 60% ABSD rate?

The honest answer is that for most foreigners at the price points that dominate the Singapore new launch market, the ABSD does not kill the decision for the subset of buyers who are primarily buying for security of tenure, lifestyle, or diversification rather than short-term capital return.

The most common profile among foreigners who continue to buy is a high-income expat couple on a long-term stay in Singapore, or a foreign HNWI who wants Singapore freehold property as part of a broader asset allocation. For them, the primary considerations are: Singapore's political stability, the quality of the property and the building, the freehold or 999-year leasehold tenure structure, and the relative safety of the Singapore Dollar as a store of value. The 60% ABSD is a known, fixed cost. They model it in, and if the asset still makes sense at the total acquisition cost, they proceed.

Freehold tenure is a significant draw specifically because Singapore's leasehold stock decays over time (see lease decay in the glossary), and genuinely freehold new launch condominiums in Singapore are rare and concentrated in specific locations. Foreign buyers who identify the right asset, at the right price, in the right tenure structure, and intend to hold for 10 or more years, can build a case for the purchase even with a 60% entry cost.

This does not mean 60% ABSD is easily absorbed. On a SGD 3 million property, that is SGD 1.8 million in ABSD alone. The math needs to work. But it is not accurate to say foreigners simply are not buying, because some are, selectively and strategically.

10. What are the most common ABSD mistakes made by foreigners and SPRs?

In working with buyers from outside the Singapore Citizen profile, a few mistakes come up repeatedly.

Not accounting for ABSD in the upfront cash plan

ABSD cannot be paid through a mortgage. It must come from your cash or CPF (for those with CPF savings). Foreigners, in particular, often have no CPF balance, which means the full ABSD amount must be liquid cash available within 14 days of exercising the OTP. A SGD 1.5 million purchase with 60% ABSD means SGD 900,000 due within 14 days, on top of the initial option fee and any deposit. Buyers who have not planned this carefully can find themselves in a tight position.

Structuring a joint purchase without thinking about the rate

When two buyers of different profiles buy together, ABSD is assessed at the higher rate applied to the full purchase price. An SC who already owns one property buying jointly with a foreigner spouse pays at the 60% foreigner rate on the entire purchase, not a blended rate. This is a very common source of unexpected cost, and it is entirely avoidable by getting the ownership structure right before signing the OTP.

Assuming FTA exemption applies automatically

US, EU, and EFTA nationals are eligible for ABSD remission but it is not applied without a formal application to IRAS. Buyers who assume they will be automatically charged at the SC rate and do not apply in advance risk missing the window and paying the full foreigner rate. The IRAS process requires documentation and must be handled as part of the conveyancing process.

Missing the 14-day payment window

ABSD must be paid within 14 days of exercising the OTP or signing the Sales and Purchase Agreement for a new launch. There is no grace period and late payment attracts penalties and interest. If you are buying from overseas and your conveyancing process is not properly coordinated, this deadline can slip. Use a Singapore conveyancing lawyer and make sure they are tracking this deadline for you from the moment you exercise the OTP.

Buying on the wrong basis of ABSD computation

ABSD is computed on the higher of purchase price or market valuation. If you negotiate a below-valuation purchase price, your ABSD is still based on the valuation, not the price you paid. This means in a negotiated or distressed purchase scenario, you may owe more ABSD than a straight percentage of the price you agreed would suggest. Always get a sense of the current valuation before you sign.

11. What should foreigners and SPRs do before committing to a Singapore property purchase?

A few things that genuinely make a difference before you sign anything.

Run your total acquisition cost with real numbers, not estimates. BSD plus ABSD plus legal fees plus agent commissions plus any renovation budget gives you the full cash requirement at acquisition. The ABSD calculator and the stamp duty calculator on this site can get you to the stamp duty component quickly.

Determine your exact buyer profile and any FTA eligibility before approaching a property. If you hold a US, EU, or EFTA passport, verify your FTA remission eligibility with IRAS before structuring a purchase.

If you are buying jointly with a spouse or partner, model both the separate and joint ownership scenarios before signing. The difference in ABSD can be substantial depending on each person's existing property count and residency status.

If you are an SPR considering a second property, run the decoupling analysis properly. The question of whether to decouple, and which asset to restructure, has a quantitative answer based on your specific numbers. It is worth the effort of modelling it before you commit to a strategy.

And if you are a foreigner factoring 60% ABSD into a purchase decision, be honest about your holding horizon. The ABSD is sunk at entry. The only way it becomes tolerable from a return perspective is if the property appreciates significantly or the yield during the hold period is strong. A proper cash flow model covering BSD, ABSD, loan costs, rental income, and a realistic exit assumption will tell you if the investment makes sense at current prices.
