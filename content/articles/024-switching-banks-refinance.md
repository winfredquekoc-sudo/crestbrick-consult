# Switching Banks: Early Repayment Penalties and Break-Even Analysis

Switching from one bank to another for your HDB mortgage can save substantial money if interest rates have dropped or a competitor offers a better deal. However, early repayment penalties can eat into your savings, so you need to calculate your true break-even before committing.

## Why Switch Banks?

**Rate difference:** Your current bank charges 2.8%; a competitor offers 2.2%. Over 20 years, that 0.6% difference on a $250k loan costs you ~$30,000 extra.

**Service quality:** Your current bank has poor customer service; another offers better support or digital tools.

**Loan restructuring:** You want to change tenure or shift from floating to fixed, and your current bank won't negotiate.

## The Costs of Switching

**Early repayment penalty:** 0.25–1% of the outstanding loan, or a flat fee ($500–2,000), depending on your bank and loan agreement.

**Legal fees:** Discharge of old mortgage + registration of new mortgage: $800–1,500.

**Bank processing/admin fees:** $300–600.

**Valuation fee (if required):** $500–800.

**Total switch cost:** Typically $2,200–4,000.

## Example: Switching from Bank A to Bank B

**Your situation:**
- Current loan: $250,000 with Bank A at 2.8% (20 years remaining)
- Current monthly payment: $1,380
- Bank B offers: 2.2% rate on refinance

**Bank A's early repayment penalty:**
- 0.25% of $250,000 = $625

**Total switching costs:**
- Penalty: $625
- Legal: $1,200
- Processing: $400
- Valuation: $600
- **Total: $2,825**

**Bank B refinance (new loan: $250,000 at 2.2% over 20 years):**
- New monthly payment: $1,270
- **Monthly savings: $110**

**Break-even calculation:**
- $2,825 ÷ $110 = 25.7 months
- **Break-even: ~26 months (~2.2 years)**

After 26 months, you're in pure savings territory. Over the remaining 17.7 years, you save $110 × 212 months = $23,320.

## Penalties Explained

**Percentage-based penalty:**
- **0.25% of outstanding loan:** Most common, usually lowest penalty.
- **0.5–1% of outstanding loan:** Less common, charged by some banks for early exit.
- **Percentage of unearned interest:** Rare; calculated as a percentage of interest you would have paid if you didn't prepay. Can be expensive (1–3% of the outstanding loan balance).

**Flat fee:**
- **Fixed penalty (e.g., $1,000):** Some banks charge a flat amount regardless of loan size.
- **Sliding scale:** Penalty decreases the longer you've held the loan. E.g., $2,000 in year 1, $1,000 in year 3, $0 after year 5.

**No penalty:**
- Increasingly rare, but some banks (especially during rate wars) offer penalty-free refinancing for qualified borrowers. Check your loan agreement.

## Hidden Costs of Bank Switching

**Lost rewards or rebates:** If your current bank offers cashback or loyalty rebates, you forfeit them by switching.

**Rate lock-in period:** If you've locked in a fixed rate, breaking it early may incur an additional penalty.

**Processing delays:** Switching typically takes 4–6 weeks. During this time, you might miss market opportunities or face rate fluctuations.

## When Switching Is Worth It

**Rate drop > 0.4%:** The monthly savings offset switching costs quickly.

**Planning to stay 2+ years:** Your break-even is within 24 months, leaving time to recoup the cost.

**No penalty or low penalty:** 0.25% penalty is acceptable; anything over 1% makes switching less attractive unless the rate drop is large.

**Stable income:** You can afford the (possibly higher) payments if rates rise again before your fixed rate expires.

## When NOT to Switch

**Less than 2 years remaining:** Switching costs don't pay off; you're leaving soon anyway.

**Rate drop < 0.3%:** Monthly savings are minimal ($30–50); costs exceed benefits.

**High penalty (1%+):** Break-even extends beyond 3 years; risky if you move or rates drop further elsewhere.

**Fixed rate locked in:** If you have a fixed rate that expires soon, wait out the lock-in period before refinancing.
