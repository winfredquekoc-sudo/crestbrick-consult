# Rate vs Tenure Trade-off: Refinancing Decisions Explained

When you refinance, you don't just negotiate a lower rate—you also choose a new loan tenure (payment term). Shorter tenures (20 years) mean higher monthly payments but less total interest. Longer tenures (25–30 years) mean lower payments but more interest paid overall.

## Example: $250,000 Remaining Loan Balance at 2.2% Rate

**Option A: Refinance to 20-year tenure**
- Monthly payment: $1,268
- Total interest over 20 years: $54,320
- Monthly cash impact: Higher

**Option B: Refinance to 25-year tenure**
- Monthly payment: $1,070
- Total interest over 25 years: $70,960
- Monthly cash impact: Lower (~$200/month savings)

**Difference:** Choosing 25 years costs an extra $16,640 in interest, but saves $200/month in cash flow for 5 years.

## When to Shorten Tenure (20 years)

**If you:**
- Have stable, high income
- Plan to stay in the property 20+ years
- Don't have competing financial needs (kids' education, aging parents)
- Want to be mortgage-free by a target age (e.g., 55–60)

**Trade-off:** Higher monthly payment, but you're debt-free sooner and pay less total interest.

## When to Extend Tenure (25–30 years)

**If you:**
- Have tight cash flow
- Need flexibility for other investments or life events
- Plan to invest the "saved" money elsewhere at returns > mortgage rate (e.g., stock market, property)
- Prefer monthly flexibility over total interest cost

**Trade-off:** Lower monthly payment, but you carry debt longer and pay more interest overall.

## The Math: Should You Invest the Savings?

**Scenario:** You refinance and choose 25 years instead of 20 years, saving $200/month.

**If you invest that $200/month at 5% annual return:**
- 5 years: $12,800 invested
- Less: Extra interest paid over 5 years (25-year vs 20-year loan): $13,900
- **Net loss: $1,100** (because mortgage interest is higher than typical investment returns)

**BUT if market returns are 8%+** (or you deploy the capital into a higher-yielding property), the math changes. Then extending the tenure might make sense.

## Example: Refinancing at Midpoint

**Year 13 of a 25-year mortgage:**
- Original balance: $300,000
- Remaining balance: $180,000
- Original rate: 3.0%
- New market rate: 2.2%

**Refinance options:**

**A) Maintain 25-year tenure (12 years remaining)**
- New tenure after refinance: 12 years (finish at original end date)
- New payment: $1,300/month
- No extension, no change to payoff date

**B) Extend to 25-year tenure**
- New tenure after refinance: 25 years
- New payment: $830/month
- Payoff date moves from year 25 to year 38
- Extra interest: ~$45,000
- Monthly savings: $470

**C) Shorten to 15-year tenure**
- New tenure after refinance: 15 years
- New payment: $1,350/month
- Payoff date moves forward to year 28
- Total interest: ~$30,000
- Monthly increase: $50 vs option A

Most people choose **Option A** or **B** depending on cash flow needs.

## The Psychology of Tenure Extension

**Beware:** Banks encourage tenure extension (longer loans = more interest revenue). When refinancing, ask yourself:

- Do I NEED lower payments, or do I just prefer them?
- Am I using the freed-up cash flow productively (investing, saving for emergencies)?
- Or am I just spending it on lifestyle?

If it's the latter, keep your original tenure to force yourself to pay off faster.
