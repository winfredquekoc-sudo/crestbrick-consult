# MSR/TDSR Affordability Checklist: Calculate Your Maximum HDB Price

Singapore's **MSR (Monthly Servicing Ratio) and TDSR (Total Debt Servicing Ratio)** are the gates that determine how much you can borrow for an HDB flat. Most buyers don't understand these rules and end up disappointed when their dream flat doesn't pass qualification.

This checklist walks through the calculation.

## MSR Rule: 30% Maximum

**MSR = Monthly HDB Loan Payment ÷ Monthly Gross Income**

Maximum: 30%

**Example:** If you earn $5,000/month, your HDB loan payment cannot exceed $1,500/month.

## TDSR Rule: 60% Maximum

**TDSR = (HDB Loan + All Other Debts) ÷ Monthly Gross Income**

Maximum: 60%

**Example:** If you earn $5,000/month, your total debt payments cannot exceed $3,000/month.

## Worked Example

**Your Profile:**
- Monthly gross income: $6,000
- Existing car loan payment: $800/month
- Existing credit card debt: $0
- Target HDB price: $500,000
- Target down payment: 15% ($75,000 from CPF + cash)
- Mortgage needed: $425,000 at 2.6% over 25 years

**MSR Calculation:**
- Mortgage payment: $425,000 ÷ (300 months at 2.6%) ≈ $1,940/month
- MSR = $1,940 ÷ $6,000 = 32.3%
- **MSR FAILS** (32.3% > 30% limit)

**What to do:** Increase down payment to $100,000 (reduces mortgage to $400,000; payment drops to $1,825/month; MSR = 30.4%, still fails by 0.4%). Increase to $110,000 down (mortgage $390,000; payment $1,770; MSR = 29.5%, **PASSES**).

**TDSR Calculation (using $110k down, $390k mortgage):**
- HDB payment: $1,770
- Car loan: $800
- Total debts: $2,570
- TDSR = $2,570 ÷ $6,000 = 42.8%
- **TDSR PASSES** (42.8% < 60% limit)

**Maximum affordable price with $110k down:** ~$500,000

**If you had kept car loan:** Same. But if you also had a personal loan (+$500/month), TDSR = ($1,770 + $800 + $500) ÷ $6,000 = 54.2%, still passes. But any higher debt and you're capped.

## Checklist for Your Affordability

1. **Calculate monthly gross income:** Salary + bonus (if guaranteed) + spouse's salary + rental income. Exclude CPF deductions, taxes, variable pay.

2. **List all existing debts:** Car loan, credit card, personal loan, other mortgages. Calculate monthly payment for each.

3. **Target HDB price:** What price range interests you?

4. **Estimate MSR for your target price:**
   - Loan amount = (Target price × 0.8) - your down payment
   - Estimated monthly payment = Loan ÷ (number of months for 2.6% interest at your tenure)
   - MSR = Payment ÷ Monthly gross income
   - **Does it pass 30%?** If no, increase down payment or lower target price.

5. **Estimate TDSR for your target price:**
   - Total monthly debt = HDB payment + all other debts
   - TDSR = Total debt ÷ Monthly gross income
   - **Does it pass 60%?** If no, reduce other debts first, then re-calculate HDB affordability.

6. **Get HDB pre-qualification:** If MSR/TDSR pass, HDB will pre-qualify you for your target loan amount (final qualification happens after flat selection).

## Income Recognition in MSR/TDSR

**What counts as monthly gross income:**
- Base salary (full amount)
- Spouse's salary (if married/co-borrower)
- Guaranteed bonuses (annual ÷ 12, but lender may discount by 50%)
- Rental income (gross rent × 0.8, accounting for vacancy)
- Self-employed income (based on 1–2 years of tax returns, usually conservative)

**What does NOT count:**
- CPF deductions (already excluded from gross)
- Investment returns (stocks, crypto—too volatile)
- Expected raises (until they're in your tax return)
- Bonus if you just started a job (need 1-year history)

**Rental income gotcha:** If you own a rental property, that income counts toward affordability BUT eating into your TDSR ceiling. E.g., if you earn $5k salary + $1k rental (= $6k total), and rental income uses $600 toward TDSR, your HDB payment ceiling is lower than if you had $6k pure salary.

## Common Mistakes

1. **Forgetting existing debts in TDSR:** Car loan reduces your HDB affordability. Pay it off first.
2. **Assuming bonus is guaranteed income:** Most lenders count only base salary (or 50% of bonus if proven over 2+ years).
3. **Not accounting for interest rate rises:** Today's 2.6% might be 3.5% in 2-3 years; plan conservatively.
4. **Overestimating down payment available:** CPF limits apply; don't assume you can use all your OA.
5. **Counting spousal income without co-borrower status:** If spouse co-borrows, their debt counts in TDSR too. If spouse is only providing down payment, their income doesn't count.
