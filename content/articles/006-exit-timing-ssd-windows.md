# Exit Timing Strategy: Stamp Duty Windows and When to Sell

When you sell a property, you pay **Seller's Stamp Duty (SSD)** based on your holding period. Shorter holds face punitive rates; longer holds face lower rates. Understanding SSD windows allows you to plan exits strategically and save $10,000-50,000+ per property.

## SSD Rates (2026)

For HDB flats:
- Held < 1 year: 12% of sale price
- Held 1-3 years: 8%
- Held 3-5 years: 5%
- Held 5+ years: 0% (no SSD)

For private property:
- Held < 1 year: 16%
- Held 1-3 years: 8%
- Held 3-5 years: 5%
- Held 5+ years: 0%

**Key insight:** Holding 5+ years eliminates SSD entirely.

## Example: HDB Flat Sale Strategy

**Scenario:** You bought a HDB flat for $350,000 at age 30. It appreciates to $450,000 by year 4.

**Option A: Sell at Year 3**
- Sale price: $450,000
- SSD (5%): -$22,500
- Agent commission (2.5%): -$11,250
- Net proceeds: $416,250

**Option B: Sell at Year 5**
- Sale price: $450,000 (assume no additional appreciation)
- SSD (0%): $0
- Agent commission (2.5%): -$11,250
- Net proceeds: $438,750

**Difference:** $22,500 saved by waiting 2 years

If the flat appreciates $40,000 over that 2-year wait (3% annually), your sale price becomes $490,000, and your net after SSD savings is $478,750—a $62,500 gain from waiting. The key: 5-year hold eliminates SSD; even without appreciation, waiting pays.

## HDB First Sale Exception

Many Singaporeans don't know this: **If you're selling your first HDB flat that you owner-occupied, you may be exempt from SSD if you meet certain conditions:**

- First HDB flat purchase
- Owner-occupied (not rented)
- Selling to upgrade to another HDB or private property

If you qualify, you owe **0% SSD regardless of holding period** (as long as you're upgrading, not speculating).

This changes the strategy completely. For first-time buyers, SSD is not a penalty—it's a non-issue if you're upgrading.

## Private Property SSD Strategy

Private property SSD is harsher (16% if held < 1 year; 8% if held 1-3 years). Most investors plan for at least a 3-year hold to hit the 5% rate, and ideally 5+ years to eliminate SSD.

Flipping private property within 3 years faces 5-8% SSD—unsustainable for most investors unless appreciation is >8% annually.

## Timing Windows: The Upgrade Play

Many Singaporeans follow a progression: **HDB → Private Condo → Larger Private Home**

Each step triggers SSD considerations:

**Step 1: HDB to Private Condo (Year 5+)**
- Sell HDB at 5+ year mark: 0% SSD (first sale exception or holding window)
- Buy private condo: Subject to ABSD (if not citizen) or BSD (2%)
- Net cost: BSD only, no SSD upside

**Step 2: Private Condo to Larger Home (Year 8+)**
- Sell private condo at 5+ year mark: 0% SSD
- Buy larger private home: Subject to ABSD (20% if citizen, 2nd property) or BSD
- Net cost: ABSD (if upgrading second residential) + agent commission

## Coordinating SSD & ABSD

If you're upgrading from private to private (e.g., from a $1M condo to a $1.5M house):

**Timing matters:**
- If you hold 5+ years, you eliminate SSD on the sale ($25,000+ saved)
- But you trigger ABSD on the new purchase ($300,000 for citizen, 20% of $1.5M, if it's your 2nd residential property)
- Net cost: $300,000 ABSD - $25,000 SSD savings = $275,000 additional cost vs. a direct exchange with no intermediate holdings

The lesson: long holding periods save SSD but trigger ABSD. Plan for both.

## Checklist: Exit Timing Strategy

- [ ] How long have you held the property? (Check if SSD is 12%, 8%, 5%, or 0%)
- [ ] If holding < 5 years, is upgrading (first sale exception applies for HDB?)
- [ ] What's the potential appreciation over 2-3 more years? (Worth waiting for SSD savings?)
- [ ] If upgrading, what ABSD rate applies on the new purchase?
- [ ] Are you coordinating multiple sales? (Order matters for ABSD; sell one before buying next)
