# CPF Refund Real Numbers: Case Study of an HDB Sale & Cash Withdrawal

Most Singaporeans don't understand what happens to their CPF when they sell an HDB flat. They assume: sell flat, get the sale proceeds, take CPF out. Reality is more complex. CPF refunds come with rules and retirement account preservation requirements.

This case study uses real numbers to show exactly what happens.

## The Scenario

**Seller Profile:** Mr. Tan, age 50, selling his HDB flat.

**Flat Details:**
- Purchase price 10 years ago: $300,000
- Current sale price: $500,000
- Remaining mortgage: $280,000 (10 years into 35-year loan)
- Property tax owed: $200

**CPF Account Status:**
- Ordinary Account (OA) balance: $120,000
- Special Account (SA) balance: $95,000
- Medisave Account (MA) balance: $55,000
- Total CPF: $270,000

**Sale Costs:**
- Agent commission (2.5%): $12,500
- Lawyer fees: $1,500
- Property tax: $200
- Total costs: $14,200

## Step 1: Calculate Net Sale Proceeds

Gross sale price: $500,000
Less: Mortgage repayment: -$280,000
Less: Agent commission: -$12,500
Less: Lawyer fees: -$1,500
Less: Property tax: -$200
**Net cash from sale (before CPF): $205,800**

## Step 2: CPF Refund Calculation

When Mr. Tan sells, HDB automatically refunds his CPF contributions from the sale proceeds. But there's a catch: CPF contributions to the Special Account (SA) cannot be withdrawn until age 55 unless the property is sold (withdrawal allowed at sale, but only after retirement sum is set aside).

**CPF Refund Mechanism:**

1. **OA refund:** Full $120,000 OA balance is refunded (no restrictions). Mr. Tan can take this all out.

2. **SA refund:** $95,000 SA balance, but here's the rule: **CPF mandates that a retirement sum (currently SGD 211,800 in combined OA+SA) must be set aside**. Mr. Tan's combined OA+SA is $215,000. After setting aside the minimum retirement sum of $211,800, only $3,200 of SA can be withdrawn.

3. **MA balance:** Medisave ($55,000) stays locked in the Medisave Account for healthcare. It's not refunded upon flat sale—it can only be used for medical expenses or remain for retirement healthcare.

**CPF Refund Summary:**
- OA refund available: $120,000
- SA refund available: $3,200 (after retirement sum set-aside)
- MA refund available: $0 (locked for healthcare)
- **Total CPF refund: $123,200**

## Step 3: Final Cash to Mr. Tan

Net sale proceeds: $205,800
Plus: CPF refund: $123,200
**Total cash to Mr. Tan: $329,000**

But wait—Mr. Tan still owes taxes.

## Step 4: Tax Impact (For Rental Property Scenario)

If this flat was owner-occupied (which it is for Mr. Tan), there's **no capital gains tax**. Singapore doesn't tax gains on owner-occupied residential property.

If it were a rental property or investment flat held under a company, there would be corporate income tax on the gain. But for an owner-occupied HDB, no tax.

**Net cash to Mr. Tan (after all taxes): $329,000**

## Step 5: Scenarios If Mr. Tan Wants to Buy Again

**Scenario A: Buy another HDB flat for $450,000**

Down payment needed: $45,000 (10%)
Cash available from sale: $329,000
After down payment: $284,000 remaining

Mr. Tan can use this $284,000 to:
- Pay for a new HDB flat purchase (as a "second property," different subsidy rules apply)
- OR invest the remainder if a smaller flat is chosen

**Scenario B: Upgrade to Private Condo for $1.2M**

Mr. Tan now wants a private condo and must pay ABSD (he's a citizen buying his second residential property).

ABSD for citizen, 2nd property: 20% of purchase price
ABSD on $1.2M: $240,000

Down payment (20% for private): $240,000
ABSD: $240,000
Total cash needed: $480,000

Cash available: $329,000
**Shortfall: $151,000**

Mr. Tan needs additional cash or financing to bridge the gap. This is the real-world check: property upgrade calculations must account for ABSD and financing availability.

## The Retirement Account Preservation Trap

Here's the critical insight many sellers miss: **CPF doesn't fully refund at property sale**. A portion (the retirement sum) stays locked.

If Mr. Tan were age 65+ (past retirement), he could withdraw from both OA and SA freely. But at age 50, the retirement sum rules apply, and he leaves $208,600 locked in the CPF retirement account until age 55 (and beyond, depending on his withdrawals and reinvestment).

This isn't a loss—it's forced retirement savings. But it's crucial for cash-flow planning: if you sell a flat at age 50 expecting to access all your CPF, you'll be disappointed. A portion stays locked.

## Comparison: If Mr. Tan Rented Instead of Sold

What if Mr. Tan kept the flat and rented it out instead of selling?

**Annual rental income:** $24,000 (typical for a $500k flat area, 4.8% yield)
**Annual costs:** $3,000 (property tax, maintenance reserve)
**Net annual cash flow:** $21,000
**CPF refund at sale:** $0 (no refund until sale)

Over 10 years of continued ownership:
- Rental cash flow: $210,000
- Potential appreciation: Flat appreciates 2-3%/year = $100,000-150,000 unrealized gain
- CPF stays locked: Can't access for other investments

Versus selling now:
- Immediate cash: $329,000 (accessible immediately)
- Ability to reinvest in multiple assets

The trade-off: Cash liquidity now vs. long-term wealth from property appreciation and rental yield.

## Real-World Tax Scenario (Rental Income)

If the flat were rented, the sale would have different tax treatment:

**Seller's cost base:** $300,000
**Sale price:** $500,000
**Gain:** $200,000

**Singapore income tax on real property gains:** ~5-20% depending on holding period and seller's tax bracket (progressive rates). Short holding periods are taxed at higher rates; longer holdings at lower rates.

Estimated tax: $20,000-40,000 (rough; actual depends on income bracket and holding period).

**After-tax cash to seller (rental scenario):** ~$289,000-309,000 (vs. $329,000 for owner-occupied because no gain tax).

This illustrates why many investors hold investment properties: the tax savings outweigh the rental yield in some cases.

## Takeaway Checklist for Flat Sellers

- [ ] Get a written estimate from your lawyer of all sale costs (commission, legal, taxes)
- [ ] Check your CPF OA/SA/MA balances via your CPF account online
- [ ] Understand that SA may be partially locked if you haven't hit retirement age
- [ ] Calculate net proceeds (sale price - costs - mortgage - CPF set-aside)
- [ ] Plan for ABSD or taxes if buying another property
- [ ] If upgrading, confirm financing is available for the new property price + ABSD
- [ ] Speak with a tax accountant if the flat was ever rented (rental income = taxable gain)
