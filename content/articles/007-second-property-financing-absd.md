# Second Property Financing: ABSD, LTV Caps, and Cash Stack Requirements

Buying a second residential property as a Singapore citizen is dramatically different from the first. **ABSD jumps to 20%, LTV drops to 45% (from 80%), and rates increase.** Most second-time buyers underestimate the cash needed and face surprise rejections.

## The Second Property Penalty

**ABSD:** 20% (compared to 0% on first property)

**LTV Cap:** 45% (compared to 75-80% on first property)

**Interest Rate Premium:** +0.5-1.0% above first property rates

**Loan Tenure:** 30 years (can be longer to manage monthly payment, but interest cost rises)

## Example: $1,000,000 2nd Property Purchase

**Financing Calculation:**
- Purchase price: $1,000,000
- ABSD (20%): $200,000 (due within 14 days of SPA)
- Maximum LTV available: 45% = $450,000 mortgage
- **Required down payment: $550,000 ($1M - $450k)**

**Total cash needed upfront:**
- Down payment: $550,000
- ABSD: $200,000
- Lawyer/agent fees: $25,000
- **Total: $775,000**

**Monthly payment (if borrowing $450k at 3.2% over 25 years):**
- ~$2,160/month

**Comparison to first property:**
- Same $1M price, first property would require ~$200k down + minimal ABSD ($0-5% depending on scheme)
- Same loan ($450k) on first property would have 80%+ LTV available, meaning only $200k down needed
- Second property requires $350k more cash upfront for the same monthly payment

## The Cash Crunch Scenario

Many buyers assume they can leverage their first property's equity. Here's why that often fails:

**You own:** HDB flat worth $400,000, mortgage-free
**You want to buy:** 2nd property at $900,000
**Cash in hand:** $200,000

**Math:**
- ABSD on $900k: $180,000
- Down payment needed (55%): $495,000
- **Total cash needed: $675,000**
- **Shortfall: $475,000**

**"Solution" many consider:** Refinance the HDB to unlock equity

**Reality:** Refinancing an HDB flat to pull out cash for a private property purchase is complex. HDB rules restrict cash-out refinances for investment purposes. You'd likely need a private housing loan or a personal loan—both more expensive than HDB rates.

**Better solution:** Sell the HDB, recover the equity, and use that to buy the 2nd property. But then you lose the first property.

## The 45% LTV Reality Check

The 45% LTV cap is the tightest constraint on second property purchases.

**Why 45%?** Banks are conservative on second properties (higher default risk) and regulatory pressure (macroprudential rules) cap leverage.

**Impact:** You cannot just "take on more debt." A $1M property can only support a $450k mortgage, period. No exception, no negotiation.

This forces one of three outcomes:

1. **Reduce target price:** Instead of $1M property, buy $500k property (supports $225k mortgage, requires $275k down).
2. **Increase down payment source:** Partner with co-buyer, refinance existing property, or delay purchase until you save more.
3. **Accept rental property status:** If the property is investment/rental (not owner-occupied 2nd residence), some banks relax LTV to 50-60%. But rental has higher rates and tighter qualification.

## Rental Property Financing (Alternative)

If you frame the 2nd property as an **investment/rental property** (not owner-occupied 2nd residence), financing rules change:

- **ABSD:** Still 20% for citizens
- **LTV Cap:** 50-60% (less restrictive than owner-occupied)
- **Interest rates:** Higher (+0.5-1.0% premium vs. first residential)
- **Qualification:** Based on rental yield as well as income (must demonstrate positive cash flow)

**Trade-off:** Rental property financing is easier to approve but rates are higher. Monthly payment on a $450k rental loan might be $2,400+ vs. $2,160 for owner-occupied.

## The Multi-Property Ladder Strategy

Sophisticated investors use this approach:

**Year 1:** Buy first HDB flat, owner-occupied, $300k total cost
**Year 6:** Upgrade to private property (1st residential), owner-occupied, $500k total cost, sell HDB
**Year 12:** Buy 2nd private property as rental/investment, $600k, structured as rental for better LTV
**Year 18:** Refinance or sell one of the properties to fund 3rd acquisition

Each step strategically manages ABSD, LTV caps, and available capital. Jumpers who try to buy 2nd private property immediately after 1st often run into cash-flow and LTV walls.

## Checklist: 2nd Property Financing

- [ ] **Target price:** What's your ideal 2nd property price?
- [ ] **ABSD cost:** 20% × target price = total ABSD due
- [ ] **Maximum mortgage:** 45% × target price
- [ ] **Required down payment:** 55% × target price
- [ ] **Other costs:** Lawyer ($1,500-2,000), agent commission (2-2.5%), inspection ($1,500-2,000)
- [ ] **Total cash needed:** Down payment + ABSD + costs
- [ ] **Cash on hand:** How much do you have available?
- [ ] **Shortfall:** If any, where will it come from? (Savings? Refinance existing? Delay?)
