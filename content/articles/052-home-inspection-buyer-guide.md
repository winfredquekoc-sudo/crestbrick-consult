# Home Inspection: The Buyer's Reality Check

You're buying a $700k condo. Inspection costs $1,000-$1,500. Inspector finds: paint cracks, misaligned door frame, outdated appliances. Your realtor says "this is normal age wear." Should you walk? Renegotiate? Ignore it?

The answer depends on whether findings are major ($10k+ repairs) or cosmetic ($1-3k). Here's how to separate real issues from noise.

## The Three Categories of Findings

**Category 1: Structural/Major (Walk-away or renegotiate)**

These threaten safety or require $10k+ repairs:
- Foundation cracks (subsidence, water seepage)
- Structural steel visible (rusting, degradation)
- Roof leaks (visible water damage, mold)
- Electrical: outdated wiring, panel overload, fire hazard
- Plumbing: corrosion, concealed leaks (burst pipes in wall)
- HVAC: compressor failure, no cooling/heating
- Mold (extensive, not cosmetic)
- Termite/pest infestation (structural damage)

**Repair cost: $10,000-50,000+**

**Action:** Renegotiate price down by 50-75% of repair cost, or walk.

---

**Category 2: Moderate (Use for negotiation)**

These are fixable but annoying, $3-10k repairs:
- Ceiling cracks (cosmetic or minor structural)
- Bathroom/kitchen wear (fixtures need replacement)
- Flooring damage (need refinishing or replacement)
- Window seals broken (condensation between panes)
- Paint peeling (cosmetic, $500-1k)
- Door frames misaligned (cosmetic, fixable)

**Repair cost: $3,000-10,000**

**Action:** Use as negotiation anchor ("Inspection found $8k in fixes; reduce price by $5k").

---

**Category 3: Cosmetic (Ignore)**

These are minor, $0-3k, or obvious age wear:
- Paint cracks/stains
- Light fixtures dated
- Appliances old but functional
- Flooring color not modern
- Kitchen/bathroom not renovated recently
- Carpet stains
- Caulking needs refresh

**Repair cost: $0-3,000**

**Action:** Don't mention in negotiation. Don't walk over this.

---

## When Inspection Kills a Deal (Legitimately)

**You should walk if:**

1. **Structural: Foundation cracks + water damage**
   - Subsidence risk (land is sinking)
   - Repair cost: $30-100k (reinforcement, drainage)
   - Not negotiable; walk

2. **Electrical: Major code violations**
   - Outdated wiring, fire hazard
   - Repair cost: $15-30k (rewire)
   - Insurance may refuse to cover; walk

3. **Plumbing: Extensive corrosion + active leaks**
   - Burst pipes in walls (hidden damage)
   - Repair cost: $20-40k (replumb entire unit)
   - Mold risk; walk

4. **HVAC: System completely failed**
   - No cooling/heating; compressor dead
   - Repair cost: $8-15k (new system)
   - Negotiable but major; walk unless significant price reduction

5. **Mold: Extensive black mold**
   - Structural damage behind walls
   - Health risk (respiratory issues)
   - Repair cost: $15-50k (remediation)
   - Walk

---

## When Inspection Should NOT Kill a Deal

**Don't walk over:**

1. **Cosmetic issues** (paint, old appliances, fixtures)
2. **Normal age wear** (wear on flooring, kitchen dated)
3. **Minor ceiling cracks** (not foundation-related)
4. **Outdated look** (just means you'll renovate)

These are factored into price already. Use for negotiation, not as walk triggers.

## The Inspection Process: What Happens

**Step 1: Hire inspector ($1,000-1,500)**

Don't use: Online home inspection ($200, cursory)

Do use: Licensed structural engineer or comprehensive home inspector (Singapore Building & Construction Authority registered)

Cost: $1,000-1,500 for 2-3 hour inspection

Timeline: Book 1-2 weeks in advance

---

**Step 2: Inspector examines (3-4 hours)**

They look at:
- Foundation (cracks, water damage)
- Structural (beams, columns, visible steel)
- Roof (leaks, flashing, condition)
- Electrical (panel, wiring, outlets, fire hazard)
- Plumbing (leaks, water pressure, drainage)
- HVAC (compressor, ductwork, performance)
- Appliances (age, function, safety)
- Windows/doors (seals, operation, weatherproofing)
- Interior (mold, water stains, cracks)
- Exterior (paint, balcony, common areas)

**They do NOT:**
- Move furniture
- Fully access concealed areas (behind walls)
- Break anything open
- Test hidden plumbing extensively

---

**Step 3: Report (20-40 pages, $1,500+ in detail)**

Report categorizes findings:

- **Critical:** Immediate action needed (foundation crack, electrical fire hazard)
- **Major:** Needed within 1-2 years (roof leak, HVAC failure)
- **Minor:** Cosmetic or age-related (paint, fixtures, outdated)

---

**Step 4: You decide: renegotiate, walk, or accept**

## The Real Example: Parc Riviera 4-bed, $725k asking

**You hire inspector.** Inspector finds:

1. **Ceiling crack in master bedroom** (hairline, cosmetic)
   - Category: Cosmetic
   - Fix cost: $0 (cosmetic only)
   - Action: Ignore

2. **Water stain on living room ceiling**
   - Inspection detail: No active leak; old stain
   - Category: Moderate (may indicate past leak)
   - Fix cost: $2k (paint over, monitor)
   - Action: Negotiate, not walk

3. **Electrical panel is outdated, but functioning**
   - Inspector note: "Safe but not modern; would recommend upgrade in 5 years"
   - Category: Moderate
   - Fix cost: $5-8k (new panel)
   - Action: Negotiate

4. **Bathroom renovation needed**
   - Fixtures 15+ years old, fixtures function
   - Category: Cosmetic (you expected this)
   - Fix cost: $8-15k (new bathroom)
   - Action: Ignore (factored into price already)

5. **HVAC compressor near end of life**
   - Inspector: "Works now, may fail in 2-3 years"
   - Category: Major
   - Fix cost: $8-10k (new compressor)
   - Action: Negotiate

**Total repair estimate: $23-25k**

**Your negotiation:**
- Original offer: $680k (asking $725k)
- Seller counters: $710k
- Your counter: "Inspection found $20k in issues (water stain, electrical, HVAC). I'll do $695k."
- Seller: "Fine, $705k"
- Result: You saved $20k in negotiation room (even though inspection wasn't a walk-trigger)

## When NOT to Do Inspection

**Skip inspection if:**
- You're buying from a developer (new building; warranty covers defects)
- You're buying in a pristine brand-new building (<1 year old)
- You can't get access (seller won't allow)

**Still do inspection if:**
- Resale property, any age
- Private property (landlord-owned)
- Building >10 years old
- You're buying below market (may explain why)

## Contractor Quotes: Using Inspection to Negotiate

Once inspector identifies findings, get contractor quotes for 2-3 major items:

**Example: $20k plumbing issue**
- Inspector says: "Corrosion in main line; $20-25k to replumb"
- Get 2-3 contractor quotes: $18k, $23k, $20.5k
- Average: $20.5k
- Use in negotiation: "Average quote is $20.5k; reduce price by $15k"

**Seller thinks:** "Buyer has real quotes; $20k repair is legitimate. I'll accept 50% of repair cost ($10k reduction)."

vs.

**Without quotes:**
- You: "Inspection found plumbing issue; reduce price by $15k"
- Seller: "Inspection is just opinion; I'm not reducing $15k for unconfirmed problem"
- Negotiation stalls

**Lesson:** Contractor quotes are your proof. Use them.

## The Checklist: Inspection & Negotiation

- [ ] Hire licensed inspector (not online cursory check)
- [ ] Budget $1,000-1,500 for inspection
- [ ] Book inspection 1-2 weeks in advance
- [ ] Attend inspection; ask questions in real-time
- [ ] Get detailed written report (20+ pages)
- [ ] Identify Category 1 (walk-trigger) vs Category 2 (negotiate) vs Category 3 (ignore)
- [ ] Get 2-3 contractor quotes for major findings (Category 2 items >$5k)
- [ ] Use quotes in negotiation ("Average repair quote: $18k; I'll reduce offer by $12k")
- [ ] Don't renegotiate after agreement (negotiate upfront, based on inspection findings)
