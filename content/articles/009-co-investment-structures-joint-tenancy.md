# Co-Investment Structures: Joint Tenancy vs Tenancy in Common for Singapore Property

When two or more people buy property together, the ownership structure matters enormously for ABSD, estate planning, and dispute resolution. Most co-buyers don't understand the legal difference and stumble into the wrong structure.

## Joint Tenancy

**Definition:** All co-owners own the entire property equally. Upon death, the surviving co-owner automatically inherits the deceased's share (right of survivorship).

**Ownership:** Equal shares (2 owners = 50/50 each, regardless of capital contribution)

**ABSD Treatment:** If any co-owner is foreign, the entire property triggers ABSD (60% rate for the foreign co-owner's share, or full 60% if majority is foreign).

**Example:** 
- Partner A (citizen) + Partner B (foreign) buy $1M property
- A contributes $600k, B contributes $400k
- Ownership: 50/50 (equal by law, not by capital)
- ABSD: Full 60% applies (~$600k) because one co-owner is foreign

**Succession:** A dies. B automatically becomes sole owner. No probate, no will involved. Smooth but irreversible.

**Estate implications:** A's heirs have no claim to the property. It passes entirely to B by operation of law. Some families find this problematic (if B remarries, A's biological children have no claim to "their parents' asset").

## Tenancy in Common

**Definition:** Co-owners hold separate fractional shares (e.g., 40% and 60%). Upon death, the deceased's share passes to their heirs via their will, not to the surviving co-owner.

**Ownership:** Unequal shares permitted (based on capital contribution or agreement). Example: A owns 60%, B owns 40%.

**ABSD Treatment:** Each owner's share is assessed separately. If A is citizen buying 60% and B is foreign buying 40%, ABSD applies to B's 40% share only (possibly at a lower rate if B's stake is small).

**Example (same property):**
- A (citizen, 60% = $600k) + B (foreign, 40% = $400k)
- ABSD: 60% of B's $400k share only = $240k (NOT full 60% on entire property)

**Succession:** A dies. A's 60% share passes to A's heirs per A's will (or intestacy if no will). B's 40% remains with B. Clean division; heirs can negotiate buy-out, hold jointly with B, or partition and sell.

**Estate implications:** A's estate has control over who inherits (can leave to spouse, children, or charity). More flexible.

## ABSD Comparison: Critical Difference

For mixed-citizen/foreign co-ownership, **tenancy in common is significantly cheaper**.

**Joint Tenancy scenario:**
- $1M property, 50/50 A (citizen) + B (foreign)
- ABSD triggered on full $1M (60%) = $600k
- B's share of ABSD: $300k (half of total)

**Tenancy in Common scenario:**
- $1M property, 60% A (citizen) + 40% B (foreign)
- ABSD triggered on B's $400k share only (60%) = $240k
- B's full cost for ABSD: $240k (40% of total)

**Savings: $60,000 for B** by using tenancy in common instead of joint tenancy.

## Dispute Resolution & Partition

**Joint Tenancy:** Disputes are harder to resolve. If one co-owner wants to exit, they can force a partition sale (sell entire property, split proceeds 50/50 regardless of contribution mismatch). Ugly and costly.

**Tenancy in Common:** Cleaner exit. Co-owner can sell their fractional share to the other (buyout) or force partition based on their actual ownership percentage. If A owns 60% and B owns 40%, partition sale splits proceeds 60/40—aligned with actual investment.

## Which Structure to Choose?

**Joint Tenancy suits:**
- Married couples (automatic succession is natural)
- Same-generation partners planning life together (mutual protection)
- When co-owners want simplicity and no need for separate inheritance plans

**Tenancy in Common suits:**
- Mixed-citizen/foreign co-ownership (ABSD savings substantial)
- Unequal capital contributions (A invests $600k, B invests $400k; want shares to match)
- Multi-generational investments (grandparent + adult child; want child to inherit grandparent's share)
- Business partners or friends pooling capital (want clear exit paths)

## Practical Example: Friends Buying Together

**Scenario:** Friends A, B, C want to buy a $600k property together.
- A contributes: $300k (50%)
- B contributes: $200k (33%)
- C contributes: $100k (17%)

**Joint Tenancy outcome:** Regardless of money, each owns 33.33%. If A dies, B and C split A's share (16.67% each now). A's widow/heirs get nothing. Ugly.

**Tenancy in Common outcome:** A owns 50%, B owns 33%, C owns 17%. If A dies, A's heirs own A's 50% (can buy out B&C or hold as co-owner). Everyone's interests protected. Clear.

## Checklist: Choosing Your Structure

- [ ] Are all co-owners the same citizenship/PR status? (If mixed, tenancy in common cheaper for ABSD)
- [ ] Are capital contributions equal? (If not, tenancy in common is fairer)
- [ ] Do you want automatic right of survivorship? (Joint tenancy = automatic; tenancy in common = via will)
- [ ] How long will you co-own? (Temporary partnership → tenancy in common for clean exit; life partners → joint tenancy for simplicity)
- [ ] Have you drafted a co-ownership agreement? (Critical for tenancy in common to avoid disputes)
