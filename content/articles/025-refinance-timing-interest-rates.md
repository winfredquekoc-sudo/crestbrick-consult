# Refinance Timing: Interest Rate Cycles and When to Lock In

The best time to refinance isn't random—it's tied to broader economic cycles and central bank policy. Understanding these cycles helps you refinance at rates troughs, not peaks.

## What Drives HDB Mortgage Rates?

**SIBOR (Singapore Interbank Offered Rate):** The benchmark rate that most HDB and bank mortgages track. Moves 0.2–0.5% when the Monetary Authority of Singapore (MAS) or US Federal Reserve shifts policy.

**Bank prime lending rate:** Another benchmark; used by some mortgages. Also moves with MAS policy.

**Global interest rates:** US Fed rate hikes/cuts ripple into Singapore. When the Fed rises, SIBOR usually follows 6–12 months later.

**Inflation expectations:** High inflation → central banks raise rates. Low inflation → rates stay low or fall.

## Historical HDB Mortgage Rate Cycles

**2020–2021:** Near-zero rates; mortgages at 2.0–2.2%.

**2022–2023:** Fed rate hikes to combat inflation; HDB rates rose to 3.0–3.5%.

**2024–2026:** Rate stabilization; rates hover 2.3–2.8%.

**Lesson:** Rates move in 2–3 year cycles, not yearly. Refinancing too early (before a trough) wastes opportunity.

## Key Signals to Watch

**Federal Reserve policy:**
- Fed rate hikes → SIBOR rises 6–12 months later
- Fed rate cuts → SIBOR falls 3–6 months later
- Watch Fed press conferences and forward guidance

**MAS signals:**
- MAS Monetary Policy Statements (issued quarterly)
- Language like "neutral" or "accommodative" signals rate direction
- MAS acts more cautiously than Fed; look for lagged moves

**Yield curve:**
- A flat or inverted yield curve (short-term rates > long-term rates) signals recession risk and eventual rate cuts
- Steep yield curve signals growth and potential rate hikes

**Market consensus:**
- Bloomberg, Reuters, and bank forecasts on 12-month forward rates
- Use these as a guide; they're rarely perfect, but directionally useful

## Refinancing Strategies by Rate Cycle

**Phase 1: Rates Falling (Best time to lock in)**
- Signal: Fed cutting rates; SIBOR trending down
- Action: Refinance to a fixed rate or lock in for 2–3 years
- Benefit: You lock in the bottom rate before it reverses

**Phase 2: Rates Bottoming (Good time)**
- Signal: Fed/MAS on hold; no further cuts expected
- Action: Refinance if rates > 0.3% above your current rate
- Benefit: You get near-optimal rates; little room to fall further, but risk is small

**Phase 3: Rates Rising (Wait)**
- Signal: Fed hiking or on hold; inflation persists
- Action: Don't refinance. Rates will keep rising for 12+ months
- Benefit: Avoid locking in high rates; wait for the cycle to peak

**Phase 4: Rates Peaking (Prepare)**
- Signal: Fed signaling pause or pivot; market expects cuts within 6–12 months
- Action: Monitor closely. When cuts start, refinance immediately
- Benefit: You'll catch the peak-to-bottom move

## Example Timing Scenario

**Scenario:** It's Q1 2026. Fed rates are at 5.25–5.50%, stable for 6 months. Market consensus: rates to stay flat, possibly cut in late 2026.

**Your mortgage:** $300,000 at 3.2% (current rate)

**Q1 2026 decision:**
- Refinance offer: 2.9% (0.3% drop, not compelling; wait for Fed cuts)
- **Action: WAIT**

**Q3 2026:** Fed cuts rates by 0.25%; SIBOR starts falling.
- Refinance offer: 2.6% (0.6% drop, now compelling)
- **Action: REFINANCE IMMEDIATELY**

**Why wait?** Because waiting from Q1 to Q3, you caught a larger rate drop. The extra 0.3% savings over 20 years = $18,000+ in interest savings vs. locking in at 2.9% three months earlier.

## Rate Lock-In vs. Floating

**Fixed-rate refinance:** Rate locked for 2–3 years, regardless of SIBOR moves. Protects you if rates rise, but you can't benefit if rates fall (unless you refinance again, paying costs).

**Floating-rate refinance:** Rate moves with SIBOR + bank spread. Cheaper initially, but risky if rates jump 1%+ overnight.

**Optimal strategy:**
- If rates are falling or at a trough → lock in a fixed rate for 2–3 years
- If rates are rising → consider floating for 1–2 years, then refinance when they peak

## When NOT to Time the Market

**Psychological trap:** Waiting for the "perfect" rate is costly. If rates are 0.4%+ below your current rate and your break-even is under 2 years, refinance now instead of waiting for an extra 0.1% drop.

**Diminishing returns:** The difference between 2.3% and 2.2% is $100/month on a $250k loan—worth waiting for. The difference between 2.2% and 2.1% is $50/month—probably not worth delaying.
