# MSR/TDSR for Second Property: Why 2nd Mortgages Are Harder to Afford

Buying a second property is not just about saving an extra down payment—your existing debts suddenly matter. TDSR constraints can make a 2nd property purchase impossible unless your income is significantly higher.

## TDSR Constraint Tightening

**For 1st property:**
- TDSR: 60% of income max for all debts
- Your first HDB mortgage is typically the only major debt

**For 2nd property:**
- TDSR: Still 60%, BUT now it includes:
  - 1st property mortgage (still being paid)
  - 2nd property mortgage (new)
  - Car loan, credit cards, personal loans, etc.

**Example: 1st vs 2nd property**

**1st Property (age 30, income $6,000):**
- 1st HDB mortgage: $1,500/month
- Car loan: $600/month
- Credit card (estimated): $300/month
- **Total debt: $2,400/month**
- **TDSR: $2,400 ÷ $6,000 = 40%** ✅ Passes (under 60% limit)

**6 years later (age 36, 2nd property purchase, income $7,000):**
- 1st HDB mortgage (5 years into 25-year term): $1,480/month (still owed)
- Car loan (paid down): $200/month
- New 2nd property mortgage: $1,500/month (proposed)
- **Total debt: $3,180/month**
- **TDSR: $3,180 ÷ $7,000 = 45.4%** ✅ Still passes, but tight

**If you had added another car loan:**
- 1st HDB mortgage: $1,480/month
- Car loans (two cars): $1,000/month
- New 2nd property mortgage: $1,500/month
- **Total debt: $3,980/month**
- **TDSR: $3,980 ÷ $7,000 = 56.9%** ✅ Barely passes, $20 margin

**The risk:** Any income drop or interest rate rise, and you're over the limit.

## LTV Cap Tightening (2nd Property)

**1st property LTV:** 75–80% (borrow 75–80%, down 20–25%)

**2nd property LTV:** 45% (borrow 45%, down 55%)

**Impact:** A $600k 2nd property requires:
- Down payment: 55% = $330,000
- Mortgage: 45% = $270,000

**Compare to 1st property at $600k:**
- Down payment: 20% = $120,000
- Mortgage: 80% = $480,000

**Difference:** $210,000 MORE cash needed upfront for 2nd property (vs 1st property at same price).

## Scenario: Can You Afford a 2nd Property?

**Your situation (age 36):**
- Income: $7,000/month
- 1st HDB debt remaining: $1,480/month (15 years left on 25-year mortgage)
- Car loan: $200/month
- Target 2nd property: $500,000 (private condo)
- Available down payment: $200,000 (liquid cash + partial CPF withdrawal)

**Scenario A: Buy $500k condo with 45% LTV**
- Down payment: $200,000 (you have this)
- Mortgage: 45% = $225,000
- Monthly payment at 2.8% over 25 years: $1,075

**TDSR check:**
- 1st HDB: $1,480
- Car: $200
- 2nd property: $1,075
- **Total: $2,755**
- **TDSR: $2,755 ÷ $7,000 = 39.3%** ✅ Passes

**Scenario B: Try to buy $650k condo with 45% LTV**
- Down payment needed: 55% = $357,500 (you don't have this)
- Mortgage: 45% = $292,500
- Monthly payment: $1,395

**TDSR check:**
- 1st HDB: $1,480
- Car: $200
- 2nd property: $1,395
- **Total: $3,075**
- **TDSR: $3,075 ÷ $7,000 = 44%** ✅ Passes, but $130k more cash needed

**Reality:** Most 2nd-time buyers can't afford the same price point as 1st property at 80% LTV. They must:
1. Use more cash (which limits capital efficiency), OR
2. Accept a lower price point, OR
3. Wait 5+ years until income grows significantly

## Strategies to Stretch 2nd Property Affordability

**Strategy 1: Pay down 1st property debt first**
- Target: Reduce 1st HDB to $1,000/month (pay aggressively for 3–5 years)
- This frees ~$480/month for 2nd property debt

**Strategy 2: Pay off car loan**
- Eliminate $200/month car debt
- Use freed-up $200 + income growth for 2nd property

**Strategy 3: Secure higher income (promotion, bonus, spouse starts working)**
- Income grows from $7k to $8k
- New TDSR ceiling: $4,800/month for all debts
- Allows $1,300/month 2nd property payment (vs. $1,075)

**Strategy 4: Wait for 1st property to appreciate, then refinance**
- Year 6: 1st HDB worth $450k (up from $350k)
- Refinance for $300,000 (up from $280,000 currently owed)
- Use $20k equity extraction for 2nd property down payment
- Refinanced payment: ~$1,430 (still manageable)
